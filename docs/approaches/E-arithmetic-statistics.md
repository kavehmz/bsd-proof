## E.0 Scope, notation, and executive summary

### E.0.1 Notation (used throughout this file)

- Every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ has a unique model $E_{A,B}: y^2 = x^3 + Ax + B$ with $A,B\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $p^6\nmid B$ whenever $p^4\mid A$. Its (naive) height is $H(E) := \max\{4|A|^3, 27B^2\}$. "Ordered by height" means: for a property $P$, the *density* of $P$ is $\lim_{X\to\infty}\#\{E : H(E)<X,\ P(E)\}/\#\{E: H(E)<X\}$ (with $\liminf$/$\limsup$ giving lower/upper densities). One has $\#\{E : H(E) < X\} \asymp X^{5/6}$.
- $\operatorname{Sel}_n(E) = \operatorname{Sel}_n(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is the $n$-Selmer group; $s_p(E) := \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)$; $t_p(E) := \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}E(\mathbb{Q})[p]$. For a prime $p$,
$$ s_p(E) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) + t_p(E) + \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p]. $$
- $r_{p^\infty}(E) := \operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E) = \lim_k \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\big(p^{k-1}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^k}(E)/\mathrm{im}\,E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\big)$. Unconditionally $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\le r_{p^\infty}(E)$, with equality iff $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ is finite.
- For $d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, $E^d$ is the quadratic twist by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$; for $E_{A,B}$ it is $y^2 = x^3 + d^2Ax + d^3B$. $w(E)\in\{\pm1\}$ is the global root number; $w(E) = (-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)}$.
- "Large family" (Bhargava–Shankar): for each prime $\ell$ let $\Sigma_\ell\subset\{(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2 : \Delta(A,B) := -4A^3-27B^2\ne0\}$ be closed with boundary of measure $0$; $F_\Sigma := \{E_{A,B} : (A,B)\in\Sigma_\ell\ \forall\ell\}$ is a *family defined by congruence conditions*; it is *large* if for all sufficiently large $\ell$, $\Sigma_\ell$ contains all $(A,B)$ with $\ell^2\nmid\Delta(A,B)$. The family of all curves, any family defined by finitely many congruence conditions, and the family of semistable curves are large; every large family has positive density [BSZ14, §2.3, citing BS15a, Thm. 3.17].
- "BSD(rank) holds for $E$" means $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) = r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)$. Nothing in this file addresses the leading-term formula except where explicitly stated.

### E.0.2 Executive summary

1. `[THEOREM]` Averages of $|\operatorname{Sel}_n(E)|$ over all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by height are known exactly for $n = 2,3,4,5$: $3,4,7,6$ (Bhargava–Shankar), i.e. $\sigma(n)$, matching the Poonen–Rains/BKLPR models; nothing is known for any prime $p\ge7$ (§E.1).
2. `[THEOREM]` Bhargava–Skinner–Zhang: at least $66.48\%$ of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by height satisfy $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}}\le 1$ and have finite $\operatorname{Sha}$; the proof is $5$-Selmer statistics + $5$-parity + $5$-adic converse theorems + Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin (§E.2). "$100\%$" is **open**; it would follow from any bound $\operatorname{avg}|\operatorname{Sel}_p| = o(p^2)$ along an infinite set of primes (Proposition E.2.9, `[NEW, elementary]`; strengthening [BSZ14, Thm. 27]).
3. `[THEOREM]` Smith (JAMS 2026, two parts; and arXiv:2503.17619, 2025): for **every** $E/\mathbb{Q}$, $50\%$ of quadratic twists have $r_{2^\infty} = 0$, $50\%$ have $r_{2^\infty} = 1$, $0\%$ have $r_{2^\infty}\ge2$. Hence $100\%$ of twists with $w = +1$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$, $100\%$ with $w=-1$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le1$, and BSD for the twist family implies Goldfeld's conjecture (§E.3).
4. `[THEOREM]` Converse theorems at $p=2$ exist only for CM curves (Burungale–Tian, Ann. of Math. 2026, rank $0$, every $p$; Burungale–Skinner, rank $1$, $p$ of good reduction). Consequences: even-parity Goldfeld (analytic rank $0$ for $100\%$ of $w=+1$ twists) holds for every CM $E/\mathbb{Q}$; for $E$ with CM by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ and good reduction at $2$ (e.g. $X_0(49)$), BSD(rank) and finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ hold for $100\%$ of the twists $E^d$, $d\equiv1\pmod 4$, of *both* parities (Proposition E.3.10, `[NEW]`, assembled from published theorems). For non-CM curves, analytic rank $0$ for $100\%$ of even twists is **open** ([GAP E.2]); the odd-parity analogue is open for all $E$ outside the subfamily of Proposition E.3.10 ([GAP E.3]).
5. Structural limit: every statistical theorem controls Selmer coranks; a curve of rank $\ge2$ lies, by construction, in the density-zero exceptional set of its own twist family, and no effective error term can remove it (Propositions E.4.1–E.4.2). No result in the literature deduces BSD for a specific curve of rank $\ge2$ from a statement about a family (§E.4.4; searched 2026-09-11). The only "family $\to$ individual" mechanisms are existence statements (Koymans–Pagano, Alpöge–Bhargava–Ho–Shnidman, Zywina, 2024–25).
6. `[HEURISTIC]` Park–Poonen–Voight–Wood predict $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le21$ for all but finitely many $E/\mathbb{Q}$. Boundedness of rank is logically independent of BSD in both directions (Ulmer: BSD holds and ranks are unbounded over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$). Its bearing on "finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ for all $p$ at once" is that the PPVW model couples rank and $\operatorname{Sha}$ through a single integer matrix and predicts $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p] = 0$ for all $p > H(E)^{1/12+o(1)}$; the unconditional problem is an *effective* bound of this type ([GAP E.5]), and via Kato it is equivalent to a uniform-in-$p$ statement about $p$-adic $L$-functions ([GAP E.6]). Rank records: $\ge 29$ (Elkies–Klagsbrun 2024), $\ge30$ and $\ge31$ (August 2026, leaderboard entries; §E.5.2).

## E.1 Average sizes of Selmer groups and the random models

### E.1.1 Bhargava–Shankar: $n = 2,3,4,5$

**Theorem E.1.1** `[THEOREM]` (Bhargava–Shankar, $n=2$ [BS15a]). When all elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ are ordered by height, the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_2(E)$ is $3$. The same holds in any large family [BSZ14, Thm. 13]. Consequently $\limsup$ of the average of $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is at most $1.5$.

**Theorem E.1.2** `[THEOREM]` (Bhargava–Shankar, $n=3$ [BS15b]). Ordered by height (all curves, or any large family), the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_3(E)$ is $4$. Consequently the average rank is $< 1.17$; combined with the $3$-parity theorem of Dokchitser–Dokchitser, a positive proportion of curves have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$; combined with Skinner–Urban, a positive proportion have $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 0$ (hence satisfy BSD); assuming finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$, a positive proportion have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 1$.

**Theorem E.1.3** `[THEOREM]` (Bhargava–Shankar, $n=4$ [BS13a]). Ordered by height, the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_4(E)$ is $7$. Consequently at least one fifth of all $2$-Selmer elements (ordered by height) do not lift to $4$-Selmer elements, hence give nontrivial $2$-torsion in $\operatorname{Sha}$.

**Theorem E.1.4** `[THEOREM]` (Bhargava–Shankar, $n=5$ [BS13b]). Ordered by height (all curves, or any large family), the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_5(E)$ is $6$. Consequently the average rank is $< 0.885$; at least four fifths of all curves have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\in\{0,1\}$ and at least one fifth have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$. A key auxiliary result: there is a (finite union of) large subfamilies of density $\ge 55\%$ (more precisely $> 55.01\%$, [BSZ14, Thm. 16]) in which root numbers are equidistributed; more precisely, for any large family $F$ defined by congruence conditions modulo powers of primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$, there is a finite union $F'$ of large subfamilies with $\mu(F')>0.5501\,\mu(F)$ such that $E\in F'\Rightarrow E^{-1}\in F'$ and $w(E^{-1}) = -w(E)$.

**Remarks.** (i) The averages $3,4,7,6$ are $\sigma(n) = \sum_{d\mid n} d$; this is what Poonen–Rains (prime $n$) and BKLPR (all $n$) predict, see §E.1.3. (ii) The methods count integral orbits in coregular representations parametrizing $n$-coverings (binary quartic forms, ternary cubic forms, pairs of quaternary quadratic forms, quintuples of $5\times5$ alternating forms) [BSZ14, Rem. 14]; no analogous parametrization is known for any prime $p\ge7$, and **no average of $|\operatorname{Sel}_p|$ is known for any prime $p\ge7$**, not even an upper bound of the form $O(p^{2-\epsilon})$ (this is [GAP E.1] below).

**Theorem E.1.5** `[THEOREM]` (second moment; Bhargava–Shankar–Swaminathan [BSS21]). Ordered by height, $\limsup_X \operatorname{avg}_{H(E)<X}|\operatorname{Sel}_2(E)|^2\le 15$, for all curves and for any subfamily defined by finitely many (or acceptable infinitely many) congruence conditions; $15 = (2+1)(2^2+1)$ is the Poonen–Rains prediction [PR12, Conj. 1.1(c)]. The bound is an inequality; equality is proved only under a tail estimate.

**Theorem E.1.6** `[THEOREM]` (secondary term; Shankar–Taniguchi [ST24]). For $\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_X$ the curves of height $<X$ with $\operatorname{sign}\Delta = \pm$,
$\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^\pm_X}|\operatorname{Sel}_2(E)| = 3\cdot\#\mathcal{E}^\pm_X + C^{\pm}X^{3/4} + O_\varepsilon(X^{3/4-1/3804+\varepsilon})$,
the first power-saving error term for a Selmer counting function; the (negative) secondary term is consistent with the persistently low $2$-Selmer averages in data.

**Theorem E.1.7** `[THEOREM]` (conductor ordering; Shankar–Shankar–Wang [SSW21]). For the family $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{sf}}$ of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with good reduction at $2,3$ and $\Delta(E)/N(E)$ squarefree, ordered by *conductor*, the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_2$ is $3$ (so the average rank in this family is $\le 1.5$). Ordering *all* curves by conductor is out of reach: even $\#\{E : N(E)\le X\}\asymp X^{5/6}$ is conjectural.

### E.1.2 Function-field confirmation

**Theorem E.1.8** `[THEOREM]` (Landesman [Lan21]; Feng–Landesman–Rains [FLR20]). Over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$, in the large-$q$ limit, the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_n$ of elliptic curves of bounded height is $\sigma(n)$ for every $n$ (so, in that limit, $100\%$ of curves of bounded height have rank $0$ or $1$); and the joint distribution of $(\mathrm{rank}, \operatorname{Sel}_n, \operatorname{Sha}[n])$ for curves of fixed height $d\ge2$ has a large-$q$ limit which, as $d\to\infty$, is the BKLPR distribution. This is the strongest available *proof* that the models of §E.1.3 are correct in a setting; it says nothing about $\mathbb{Q}$.

### E.1.3 The Poonen–Rains and BKLPR models

**Theorem/Model E.1.9** `[THEOREM]` for the structure, `[HEURISTIC]` for the prediction (Poonen–Rains [PR12]). For a prime $p$, $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)$ is naturally the intersection of a *discrete* maximal isotropic subspace (the image of $H^1(\mathbb{Q},E[p])$ under restricted localisation) with a *compact open* maximal isotropic subspace (the local Kummer images) in a locally compact quadratic space over $\mathbb{F}_p$. Modelling the discrete subspace as random with respect to a natural measure gives:
$$ \operatorname{Prob}(s_p(E) = s) = \prod_{j\ge0}(1+p^{-j})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{s}\frac{p}{p^j-1},\qquad \mathbb{E}\,|\operatorname{Sel}_p|^m = \prod_{i=1}^m (p^i+1). $$
In particular the predicted average of $|\operatorname{Sel}_p|$ is $p+1$, the predicted average rank is $1/2$, and rank $\ge2$ has predicted density $0$. The model is consistent with Heath-Brown [HB94], Swinnerton-Dyer and Kane [Kan13] for $2$-Selmer in quadratic twist families, and with Theorems E.1.1–E.1.5.

**Model E.1.10** `[HEURISTIC]` (Bhargava–Kane–Lenstra–Poonen–Rains [BKLPR15]). Let $A\in M_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ be a random alternating matrix ($n\to\infty$ through each parity with probability $1/2$). BKLPR construct a probability distribution on isomorphism classes of exact sequences $0\to (\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)^r\to S\to T\to0$ of cofinite-type $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules (with $T$ finite carrying a nondegenerate alternating pairing), and conjecture that as $E$ varies over elliptic curves over a fixed global field ordered by height, the sequence $0\to E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\to\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E)\to\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]\to0$ has this distribution. Consequences of the conjecture: $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\in\{0,1\}$ each with probability $1/2$; and for $r\in\{0,1\}$ and a finite abelian $p$-group $G$ with a nondegenerate alternating pairing,
$$ \operatorname{Prob}\big(\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\simeq G \mid r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r\big) = \frac{(\#G)^{1-r}}{\#\operatorname{Sp}(G)}\prod_{i\ge r+1}\big(1-p^{1-2i}\big), $$
$\operatorname{Sp}(G)$ the automorphisms of $G$ preserving the pairing; this recovers Delaunay's heuristics [Del01, Del07]. The predicted average of $|\operatorname{Sel}_n|$ is $\sigma(n)$ for every $n$.

**Remark E.1.11** (what the models presuppose). In both models $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ is *finite by construction* (it is the torsion of the cokernel of a matrix). The models therefore cannot be evidence for finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$; they are models of *how* a finite $\operatorname{Sha}$ is distributed. Likewise, they model one prime at a time; only the PPVW refinement (§E.5) couples all $p$ through a single integer matrix.

### E.1.4 What averages do and do not say

`[THEOREM]` (elementary Markov-type inequalities, used throughout [BS15b, BS13b, BSZ14]). If $\operatorname{avg}|\operatorname{Sel}_p| \le A$ over a family, then the upper density of $\{E : s_p(E)\ge k\}$ is at most $(A-1)/(p^k-1)$, and if root numbers are equidistributed and $s_p(E)-t_p(E)\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\bmod 2$ (Theorem E.2.4 below), one can refine this parity by parity. Nothing in an average says anything about an individual member: an average is invariant under changing the invariants of any density-zero set of curves.

## E.2 From Selmer statistics to BSD: Bhargava–Skinner–Zhang

### E.2.1 The theorem, exactly

**Theorem E.2.1** `[THEOREM]` (Bhargava–Skinner–Zhang [BSZ14, Thms. 1–3, Cor. 4, eq. (1)]). When elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ are ordered by height,
$$ \liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{E : r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) = r_{\mathrm{an}}(E),\ \operatorname{Sha}(E)\text{ finite},\ H(E)<X\}}{\#\{E : H(E)<X\}} > 0.6648. $$
Moreover at least $16.50\%$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}} = 0$, at least $20.68\%$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}} = 1$, and the $\liminf$ of the average (algebraic or analytic) rank is $\ge 0.2068$.

*What "satisfy BSD" means here.* Exactly: $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}}\in\{0,1\}$ and $\#\operatorname{Sha}<\infty$. No statement about the leading term, and no statement about the $p$-part of the leading term for any $p$, is made or implied (the Skinner–Urban input does give the $5$-part of the BSD formula for the rank-$0$ curves in their family — [BSZ14, Rem. 8] — but this is not asserted in Theorem E.2.1). The curves covered all have $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1$; the theorem says nothing about any curve of rank $\ge2$.

### E.2.2 The exact chain of theorems

The proof of Theorem E.2.1 is the conjunction of the following five inputs, combined by an explicit optimisation.

**Theorem E.2.2** `[THEOREM]` (rank-$0$ criterion; Skinner–Urban [SU14, Thm. 2(b)] for good ordinary $p$, Skinner [Sk16, Thm. B] for multiplicative $p$; as formulated in [BSZ14, Thm. 5]). Let $p$ be an odd prime and $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of conductor $N$ such that (a) $E$ has good ordinary or multiplicative reduction at $p$; (b) $E[p]$ is an irreducible $G_\mathbb{Q}$-module; (c) there is a prime $\ell\ne p$ with $\ell\,\|\,N$ and $E[p]$ ramified at $\ell$; (d) $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E) = 0$. Then $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) = r_{\mathrm{an}}(E) = 0$. (The underlying result is the Iwasawa–Greenberg main conjecture; it identifies the $p$-adic valuation of $\#\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E)\cdot\prod c_\ell$ with that of $L(E,1)/\Omega_E$, so finiteness of $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}$ forces $L(E,1)\ne0$.)

**Theorem E.2.3** `[THEOREM]` (rank-$1$ criterion; W. Zhang [Zh14, Thm. 1.3] for good $p$, Skinner–Zhang [SZ14, Thm. 1.1] for multiplicative $p$; as formulated in [BSZ14, Thm. 9]). Let $p\ge5$ and $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of conductor $N$ such that (a) good ordinary or multiplicative reduction at $p$; (b) $E[p]$ irreducible; (c) for all $\ell\,\|\,N$ with $\ell\equiv\pm1\pmod p$, $E[p]$ is ramified at $\ell$; (d) if $N$ is not squarefree, there are at least two primes $\ell\,\|\,N$, $\ell\ne p$, at which $E[p]$ is ramified; (e) if $E$ has multiplicative reduction at $p$ then $E[p]$ is not finite at $p$, and if split multiplicative then $\operatorname{ord}_p\mathfrak{L}(E) = 1$ for the Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum $\mathfrak{L}$-invariant; (f) $\#\operatorname{Sel}_p(E) = p$. Then $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) = r_{\mathrm{an}}(E) = 1$. (Proof structure: by Cassels, $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}\simeq(\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)^r\oplus F\oplus F$; (b) gives $E(\mathbb{Q})[p] = 0$, so (f) gives $r_{p^\infty} = 1$ and $F = 0$; then the cited theorems, which prove $p$-indivisibility of derived Heegner points — Kolyvagin's conjecture — by comparing Theorem E.2.2 with Gross's special value formula à la Bertolini–Darmon, give $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s) = 1$; Gross–Zagier (in S. Zhang's Shimura-curve generality) and Kolyvagin give rank $1$ and finite $\operatorname{Sha}$.)

**Theorem E.2.4** `[THEOREM]` ($p$-parity; Dokchitser–Dokchitser [DD10]; see also Nekovář [Nek06]). For every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and every prime $p$: $s_p(E) - t_p(E)$ is even iff $w(E) = +1$. Equivalently $(-1)^{r_{p^\infty}(E)} = w(E) = (-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)}$. (For $p=2$ this is due to Monsky [Mon96].)

**Theorem E.2.5** `[THEOREM]` (Selmer averages; Theorems E.1.2, E.1.4) and **Theorem E.2.6** `[THEOREM]` (root-number equidistribution in a $\ge55.01\%$ subfamily; Theorem E.1.4, [BSZ14, Thm. 16]).

**Lemma E.2.7** `[THEOREM]` ([BSZ14, Lemma 20]). For any prime $p$, $100\%$ of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by height have $E[p]$ irreducible (Duke [Du97]: $100\%$ have $E[p]$ irreducible for all $p$ simultaneously) and have at least two primes $\ell\,\|\,N$, $\ell\ne p$, at which $E[p]$ is ramified.

*The optimisation* [BSZ14, §3]. With $p=5$: $S_0(5)$ := curves with good ordinary or multiplicative reduction at $5$ (density $\mu(S_0(5)) = 4\cdot5^{10}/(5(5^{10}-1)) > 0.8$); $S_1'(5)\subset S_0(5)$ adds hypothesis (e) of Theorem E.2.3 ($\mu = 0.7918054\ldots$); $S_1(5)\subset S_1'(5)$ adds (c) ($\mu>0.7917957$). In a finite union $F$ of large families with equidistributed root numbers and $\operatorname{avg}|\operatorname{Sel}_5| = 6$: at least $3/8$ of $F$ has $s_5 = 0$ [Thm. 21], at least $19/40$ has $s_5 = 1$ [Thm. 23], at least $7/8$ has $s_5\le1$ [Thm. 25]; without equidistribution, at least $19/24$ has $s_5\le1$. Assembling with $\kappa\ge0.5501$: $\frac38\cdot0.5501\cdot0.8>0.1650$; $\frac{19}{40}\cdot0.5501\cdot0.7918054-0.00001>0.2068$; and
$$ \Big(\tfrac78\cdot0.5501+\tfrac{19}{24}\cdot0.4499\Big)\cdot0.7918054 - \Big(\tfrac78+\tfrac{19}{24}\Big)\cdot0.00001+0.00169 = 0.664816\ldots $$
Finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ for the covered curves is Kolyvagin's theorem [Kol90] with Gross–Zagier [GZ86] (and the non-vanishing theorems of Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein [BFH90] and Murty–Murty [MM91] to choose the auxiliary imaginary quadratic field).

*Expected improvements* [BSZ14, Rems. 6, 10, §4]: extending Theorem E.2.2 to supersingular $p$ and removing (b),(c) would raise $16.50\%\to19.8\%$ and $66.48\%\to69.6\%$; extending Theorem E.2.3 to supersingular $p$ and to $p=3$ would give $20.68\%\to24.8\%$ and $66.48\%\to79.7\%$ (over $80\%$ using also $p=3$). We found no published unconditional improvement of the $66.48\%$ figure for the full family ordered by height (search log §E.8).

### E.2.3 Is "$100\%$ of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ satisfy BSD(rank)" proved, conditional, or open?

**Open.** The strongest unconditional statement for the full family is Theorem E.2.1 ($>66.48\%$). The conditional statement in the literature is:

**Theorem E.2.8** `[CONDITIONAL]` ([BSZ14, Thm. 27]). If for all primes $p$ the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)$ over all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by height is $p+1$, then BSD(rank) holds for $100\%$ of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by height. (Proof: the density of $S_0(p)\cap S_1(p)$ is at least $(1-1/p)^2\prod_{\ell\equiv\pm1(p)}(1-\ell^{-10})^{-1}(1-\ell^{-5})^2$, of which a proportion $\ge(p^2-p-1)/(p^2-1)$ has $s_p\le1$; the product tends to $1$ as $p\to\infty$.)

The hypothesis of Theorem E.2.8 is far stronger than what the argument uses. We record the precise minimal form.

**Proposition E.2.9** `[NEW, elementary]` (weak Selmer bounds along one sequence of primes suffice). Suppose there exist an infinite set $\mathcal{P}$ of primes and a function $A:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ with $A(p) = o(p^2)$ as $p\to\infty$ in $\mathcal{P}$, such that for every $p\in\mathcal{P}$,
$$ \limsup_{X\to\infty}\ \frac{1}{\#\{E:H(E)<X\}}\sum_{H(E)<X}\#\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)\ \le\ A(p). $$
Then $\liminf_{X}\#\{E : H(E)<X,\ r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) = r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\le1,\ \operatorname{Sha}(E)\text{ finite}\}/\#\{E:H(E)<X\} = 1$.

*Proof.* Fix $p\in\mathcal{P}$, $p\ge5$. Let $F_p := S_0(p)\cap S_1(p)$ (defined for general $p\ge5$ exactly as in §E.2.2; these are large families [BSZ14, §3.1]) with density $\mu(F_p)\ge\delta_p := (1-1/p)^2\prod_{\ell\equiv\pm1(p)}(1-\ell^{-10})^{-1}(1-\ell^{-5})^2$ [BSZ14, eq. (3)]. Since $\sum_{\ell>p}\ell^{-5}\to0$, $\delta_p\to1$ as $p\to\infty$. Let $G_p := \{E : s_p(E)\ge2\}$. Because $\#\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)\ge p^2$ on $G_p$ and $\ge1$ off $G_p$, the upper density of $G_p$ is at most $(A(p)-1)/(p^2-1)$. Let $B_p$ be the density-$1$ set of Lemma E.2.7. For $E\in F_p\cap B_p\setminus G_p$ we have $s_p(E)\in\{0,1\}$. If $s_p(E) = 0$, hypotheses (a)–(d) of Theorem E.2.2 hold ((a) from $S_0(p)$, (b),(c) from $B_p$), so $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}} = 0$. If $s_p(E) = 1$: irreducibility of $E[p]$ forces $E(\mathbb{Q})[p] = 0$, so $\#\operatorname{Sel}_p(E) = p$, and hypotheses (a)–(f) of Theorem E.2.3 hold ((a),(c),(e) from $S_1(p)$, (b),(d) from $B_p$), so $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}} = 1$. In both cases $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ is finite by Kolyvagin. Hence the lower density of curves with $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1$ and finite $\operatorname{Sha}$ is at least $\delta_p - (A(p)-1)/(p^2-1)$ for every $p\in\mathcal{P}$, $p\ge5$; letting $p\to\infty$ in $\mathcal{P}$ gives $1$. $\square$

*Remarks.* (i) The conjectural value is $A(p) = p+1$; the proposition shows that the "$100\%$" problem needs only $A(p) = o(p^2)$ along *some* sequence of primes, e.g. $A(p)\le Cp$ or even $A(p)\le p^{2-\epsilon}$. (ii) If only $A(p)\le cp^2$ with $c<1$ is available for infinitely many $p$, the same argument gives lower density $\ge 1-c$. (iii) The hypotheses are on the average over *all* curves; no large-family version of the Selmer bound is needed, because $F_p$ has density $\to1$.

**[GAP E.1]** (Selmer averages for large $p$). *Statement.* Exhibit an infinite set $\mathcal{P}$ of primes and, for each $p\in\mathcal{P}$, a bound $\limsup_X\operatorname{avg}_{H(E)<X}\#\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)\le A(p)$ with $A(p) = o(p^2)$. *Status.* Open for every single prime $p\ge7$; no bound $o(p^2)$, not even $O(p^{2-\epsilon})$, is known for any $p\ge7$. *Attempt (this file).* (1) The geometry-of-numbers route needs a representation whose integral orbits parametrize $p$-coverings with a computable height; the cases $n\le5$ use the classical coregular representations listed in Remark E.1.1(ii); for $p\ge7$ the $p$-covering of an elliptic curve is a genus-one curve of degree $p$ in $\mathbb{P}^{p-1}$, and no coregular parametrization exists, so the method stops (this is the standard explanation; we found no way around it). (2) A descent route: $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E)\hookrightarrow H^1(\mathbb{Q},E[p])$ is controlled by class groups and units of the degree-$(p^2-1)$ field $\mathbb{Q}(E[p])$; averaging $p$-torsion of class groups over this family is not accessible (only $2$-torsion of class groups is averaged in any family, and only via the same coregular spaces). (3) The function-field large-$q$ theorem E.1.8 shows the statement is *true in a model*, which transfers no information to $\mathbb{Q}$. *Conclusion.* Proposition E.2.9 is the sharpest reduction we can prove; the gap is exactly a bound $o(p^2)$ for infinitely many $p$.

## E.3 Twist families: Smith's theorems, converse theorems, Goldfeld

### E.3.1 Goldfeld's conjecture and the classical $2$-Selmer results

**Conjecture E.3.1** `[CONJECTURE]` (Goldfeld [Gol79, Conj. B]; also Katz–Sarnak [KS99]). For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $r\ge0$,
$$ \lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{\#\{d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\} : |d|\le H,\ r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = r\}}{2H} = \begin{cases}1/2 & r\in\{0,1\}\\ 0 & r\ge2.\end{cases} $$
The "even parity" (resp. "odd parity") Goldfeld conjecture is the $w=+1$ (resp. $w=-1$) half: $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = 0$ for $100\%$ of $d$ with $w(E^d) = +1$ (resp. $=1$ for $100\%$ of $d$ with $w(E^d) = -1$). Root numbers $\pm1$ each have density $1/2$ in the twist family.

`[THEOREM]` Heath-Brown [HB94] found the limiting distribution of $s_2(E^d)$ for $E: y^2 = x^3-x$; Swinnerton-Dyer and Kane [Kan13] extended it (natural density) to all $E$ with $E(\mathbb{Q})[2]\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ and no rational cyclic $4$-isogeny; Klagsbrun–Mazur–Rubin [KMR13] showed that for $E$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_3$ over general number fields the parity of $s_2$ need not be equidistributed ("disparity"), and gave a Markov model for $s_2$ in twist families. These control only $s_2$, hence give only upper bounds $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_2 - t_2$ for the rank.

### E.3.2 Smith's theorems on $2^\infty$-Selmer groups

The 2017 preprint [Smi17] has been superseded: its author states that the two-part paper [Smi26a, Smi26b] "developed out of the revision of the preprint [Smi17] and constitute the final form of that work" [Smi26a, §1]. We state the final forms.

**Assumption E.3.2** ([Smi26a, Assumption 1.1]). $A/\mathbb{Q}$ satisfies one of: (1) $A(\mathbb{Q})[2] = 0$; (2) $A(\mathbb{Q})[2]\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2$ and, for the unique $\mathbb{Q}$-isogeny $\varphi:A\to A_0$ of degree $2$, $\mathbb{Q}(A_0[2])\ne\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(A_0[2])\ne\mathbb{Q}(A[2])$; (3) $A(\mathbb{Q})[2]\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ and $A$ has no cyclic degree-$4$ isogeny defined over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Theorem E.3.3** `[THEOREM]` (Smith [Smi26a, Thm. 1.2, Rem. 1.3, Thm. 1.5]; proofs completed in [Smi26b]). Let $A/\mathbb{Q}$ satisfy Assumption E.3.2. Then for $r\ge0$,
$$ \lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{\#\{d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}: |d|\le H,\ r_{2^\infty}(A^d) = r\}}{2H} = \begin{cases}1/2& r=0\\1/2& r=1\\0& r\ge2.\end{cases} $$
Effective form: there are $c,C>0$ (depending on $A$) with, for $H>C$,
$$ \frac{\#\{0<|d|\le H : r_{2^\infty}(A^d)\ge2\}}{2H}\ \le\ \exp\big(-c\,(\log\log\log H)^{1/2}\big); $$
the conjectured truth is $H^{-1/4+\epsilon}$. In cases (1) and (3) the full sequence $(r_2(A^d), r_4(A^d), r_8(A^d),\dots)$ of $2^k$-Selmer ranks is distributed as a time-homogeneous Markov chain with transition probabilities $P^{\mathrm{Alt}}(j\mid n)$ = probability that a uniform alternating $n\times n$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}_2$ has kernel of dimension $j$, started at $P^{\mathrm{Alt}}(\cdot\mid\infty)$; this is the BKLPR distribution for $\operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}(A^d)$ [BKLPR15, Conj. 1.3]. Case (2) is [Smi26b, Thm. 1.4], via "favored" twists (Tamagawa ratios $\le1$).

**Theorem E.3.4** `[THEOREM]` (Smith [Smi26b, Thm. 1.1]; bounded average rank in twist families of *any* abelian variety). Let $\ell$ be a prime, $F$ a number field, $A/F$ an abelian variety, $X(H)$ the set of cyclic degree-$\ell$ extensions $K/F$ of absolute discriminant $\le H$. There are $c,C>0$ (depending on $A/F,\ell$) such that for $m>0$ and $H>C$ with $m<c\log\log\log H$,
$$ \sum_{K\in X(H)}\exp\big(m\cdot\operatorname{rank}A(K)\big)\le\exp(Cm^2)\cdot\#X(H). $$
In particular the average rank in the quadratic twist family of any abelian variety over any number field is bounded. Also: for $\ell$ odd and $A$ such that the twistable module $A[\ell^\infty]\otimes\mathbb{Z}_\ell[\xi]$ satisfies the conditions of the "alternating case" other than $\#\mathbb{F} = 2$, one gets $\operatorname{rank}A(K)-\operatorname{rank}A(F)\le\ell-1$ for $100\%$ of cyclic degree-$\ell$ $K/F$ [Smi26b, §3.3]; the expected value $0$ is not reached because the diagonal terms of the even-indexed Cassels–Tate pairings (symmetric, not alternating, for $\ell$ odd) are not controlled. For $F\supseteq\mu_3$ and $A: y^2 = x^3+m$, the method gives $r_{3^\infty}\le1$ for $100\%$ of cubic twists $y^2 = x^3+md^2$, but **fails over $\mathbb{Q}$** because the order-$3$ automorphism is defined only over $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_3)$ [Smi26b, Ex. 3.10].

**Theorem E.3.5** `[THEOREM]` (Smith 2025 [Smi25, Thm. 1.1, Cors. 1.2–1.3]; **all** $E/\mathbb{Q}$). For every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$, without any hypothesis,
$$ \lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{\#\{d\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}: |d|\le H,\ r_{2^\infty}(E^d) = r\}}{2H} = \begin{cases}1/2& r\in\{0,1\}\\0& r\ge2.\end{cases} $$
Consequently: (i) if BSD(rank) holds for all $E^d$, then Goldfeld's conjecture holds for $E$; (ii) among twists with $w(E^d) = +1$, $100\%$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d) = 0$; among twists with $w(E^d) = -1$, $100\%$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d)\le1$. The new cases are those with a *balanced* $2$-isogeny $\varphi:E\to E_0$, i.e. $\mathbb{Q}(E[2]) = \mathbb{Q}(E_0[2])$ ("Cases IV, V"); there the distribution of $s_2(E^d)$ is *not* the Poonen–Rains distribution (it is built from $\varphi$-Selmer ranks with a shift $u(\varphi,d_0)$), although the $2^\infty$-corank distribution is still $1/2,1/2,0$.

*Proof of (i)–(ii) from Theorem E.3.5 (as in [Smi25]).* $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le r_{2^\infty}$ always, and $(-1)^{r_{2^\infty}} = w = (-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}}$ (Theorem E.2.4 for $p=2$, [Mon96]). If $w=+1$ then $r_{2^\infty}\in\{0\}\cup\{2,4,\dots\}$, and the latter has density $0$; so $r_{2^\infty} = 0$ hence $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$ for $100\%$. If $w=-1$, $r_{2^\infty} = 1$ for $100\%$, so $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le1$. Under BSD, $r_{\mathrm{an}} = r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le r_{2^\infty}\le1$ with equal parity forces $r_{\mathrm{an}} = r_{2^\infty}$. $\square$

*What Smith's theorems do not give.* They never produce a rational point or a non-vanishing $L$-value: the odd-parity half gives only $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le1$, and the even-parity half gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$ but not $L(E^d,1)\ne0$. Passing to analytic ranks requires a $2$-converse theorem (§E.3.3).

### E.3.3 Converse theorems relevant to twist families, with exact hypotheses

The statements below are the ones that can be combined with Theorem E.3.5, which controls only $2^\infty$-Selmer groups; converse theorems at odd $p$ (Theorems E.2.2–E.2.3) are useless here, because $r_{p^\infty}(E^d)$ for odd $p$ is not controlled by $r_{2^\infty}(E^d)$ (it equals $r_{\mathrm{alg}} + \operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$, and finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is unknown).

**Theorem E.3.6** `[THEOREM]` (rank-$0$ $p$-converse for CM curves, every $p$; Burungale–Tian [BT26, Thm. 1.1]). Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over an imaginary quadratic field $K$ with CM by an order of $K$, and $p$ any prime. Then $\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/K) = 0\Rightarrow\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(s,E/K) = 0$. In particular, for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with CM, $r_{p^\infty}(E) = 0\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}(E) = 0$ for every prime $p$, including $p=2$ and primes of bad reduction. (Proof via the CM case of Kato's main conjecture in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}$, using elliptic units, Johnson-Leung–Kings, and Kato's explicit reciprocity law; [BT26, Rem. 3.2]: the deduction of the rank-$0$ converse from Kato's main conjecture holds for any newform.)

**Theorem E.3.7** `[THEOREM]` (rank-$1$ $p$-converse for CM curves; Burungale–Skinner, Appendix A of [ABS22, Thms. A.1, A.3, Rem. A.5]). Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be a CM elliptic curve.
(a) If $p$ is a prime of good *ordinary* reduction and $r_{p^\infty}(E) = 1$, then $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s) = 1 = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$ (and $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ is finite). This "follows immediately from" Burungale–Tian [BT20], Burungale–Castella–Skinner–Tian [BCST22], and Q. Yu's 2021 Caltech thesis for small primes [Yu21]; **all of these allow $p=2$** [ABS22, Rem. A.5(i)].
(b) If $p$ is a prime of good *supersingular* reduction, $r_{p^\infty}(E) = 1$, and the localisation $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E)\to E(\mathbb{Q}_p)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ is surjective, then the same conclusion holds.
No rank-$1$ $2$-converse is available for CM curves with *bad* reduction at $2$, nor for any non-CM curve: "Relatively few converse theorems have been proved for the case $p=2$, and those that have been proved require $E$ to be a CM elliptic curve" [Smi25, Rem. 1.5].

**Theorem E.3.8** `[THEOREM]` (rank-$1$ $p$-converse, non-CM; Skinner [Sk20], W. Zhang [Zh14]; and for CM with $p\nmid6N$ ordinary, Burungale–Tian [BT20]). See Theorem E.2.3 for the exact hypotheses in the form used by [BSZ14]; Skinner's version: $E$ semistable with non-split multiplicative reduction at an odd prime or split multiplicative reduction at two odd primes, $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 1$ and $\operatorname{Sha}$ finite $\Rightarrow\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s) = 1$. All require $p$ odd ($p\ge5$ in [Zh14] and [BT20]).

### E.3.4 Consequences: what is proved about BSD in twist families

**Corollary E.3.9** `[THEOREM]` (even-parity Goldfeld and BSD(rank) for $100\%$ of even twists of every CM curve; [BT22, Rem. 5.11] together with Theorem E.3.5, which removes the exception $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ noted there). Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have CM. Then for $100\%$ of $d$ with $w(E^d) = +1$: $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d) = 0$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E^d)$ is finite. For $E: y^2 = x^3-x$ this is the theorem of Burungale–Tian [BT26, Thm. 1.2]: for a density-one subset of positive squarefree $n\equiv1,2,3\pmod8$, $L(E^{(n)},1)\ne0$ (so these $n$ are not congruent numbers).

*Proof.* $E^d$ has CM by the same order. By Theorem E.3.5 and Theorem E.2.4, $r_{2^\infty}(E^d) = 0$ for $100\%$ of $d$ with $w(E^d)=+1$. Theorem E.3.6 with $p=2$ gives $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = 0$. Kolyvagin [Kol90] (or Coates–Wiles and Rubin [Ru87] for CM) gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$ and $\#\operatorname{Sha}<\infty$. $\square$

**Proposition E.3.10** `[NEW]` (both parities for a positive-density subfamily of a CM curve; assembled from published theorems — we did not find this statement in print). Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have complex multiplication by an order of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ and good reduction at $2$ (for instance $E = X_0(49)$, Cremona label 49a1, $y^2+xy = x^3-x^2-2x-1$, conductor $49$, CM by $\mathbb{Z}[(1+\sqrt{-7})/2]$, $a_2 = 1$). Let $\mathcal{D} := \{d\in\mathbb{Z} : d\text{ squarefree},\ d\equiv1\pmod4\}$. Then for $100\%$ of $d\in\mathcal{D}$ (density one inside $\mathcal{D}$):
$$ r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d) = r_{2^\infty}(E^d) = \tfrac{1-w(E^d)}{2}\in\{0,1\},\qquad \#\operatorname{Sha}(E^d)<\infty. $$
In particular BSD(rank) holds for $100\%$ of the twists in $\mathcal{D}$, and both halves of Goldfeld's conjecture hold within $\mathcal{D}$.

*Proof.* (1) *Reduction at $2$.* For $d\in\mathcal{D}$, $L := \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt d)$ is unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ (possibly $L = \mathbb{Q}_2$), and $E^d\simeq E$ over $L$. The valuation of the minimal discriminant is unchanged by unramified base change, so $v_2(\Delta_{\min}(E^d/\mathbb{Q}_2)) = v_L(\Delta_{\min}(E^d/L)) = v_L(\Delta_{\min}(E/L)) = v_2(\Delta_{\min}(E/\mathbb{Q}_2)) = 0$. So $E^d$ has good reduction at $2$. Moreover $a_2(E^d) = \chi_d(2)a_2(E)$ and $a_2(E)$ is odd: writing $a_2(E) = \pi+\bar\pi$ with $\pi\in\mathcal{O}_K$ of norm $2$ (possible because $2$ splits in $K$, as $-7\equiv1\pmod8$), if $a_2(E)$ were even then $a_2(E)\in\{0,\pm2\}$; $a_2 = 0$ would give $2 = \pi\bar\pi = -\pi^2$, making $2$ ramified in $K$, and $a_2 = \pm2$ would give $\pi$ a root of $x^2\mp2x+2$, whose discriminant $-4$ forces $K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$; both contradict $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$. Hence $2$ is a prime of good *ordinary* reduction for every $E^d$, $d\in\mathcal{D}$. $E^d$ has CM by the same order (CM is a geometric property).
(2) *Selmer coranks.* By Theorem E.3.5 the set $\{d : r_{2^\infty}(E^d)\ge2\}$ has density $0$ in $\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$; a set of squarefree integers with positive lower density inside $\mathcal{D}$ would have positive lower density in $\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ (each such $d$ already contributes to the count $|d|\le H$), so $r_{2^\infty}(E^d)\le1$ for $100\%$ of $d\in\mathcal{D}$. By Theorem E.2.4 ($p=2$), $r_{2^\infty}(E^d)\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d)\pmod 2$ and $(-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d)} = w(E^d)$; hence for $100\%$ of $d\in\mathcal{D}$, $r_{2^\infty}(E^d) = (1-w(E^d))/2$.
(3) *Even parity.* If $r_{2^\infty}(E^d) = 0$, Theorem E.3.6 ($p=2$) gives $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = 0$, and Kolyvagin gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d) = 0$, $\#\operatorname{Sha}(E^d)<\infty$.
(4) *Odd parity.* If $r_{2^\infty}(E^d) = 1$, Theorem E.3.7(a) applies to the CM curve $E^d$ at the good ordinary prime $p=2$ (by step (1)), giving $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = 1 = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d)$ and finite $\operatorname{Sha}(E^d)$ (Remark A.5(ii) of [ABS22]; or Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin from $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 1$). $\square$

*Remarks.* (i) The set $\mathcal{D}$ has relative density $1/3$ among squarefree integers, and both signs of $w(E^d)$ occur with density $1/2$ inside $\mathcal{D}$ (for $d\in\mathcal{D}$ coprime to $7$, $w(E^d) = w(E)\chi_d(-49) = w(E)\operatorname{sign}(d)$). (ii) The choice $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ is forced: among the nine imaginary quadratic fields of class number one, $2$ splits only in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$, so it is the only CM field for which a CM curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ can have *ordinary* reduction at $2$. For the other CM fields with $2$ inert ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-11}),\dots$) one would use Theorem E.3.7(b) and would need the surjectivity of the localisation map at $2$ for $100\%$ of the corank-one twists, which Smith's theorems do not supply ([GAP E.3](iii)). (iii) The dependence on Q. Yu's thesis for $p=2$ in Theorem E.3.7(a) is inherited by this proposition; the adversarial reviewer should check the published status of that ingredient.

**Theorem E.3.11** `[THEOREM]` (Kriz–Li [KL19]). Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$. (a) If $E$ has a rational $3$-isogeny, a positive proportion of quadratic twists have $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 0$ and a positive proportion have $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 1$ (Goldfeld's conjecture in the weak "positive proportion" form). (b) The analogous statement holds for sextic twists of $j$-invariant-$0$ curves. (c) For general $E$, the number of twists $E^d$, $|d|\le X$, with $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 0$ (resp. $1$) is $\gg X/\log^{5/6}X$, improving Ono–Skinner (resp. Perelli–Pomykała). (d) For the explicitly constructed twists, the $p$-part ($p=2$ or $3$) of the BSD formula holds. *Method:* a congruence formula between $p$-adic logarithms of Heegner points on two curves with isomorphic semisimplified $\bmod p^m$ Galois representations, applied with $E\equiv E^d\pmod 2$ (and $\bmod 3$ for $3$-isogenies). The method starts from a *base curve with a non-torsion Heegner point* (rank $\le1$) and propagates to twists; it is a transfer *from* an individual *to* a family, never the reverse.

**Theorem E.3.12** `[THEOREM]` (cubic twist families; Alpöge–Bhargava–Shnidman [ABS22]). In any cubic twist family $E_{d,n}$ ($x^3+y^3 = n$ up to twist), the average size of $\operatorname{Sel}_2$ over curves of either root number is $3$; root numbers are equidistributed; hence at least $1/6$ of the curves have rank $0$ and at least $1/6$ of those with good reduction at $2$ have rank $1$, using the $2$-parity theorem and Theorem E.3.7(b) at $p=2$. In particular a positive proportion of integers are sums of two rational cubes and a positive proportion are not. (Note the use of $p=2$ converse theorems for CM curves, and that $\operatorname{avg}\#\operatorname{Sel}_3 = \infty$ in these families when $d$ is not a square [ABS22, Thm. 1.11].)

**Theorem E.3.13** `[THEOREM]` (Koymans–Pagano; Alpöge–Bhargava–Ho–Shnidman; Zywina; Koymans–Morgan, 2024–25). (a) [KP24] For every number field $K$ and (suitable) quadratic $L/K$ there is $E/K$ with $\operatorname{rank}E(L) = \operatorname{rank}E(K)>0$; with Poonen–Shlapentokh this gives the negative answer to Hilbert's Tenth Problem over $\mathcal{O}_K$ for every $K$ (also [ABHS25], via abelian varieties). (b) [KP25, Thm. 1.3] For every number field $K$ and every "$3$-generic" $E/K$ with $E(K)[2]\simeq\mathbb{F}_2^2$, infinitely many quadratic twists $E^t$ have $\operatorname{rank}E^t(K) = 1$ exactly; [Zyw25b, Thm. 1.2] for every quadratic $L/K$ there are infinitely many $E/K$ with $\operatorname{rank}E(K) = \operatorname{rank}E(L) = 1$. (c) [Zyw25a] There are infinitely many $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (up to $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$-isomorphism) of rank exactly $2$: explicit models, rank proved by $2$-descent, infinitude via Tao–Ziegler. (d) [KM25] For $g\ge1$ and $f$ split of degree $2g+1$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$ (mild hypotheses), some quadratic twist of $\operatorname{Jac}(y^2 = f(x))$ has rank exactly $1$. Methods: $2$-descent for curves with full $2$-torsion, control of Selmer ranks along prime twists, and additive combinatorics (Green–Tao–Ziegler type theorems over number fields). These are *existence* statements obtained from Selmer statistics; see §E.4.3.

### E.3.5 Precise status of "$100\%$ satisfy BSD(rank)" in the twist setting

- For every $E/\mathbb{Q}$: $100\%$ of twists have $r_{2^\infty}\le1$; $100\%$ of even twists have $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 0$; $100\%$ of odd twists have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le1$ `[THEOREM]` (E.3.5).
- For every CM $E/\mathbb{Q}$: $100\%$ of even twists satisfy BSD(rank) with finite $\operatorname{Sha}$ `[THEOREM]` (E.3.9).
- For $E$ CM by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$ with good reduction at $2$: $100\%$ of twists with $d\equiv1\pmod4$ satisfy BSD(rank) with finite $\operatorname{Sha}$, both parities `[NEW]` (E.3.10).
- For every $E$: BSD(rank) for the twist family $\Rightarrow$ Goldfeld `[THEOREM]` (E.3.5(i)). Goldfeld's conjecture in full is not proved for any single $E/\mathbb{Q}$: for non-CM $E$ both halves are open; for CM $E$ the even half is proved (E.3.9) and the odd half is open, except inside the subfamily of E.3.10.
- For the full family of all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by height: $>66.48\%$ `[THEOREM]`, $100\%$ open ([GAP E.1]).

**[GAP E.2]** (rank-$0$ $2$-converse for non-CM curves). *Statement.* Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be non-CM with good ordinary reduction at $2$ (or a natural subclass thereof). Prove: $r_{2^\infty}(E) = 0\Rightarrow L(E,1)\ne0$. *Why it matters.* With Theorem E.3.5 it gives the even-parity Goldfeld conjecture — and BSD(rank) for $100\%$ of even twists — for such $E$. *Status.* Open. Burungale–Tian [BT26, §1] announce the intention to treat Kato's main conjecture for $2$-ordinary non-CM curves. *Attempt.* By [BT26, Rem. 3.2] the rank-$0$ converse follows for any newform from Kato's main conjecture (in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}$, i.e. up to $\mu$-invariants) at $p$, via: $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V) = 0\Rightarrow H^2(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)\otimes\mathbb{Q} = 0\Rightarrow$ the Beilinson–Kato element is a basis of the one-dimensional $H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\Rightarrow$ its localisation at $p$ is not in $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)\Rightarrow L(E,1)\ne0$ by Kato's explicit reciprocity law. Thus [GAP E.2] is exactly the *reverse divisibility* in Kato's main conjecture at $p=2$ for non-CM $E$ (the divisibility $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)\mid(\mathbf{z}_E)$ being Kato's theorem). Skinner–Urban's proof of the reverse divisibility uses Eisenstein congruences on $\mathrm{U}(2,2)$ and requires $p$ odd; we see no way to adapt it here and record the gap. Cross-reference: `A-iwasawa-padic.md` for the main conjecture at $p=2$.

**[GAP E.3]** (rank-$1$ $2$-converse). *Statement.* (i) Non-CM $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (any natural subclass): $r_{2^\infty}(E) = 1\Rightarrow\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s) = 1$. (ii) CM $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with *bad* reduction at $2$ (in particular the congruent number curve $y^2 = x^3-x$ and all its twists): the same. (iii) CM $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with good supersingular reduction at $2$: remove the surjectivity hypothesis in Theorem E.3.7(b), or prove it holds for $100\%$ of the corank-one twists. *Why it matters.* (i) gives odd-parity Goldfeld and BSD(rank) for $100\%$ of odd twists of non-CM curves; (ii) settles the congruent number problem for $100\%$ of $n\equiv5,6,7\pmod 8$ in the strong form $r_{\mathrm{an}} = r_{\mathrm{alg}} = 1$. *Status.* Open (searched 2026-09-11; the literature states the odd-parity case for the congruent number curve as open). *Attempt.* (iii): Smith's method controls the $2^k$-Selmer *groups* as abstract groups with their Cassels–Tate pairings but does not control the image of $\operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}(E^d)$ in $E^d(\mathbb{Q}_2)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_2/\mathbb{Z}_2$; the local condition at $2$ is fixed in a "grid" (twists with prescribed behaviour at $2$), so one could hope to show that within each grid the corank-one twists have non-strict Selmer group with probability $1$. We could not carry this out; it would require the equidistribution of the local image at $2$, which is a genuinely new input (an analogue of Bhargava–Skinner's equidistribution of local images [BSk14]).

## E.4 Why the statistical approach cannot reach a fixed curve of rank $\ge2$

### E.4.1 Density statements carry no information about a single member

**Proposition E.4.1** `[NEW, elementary]` (no transfer from density statements). Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a countable family with a height $h:\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ such that $\#\{x : h(x)\le H\}<\infty$ for all $H$ and $\to\infty$, and let $P$ be a property of members of $\mathcal{F}$. (a) The statements "$P$ holds for $100\%$ of $\mathcal{F}$" and "$P$ holds for $100\%$ of $\mathcal{F}$ after changing the truth value of $P$ on an arbitrary finite subset" are equivalent. Hence such a statement neither implies nor refutes $P(x_0)$ for any particular $x_0\in\mathcal{F}$. (b) More generally, any statement of the form "$\#\{x : h(x)\le H,\ \neg P(x)\}\le f(H)$ for all $H\ge H_0$" with $f(H)\ge1$ for $H\ge H_0$ is compatible with $\neg P(x_0)$ for any given $x_0$ with $h(x_0)\le H_0$ or for which $f(h(x_0))\ge1$.

*Proof.* (a) Changing $P$ on a finite set changes the counting function by a bounded amount, which does not affect densities. (b) Given a counting bound with $f\ge1$, the set $\{x_0\}$ satisfies it. $\square$

The point is not this triviality but how it applies: every theorem of §E.1–E.3 is a statement about Selmer coranks $r_{p^\infty}$ (or $s_p$), and a curve $E$ of rank $\ge2$ has $r_{p^\infty}(E)\ge2$ for *every* $p$. Hence:

**Proposition E.4.2** `[NEW, elementary]` (a rank-$\ge2$ curve is in the exceptional set of its own family, for every current method). Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge2$, and let $\mathcal{T}(E) := \{E^d\}$ be its quadratic twist family, $E = E^1$. Let $\Theta$ be any theorem of the form "for all $d$ outside an exceptional set $\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, BSD(rank) holds for $E^d$", whose proof establishes BSD(rank) for $E^d$ *only* through the chain $r_{p^\infty}(E^d)\le1\Rightarrow$ (converse theorem, Gross–Zagier, Kolyvagin) $\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d)$, for some prime $p$ (this is the structure of *every* result in §E.2–E.3). Then $1\in\mathcal{X}$, and $\#\{d : |d|\le H,\ d\in\mathcal{X}\}\ge1$ for all $H\ge1$. Consequently no effective version of $\Theta$ with any error term $f(H)$ (necessarily $f(H)\ge1$) can imply BSD(rank) for $E$.

*Proof.* $r_{p^\infty}(E)\ge r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge2$ for every $p$, so the hypothesis $r_{p^\infty}(E^d)\le1$ fails at $d=1$; by construction $1\in\mathcal{X}$. The rest is Proposition E.4.1(b). $\square$

*Remarks.* (i) Smith's effective bound $\exp(-c(\log\log\log H)^{1/2})\cdot2H$ for the exceptional set (Theorem E.3.3) and even the conjectured $H^{3/4+\epsilon}$ are, in this sense, irrelevant to $E$ itself: the exceptional set is *known* to contain $d=1$ and infinitely many other $d$ (Theorem E.4.3 below). (ii) A hypothetical counting theorem that *could* say something about $E$ would have to count a property whose failure at $d=1$ is not known a priori — e.g. $Q(d)$: "$\operatorname{Sha}(E^d)$ is infinite" — and prove $\#\{|d|\le H : Q(d)\}<1$ for some $H\ge1$. No method counts $Q$: all methods see $r_{p^\infty}$ and never $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ directly (rational points enter only through Gross–Zagier via $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 1$). (iii) Uniformity in $E$ does not help either: a uniform-in-$E$ effective statement about twist families (with constants independent of $E$) would still have exceptional sets containing every rank-$\ge2$ member.

### E.4.2 Rank $\ge2$ twists exist in abundance, so the exceptional set is infinite

**Theorem E.4.3** `[THEOREM]` (lower bounds for rank-$\ge2$ twists; as collected in Rubin–Silverberg [RS02, Thm. 8.2]). Write $N_{\ge r}(X) := \#\{d\text{ squarefree}: |d|\le X,\ r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^d)\ge r\}$. (i) $N_{\ge1}(X)\gg X^{1/2}$ for every $E$ (Gouvêa–Mazur [GM91], improved by Stewart–Top [ST95]). (ii) If $E: y^2 = x^3+ax+b$ with $ab\ne0$ (i.e. $j(E)\notin\{0,1728\}$), then $N_{\ge2}(X)\gg X^{1/7}/\log^2X$ (Stewart–Top [ST95, Thm. 3], via Mestre's degree-$14$ rank-$2$ twist over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$). (iii) If $x^3+ax+b$ has a nonzero rational root, $N_{\ge2}(X)\gg X^{1/3}$; (iv) for certain families (including $y^2 = x^3-x$), $N_{\ge3}(X)\gg X^{1/6}$ [RS02]. Conditionally on the parity conjecture, Gouvêa–Mazur [GM91] gave $N_{\ge2}(X)\gg_\epsilon X^{1/2-\epsilon}$, and Rubin–Silverberg give $N_{\ge3}(X)\gg X^{1/3}$ for many $E$ [PPVW19, §3.4(a)]. Conjecturally $N_{\ge2,\mathrm{even}}(D) = D^{3/4+o(1)}$ [PPVW19, (3.3.1)] and $N_{\ge3}(D) = D^{1/2+o(1)}$ [PPVW19, §3.4].

So for every $E$ the density-zero exceptional set of Theorem E.3.5 is infinite (for $j\notin\{0,1728\}$ of size at least $X^{1/7}/\log^2X$, at most $2X\exp(-c(\log\log\log X)^{1/2})$).

### E.4.3 Which "transfer" mechanisms exist at all

We list every mechanism we know that connects a statistical statement to an individual object, and check each against "BSD for a fixed rank-$\ge2$ curve".

1. *Existence from positive proportion.* If a positive proportion of a family has property $P$, some member has $P$. Used by ABS (some $n$ are sums of two cubes), Koymans–Pagano, ABHS, Koymans–Morgan (existence of curves/Jacobians of rank exactly $1$ with rank stability; Theorem E.3.13), and by PPVW [PPVW19, §12] (Bhargava–Skinner–Zhang + the multidimensional density Hales–Jewett theorem give number fields $K$ with $\limsup\operatorname{rank}E(K)$ growing linearly in $[K:\mathbb{Q}]$). This produces *some* curve with a property, never a property of a *prescribed* curve. Not applicable.
2. *Individual to family via congruences* (Kriz–Li, Theorem E.3.11): requires a base curve of analytic rank $\le1$ with a suitable Heegner point; the direction is the wrong one.
3. *Averaging a non-negative quantity.* If $\operatorname{avg}Q = 0$ for $Q\ge0$ then $Q\equiv0$ on the family. Here the relevant $Q$ would be $\dim\operatorname{Sha}[p]$ or $r_{p^\infty}-r_{\mathrm{alg}}$, whose averages are *positive* (Theorem E.1.3: $\ge1/5$ of $2$-Selmer elements are $\operatorname{Sha}$ elements). Not applicable.
4. *Quadratic base change.* `[THEOREM]` (standard) For $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$: $L(E/K,s) = L(E,s)L(E^d,s)$, $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E/K) = r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)+r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^d)$, $\operatorname{rank}E(K) = \operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})+\operatorname{rank}E^d(\mathbb{Q})$. Hence if BSD(rank) is known for $E^d$, then BSD(rank) for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ $\Leftrightarrow$ BSD(rank) for $E/K$. Combining with Corollary E.3.9/Proposition E.3.10: for a CM rank-$2$ curve $E$ and $100\%$ of admissible $d$, BSD(rank) for $E$ is equivalent to BSD(rank) for $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$, where $\operatorname{rank}E(K)\in\{2,3\}$. This *relocates* the problem to a rank-$2$ or $3$ problem over a quadratic field; no tool is available there either (over imaginary quadratic $K$ the bipartite Euler systems of Bertolini–Darmon control rank $\le1$ only; see `C-higher-rank-euler-systems-diagonal-cycles.md`). We record it as a reformulation, not progress.
5. *Twist by a fixed $E$ to move $E$ into a "good" position.* One might try to choose $d$ with $E^d$ of rank $0$ (available for $100\%$ of even $d$) and exploit $E\times E^d$ or $\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}E$; this is item 4 again.

**Conclusion E.4.4** (answer to the question in the brief). To our knowledge (searches listed in §E.8), *no result in the literature uses a statistical statement about a family to deduce BSD — rank part, finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$, or any $p$-part — for a specific elliptic curve of rank $\ge2$*, and Proposition E.4.2 explains why none of the current methods can: they certify $r_{\mathrm{an}} = r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ only through $r_{p^\infty}\le1$.

### E.4.4 What is known for individual curves of rank $\ge2$, and the (absent) role of statistics

`[THEOREM]` (Stein–Wuthrich [SW13, §1]; still accurate on 2026-09-11 as far as we can determine, cf. `../03-obstructions-rank-ge-2.md`): "The group $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is not known to be finite for even a single elliptic curve with $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$"; "there is no known algorithm (procedure that has been proved to terminate) that computes $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ in all cases".

For a *given* curve $E$ with $w(E) = +1$, however, BSD(rank) in the form $r_{\mathrm{alg}} = r_{\mathrm{an}} = 2$ is certifiable by finite computation, and this is how the rank-$2$ entries of the tables are established:
- `[THEOREM]` (standard; [Cre97, §2.13], [GJPST09, §2.2]) $L(E,1)/\Omega_E\in\mathbb{Q}$ is computable exactly via modular symbols, so "$L(E,1) = 0$" is decidable; then $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ and even. A rigorous numerical evaluation showing $L''(E,1)\ne0$ (a nonzero real number computed with an explicit error bound) proves $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 2$. (For $w=-1$ and $r_{\mathrm{an}} = 3$, Gross–Zagier over an auxiliary imaginary quadratic $K$ with $L(E^K,1)\ne0$ reduces "$L'(E,1) = 0$" to whether a Heegner point is torsion, which is decidable [GJPST09, §3]; for $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge4$ no exact method to certify $L''(E,1) = 0$ is known.)
- `[THEOREM]` $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge2$ by exhibiting two independent points; $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le2$ by an $n$-descent when $s_2(E)-t_2(E) = 2$ (or higher descents when $\operatorname{Sha}[2]\ne0$ obstructs), [SW13, §1.1].
- `[THEOREM]` (Stein–Wuthrich [SW13, Thm. 1.1]) For each of the $1{,}534{,}422$ pairs $(E,p)$ with $E$ non-CM, $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge2$, $N(E)\le30{,}000$, $5\le p<1000$ good ordinary with surjective $\bar\rho_{E,p}$: $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p] = 0$. Method: Kato's theorem gives $r_{p^\infty}(E)\le\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)$, and a rigorous computation of $L_p(E,T)$ to finite precision certifies $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}$, hence $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite, and a $p$-adic regulator computation gives $\operatorname{Sha}[p] = 0$. This is *one prime at a time*, with no uniformity in $p$ (§E.5.3).

None of these uses statistics. Hence the individual rank-$2$ problem is exactly: finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$, i.e. $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite for *all* $p$ and $\operatorname{Sha}[p] = 0$ for all $p$ beyond an explicit bound — the "all $p$ at once" problem of §E.5.3 and of `../03-obstructions-rank-ge-2.md`.

## E.5 Rank-boundedness heuristics and their bearing on BSD

### E.5.1 The Park–Poonen–Voight–Wood model

**Model E.5.1** `[HEURISTIC]` (PPVW [PPVW19, §1.1, §7]). Fix increasing $\eta(H), X(H)$ with $X(H)^{\eta(H)} = H^{1/12+o(1)}$ ($\eta$ growing slowly). To model $E$ of height $H$: choose $n$ uniformly from $\{\lceil\eta(H)\rceil,\lceil\eta(H)\rceil+1\}$ and $A_E\in M_n(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{alt}}$ with entries bounded by $X(H)$ uniformly at random; then $\operatorname{rk}(\ker A_E)$ models $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$ and $(\operatorname{coker}A_E)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ models $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$. The calibration $H^{1/12}$ comes from Lang's idea of solving BSD for $\#\operatorname{Sha}$: under BSD, $\operatorname{Sha}_0(E) := L(E,1)\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2/(\Omega\prod c_p)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Sha}_0(E) = H^{1/12+o(1)}L(E,1)$, and under RH for $L(E,s)$, $\operatorname{Sha}_0(E)\le H^{1/12+o(1)}$ [PPVW19, Thm. 6.4.2] (using $H^{-1/12}\ll\Omega\ll H^{-1/12}\log H$, $\prod c_p = H^{o(1)}$, $\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\le16$).

**Theorem E.5.2** `[THEOREM]` about the model ([PPVW19, Thm. 1.1.1 = Thm. 7.3.3, Thm. 9.1.1]). With probability $1$: (a) all but finitely many $E$ satisfy $\operatorname{rk}'_E\le21$; (b) for $1\le r\le20$, $\#\{E : H(E)\le H,\ \operatorname{rk}'_E\ge r\} = H^{(21-r)/24+o(1)}$; (c) $\#\{E: H(E)\le H,\ \operatorname{rk}'_E\ge21\}\le H^{o(1)}$. Underlying count: $\operatorname{Prob}(\operatorname{rk}(\ker A)\ge r) = H^{-(r-1)/24+o(1)}$ for fixed $r\ge1$.

**Conjecture E.5.3** `[HEURISTIC]` (PPVW's predictions for actual curves [PPVW19, §8]). $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\le21$ for all but finitely many $E/\mathbb{Q}$; $\#\{E : H(E)\le H, r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge r\} = H^{(21-r)/24+o(1)}$ for $1\le r\le20$; in the quadratic twist family of a fixed $E_0$ (which has $\asymp H^{1/6}$ members of height $\le H$) only finitely many twists have rank $>5$; $N_{\ge2,\mathrm{even}}(D) = D^{3/4+o(1)}$, $N_{\ge3}(D) = D^{1/2+o(1)}$. PPVW explicitly caution [Rem. 1.1.2–1.1.3] that special parametric families may behave differently and that the heuristic "cannot be considered convincing" until it predicts rank distributions in such families; and [Rem. 1.1.4] that *no natural alternating matrix in the arithmetic of an elliptic curve is known* whose kernel is $E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ (Deninger's conjectural $\theta$-operator being the only candidate).

Historical guesses [PPVW19, §3.1]: Néron (1950) bounded; Honda (1960) `[CONJECTURE]` $\operatorname{rank}A(K)\le c_A[K:\mathbb{Q}]$, which would bound ranks in every quadratic twist family; Cassels, Tate, Mestre, Silverman, Brumer: unbounded ("folklore"). Ulmer [Ulm02, Conj. 10.5] conjectured $\limsup r_{\mathrm{alg}}/(\log N/\log\log N)>0$; Farmer–Gonek–Hughes suggest $\limsup r/\sqrt{\log N\log\log N} = 1$; both contradict boundedness.

### E.5.2 Records and analytic bounds

`[THEOREM]` (computational) Elkies (2006): rank $\ge28$; Klagsbrun–Sherman–Weigandt [KSW19]: exactly $28$ under GRH. Elkies–Klagsbrun (August 2024): rank $\ge29$, exactly $29$ under GRH [EK24]. The largest rank known *exactly and unconditionally* is $20$ (Elkies–Klagsbrun 2020, [EK20], as recorded by Dujella [Duj]). *Very recent, not refereed:* the ICARM Elliptic Curve Rank Leaderboard [ICARM] (which certifies independence of submitted points by an exact $2$-descent computation) records curves of rank $\ge30$ (2026-08-20, credited to Claude with L. Alpöge and A. Howell; "BSD + GRH certified" rank exactly $30$) and $\ge31$ (2026-08-23, same team; exactly $31$ under BSD + GRH), and an Elkies–Klagsbrun curve of rank $\ge30$ (found September 2025, announced August 2026; exactly $30$ under GRH for number fields) [MW]. These do not contradict Conjecture E.5.3 (finitely many curves of rank $>21$ are allowed), but the pace of records since 2024 is worth noting against a heuristic that predicts a hard ceiling.

`[THEOREM]` (Mestre [Mes86, II.1.1–II.1.2], with modularity): $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ll\log N$ unconditionally and $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ll\log N/\log\log N$ under RH for $L(E,s)$. `[CONDITIONAL]` (Brumer [Bru92]): under GRH (and modularity) the average analytic rank over all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is $\le2.3$; Heath-Brown [HB04]: $\le2$. On the algebraic side the only unconditional uniform statement is $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\le s_2(E)\ll\log N$ (elementary from $2$-descent and the trivial class-number bound for the cubic field cut out by $E[2]$; the classical reference is Brumer–Kramer, Duke Math. J. 44 (1977) [unverified: not re-fetched]); nothing of the form $o(\log N)$, let alone a constant bound, is known.

### E.5.3 Why boundedness of rank would not help prove BSD, and what it would change

**Proposition E.5.4** `[THEOREM]` (logical independence, as far as is known).
(a) *Boundedness does not imply BSD.* BSD is a statement about each curve separately; a uniform bound $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le B$ restricts $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$ to $\{0,\dots,B\}$ but provides no relation with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)$, no method to compute $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$, and no information on $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$. Formally: for any $E$ and any $B\ge r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$, the statement "$r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E')\le B$ for all $E'$" is consistent with both truth and falsity of BSD for $E$ in the absence of further input.
(b) *BSD does not imply boundedness.* `[THEOREM]` (Ulmer [Ulm02]) Over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ there are explicit non-isotrivial elliptic curves of arbitrarily large rank *for which BSD is proved* (the rank is computed from the zeta function after proving the Tate conjecture for the associated surfaces). So over global fields in general, "BSD for all $E$" and "ranks unbounded" are simultaneously true; boundedness over $\mathbb{Q}$, if true, is an arithmetic input independent of BSD.
(c) *But BSD + boundedness would be a strong analytic statement.* If BSD(rank) holds for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le B$, then $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\le B$ for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ — a uniform bound on orders of vanishing of $L$-functions in a family of unbounded conductor, of a kind for which no analytic technique exists (the best bounds being Mestre's, §E.5.2). Conversely, a proof of boundedness through BSD would have to come from the analytic side, which is hopeless; and a proof of boundedness through Selmer groups would have to bound $s_p(E)$ uniformly in $E$ for some $p$, which is false ($s_2$ is unbounded, e.g. among the non-favored twists of a curve with a rational $2$-isogeny, where $s_2(E^d)>(\log\log H)^{1/2-\epsilon}$ for $100\%$ of such $d$ [Smi26b, Prop. 1.3]) — unless one bounds $s_p(E) - \dim\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p]$, i.e. controls $\operatorname{Sha}$ at the same time.

*Proof.* (a) is a statement about logical form (no known implication); (b) is Ulmer's theorem; (c) is immediate from the definitions and the cited results. $\square$

Point (c) is where the PPVW model becomes relevant to the "all $p$ at once" problem. We first make that problem precise.

**Proposition E.5.5** `[THEOREM]` (standard; proof included). For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $r := r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$, the following are equivalent: (i) $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite; (ii) $r_{p^\infty}(E) = r$ for every prime $p$, and there is $P = P(E)$ such that $s_p(E) = r$ for all primes $p>P$.

*Proof.* $s_p = r + t_p + \dim\operatorname{Sha}[p]$ and $t_p = 0$ for $p>7$ (Mazur: $\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\le16$). (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): a finite group has finite $p$-primary parts, so $r_{p^\infty} = r$ for all $p$, and $\operatorname{Sha}[p] = 0$ for $p\nmid\#\operatorname{Sha}$, so take $P = \max(7,\#\operatorname{Sha})$. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): $\operatorname{Sha} = \bigoplus_p\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$; each summand is finite (as $r_{p^\infty} = r$), and for $p>P$ the $p$-primary torsion group $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ has trivial $p$-torsion hence is trivial. $\square$

So "finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ for all $p$ at once" is precisely: (A) $r_{p^\infty}(E) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$ for every $p$ **and** (B) an *effective* $P(E)$ with $s_p(E) = r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$ for $p>P(E)$. For $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1$ both come at once from a *single global object*, the Heegner point $y_K$ of infinite order: Kolyvagin's theorem gives $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty] = 0$ for all $p$ not dividing an explicit index, with $P(E)$ effectively computable [GJPST09]. Nothing analogous exists for $r\ge2$ (`C-higher-rank-euler-systems-diagonal-cycles.md`).

**How the PPVW model changes the *nature* of (A)+(B)** `[HEURISTIC]`. In the model, rank and $\operatorname{Sha}$ are the kernel and torsion cokernel of *one* integer alternating matrix $A_E$ with entries $\le X(H)$, $X(H)^{\eta(H)} = H^{1/12+o(1)}$. Consequences inside the model: (A) is automatic ($\operatorname{coker}A_E$ is finite for every $p$); (B) holds with $P(E)\le\#(\operatorname{coker}A_E)_{\mathrm{tors}}\le H^{1/12+o(1)}$, since $s_p$ corresponds to $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\ker(A_E\bmod p) = \operatorname{rk}\ker A_E$ for $p$ not dividing the elementary divisors; and rank boundedness is the statement that the *same* matrices have large kernel only with probability $H^{-(r-1)/24}$. The model therefore predicts:

**Conjecture E.5.6** `[CONDITIONAL]` on (BSD formula for $E$) + (RH for $L(E,s)$) + (Lang's height conjecture), following [PPVW19, §11.5 items 1–4]: for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank $r$, $\#\operatorname{Sha}(E)\le H(E)^{1/12+o(1)}$, hence $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p] = 0$ for all $p>H(E)^{1/12+o(1)}$. (Under RH, $L^{(r)}(E,1)\ll_r H^{o(1)}$; under Lang's conjecture $\operatorname{Reg}(E)\ge H^{-o(1)}$; $\Omega = H^{-1/12+o(1)}$, $\prod c_p = H^{o(1)}$.)

This changes the nature of the problem in two ways, one useful and one sobering. *Useful:* the truth of (B) is expected to be a *growth* statement — $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ is bounded polynomially in the height — and any proof of rank boundedness that proceeds by controlling Selmer groups uniformly (as the model suggests it must, Proposition E.5.4(c)) would deliver (B) simultaneously; conversely, a proof of (B) with $P(E)$ polynomial in $H(E)$ would be the first unconditional "size" statement about $\operatorname{Sha}$ beyond rank $1$, and would reduce finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ to (A) for the finitely many $p\le P(E)$, each of which is a *finite computation* by the Stein–Wuthrich method under Kato's hypotheses (§E.4.4). *Sobering:* the model offers no mechanism — it presupposes (A) and calibrates (B) using BSD itself; and Conjecture E.5.6 is circular for our purposes (it assumes the BSD formula). The unconditional content that would be needed is:

**[GAP E.5]** (effective all-$p$ bound for $\operatorname{Sha}$ of a rank-$\ge2$ curve). *Statement.* Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge2$. Prove there exists an explicit $P(E)$ (any function of $E$, e.g. of $N(E)$ or $H(E)$) such that $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E) = \operatorname{im}\big(E(\mathbb{Q})/pE(\mathbb{Q})\big)$, equivalently $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p] = 0$, for all primes $p>P(E)$. Even the weaker "$s_p(E)$ is bounded independently of $p$" is open for every single curve of rank $\ge2$. *Attempt.* (1) Kolyvagin's route needs a global class of infinite order in $H^1(\mathbb{Q},T_pE)$ compatible in $p$; for rank $2$ one would need two, with a nondegenerate "height" — no construction exists (`C-…`). (2) The Iwasawa-theoretic route: see [GAP E.6]. (3) The statistical route contributes nothing: all averages are over curves at a fixed $p$, not over $p$ for a fixed curve (Remark E.1.11), and no theorem in §E.1–E.3 has any uniformity in $p$ beyond $p\le5$. *Conclusion.* Open; we could not reduce it further than [GAP E.6].

**Theorem E.5.7** `[THEOREM]` (Kato [Kat04, Thm. 17.4] with Mazur's control theorem; exact hypotheses in `A-iwasawa-padic.md`; used in this form by [SW13]). Let $p\ge5$ be a prime of good ordinary reduction for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ such that the image of $G_\mathbb{Q}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_pE)\simeq\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ contains $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Then $X_\infty := \operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$ is $\Lambda$-torsion and $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)$ divides $L_p(E,T)\Lambda$; consequently
$$ r_{p^\infty}(E)\ \le\ \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)\ \le\ \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T) =: r_p(E). $$
(The first inequality: by the control theorem $r_{p^\infty}(E) = \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty$, and for a torsion $\Lambda$-module pseudo-isomorphic to $\bigoplus\Lambda/(f_i)$ this rank is $\#\{i : f_i(0) = 0\}\le\sum_i\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f_i$.)

**[GAP E.6]** (uniform $p$-adic BSD rank). *Statement.* Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E) = r\ge2$. Prove that $r_p(E) := \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T) = r$ for all but finitely many good ordinary primes $p$ (with $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ surjective). *Consequence.* By Theorem E.5.7, $r_{p^\infty}(E) = r$, i.e. $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ is finite, for all such $p$ — that is (A) of Proposition E.5.5 for almost all $p$ at once; together with a bound on $\operatorname{Sha}[p]$ this would give [GAP E.5]. *What is known.* For $w(E) = +1$ and $L(E,1) = 0$: $r_p(E)\ge2$ and $r_p(E)$ is even (interpolation $L_p(E,0) = (1-\alpha^{-1})^2L(E,1)/\Omega_E$, and the functional equation of $L_p(E,T)$ under $T\mapsto(1+T)^{-1}-1$, whose sign forces $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\pmod 2$; see `A-iwasawa-padic.md`). Equality $r_p = 2$ is, by the $p$-adic BSD conjecture of Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum, equivalent to the non-vanishing of the $p$-adic regulator, i.e. non-degeneracy of the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing on $E(\mathbb{Q})$ — open for a single $p$ in general, and *a fortiori* for almost all $p$; it is open even for rank $1$ (where finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ is known by Kolyvagin, yet $r_p(E) = 1$ for all $p$ is not). *Attempt.* Numerically $r_p(E) = r$ is verified prime by prime [SW13, Thm. 1.1]; there is no known structure relating $L_p(E,T)$ for different $p$ (the $p$-adic $L$-functions are not specialisations of one object over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$). We see no route and record the gap; cross-reference `A-iwasawa-padic.md`.

*Summary of §E.5.* Rank boundedness is neither necessary nor sufficient for BSD and no known implication connects them. Its heuristic *source* (PPVW) is the same mechanism that predicts $\#\operatorname{Sha}(E)\le H^{1/12+o(1)}$; so the honest reformulation of "finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ for all $p$ at once" for a rank-$\ge2$ curve is the pair ([GAP E.5], [GAP E.6]) — an effective bound on the primes where $\operatorname{Sha}[p]\ne0$, and $p$-adic BSD rank for almost all $p$ — neither of which any statistical theorem addresses.
