## 0. Summary and reading guide

The Tamagawa number conjecture (TNC) of Bloch–Kato and Fontaine–Perrin-Riou for the motive
$M=h^1(E)(1)$ is *exactly* the conjunction "$\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ finite $+$ BSD leading term
(up to sign)", prime by prime (§1, Theorem 1.6). Its only known proof mechanism is the
Iwasawa-theoretic one: *zeta element with an explicit reciprocity law* $+$ *main conjecture*
$\Rightarrow$ TNC at $p$ (§2). That mechanism is complete in analytic rank $0$ (and, with Heegner
points as an extra transcendental input, in analytic rank $1$), and it stalls in rank $r\ge 2$ at one
precisely identifiable point: the *Generalized Perrin-Riou conjecture* of Burns–Kurihara–Sano, i.e.
the identification of the $(r-1)$-st Darmon derivative of Kato's zeta element with the $r$-th complex
derivative $L^{(r)}(E,1)$ — equivalently (given non-degeneracy of the $p$-adic height) the
"$p$-adic Beilinson formula" comparing the leading terms of $L_p(E,T)$ at $T=0$ and of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$
(§2.4, [GAP G1]). Everything else in the higher-rank Iwasawa route is now known, or reduces to
main conjectures and to the non-degeneracy of the cyclotomic $p$-adic height ([GAP G2]).

The equivariant refinements (§3) and the Mazur–Tate refined conjectures (§4) are the same
conjecture (eTNC) viewed through group rings; their *order-of-vanishing* and *Fitting-ideal*
components are now largely theorems (Kato $\Rightarrow$ Kurihara, Kim–Kurihara, Ota, Kataoka,
Emerton–Pollack–Weston, Bullach–Honnor 2025; C.-H. Kim 2026 shows that "Kurihara numbers"
determine the full structure of $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$), while their *leading-term*
components in rank $\ge 2$ reduce again to [GAP G1]. The precise answer to the question "does the
augmentation order of $\theta$ see the complex analytic rank?" is *no*: it sees the Selmer corank
(algebraic side) exactly, and it sees $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ only through Conjecture $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$
(§4.4–4.6; Proposition 4.6 [NEW] and Theorem 4.7).

Sections: §1 Bloch–Kato/Fontaine–Perrin-Riou for $h^1(E)(1)$; §2 zeta elements, main conjectures,
rank $0$ mechanism, higher rank; §3 equivariant refinements (Burns–Flach, Burns–Macias Castillo–Wuthrich,
Bley–Macias Castillo, Burns–Macias Castillo); §4 Mazur–Tate; §5 the gaps, each with the attempt made;
§6 2024–2026 developments the team must know; §7 references (all verified as indicated); §8 search log.

## Notation (in addition to `../00-charter.md`)

$p$ is an odd prime unless said otherwise; $T:=T_pE$, $V:=V_pE$, $A:=E[p^\infty]=V/T$. $S$ is a finite set

$R\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_S,-)$, $R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,-)$ denote continuous étale cohomology (with compact
support) of $G_{\mathbb{Q},S}$. For a number field $F$ with $G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$ abelian, $I_F=I(G)$
is the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$ (or of $R[G]$ when a coefficient ring $R$ is specified),
and for $x\in R[G]$ we write $\operatorname{ord}_I(x):=\sup\{n: x\in I^n\}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}\cup\{\infty\}$.
$\operatorname{Fitt}_R(M)$ is the (initial) Fitting ideal. $\operatorname{Tam}(E)=\prod_{\ell\mid N}c_\ell$,
$c_\ell=[E(\mathbb{Q}_\ell):E_0(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)]$. $L^*(E,1):=\lim_{s\to1}(s-1)^{-r_{\mathrm{an}}}L(E,s)$.
$R_\infty:=\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}})$ for a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis $(P_i)$ of $E(\mathbb{Q})/E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$.
$\Omega^+:=|\int_{\gamma^+}\omega|$ for $\omega$ a Néron differential and $\gamma^+$ a generator of
$H_1(E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})^{+}$; it equals $\int_{E(\mathbb{R})}|\omega|$ up to a factor in $\{1,2\}$, which is
invisible at odd $p$ and will be ignored. $s_p:=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})
= r_{\mathrm{alg}}+\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$.

"$\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$" denotes the statement: $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite and
$$\operatorname{ord}_p\Big(\frac{L^*(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}\Big)=\operatorname{ord}_p\Big(\frac{\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\cdot\operatorname{Tam}(E)}{\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2}\Big).$$
The rank conjecture is not part of $\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$, but the statement only makes sense when $r_{\mathrm{an}}=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ ($L^*(E,1)$ is defined through $r_{\mathrm{an}}$, $R_\infty$ through $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$); see Remark 1.7.

## 1. The Tamagawa number conjecture for $h^1(E)(1)$

### 1.1 The motive and Beilinson's conjecture

Let $M:=h^1(E)(1)$, a pure motive over $\mathbb{Q}$ of weight $-1$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Q}$. Its realisations:
$M_B=H^1(E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})(1)\cong H_1(E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})$ with $\dim M_B^{+}=1$ ($+$: fixed by complex conjugation);
$M_{dR}=H^1_{dR}(E/\mathbb{Q})(1)$ with tangent space $t_M:=M_{dR}/F^0M_{dR}\cong H^1(E,\mathcal{O}_E)=\operatorname{Lie}(E)$, of dimension $1$;
$M_p=V_pE=V$. The Weil pairing gives $M^*(1)\cong M$ (self-duality). Its $L$-function is $L(M,s)=L(E,s+1)$, so the
"special point" $s=0$ of $M$ is $s=1$ for $E$. Following Bloch–Kato [BK90] and Fontaine–Perrin-Riou [FPR94] one sets
$$H^0_f(M)=0,\qquad H^1_f(M):=E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q},$$
the "motivic cohomology" of $M$ (the group of $1$-extensions of $\mathbb{Q}(0)$ by $M$; it maps to $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$ by the Kummer map).

**[CONJECTURE] 1.1 (Beilinson; Deligne–Gross; [Be85], [FPR94], surveyed in [Ne94]).** For every pure motive $M$ over $\mathbb{Q}$,
$$\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}L(M,s)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^1_f(M^*(1))-\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^0_f(M^*(1)).$$
For $M=h^1(E)(1)$ this reads $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q})$, i.e. **BSD (rank)**. For the twists $M\otimes\rho$ by Artin representations it is the Deligne–Gross conjecture $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,\rho,s)=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(V_\rho,\mathbb{C}\otimes E(F))$ ([BM24, (2)] with references).

Thus the motivic framing contributes nothing new to the *rank* statement: Beilinson's conjecture for $h^1(E)(1)$ *is* BSD-rank. Its content lies in the leading term.

### 1.2 The fundamental line and the rationality conjecture

Following [FPR94] (and [Fo92], [Ki03, §1], [Ve07, §3]) the *fundamental line* is the $\mathbb{Q}$-line
$$\Delta(M):=\det_{\mathbb{Q}}H^0_f(M)^{-1}\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}H^1_f(M)\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}\big(H^0_f(M^*(1))^*\big)\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}\big(H^1_f(M^*(1))^*\big)^{-1}\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}M_B^+\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(t_M)^{-1}.$$
For $M=h^1(E)(1)$:
$$\Delta(M)=\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q})\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}\big(\operatorname{Hom}(E(\mathbb{Q}),\mathbb{Q})\big)^{-1}\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}H_1(E(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})^+\otimes\det_{\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{Lie}(E)^{-1}.$$
Conjecture 3.3 of [Ve07] (Fontaine–Perrin-Riou) is here a theorem: the period map $\alpha_M:M_B^+\otimes\mathbb{R}\to t_M\otimes\mathbb{R}$ is an isomorphism (both sides are lines and the real period is non-zero), and the Néron–Tate height gives an isomorphism $h:(E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{R})^*\cong E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{R}$ (non-degeneracy of the height). Together these define the *period–regulator isomorphism*
$$\vartheta_\infty:\ \Delta(M)\otimes\mathbb{R}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ \mathbb{R}.$$
Choosing the integral bases $(P_i)$ of $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tf}}$, the dual basis, $\gamma^+$, and $\omega$ produces a canonical generator $\mathrm{can}_{\mathbb{Z}}$ of an integral structure $\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(M)\subset\Delta(M)$ with
$$\vartheta_\infty(\mathrm{can}_{\mathbb{Z}})=\pm\,\Omega^+R_\infty\qquad\text{[Ve07, (3.25)]}.$$

**[CONJECTURE] 1.2 (Rationality; Deligne–Beilinson; [Ve07, Conj. 3.5]).** Assume Conjecture 1.1 for $M$ (so $L^*(M,0)=L^*(E,1)$ is defined). There is a (unique) $\zeta(M)\in\Delta(M)$ with $\vartheta_\infty(\zeta(M))=L^*(M,0)$. For $h^1(E)(1)$ this is the statement
$$\frac{L^*(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}\in\mathbb{Q}^\times ,\qquad\text{and then }\ \zeta(M)=\frac{L^*(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}\,\mathrm{can}_{\mathbb{Q}}.$$
This is the "BSD leading term formula up to a rational factor". Note that even this weak statement is unknown for every $E$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ (cf. `../03-obstructions-rank-ge-2.md`).

### 1.3 The $p$-adic structure and $\mathrm{TNC}_p$

Fix $p$ and $S$ as in the notation. Bloch–Kato [BK90] define the finite-part local conditions $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_v,V)$ ($=H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}$ for $v\nmid p$, $=\ker(H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)\to H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,B_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes V))$ for $v=p$) and the "finite support cohomology" $R\Gamma_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$, sitting in an exact triangle
$$R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,V)\to R\Gamma_f(\mathbb{Q},V)\to\bigoplus_{v\in S}R\Gamma_f(\mathbb{Q}_v,V)\to .$$
There are canonical trivialisations $\eta_v:\mathbb{1}\cong\det_{\mathbb{Q}_p}R\Gamma_f(\mathbb{Q}_v,V)$ for $v\nmid p$ (from $[V^{I_v}\xrightarrow{1-\mathrm{Fr}_v^{-1}}V^{I_v}]$) and $\eta_p:\mathbb{1}\cong\det R\Gamma_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)\otimes\det t(V)$ (from the Bloch–Kato exponential $D_{dR}(V)/F^0\cong H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$, using $H^0(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)=0$). Together with the comparison isomorphisms $M_B^+\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\cong V^+$ and $t_M\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\cong D_{dR}(V)/F^0$ and

**[CONJECTURE] 1.3 ([BK90], [FPR94]; [Ve07, Conj. 3.6]).** The Kummer map induces $E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\cong H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$ (equivalently, $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite),

one obtains the *$p$-adic period–regulator isomorphism* $\vartheta_p:\Delta(M)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\cong\det_{\mathbb{Q}_p}R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,V)^{-1}$ [Ve07, (3.14)].

**[CONJECTURE] 1.4 ($\mathrm{TNC}_p(M)$; Bloch–Kato, Fontaine–Perrin-Riou; [Ve07, Conj. 3.7]).** Assume Conjectures 1.1–1.3 for $M$. Then
$$\mathbb{Z}_p\cdot\vartheta_p(\zeta(M))=\det_{\mathbb{Z}_p}R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)^{-1}\quad\text{inside }\det_{\mathbb{Q}_p}R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,V)^{-1}.$$
The statement is independent of $S$ and of the lattice $T$ [FPR94], [BF01]. "$\mathrm{TNC}(M)$" means Conjectures 1.1, 1.2 and 1.4 for all $p$.

**[CONJECTURE] 1.5 (equivariant version, Burns–Flach [BF01, Conj. 4(iv)]).** For $F/\mathbb{Q}$ finite Galois with group $G$, the pair $(h^1(E_F)(1),\mathbb{Z}[G])$ satisfies an equality in the relative $K$-group $K_0(\mathbb{Z}[G],\mathbb{R}[G])$; its projection to $K_0(\mathbb{Z}_p[G],\mathbb{C}_p[G])$ is "$\mathrm{eTNC}_p$". For $G=1$ it is $\mathrm{TNC}$; in general it is a simultaneous refinement of BSD for $E$ over all intermediate fields of $F/\mathbb{Q}$ [BMW18, Introduction]. Kato's Λ-adic formulation [Ka93a, Ka93b] and Fukaya–Kato's [FK06] make the main conjectures of Iwasawa theory special cases of the same conjecture with coefficients in Iwasawa algebras; Burns–Venjakob [BV11] prove that (under standard hypotheses) the non-commutative main conjecture descends to $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ for all finite layers.

### 1.4 The equivalence with BSD

**[THEOREM] 1.6 (Bloch–Kato, Fontaine–Perrin-Riou, Burns–Flach; written out in Kings [Ki11, Lecture 3] and Venjakob [Ve07, §3.1]).** Let $p$ be odd and assume $r_{\mathrm{an}}=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ (Conjecture 1.1) and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite (Conjecture 1.3). Then:

1. $H^0_f(\mathbb{Q},T)=0$, $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},T)\cong E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p$, $H^3_f(\mathbb{Q},T)\cong(E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p)^\vee$, and there is an exact sequence $0\to\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\to H^2_f(\mathbb{Q},T)\to\operatorname{Hom}(E(\mathbb{Q}),\mathbb{Z}_p)\to0$; locally $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_\ell,T)\cong E(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)^\wedge_p$ and the trivialisations $\eta_\ell$ differ from the integral ones by the $p$-part of $c_\ell$ ([Ve07, Prop. 3.9, (3.27)–(3.32)]).
2. Consequently $\mathrm{TNC}_p(M)$ holds if and only if
$$\frac{L^*(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}\ \equiv\ \frac{\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\cdot\operatorname{Tam}(E)}{\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times},$$
i.e. if and only if $\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$ holds [Ve07, (3.35)].
3. Hence $\mathrm{TNC}(M)$ (all $p$, with Conjectures 1.1–1.3 built in) is equivalent to: $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite and the BSD leading term formula holds up to sign. (Finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ follows because, given Conjecture 1.2, $\mathrm{TNC}_p$ for all $p$ forces $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]=0$ for every $p$ dividing neither the numerator nor the denominator of the rational number $\frac{L^*(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}$ nor $\operatorname{Tam}(E)\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$.)

*Proof.* [Ve07, §3.1] (which assumes $p\ne2$ and credits M. Flach), [Ki11, Lecture 3]; the local computation of the Tamagawa factors is [FPR94, I §4], [BK90, §5]. $\square$

**Remark 1.7 (what is and is not encoded).** (i) Kings' Bordeaux survey [Ki03] explicitly *excludes* the BSD point from its list of known cases; the equivalence above is in his Park City lectures [Ki11]. (ii) The formulation of $\mathrm{TNC}_p$ needs Conjecture 1.3 (finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$) merely to *define* $\vartheta_p$; so "$\mathrm{TNC}_p\Rightarrow\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite" is not a theorem but part of the framework. Any route to $\mathrm{TNC}_p$ must therefore prove finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ separately; in the Iwasawa route this is exactly Kato's Theorem 2.6 below when $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$. (iii) The sign of $L^*(E,1)$ is not determined by $\mathrm{TNC}$ (lattices see no sign). (iv) The equivariant conjecture 1.5 for $(h^1(E_F)(1),\mathbb{Z}[G])$ with $p\mid\#G$ is strictly stronger than $\mathrm{BSD}_p$ over all intermediate fields (§3).

### 1.5 Status of $\mathrm{TNC}_p(h^1(E)(1))$ (summary; details and exact hypotheses in `../02-state-of-the-art.md`)

**[THEOREM] 1.8.** (a) *Rank $0$.* If $L(E,1)\neq0$, $E$ has good ordinary reduction at $p\ge3$, $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is surjective, and there is a prime $q\ne p$ with $q\,\|\,N$ and $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ ramified at $q$, then $\mathrm{TNC}_p(M)$ holds: this is Skinner–Urban [SU14, Thm. 2(a)] (upper bound for $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$: Kato [Ka04, §14]; lower bound: the main conjecture [SU14, Thm. 1] plus descent). The auxiliary prime $q$ can be removed in the good ordinary case by work of X. Wan (as used in [Kim26, proof of Cor. 1.5]; not re-verified here), and $p$ multiplicative is treated by Skinner [Sk16].
(b) *Rank $1$.* If $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$, $\mathrm{TNC}_p(M)$ is known under hypotheses of the same type by Jetchev–Skinner–Wan [JSW17] (good ordinary), Castella [Ca18] (multiplicative), with the Gross–Zagier formula and Kolyvagin's theorem as the transcendental input; see `B-euler-systems-heegner-kolyvagin.md`.
(c) *Rank $\ge2$.* No case of $\mathrm{TNC}_p(M)$, nor of Conjecture 1.2, is known for any $E$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ and any $p$.

(For modular motives of higher weight in analytic rank $\le1$ see Longo–Vigni [LV22] (conditional on standard conjectures); for the CM case Rubin, Kings [Ki01]. Not needed here.)

## 2. The Iwasawa-theoretic route to Bloch–Kato

### 2.1 Kato's zeta elements

Let $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ be the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension, $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]$, and for an abelian $F/\mathbb{Q}$ let $\Lambda_F=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\operatorname{Gal}(F\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q})]]$. Kato [Ka04, §§8, 12–13] constructs from Beilinson's Eisenstein symbols in $K_2$ of modular curves a canonical family (an Euler system) of classes
$$z_F\in H^1(\mathcal{O}_{F,S},T)\qquad(F/\mathbb{Q}\ \text{finite abelian}),$$
compatible under corestriction, whose limit $z_\infty\in H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q},T):=\varprojlim_n H^1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_n,S},T)$ we call *Kato's zeta element*. (Integrality: $z_F\in H^1(\mathcal{O}_{F,S},T)$ when $E[p]$ is irreducible and $E$ has good reduction at $p$; in general $z_F\in H^1(\mathcal{O}_{F,S},V)$ [Ka04, §13], [BKS25, §3.2].)

**[THEOREM] 2.1 (Kato's explicit reciprocity law; [Ka04, Thms. 6.6, 9.7, 12.5]; in the normalisation of [BKS24, Def. 6.8], [BKS25, (14)]).** For $F$ real abelian with group $G$, every $\chi\in\widehat G$ and $\Sigma=S\cup\{\text{primes ramified in }F\}$,
$$\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi(\sigma)\,\exp^*_\omega(\sigma z_F)=\frac{L_\Sigma(E,\chi,1)}{\Omega^+},$$
where $\exp^*_\omega:H^1(\mathcal{O}_{F,\Sigma},V)\to H^1_{/f}(F_p,V)\to F\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ is the Bloch–Kato dual exponential with respect to $\omega$.

Thus the zeta element *interpolates critical values* $L(E,\chi,1)$; nothing in its construction sees derivatives.

**[THEOREM] 2.2 (Kato [Ka04, Thm. 12.4, Thm. 12.5]).** Let $\mathcal{H}^i:=H^i_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q},T)$. Then $\mathcal{H}^2$ is $\Lambda$-torsion, $\mathcal{H}^1\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ is free of rank one over $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}$, $z_\infty\neq0$, and
$$\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathcal{H}^2)\ \Big|\ \operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathcal{H}^1/\Lambda z_\infty)\quad\text{in }\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p,\ \text{and in }\Lambda\text{ if }\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{E,p})\supseteq SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p).$$
(Kato requires $p$ odd in parts of §12–17; for the exact list of hypotheses in each statement see [Ka04].)

**[CONJECTURE] 2.3 (Kato's main conjecture without $p$-adic $L$-functions, [Ka04, Conj. 12.10]; equivalently [BKS24, Conj. 7.1]: $\Lambda z_\infty=\det_\Lambda(C_\infty)$).**
$\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathcal{H}^1/\Lambda z_\infty)=\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\mathcal{H}^2)$. Here $C_\infty=R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(R\Gamma_c(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_\infty,S},T^*(1)),\mathbb{Z}_p[-2])$, and $\mathcal{H}^2$ is dual to the fine ($p$-strict) Selmer group $\operatorname{Sel}_0(\mathbb{Q}_\infty,A)$ up to the local terms at $p$. This is the Λ-adic $\mathrm{TNC}$ of [Ka93a] for $T\otimes\Lambda$: "the zeta element is a basis of the determinant of Iwasawa cohomology".

**[THEOREM] 2.4 (Kato [Ka04, Thm. 16.4, 16.6, 17.4]; ordinary case).** Let $E$ have good ordinary reduction at $p$ and let $X_\infty:=\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$. Perrin-Riou's Coleman map $\mathrm{Col}:H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,T)\to\Lambda$ satisfies $\mathrm{Col}(\mathrm{loc}_p z_\infty)=L_p(E)$ (the Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer $p$-adic $L$-function, up to the involution $\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}$ and a unit), and
$$X_\infty\text{ is }\Lambda\text{-torsion and}\quad \operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)\ \big|\ L_p(E)\ \text{ in }\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\ (\text{in }\Lambda\text{ if }\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\supseteq SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)).$$
Conjecture 2.3 is equivalent to the classical main conjecture $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=(L_p(E))$ in this case ([Ka04, §17], [Kat21, Rem. 1.4]).

**[THEOREM] 2.5 (Skinner–Urban [SU14, Thm. 1]; as stated in loc. cit., §1.2).** If $E$ has good ordinary reduction at $p\ge3$, $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is irreducible, and there is a prime $q\neq p$ with $q\,\|\,N$ and $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ ramified at $q$, then $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=(L_p(E))$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, and in $\Lambda$ if moreover $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is irreducible (which is assumed). These hypotheses hold for all semistable non-CM $E$ and all good ordinary $p\ge11$ [SU14, §1.2]. Multiplicative $p$: [Sk16, Thm. A]. The other reduction types are discussed in `A-iwasawa-padic.md`.

**[THEOREM] 2.6 (Kato; the $p$-adic order of vanishing bounds the Selmer corank; [Ka04, Thm. 17.4] + Mazur's control theorem [Ma72]; stated as [Co04, Thm. 0.15, Thm. 0.23]).** Let $E$ have good ordinary reduction at the odd prime $p$. Then
$$r_p:=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)\ \ge\ s_p=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\ \ge\ r_{\mathrm{alg}},$$
and if $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ then $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite. (Proof: by 2.4, $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p\ge\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)\ge\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty$, the last by the structure theory of torsion $\Lambda$-modules; and $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty=s_p$ by the control theorem, which gives $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\Gamma$ with finite kernel and cokernel for good ordinary $p$.) In the split multiplicative case one has $r_p\ge s_p+1$ [Co04, Thm. 0.15].

### 2.2 The mechanism "zeta element $+$ main conjecture $\Rightarrow$ $\mathrm{TNC}_p$" in analytic rank $0$

We make explicit the mechanism as it applies to $h^1(E)(1)$; it is the specialisation of the descent formalism of Kato [Ka93a], Perrin-Riou [PR95], Huber–Kings [HK03] (Dirichlet motives), Burns–Greither [BG03] (Tate motives), Burns–Venjakob [BV11], and in the present case Kings [Ki11].

Write $C_0:=R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,T^*(1)),\mathbb{Z}_p[-2])$, so that $\det_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(C_0)^{-1}\cong\det_{\mathbb{Z}_p}R\Gamma_c(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)^{\pm1}$ is the lattice appearing in Conjecture 1.4, and $C_\infty\otimes^{L}_\Lambda\mathbb{Z}_p\cong C_0$ (descent). Then:

(A) *Descent of the main conjecture.* Conjecture 2.3 says $\Lambda z_\infty=\det_\Lambda(C_\infty)$ (after the identification $\det_\Lambda(C_\infty)\otimes Q(\Lambda)\cong\mathcal{H}^1\otimes Q(\Lambda)\otimes\det(T^*(1)^+)$, [BKS24, (7.1.2)]). Base change along $\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}_p$ gives $\mathbb{Z}_p\cdot z_{\mathbb{Q}}=\det_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(C_0)$ provided $z_\infty$ is a basis (i.e. provided the main conjecture holds *in $\Lambda$*, not merely in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$; the $\mu$-invariant matters here).

(B) *Identification of the zeta element with the motivic element.* Assume $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$. Then $L(E,1)\ne0$, and by Theorem 2.1, $\exp^*_\omega(z_{\mathbb{Q}})=\prod_{\ell\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}P_\ell(\ell^{-1})\cdot L(E,1)/\Omega^+\neq0$ (with $P_\ell$ the Euler factor). Kato's Euler system argument [Ka04, Thm. 14.2] (Kolyvagin–Rubin machinery) gives: $E(\mathbb{Q})$ and $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ are finite. Hence Conjecture 1.3 holds, $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)=0$, $H^2_f(\mathbb{Q},T)\cong\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ (finite), and $\det_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(C_0\otimes\mathbb{Q})$ is trivialised by the dual exponential: $z_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\vartheta_p(\zeta(M))$ are both identified with elements of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, equal respectively to $\exp^*_\omega(z_{\mathbb{Q}})$ and to $L(E,1)/\Omega^+$ times the *same* Euler factors (the Euler factors are precisely the $\eta_\ell$ trivialisations of §1.3). So $z_{\mathbb{Q}}=\vartheta_p(\zeta(M))$ up to the explicit unit normalisations, *unconditionally* in rank $0$.

(C) Combining (A) and (B): main conjecture in $\Lambda$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mathbb{Z}_p\vartheta_p(\zeta(M))=\det_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(C_0)$, i.e. $\mathrm{TNC}_p(M)$, i.e. $\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$ by Theorem 1.6. The divisibility of Theorem 2.2 alone gives one inclusion, $\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\cdot\operatorname{Tam}(E)/\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2\ \big|\ L(E,1)/\Omega^+$ up to the $\mu$-ambiguity — this is Kato's upper bound for $\operatorname{Sha}$; the reverse inclusion is the content of the main conjecture (Skinner–Urban). This is Theorem 1.8(a).

The essential features to retain: (i) the only *analytic* input is the interpolation formula 2.1 for the *value* $L(E,1)$; (ii) the "unit ambiguity" inherent in a statement about lattices is resolved because both sides are canonically pinned down in a one-dimensional space by the same explicit element; (iii) $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite comes out of the Euler system, not out of the main conjecture.

### 2.3 What changes in rank $r\ge1$

Assume $r:=r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge1$ and (for this subsection) $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite and $E(\mathbb{Q})[p]=0$ (so $H^1(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)$ is $\mathbb{Z}_p$-free) — these are Hypothesis 2.2 of [BKS24].

*The bottom class vanishes for $r\ge2$.* By Theorem 2.1, $\exp^*_\omega(z_{\mathbb{Q}})=0$ (as $L(E,1)=0$), so $z_{\mathbb{Q}}\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},T)=E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p$. Moreover [BKS24, Prop. 4.5] (proved from [Ka04, Thm. 12.4(2), 12.5(3)]) shows that the ($c,d$-modified) elements ${}_{c,d}z_{\mathbb{Q}_n}$ lie in $I_n^{\,r-1}\cdot H^1(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_n,S},T)$ for all $n$; hence $z_\infty\in I_\infty^{\,r-1}(\mathcal{H}^1\otimes\mathbb{Q})$, and since $\mathcal{H}^1/I_\infty\mathcal{H}^1\hookrightarrow H^1(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)$, $z_{\mathbb{Q}}=0$ in $H^1(\mathbb{Z}_S,V)$ whenever $r\ge2$. The information carried by the Euler system in rank $r$ sits in the $(r-1)$-st *Darmon derivative*
$$\kappa_\infty:=\varprojlim_n\ \Big[\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_n}\sigma(z_{\mathbb{Q}_n})\otimes\sigma^{-1}\Big]\ \in\ H^1(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p}I_\infty^{\,r-1}/I_\infty^{\,r}\ \cong\ E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes I_\infty^{\,r-1}/I_\infty^{\,r}$$
[BKS24, §1.3.1, Def. 4.6]. For $r=1$, $\kappa_\infty=z_{\mathbb{Q}}$.

*The BSD element and the Bockstein regulator.* Using the leading term $L^*_S(E,1)$, the Néron–Tate height and $\log_\omega$, [BKS24, Def. 2.4] define a canonical $\eta^{\mathrm{BSD}}\in\mathbb{C}_p\cdot\bigwedge^r_{\mathbb{Z}_p}H^1(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)$ (a "rank-$r$ zeta element" in the sense needed for BSD; see 2.4 below), and a canonical *Bockstein regulator* $R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega\in H^1(\mathbb{Z}_S,T)\otimes I_\infty^{\,r-1}/I_\infty^{\,r}$ [BKS24, Def. 4.11], with $R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega=\log_\omega(x)\,x$ if $r=1$ and, when $E$ is not additive at $p$,
$$\langle x,R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega\rangle_p=\log_\omega(x)\cdot R_p\quad(x\in E(\mathbb{Q}))\qquad\text{[BKS24, Thm. 5.6, 5.11]},$$
$R_p$ the $p$-adic regulator for the canonical $p$-adic height $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_p$.

**[CONJECTURE] 2.7 (Generalized Perrin-Riou conjecture along $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$; [BKS24, Conj. 1.5 = Conj. 4.9]; the "finite level" version is [BKS24, Conj. 1.1 = Conj. 2.12]).** If $r=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ then
$$\kappa_\infty=\frac{L^{(r)}_S(E,1)}{\Omega^+\cdot R_\infty}\cdot R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega\qquad\text{in }E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes(I_\infty^{\,r-1}/I_\infty^{\,r}\otimes\mathbb{C}_p),$$
where $L^{(r)}_S(E,1)$ is the coefficient of $(s-1)^r$ in the Taylor expansion of the $S$-truncated $L$-function. For $r=1$ this is Perrin-Riou's conjecture [PR93, §3.3] on $\log_\omega(z_{\mathbb{Q}})$ [BKS24, Thm. 1.4].

**[THEOREM] 2.8 ([BKS24]).**
(i) *(Generalized Rubin formula, Thm. 1.8.)* If $E$ is good or non-split multiplicative at $p$, then for all $x\in E(\mathbb{Q})$: $\ \langle x,\kappa_\infty\rangle_p=(1-\alpha^{-1})^{-1}(1-\beta^{-1})\log_\omega(x)\cdot L^{(r)}_{S,p}$, where $L^{(r)}_{S,p}$ is the $r$-th Taylor coefficient of the $S$-truncated $p$-adic $L$-function at the trivial character and $\alpha,\beta$ the roots of $X^2-a_pX+p$ ($\alpha$ allowable). (Split multiplicative case: with $\mathcal{L}$-invariant and $L^{(r+1)}$.)
(ii) *(Cor. 6.7.)* Conjecture 2.7 implies the "$p$-adic Beilinson formula"
$$(1-\alpha^{-1})^{-1}(1-\beta^{-1})\,L^{(r)}_{S,p}=\frac{L^{(r)}_S(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}\,R_p ,$$
and conversely the formula implies Conjecture 2.7 when $R_p\ne0$.
(iii) *(Thm. 7.3, Cor. 7.4.)* Under Hypothesis 2.2 of loc. cit., Conjecture 2.3 (the main conjecture) implies $\kappa_\infty=u\cdot(\text{explicit Euler factors})\cdot\frac{\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\operatorname{Tam}(E)}{\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2}\cdot R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega$ for some $u\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ ("algebraic Generalized Perrin-Riou conjecture up to a unit"), hence the Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum $p$-adic BSD formula up to $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, for every reduction type.
(iv) *(Thm. 7.6.)* If $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite, $r=r_{\mathrm{an}}$, Conjecture 2.3 holds, Conjecture 2.7 holds and $R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega\ne0$, then $\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$ holds (any reduction type at $p$).

Journal reference for [BKS24]: J. Math. Soc. Japan 76 (2024), DOI 10.2969/jmsj/90699069.

**[THEOREM] 2.9 (Perrin-Riou's conjecture, $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$).** Conjecture 2.7 with $r=1$ is known, under the respective hypotheses of the papers cited: for $p$ good ordinary by Büyükboduk–Pollack–Sasaki [BPS18, via critical-slope $p$-adic Gross–Zagier] and Bertolini–Darmon–Venerucci [BDV22]; for $p$ good supersingular with $N$ square-free by Büyükboduk [Bü20, Thm. 2.4(iv)]; a weak form for split multiplicative $p$ by Venerucci [Ve16, Thm. A]. (C.-H. Kim [Kim26, proof of Cor. 1.5] summarises: "for every semistable prime $p>2$", citing in addition Burungale–Skinner–Tian–Wan.) All proofs use Heegner points and a $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula, i.e. exactly the rank-one transcendental input that is unavailable for $r\ge2$.

Consequently, in rank $1$ the chain "Kato's zeta element $+$ main conjecture $+$ Perrin-Riou's conjecture $\Rightarrow\mathrm{TNC}_p$" is complete and gives an alternative to [JSW17] (cf. [BKS24, §1.2.2]).

### 2.4 What a "rank-$r$ zeta element" would have to be, and where the analytic input enters

Two different notions of "rank" must be kept apart.

(a) *Euler-system rank.* In Perrin-Riou's theory [PR95], [PR98] and Burns–Sano [BS21] an Euler system for $T$ over the cyclotomic tower has a natural rank equal to the *core rank* $\operatorname{rank}\bigoplus_{v\mid\infty}H^0(\mathbb{Q}_v,T)=\operatorname{rank}T^{c=1}$ [BS21, Rem. 2.14], which is $1$ for $T=T_pE$. Kato's system is a rank-one Euler system in a rank-one situation, and [BS21, Thm. 1.3] shows that "basic" Euler systems of the correct core rank exist unconditionally for very general $T$, with canonical *higher Kolyvagin derivatives* $\mathcal{D}_r$ to Kolyvagin/Stark systems [BS21, Thm. 1.2], [BSS19a], [BSS19b]. So there is no shortage of "rank-$r$ Euler systems" in this sense, and none is needed for $E/\mathbb{Q}$.

(b) *BSD rank.* What higher-rank BSD needs is an element
$$\eta_r\in\bigcap\nolimits^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},T)\ \big(=\bigwedge^r_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p)\text{ if }\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\text{ is finite}\big)$$
satisfying: (Z1) *integrality* — $\eta_r$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-basis of $\det$ of the relevant Selmer complex twisted by $\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\operatorname{Tam}(E)/\#E(\mathbb{Q})^2_{\mathrm{tors}}$; (Z2) *interpolation* — under the rank-$r$ regulator (Néron–Tate height $\otimes\log_\omega$) its image is $L^{(r)}_S(E,1)/(r!\,\Omega^+R_\infty)$; (Z3) *compatibility* — $\eta_r$ is the leading coefficient of the Darmon derivative of Kato's system: $\operatorname{Boc}_{\mathbb{Q}_\infty}(\eta_r)=\kappa_\infty$. The element $\eta^{\mathrm{BSD}}$ of [BKS24, Def. 2.4] is the unique element satisfying (Z2) (it is *defined* by the complex leading term); Conjecture 2.7 is (Z3) for it; (Z1) for it is equivalent to $\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$ [BKS24, Prop. 2.6]. Conversely, the main conjecture produces unconditionally (up to $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$) an element $\eta^{\mathrm{alg}}$ satisfying (Z1) and (Z3) [BKS24, Thm. 7.3], [BKS25, Prop. 4.4] ("determinantal zeta element", [BKS25, Def. 4.3]; Kataoka's [Kat21] equivariant version).

Hence: **the only missing ingredient is (Z2) for $\eta^{\mathrm{alg}}$, i.e. Conjecture 2.7.** By Theorem 2.8(ii), when $R_p\ne0$ this is equivalent to the $p$-adic Beilinson formula, a statement comparing the leading coefficients of $L_p(E,T)$ at $T=0$ and of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$. For $r=1$ that formula is the $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula (Perrin-Riou 1987 ordinary, Kobayashi 2013 supersingular) [BKS24, Rem. 6.8]. For $r\ge2$ no algebraic cycle, no Heegner-type object and no analytic argument producing $L^{(r)}(E,1)$ is known (`../03-obstructions-rank-ge-2.md`, `C-higher-rank-euler-systems-diagonal-cycles.md`). This is [GAP G1] of §5.

**Remark 2.10 (the transcendental content is exactly a unit).** Under the main conjecture, Theorem 2.8(iii) gives $\kappa_\infty=u\cdot\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{alg}}\cdot R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega$ with $u\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ unknown, and Conjecture 2.7 asserts $\kappa_\infty=\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}\cdot R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega$. So, given the main conjecture and $R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega\ne0$, Conjecture 2.7 is equivalent to $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}=u\,\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{alg}}$ with the *specific* unit $u$ produced by the descent. $\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$ only asserts $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}/\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{alg}}\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. Thus in higher rank the Iwasawa route proves "$\mathrm{TNC}_p$ up to the identification of one $p$-adic unit with a complex-analytic quantity", and that identification is the whole problem.

## 3. Equivariant refinements

### 3.1 The framework

For $F/k$ finite Galois with group $G$ and $A/k$ an abelian variety, $\mathrm{eTNC}_p(h^1(A_F)(1),\mathbb{Z}[G])$ (Conjecture 1.5) is an equality in $K_0(\mathbb{Z}_p[G],\mathbb{C}_p[G])$. Burns–Macias Castillo–Wuthrich [BMW18, Thm. 4.3, 5.1] compute, under hypotheses (a)–(i) of loc. cit. (in particular: $p\nmid\#A(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}\#A^t(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, $p\nmid\operatorname{Tam}$, good reduction at $p$-adic places, mild ramification conditions, $\operatorname{Sha}(A_F)$ finite), the "algebraic part" of the equivariant Tamagawa number as a refined Euler characteristic of the Bloch–Kato finite-support complex $R\Gamma_f(k,T_{p,F}(A))$, whose cohomology is $A^t(F)_p$ in degree $1$ and $\operatorname{Sel}_p(A_F)^\vee$ in degree $2$ [BMW18, Lem. 4.1]. This turns $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ into explicit statements about the *leading terms* $L^*_{S_r}(A,\check\psi,1)$ of the twisted $L$-functions, normalised by equivariant regulators and periods.

**[THEOREM] 3.1 ([BMW18, Thm. 1.1]).** Let $p$ be odd, $K$ imaginary quadratic with $p$ unramified, $F$ its Hilbert $p$-class field, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$ (generalised dihedral). Let $A/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve satisfying hypotheses (a),(b),(c),(e),(f) of [BMW18, §3] with $\operatorname{Sha}(A_F)$ finite, such that all bad primes split in $K$, $\operatorname{Gal}(K(A[p])/K)\cong GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$, $L(A/K,s)$ has a simple zero at $s=1$, and the trace to $A(K)$ of the Heegner point is not divisible by $p$. Then $\mathrm{eTNC}_p(h^1(A_F)(1),\mathbb{Z}[G])$ holds.
This is the first verification of $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ for an abelian variety with positive rank over $F$, $G$ non-abelian and $p\mid\#G$; the transcendental input is the Gross–Zagier formula.

**[CONJECTURE] 3.2 (Dihedral congruences; [BMW18, Thm. 5.8] — an equivalent reformulation of $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$).** Let $F/k$ be dihedral of degree $2p^n$ with $p$-Sylow $P$, $K=F^P$, $A/k$ an elliptic curve satisfying (a)–(h), all places above $p$ split in $K/k$, $\operatorname{rank}A(K)=1$, $\operatorname{Sha}_p(A_K)=0$. Let $Q\in A(F)$ be the point of [BMW18, Prop. 5.7] and for $\psi\in\operatorname{Ir}(G)$ put
$$\mathcal{Q}_\psi:=u_\psi\cdot\frac{\sqrt{d_\psi}\cdot L^*_{S_r}(A,\psi,1)}{\Omega(A,\psi)\cdot H_\psi},\qquad H_\psi=\frac{\psi(1)}{2|G|}\big\langle T_\psi Q,T_{\check\psi}Q\big\rangle_F\ (\psi\ne\rho_A),\ H_{\rho_A}=1,$$
(so $L^*=L$ for $\psi=\rho_A$ and $L^*=L'$ otherwise). Then $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ holds iff (i) each $\mathcal{Q}_\psi$ lies in $\mathbb{Q}(\psi)$, is a unit above $p$ and is Galois-equivariant in $\psi$; (ii) for all $\pi\in P$,
$$\mathcal{Q}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{Q}_\epsilon\ \equiv\ -\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Ir}(P)\setminus\{1\}}\check\chi(\pi)\,\mathcal{Q}_{\operatorname{Ind}\chi}\pmod{p^n\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}.$$
For $n=1$: $\mathcal{Q}_1\mathcal{Q}_\epsilon\equiv-2\sum_{\dim\psi=2}\mathcal{Q}_\psi\pmod p$. These are congruences between *first derivatives* of twisted $L$-functions normalised by Néron–Tate heights of Heegner-type points.

**[CONJECTURE/THEOREM] 3.3 (cyclic $p$-power extensions; Bley–Macias Castillo [BMC17]).** Let $F/k$ be cyclic of degree $p^n$ ($p$ odd) with hypotheses (a)–(h) of [BMC17, §2] (in particular $\operatorname{Sha}_p(A_{F^H})=0$ for all $1\neq H\le G$). Yakovlev's theorem gives $A(F)_p\cong\bigoplus_{J\le G}\mathbb{Z}_p[G/J]^{m_J}$ [BMC17, Prop. 2.2]; put $h:=\sum_{t<n}m_{H_t}$. Define the leading-term element $\mathcal{L}:=\sum_\psi\frac{L^*_\psi}{\lambda_\psi(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^t)}e_\psi\in\mathbb{C}_p[G]$ with the "lower $\psi$-minors" $\lambda_\psi$ of the equivariant height matrix (loc. cit. Def. 2.4) and $L^*_\psi=L^*_{S_r}(A,\check\psi,1)\tau^*(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{ind}\psi)^d/(\Omega(A/k)w_\infty(k)^d)$. Then:
(i) [Thm. 2.9] if $\operatorname{Sha}_p(A_F)=0$, $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ is *equivalent* to $\sum_\psi\frac{L^*_\psi}{\lambda_\psi\,\varepsilon_\psi(\Phi)\,\delta_\psi}e_\psi\in\mathbb{Z}_p[G]^\times$ for explicit $\varepsilon_\psi,\delta_\psi$;
(ii) [Cor. 2.11] if $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ holds and $\operatorname{Sha}_p(A_F)=0$, then $\mathcal{L}\in I_{G,p}^{\,h}$ and $\mathcal{L}\equiv\frac{v}{\epsilon}\prod_{t<n}(\sigma^{p^t}-1)^{m_t}\pmod{I_{G,p}^{\,h+1}}$ with $v=\pm L^*_{S_r}(A/k,1)\sqrt{|d_k|}^{\,d}/(\Omega(A)\det(1_G(R)))\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$;
(iii) [Thm. 2.12] if $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ holds, $\mathcal{L}\in I^{\,h}_{G,p}$, $\mathcal{L}$ annihilates $\operatorname{Sha}_p(A^t_F)$, and $\mathcal{L}$ generates the Fitting ideal of $\operatorname{Sel}_p(A_F)^\vee/\Pi$ for a free submodule $\Pi$ of rank $m_G$ — a "strong main conjecture" of the type Mazur and Tate asked for.
These refine Mazur–Tate: *the augmentation order of the leading-term element is predicted exactly* ($h=\sum_{t<n}m_t$, a Galois-module-theoretic quantity), whereas Mazur–Tate's $\theta$ interpolates *values* and vanishes identically when ranks grow in $F$.

**[CONJECTURE] 3.4 (Burns–Macias Castillo, Memoirs [BM24]).** For $A/k$ and $F/k$ finite Galois, [BM24, §3] formulates "$\mathrm{BSD}(A_{F/k})$": the image under the connecting map of the quotient of a $K_1$-valued leading coefficient (assuming the relevant GRH for the leading terms) by a $K_1$-valued period equals the Euler characteristic of a Nekovář–Selmer complex built from a choice of global differentials, with Néron–Tate height as trivialisation and explicit Fontaine–Messing correction terms. It recovers BSD for $F=k$, and its $p$-components coincide with $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ under the hypotheses of [BM24, App. A]. Its explicit consequences include: refinements of Deligne–Gross (§7); *abelian congruence relations* (§8–9) — in particular Conjecture 9.3, "a Mazur–Tate conjecture for higher derivatives": for $J\le G$, $E=F^J$, and a $p$-separable choice of points $(Y,Y')$ of ranks $(a,a')$,
$$N_J(\eta_{Y,x_\bullet})=(-1)^{a(a'-a)}\,\nu_J\big(\mathrm{ht}^{\mathrm{MT}}_{Y'}(\eta_{Y',x_\bullet^J})\big)\ \ \text{in }\big(\textstyle\bigcap^a_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G]}A^t(F)_p\big)\otimes I_p(J)^{a'-a}/I_p(J)^{1+a'-a},$$
where $\eta_{Y,x_\bullet}$ is built from $L^{(a)}_S(A_{F/k},1)/(\Omega\,w^d)$, logarithmic resolvents and heights, and $\mathrm{ht}^{\mathrm{MT}}$ from the Mazur–Tate biextension pairing; [BM24, Thm. 9.6]: $\mathrm{BSD}(A_{F/k})$ $\Rightarrow$ Conjecture 9.3 under (H1)–(H5) and Hyp. 10.1; [BM24, Thm. 10.3]: the Mazur–Tate pairing coincides with the inverse of a Nekovář height defined by Bockstein maps. [BM24, Rem. 9.4]: for $k=\mathbb{Q}$, $J=G$, $a=0$ the congruence refines and extends [MT87]; for $k$ imaginary quadratic it incorporates Darmon's conjecture [Da92].

**What is proved in §3.** (1) $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ with $p\mid\#G$ and positive rank: only Theorem 3.1 (rank one over $K$, dihedral) and its extension via Zhang's theorem [BM24, §12], plus numerical verifications (Bley in [BM24, §9], [BMC21, Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc.] for non-projective Mordell–Weil groups). (2) Rank $0$ over $F$ with $p\mid\#G$: Bley; for tame abelian $F/\mathbb{Q}$ and $E/\mathbb{Q}$, [BM24, §11] deduces from Kato's theorem infinitely many $(p,F)$ with $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ valid and $\exp(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})_p)\ge p^n$. (3) Bullach–Burns [BB25] (preprint, 148 pp.) develop Euler systems valued in Nekovář–Selmer complexes and prove "one inclusion" of $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ for $T_pE$ over abelian fields under Hypothesis 1.2 of [BH25] — this is the input to Bullach–Honnor's Mazur–Tate theorems (§4.5). (4) Everything with $\operatorname{rank}A(F)\ge2$ over the top field and $p\mid\#G$ is open, and reduces (for $k=\mathbb{Q}$, $F$ abelian) to [GAP G1] via [BKS25, Thm. 1.1] as explained in §4.5.

## 4. The refined conjectures of Mazur and Tate

### 4.1 Modular elements

Fix the newform $f_E$ and Néron differential $\omega$. For $a/b\in\mathbb{Q}$ define the plus/minus modular symbols by
$$2\pi\int_0^\infty f_E\Big(\frac ab+iy\Big)\,dy=\Big[\frac ab\Big]^+\Omega^+_E+\Big[\frac ab\Big]^-\sqrt{-1}\,\Omega^-_E,\qquad\Big[\frac ab\Big]^\pm\in\mathbb{Q}$$
(Manin–Drinfeld; conventions as in [MT87, (1.1)], [Kim26, §1.4.1]; here $\Omega^\pm_E$ are the real/imaginary Néron periods). For $m\ge1$, $G_m:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times$, $a\mapsto\sigma_a$, set
$$\theta_m:=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times}\Big[\frac am\Big]^+\sigma_a\ \in\ \mathbb{Q}[G_m],\qquad \theta_F:=\pi_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)/F}(\theta_m)\ \ (F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)).$$
The *modular element* of [MT87] is the image of $\tfrac12\theta_m$ in $\mathbb{Q}[G_m/\{\pm1\}]$ (cf. [Ota18, Rem. 2.2], [BKS25, §2.2], [BH25, §1.1]); we use $\theta_m$ and $\theta_F$ as above. Basic properties:

**[THEOREM] 4.1.** (i) *(Interpolation.)* For $\chi$ a primitive character of $G_m$ (real fields: $\chi$ even), $\chi(\theta_m)=\tau_m(\chi)\,L(E,\chi^{-1},1)/\Omega^+$ with the Gauss sum $\tau_m(\chi)=\sum_{\sigma}\chi(\sigma)\zeta_m^{\sigma}$ ([MT87, §1], [Ota18, Prop. 2.3(2)]); $\theta_1=[0]^+=L(E,1)/\Omega^+$. (ii) *(Norm relations.)* For $\ell\nmid mN$: $\pi_{m\ell/m}(\theta_{m\ell})=-\sigma_\ell(1-a_\ell\sigma_\ell^{-1}+\sigma_\ell^{-2})\theta_m=(a_\ell-\sigma_\ell-\sigma_\ell^{-1})\theta_m$ [Ota18, Prop. 2.3(1)]; for $p\nmid N$ and $n\ge1$: $\pi_{p^{n+1}/p^n}(\theta_{p^{n+1}})=a_p\theta_{p^n}-\nu_{p^{n-1},p^n}(\theta_{p^{n-1}})$, where $\nu$ is the transfer $\sigma\mapsto\sum_{\tau\mapsto\sigma}\tau$ (the Hecke relation $\sum_{b}[\frac{a+bm}{pm}]+[\frac{pa}{m}]=a_p[\frac am]$, [MTT86, §I]). (iii) *(Integrality.)* $\theta_m\in\mathbb{Z}[1/(t\,c)][G_m]$ with $t=\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ and $c$ the Manin constant of the strong Weil curve (Stevens [St89, §3], as recorded in [Ota18, Rem. 1.3(5)]); if $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is irreducible and $p\nmid c$ then $[a/m]^+\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ [Kim26, §1.4.1]. (iv) *(Functional equation.)* $\theta_m$ and $\theta_m^\#$ ($\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}$) differ by the sign $w(E)=(-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}}$ times a unit of $\mathbb{Z}[G_m]$ when $\gcd(\gcd(m,N),N/\gcd(m,N))=1$ [BH25, Rem. 1.5(a), Rem. 2.5], [MT87, (1.6.4)].

The augmentation filtration is *integral in nature*: over $\mathbb{Q}_p[G]$ the augmentation ideal is generated by the idempotent $1-e_1$, so $I^r\otimes\mathbb{Q}=I\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ for all $r\ge1$; likewise if $\#G\in R^\times$ then $I_R=I_R^2=\cdots$ [Ota18, Rem. 2.5(1a)]. All statements below are therefore about $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$- or $\mathbb{Z}_p$-coefficients with $p\mid\#G$, and require the $p$-integrality (iii).

### 4.2 The conjectures

Let $F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)^+$ be real abelian of conductor $m$, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$, $R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}$ a ring with $\theta_F\in R[G]$ and $\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\in R^\times$, $I=I_R(G)$, $r=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$, $\operatorname{sp}(m)$ the number of primes $\ell\mid m$ of split multiplicative reduction. Mazur–Tate [MT87, Ch. II] construct, via biextensions, a canonical *$S$-height pairing* $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_S:A_S\times B_S\to C_S$ on subgroups of finite index of $E(\mathbb{Q})$ enlarged by local data, with $\operatorname{rank}A_S=r+\#S_m$ and value group $C_S=\prod_{\ell\in S\setminus S_m}\mathbb{F}_\ell^\times\times\prod_{\ell\in S_m}\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times/\{\pm1\}$ [MT87, Ch. II, §§2–4; exposition in Feng's notes on Conrad's seminar, and [BKS25, §2.2]]. Composing with $C_S\to G\cong I/I^2$ gives $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_F:E(\mathbb{Q})\times E_S(\mathbb{Q})\to I/I^2$, where $E_S(\mathbb{Q})=\ker\big(E(\mathbb{Q})\to\bigoplus_{\ell\mid m}E(\mathbb{F}_\ell)\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell\mid N}E(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)/E_0(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)\big)$, and the *Mazur–Tate regulator* $R_F:=\det(\langle x_i,x_j\rangle_F)_{i,j}\in I^r/I^{r+1}$ ($x_i$ a basis of $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tf}}$; when $p\nmid\#\text{cokernel}$ one may take $\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes E_S(\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes E(\mathbb{Q})$, [BKS25, (5)]). [BM24, Thm. 10.3]: this pairing is the inverse of a Nekovář height pairing given by a Bockstein homomorphism; [BKS25, §5.3] gives the same description for the $p$-component.

**[CONJECTURE] 4.2 (Mazur–Tate [MT87]; numbering of loc. cit. as reported in [KK21, §1.2], [Kat21, §1], [BKS25, §2.2], [BH25, (1.1),(1.6)], [LC24, §2]).**
(a) *Weak vanishing conjecture* [MT87, Conj. 1]: for every character $\chi$ of $G$, $\operatorname{ord}_{I_\chi}(\theta_F)\ge\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes E(F))^{(\chi)}$, where $I_\chi=\ker(\chi:R[\chi][G]\to R[\chi])$.
(b) *Weak main conjecture* [MT87, Conj. 3]: $\theta_F\in\operatorname{Fitt}_{R[G]}(S(E/F)\otimes R)$, where $S(E/F)$ is the "integral Selmer group" of [MT87, §1.7], whose $p$-completion is $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F)^\vee$ when $\operatorname{Sha}(E/F)$ is finite [BH25, §1.1].
(c) *Order of vanishing* [MT87, Conj. 4, first part]: $\theta_F\in I^{\,r+\operatorname{sp}(m)}$ — for $F=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)^+$ and $R$ the smallest admissible ring, [LC24, Conj. 2.4].
(d) *Leading term* [MT87, Conj. 4, second part] (for $(m,N)=1$; $p$-component as in [BKS25, Conj. 2.3], [Ota18, Conj. 2.4]): $\operatorname{Sha}$ is finite and
$$\theta_{F,S}\ \equiv\ (-1)^{\nu(m)}\cdot\frac{\#\operatorname{Sha}\cdot\operatorname{Tam}(E)}{\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^2}\prod_{\ell\in S}\frac{\#E^{\mathrm{ns}}(\mathbb{F}_\ell)}{\ell}\cdot R_F\pmod{I^{r+1}},$$
where $\theta_{F,S}$ is the $S$-truncated element ($S=\{\ell\mid mN\}$) characterised by $\chi(\theta_{F,S})=\tau_m(\chi)L_S(E,\chi^{-1},1)/\Omega^+$; in Mazur–Tate's own normalisation the right side is $(-1)^{\nu(m)}\#\operatorname{Sha}\cdot J_S\cdot R_S$ with $J_S$ the order of the cokernel of $E(\mathbb{Q})\to\bigoplus_{\ell\mid m}E(\mathbb{F}_\ell)\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell\mid N}E(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)/E_0(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ and $R_S$ the "corrected discriminant" (an alternating sum over $T\mid m$ of discriminants of $T$-pairings, [Ota18, Conj. 2.4], [MT87, (2.5),(2.6),(3.1)]); the two are equivalent [BKS25, Rem. 2.7]. For $r=0$, (d) is equivalent to the BSD leading term formula [BKS25, Rem. 2.5].
(e) *Split-multiplicative leading terms* [MT87, Conj. 5, 6]: if $m'$ is a product of split multiplicative primes, with $L\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{m'})^+$, $q_{E,\ell}$ the Tate period and $\operatorname{rec}_\ell$ the local reciprocity map,
$$\theta_L\equiv\theta_{\mathbb{Q}}\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid m'}\big(\operatorname{Tam}_\ell^{-1}(\operatorname{rec}_\ell(q_{E,\ell})-1)\big)\pmod{I^{\operatorname{sp}(m')+1}}\qquad\text{[BH25, (1.6)]};$$
Conj. 5 is the version with $\theta_{\mathbb{Q}}=L(E,1)/\Omega^+$ replaced by $\#\operatorname{Sha}\prod_{\ell\notin S_m}c_\ell/t^2$ [LC24, Conj. 2.5].
(f) *Strong main conjecture* (Kurihara [Ku02, Conj. 0.3], for $F=\mathbb{Q}_n\subset\mathbb{Q}_\infty$, $p$ good, $E(\mathbb{Q})[p]=0$, $p\nmid\operatorname{Tam}(E)$): $(\theta_{\mathbb{Q}_n},\nu(\theta_{\mathbb{Q}_{n-1}}))=\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]}(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)^\vee)$.

**Caveat (Llerena-Córdova 2024, [LC24]).** Numerical tests on $\approx4.3\cdot10^5$ pairs $(E,p)$ with split multiplicative $p$ show that Conjectures 4 and 6 of [MT87] *fail* for the smallest ring $R$ making them meaningful, precisely at primes $\ell\mid\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ with $\ell^{-1}\notin R$; all failures disappear when $\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^{-1}\in R$ ([LC24, §3], Conjectures 3.4–3.6 there). We therefore always assume $\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\in R^\times$, as do [Ota18], [BKS25], [BH25].

**Remark 4.3 (trivial zeros).** Beyond $\operatorname{sp}(m)$, a good prime $\ell\mid m$ with $a_\ell=2$ forces an extra zero: the norm relation gives $\pi_{m/(m/\ell)}(\theta_m)=(a_\ell-\sigma_\ell-\sigma_\ell^{-1})\theta_{m/\ell}$ and $a_\ell-2\equiv0$, so $\theta_m\in I$ even if $r=0$ [Ota18, Rem. 2.5(1b), Thm. 7.1]; Mazur–Tate's corrected discriminant accounts for this. Bullach–Honnor's exponent $2c^{(p)}(K)$ in Theorem 4.13 below is the general form of this phenomenon.

### 4.3 From Fitting ideals to orders of vanishing

**[THEOREM] 4.4 (Mazur–Tate [MT87, p. 720, Prop. 3]; proof on the page).** Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $M$ a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$-module, $I\subset\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$ the augmentation ideal, and $s:=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(M/IM)$. Then $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G]}(M)\subseteq I^s$. Consequently, if $\theta_F\in\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G]}(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F)^\vee)$ then $\theta_F\in I^{\,s_p}\subseteq I^{\,r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$, where $s_p=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$. (The same argument at a character $\chi$, over $\mathbb{Z}_p[\chi]$, gives (b) $\Rightarrow$ (a) in Conjecture 4.2.)

*Proof.* Write $G=\Delta\times P$ with $P$ the $p$-Sylow subgroup and let $e_1:=\#\Delta^{-1}\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}\delta\in\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$ (an idempotent since $p\nmid\#\Delta$). Then $\mathbb{Z}_p[G]=e_1\mathbb{Z}_p[G]\times(1-e_1)\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$ with $e_1\mathbb{Z}_p[G]=\mathbb{Z}_p[P]$, and since $\operatorname{aug}(1-e_1)=0$ we get $I=(1-e_1)\mathbb{Z}_p[G]\oplus e_1I(P)$ and $I^s=(1-e_1)\mathbb{Z}_p[G]\oplus e_1I(P)^s$; here $\mathbb{Z}_p[P]$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(p,I(P))$. Fitting ideals commute with base change, so $e_1\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G]}(M)=\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[P]}(e_1M)$ and $(e_1M)/I(P)(e_1M)=M/IM$; it suffices to prove $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[P]}(N)\subseteq I(P)^s$ for $N=e_1M$. Choose a presentation $\mathbb{Z}_p[P]^{a}\xrightarrow{h}\mathbb{Z}_p[P]^{t}\to N\to0$; $\operatorname{Fitt}(N)$ is generated by the $t\times t$ minors of $h$. Reducing modulo $I(P)$ gives $\bar h\in M_{t\times a}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ presenting $N/I(P)N$, of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-rank $s$, so the column space $K$ of $\bar h$ has rank $t-s$. Its saturation $K^{\mathrm{sat}}$ is a direct summand of $\mathbb{Z}_p^t$ of rank $t-s$; choose $\bar Q\in GL_t(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ with $\bar QK^{\mathrm{sat}}=\mathbb{Z}_p^{t-s}\oplus0$, and lift it to $Q\in M_t(\mathbb{Z}_p[P])$. Since $\det Q\equiv\det\bar Q\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ modulo $I(P)\subseteq\mathfrak m$, $Q\in GL_t(\mathbb{Z}_p[P])$. The last $s$ rows of $Qh$ have entries in $I(P)$, so every $t\times t$ minor of $Qh$ lies in $I(P)^s$ (each term of the Leibniz expansion contains one entry from each of these rows); the minors of $h$ are those of $Qh$ times $\det(Q)^{-1}$. Finally, for $M=\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F)^\vee$, $M/IM=(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F)^{G})^\vee$ and the restriction $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F)^G$ has finite kernel (contained in $H^1(G,E(F)[p^\infty])$), so $s\ge s_p\ge r_{\mathrm{alg}}$. $\square$

Thus every theorem placing $\theta_F$ in a Fitting ideal is a theorem on the order of vanishing ("$\theta\in I^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$"), and in fact on $\theta\in I^{s_p}$ — the Selmer corank, not the Mordell–Weil rank.

### 4.4 $p$-power conductors: Mazur–Tate elements *are* the $p$-adic $L$-function

Let $p$ be an odd prime of good ordinary reduction, $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ the unit root of $X^2-a_pX+p$, $\beta=p/\alpha$. Assume

**(Int$_p$)** $[a/p^n]^+\in\mathbb{Z}_p$ for all $n\ge0$ and $a$ (e.g. $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ irreducible and $p\nmid c$; Theorem 4.1(iii)).

Write $G_n=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^n})/\mathbb{Q})=\Delta\times\Gamma_n$ ($n\ge1$), $\Delta\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times$, $\Gamma_n$ cyclic of order $p^{n-1}$, $G_0=1$; $\theta_n:=\theta_{p^n}\in\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]$ (so $\theta_0=L(E,1)/\Omega^+$), $I_n\subset\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]$ the augmentation ideal, $\Lambda^\times:=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\mathbb{Z}_p^\times]]=\varprojlim_n\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]$ with augmentation ideal $I_\infty$, and
$$\vartheta_n:=\alpha^{-n}\theta_n-\alpha^{-n-1}\nu_{n-1,n}(\theta_{n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]\quad(n\ge1),\qquad \vartheta_0:=(1-\alpha^{-1})^2\theta_0 .$$
By Theorem 4.1(ii) the $\vartheta_n$ are compatible under $\pi_{n+1,n}$ (this is the distribution relation of [MTT86, §I]; for $n\ge1$: $\pi(\vartheta_{n+1})=\alpha^{-n-1}(a_p\theta_n-\nu\theta_{n-1})-\alpha^{-n-2}p\,\theta_n=\vartheta_n$ using $a_p-\beta=\alpha$ and $\pi\circ\nu=p$; for $n=0$ one uses $\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times}[a/p]^+=(a_p-2)\theta_0$ and $\#G_1=p-1$). Their limit
$$L_p(E):=(\vartheta_n)_n\in\Lambda^\times$$
is the Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer $p$-adic $L$-function (Amice–Vélu, Višik, [MTT86]); its trivial-$\Delta$-character branch $L_p(E,T):=e_1L_p(E)\in\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ ($\gamma\mapsto1+T$) is the function of `../00-charter.md`, with $L_p(E,0)=(1-\alpha^{-1})^2L(E,1)/\Omega^+$, and $r_p=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)$.

**Lemma 4.5 (augmentation quotients of cyclic $p$-groups).** Let $C$ be cyclic of order $m=p^k$, $R=\mathbb{Z}_p[C]=\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/((1+T)^m-1)$, $J=(T)$ its augmentation ideal. For every $s\ge1$, $J^s/J^{s+1}$ is generated by the class of $T^s$ and is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p/m\mathbb{Z}_p$.

*Proof.* $R/J^{s+1}=\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/(T^{s+1},\omega(T))$ with $\omega(T)=(1+T)^m-1=\sum_{j\ge1}\binom mjT^j$. As a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module, $\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/(T^{s+1})=\bigoplus_{j\le s}\mathbb{Z}_pT^j$ and the relation module is spanned by $\omega T^k\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{s-k}\binom mjT^{j+k}$ ($0\le k\le s-1$). $J^s/J^{s+1}$ is the image of $\mathbb{Z}_pT^s$; an element $\sum_kc_k\omega T^k$ lying in $\mathbb{Z}_pT^s$ has vanishing coefficients at $T^1,\dots,T^{s-1}$, which reads $c_0m=0$, $c_0\binom m2+c_1m=0,\dots$ (triangular with diagonal $m\neq0$), so $c_k=0$ for $k\le s-2$ and the intersection is $c_{s-1}mT^s$, i.e. $m\mathbb{Z}_pT^s$. $\square$

**[NEW] Proposition 4.6 (Mazur–Tate elements of $p$-power level and the $p$-adic analytic rank).** Assume $p$ odd, good ordinary, (Int$_p$). Let $s\ge0$ be an integer. The following are equivalent:
(i) $\theta_n\in I_n^{\,s}$ for all $n\ge0$ (for $s\ge1$ and $n=0$ this says $L(E,1)=0$);
(i$'$) $\theta_n\in I_n^{\,s}$ for all sufficiently large $n$;
(ii) $L_p(E)\in I_\infty^{\,s}$;
(iii) $r_p\ge s$.
Moreover, put $u_0:=$ the coefficient of $T^{r_p}$ in $L_p(E,T)$ (so $u_0=L_p^{(r_p)}(E,0)/r_p!\neq0$) and $v:=\operatorname{ord}_p(u_0)$. Then for all $n\ge v+2$ (and, if $r_p=0$, all $n\ge2\operatorname{ord}_p(1-\alpha^{-1})+2$),
$$\theta_n\in I_n^{\,r_p}\setminus I_n^{\,r_p+1},\qquad\text{i.e. }\operatorname{ord}_{I_n}(\theta_n)=r_p .$$
In words: *the augmentation order of the Mazur–Tate elements of $p$-power level is eventually constant and equals the $p$-adic analytic rank $r_p$.*

*Proof.* Let $e_1=(p-1)^{-1}\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}\delta$. Then $\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]=e_1\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]\times(1-e_1)\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]$ with $e_1\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]=\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_n]$, and $\Lambda^\times=e_1\Lambda^\times\times(1-e_1)\Lambda^\times$ with $e_1\Lambda^\times=\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$. As in the proof of 4.4, $I_n^{\,s}=(1-e_1)\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]\oplus e_1J_n^{\,s}$ with $J_n=(T)\subset\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_n]=\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/(\omega_{n-1})$, $\omega_{n-1}=(1+T)^{p^{n-1}}-1$; and $I_\infty^{\,s}=(1-e_1)\Lambda^\times\oplus e_1(T^s)$. Hence $x\in I_n^{\,s}$ iff $e_1x\in J_n^{\,s}$, and $y\in I_\infty^{\,s}$ iff $e_1y\in(T^s)$. In particular (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii). All maps below ($\pi$, $\nu$, reduction) are $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Delta]$-linear (the decompositions $G_n=\Delta\times\Gamma_n$ are compatible), so we may and do work with $e_1$-components throughout; we write $\theta_n,\vartheta_n$ for $e_1\theta_n,e_1\vartheta_n\in\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_n]$ and $J=J_n$.

*Transfer.* For $n\ge2$ let $H=\ker(\Gamma_n\to\Gamma_{n-1})$, of order $p$, $N_H=\sum_{h\in H}h$. Then $\nu_{n-1,n}(x)=\tilde xN_H$ for any lift $\tilde x$ of $x$ (both sides are $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_n]$-linear in $\tilde x$ and agree on group elements). Since $\pi:\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_n]\to\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_{n-1}]$ maps $J_n^{\,s}$ onto $J_{n-1}^{\,s}$, every $x\in J_{n-1}^{\,s}$ has a lift $\tilde x\in J_n^{\,s}$, so $\nu(J_{n-1}^{\,s})\subseteq J_n^{\,s}$; moreover $N_H\in p+J_n$ (each $h-1\in J_n$), so $\nu(x)\equiv p\,\tilde x\pmod{J_n^{\,s+1}}$ for $x\in J^{\,s}_{n-1}$. For $n=1$: $\Gamma_1=\Gamma_0=1$, $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_1]=\mathbb{Z}_p$, $J_1=0$, and $e_1\nu_{0,1}(\theta_0)=(p-1)\theta_0$.

(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i). Let $s\ge1$ (for $s=0$ there is nothing to prove). $L_p(E)\in I_\infty^{\,s}$ gives $\vartheta_n\in J_n^{\,s}$ for all $n$, and for $n=0$: $\vartheta_0=(1-\alpha^{-1})^2\theta_0=0$; since $\alpha\ne1$ ($1-a_p+p=\#E(\mathbb{F}_p)\ne0$), $\theta_0=0$. Induction: $\theta_0=0\in J_0^{\,s}$; if $\theta_{n-1}\in J_{n-1}^{\,s}$ then $\theta_n=\alpha^n\vartheta_n+\alpha^{-1}\nu(\theta_{n-1})\in J_n^{\,s}$ by the transfer property (for $n=1$, $\nu(\theta_0)=0$).

(i$'$)$\Rightarrow$(ii). If $\theta_n\in J_n^{\,s}$ for all $n\ge n_0$, then $\vartheta_n=\alpha^{-n}\theta_n-\alpha^{-n-1}\nu(\theta_{n-1})\in J_n^{\,s}$ for $n\ge n_0+1$. Thus $e_1L_p(E)=:f\in\bigcap_{n\ge n_0+1}\big((T^s)+(\omega_{n-1})\big)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$. Write $f=T^sg_n+\omega_{n-1}h_n$; modulo $T^s$, $f\equiv\omega_{n-1}h_n$, and every coefficient of $T^j$ ($1\le j<s$) in $\omega_{n-1}$ is $\binom{p^{n-1}}{j}$, of $p$-adic valuation $n-1-\operatorname{ord}_p(j)\ge n-1-\log_p s\to\infty$. Hence $f\bmod T^s\in\bigcap_k p^k\big(\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/T^s\big)=0$, so $f\in(T^s)$, i.e. (ii). (i)$\Rightarrow$(i$'$) is trivial, so (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(i$'$)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii).

*Exact order, case $r:=r_p\ge1$.* By (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i), $\theta_n\in J_n^{\,r}$ for all $n$; let $c_n\in J_n^{\,r}/J_n^{\,r+1}\cong\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}$ (Lemma 4.5, basis $T^r$) be its class, and $c_1=0$ ($J_1=0$). Write $L_p(E,T)=T^ru(T)$, $u(0)=u_0$. The image of $L_p(E,T)$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma_n]$ is $\vartheta_n$, and $T^ru(T)\equiv u_0T^r\pmod{T^{r+1}}$, so by Lemma 4.5 the class of $\vartheta_n$ is $u_0\bmod p^{n-1}$. By the transfer property, for $n\ge2$ the class of $\nu(\theta_{n-1})$ is $p\,c_{n-1}$ (well defined in $\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}$). Therefore
$$c_n=\alpha^nu_0+\beta\,c_{n-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}\qquad(n\ge2),\qquad c_1=0.$$
Claim: $c_n\in p^{v}\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}$ for all $n\ge1$ and $c_n\equiv\alpha^nu_0\pmod{p^{v+1}}$ for all $n\ge2$. Indeed $c_1=0$; if $c_{n-1}\in p^v\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-2}$ then $\beta c_{n-1}\in p^{v+1}\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}$ (as $\operatorname{ord}_p\beta=1$), so $c_n\equiv\alpha^nu_0\pmod{p^{v+1}}$, which lies in $p^v\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}$. For $n-1\ge v+1$ the element $\alpha^nu_0$ is non-zero modulo $p^{v+1}$, hence $c_n\ne0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p/p^{n-1}$, i.e. $\theta_n\notin J_n^{\,r+1}$.

*Exact order, case $r_p=0$.* Then $\operatorname{ord}_{I_n}(\theta_n)=0$ iff $S_n:=\operatorname{aug}(\theta_n)=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^n)^\times}[a/p^n]^+\ne0$. Applying $\operatorname{aug}$ to $\theta_n=\alpha^n\vartheta_n+\alpha^{-1}\nu(\theta_{n-1})$ gives $S_n=\alpha^nu_0+\beta S_{n-1}$ for $n\ge2$ (with $u_0=L_p(E,0)=(1-\alpha^{-1})^2\theta_0$) and $S_1=(a_p-2)\theta_0$. Hence $S_n=u_0\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\alpha^{n-j}\beta^{j}+\beta^{n-1}S_1$; the first term has valuation exactly $v=\operatorname{ord}_p\theta_0+2\operatorname{ord}_p(1-\alpha^{-1})$ (as $\alpha^{n}\sum_j(\beta/\alpha)^j$ is a unit), and the second has valuation $\ge n-1+\operatorname{ord}_p\theta_0>v$ once $n\ge2\operatorname{ord}_p(1-\alpha^{-1})+2$. So $S_n\neq0$ for such $n$. $\square$

**Remarks.** (1) The containment (iii)$\Rightarrow$(i) with $s=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$, via Kato, is recorded in [Ota18, Rem. 1.3(2)] ("Kato's result on the $p$-adic BSD conjecture proves $\theta_{p^n}\in\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes I^{r_E}$"); Mazur–Tate themselves relate their conjecture at $p$-power levels to [MTT86]. We could not locate the equivalence (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii) or the exact-order statement in the literature; both are elementary and surely known to experts, whence the tag [NEW] with this caveat. (2) For $n$ small the order can exceed $r_p$ (trivial zeros, e.g. $\theta_p\in I$ when $a_p=2$, Remark 4.3), never fall below it. (3) In the supersingular case ($a_p=0$) the same argument with Pollack's $\pm$-decomposition [Po03, Prop. 6.18] (as used in [KK21, Prop. 1.11]) expresses $\theta_n$ modulo $\omega_n$ through $\tilde\omega_n^{\mp}L_p^{\mp}$; the analogue of Proposition 4.6 then involves $\min(\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p^+,\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p^-)$ and we do not pursue it.

**[THEOREM] 4.7 (Mazur–Tate order of vanishing at $p$-power levels).** Let $p$ be odd, good ordinary, with (Int$_p$). Then $\theta_{p^n}\in\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes I^{\,s_p}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes I^{\,r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$ for all $n\ge0$, and the exact augmentation order of $\theta_{p^n}$ for $n\gg0$ is $r_p$. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 2.5 (main conjecture), $r_p=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)$; and $r_p=s_p$ holds if and only if the canonical $p$-adic height pairing on $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ (equivalently, when $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite, on $E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$) is non-degenerate [CONDITIONAL: Schneider [Sch85], Perrin-Riou [PR92], [PR93, Prop. 3.4.6]; cf. [BKS24, Rem. 7.5]].

*Proof.* Proposition 4.6 with $s=s_p$ and Theorem 2.6 ($r_p\ge s_p$). The last assertions are the cited theorems. $\square$

So at $p$-power conductors *the Mazur–Tate order of vanishing is the $p$-adic analytic rank*, and the question of its relation with $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is literally Conjecture $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ of `A-iwasawa-padic.md`.

### 4.5 Tame and general conductors: what is proved, and by which method

**[THEOREM] 4.8 (Ota [Ota18, Thm. 1.2 (=6.1), Thm. 5.17, Thm. 6.4, Thm. 7.1]; Amer. J. Math. 140 (2018)).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be non-CM, $R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}$ a ring in which every prime $p$ with (i) $p\mid6N\cdot\#E(\mathbb{F}_p)\cdot\operatorname{Tam}(E)$, or (ii) $\rho_{E,p}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_pE)$ not surjective, or (iii) $p<r_{\mathrm{alg}}$, is invertible. Let $S$ be a square-free product of good primes $\ell$ such that $E(\mathbb{F}_\ell)[p]$ is cyclic for every $p\notin R^\times$ (a set of primes of density $>0.99$; all supersingular $\ell$ qualify). Then $\theta_S\in R[G_S]$ and $\theta_S\in I_S^{\,r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$. More precisely, for $p\nmid6N\#E(\mathbb{F}_p)\operatorname{Tam}(E)$ with $\rho_{E,p}$ surjective, $\theta_S\in\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes I_S^{\min\{s_p,\,p\}}$. If moreover $\ell\equiv1\pmod p$ for all $\ell\mid S$ and the image $\tilde\theta^{(p)}_S$ of $\theta_S$ in $\mathbb{F}_p\otimes I^{r}_S/I^{r+1}_S$ is non-zero, then $\operatorname{Sha}[p]=0$ and $p\nmid J_S$. Trivial zeros: $\theta_S\in I_S^{\operatorname{sp}(S)+b_2(S)}$ with $b_2(S)=\#\{\ell\mid S:a_\ell=2\}$.
*Method:* Darmon–Kolyvagin derivatives of Kato's Euler system (adapting Darmon's argument for Heegner points [Da92]), Kurihara's and Otsuki's comparison of Kato's elements with modular symbols, giving $\theta_S\in I^{\min\{s_p-1,p\}}$ from the *strict* Selmer group; the last step to $s_p$ uses the $p$-parity theorem (Dokchitser–Dokchitser, Kim, Nekovář) and the functional equation of $\theta_S$ [Ota18, §1.3, Thm. 5.17].

**[THEOREM] 4.9 (Kim–Kurihara [KK21, Thm. 1.14, Cor. 1.16]; IMRN 2021).** Let $E$ have good reduction at odd $p$, $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ surjective if $E$ is non-CM, and either (ord) $a_p\not\equiv0,1\pmod p$ or (ss) $a_p=0$. Then for every $n$, $(\theta_{\mathbb{Q}_n},\nu(\theta_{\mathbb{Q}_{n-1}}))\subseteq\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G_n]}(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)^\vee)$ (weak main conjecture over $\mathbb{Q}_n\subset\mathbb{Q}_\infty$), hence the weak vanishing conjecture (a) over $\mathbb{Q}_n$. In case (ord), if $p\nmid\operatorname{Tam}(E)$ and the main conjecture holds, equality (strong main conjecture (f)). *Method:* $\pm$-Iwasawa theory (Kobayashi, Pollack) and explicit comparison of finite-layer, $\pm$- and fine Selmer groups; Kato's divisibility.

**[THEOREM] 4.10 (Kataoka [Kat21, Thm. 1.5, 1.8, Cor. 1.9]; Math. Z. 298 (2021)).** For $K/\mathbb{Q}$ abelian with $p$ unramified, $K_\infty=K(\mu_{p^\infty})$, $\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/\mathbb{Q})]]$, and $E$ good at $p$ with $p\nmid a_p$ or $a_p=0$: equivariant Coleman maps $\mathrm{Col}^\bullet$ send Kato's element to equivariant $p$-adic $L$-functions; the equivariant main conjecture $W^\bullet\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathcal{R}}(\operatorname{Sel}^\bullet_S(E/K_\infty)^\vee)=(\mathcal{L}^\bullet_S)$ has the divisibility "$\supseteq$" under (a)–(f) of Thm. 1.5 (in particular $\rho_{E,p}$ surjective, $p\ge5$, good reduction at ramified primes, and either $p\nmid[K:\mathbb{Q}]$ or $\mu=0$ for the fine Selmer group), and this divisibility implies the weak main conjecture (b) for $M=mp^{n+1}$, all $n\ge-1$, when $K=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)$ [Thm. 1.8, under condition $(\star)$ excluding certain $\ell\equiv1\pmod p$ with $a_\ell\equiv\pm2$].

**[THEOREM] 4.11 (Emerton–Pollack–Weston [EPW24, Thm. A, Cor. B]; arXiv:2210.02013v2).** Let $f\in S_k(\Gamma_1(N),\psi)$ be a newform, $0\le j\le k-2$, and assume (Irred) $\rho_f|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}}$ irreducible (which forces $f$ non-ordinary at $p$), (No pole at $s=j+1$), and (Euler) $\rho_f(G_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})})\supseteq SL_2(\mathcal{O})$. No crystalline or finite-slope hypothesis is needed. *Method:* local classes $c_{n,j}$ constructed through the $p$-adic local Langlands correspondence (Colmez), "algebraic $\theta$-elements", and Kato's Euler system with the explicit reciprocity law.

**[THEOREM] 4.12 (Burns–Kurihara–Sano [BKS25, Thm. 1.1, Cor. 1.2]; IMRN 2025).** Let $F$ be real abelian of square-free conductor $m$ coprime to $N$, with $[F:\mathbb{Q}]$ coprime to $6mN\operatorname{Tam}(E)$ and to $\#E^{\mathrm{ns}}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ for all $p\mid mN[F:\mathbb{Q}]$, and $G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(E[p])$ surjective for all $p\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]$. If BSD holds for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and (when $r>0$) Conjecture 2.7 holds for every $p\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]$, then the full Mazur–Tate conjecture (c)+(d) holds for $E$ and $F/\mathbb{Q}$. Unconditionally: if $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$, $N$ square-free and $E$ supersingular at every $p\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]$, then (c) holds and (d) holds up to an automorphism of $G$; and if Mazur–Tate fails for $(E,F)$ then BSD fails for $E/\mathbb{Q}$. Mechanism: "determinantal zeta elements" from equivariant Stark systems [BSS19b]; Conjecture 2.7 $\Rightarrow$ [BKS25, Conj. 3.4] $\Rightarrow$ the $p$-component of (d).

**[THEOREM]** (preprint, Nov. 2025, unrefereed) **4.13 (Bullach–Honnor [BH25, Thm. 1.4, Thm. 1.7, Cor. 1.9]).** Let $K$ be any abelian number field of conductor $m$ and $p>3$ with: (i) $\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\supseteq SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$; (ii) $\mu_p\not\subset K$, or $E$ has potentially good reduction at $p$ with $\tilde E(\mathbb{F}_p)[p]=0$; (iii) if $E$ is additive at $p$ then $p$ is unramified in $K$. Then
(a) $\theta^{\mathrm{MT}}_K\in I_{\mathbb{Z}_p,G}^{\ s_p+\operatorname{sp}(m)+2c^{(p)}(K)}$, with $s_p=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})^\vee$ (as in the proof, (58) of loc. cit.) and an explicit $c^{(p)}(K)\ge0$ counting trivial zeros from primes $\ell\mid m$, $\ell^2\nmid m$, with $a_\ell=1$, and from $\ell\mid N$ with $a_\ell=0$, $\ell^2\mid m$;
(b) $\theta^{\mathrm{MT},\#}_K\in\operatorname{Fitt}^0_{\mathbb{Z}_p[G]}(\operatorname{Sel}_p(E/K)^\vee)$ (and without $\#$ when $\gcd(\gcd(m,N),N/\gcd(m,N))=1$, e.g. $E$ semistable);
(c) a refined form of the split-multiplicative leading term conjecture (e) (Thm. 1.7).
In particular, for $E$ semistable non-CM and $p\ge11$, the $p$-parts of Conjectures 1, 3, 4 (order of vanishing) and 6 of [MT87] hold [Cor. 1.9]. *Method:* the "rank-zero component" of $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$: Kato's reciprocity law rewritten as $P_m(z^{\mathrm{Kato}}_m,Q_m)=\theta^{\mathrm{MT}}_m$ with Otsuki's explicit local class $Q_m$; "one inclusion" in $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ from [BB25]; perfect Nekovář–Selmer complexes approximating finite-support cohomology; Bockstein maps. The factor $2$ in (a) is explained by the parity $\operatorname{ord}_I(\theta)\equiv s_p\pmod2$ forced by the functional equation and the $p$-parity theorem [BH25, Rem. 1.5(b)].

**[THEOREM] 4.14 (Kurihara numbers determine the Selmer group; C.-H. Kim [Kim26, Thm. 1.4, Cor. 1.5–1.6, Thm. 1.8]; Amer. J. Math. 148 (2026) 79–129).** Let $p\ge5$, $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ surjective, $p\nmid$ Manin constant. For $n$ a square-free product of *Kolyvagin primes* $\ell\in\mathcal{P}_1=\{\ell\nmid Np:\ \ell\equiv1,\ a_\ell\equiv\ell+1\pmod p\}$, put $I_n:=\sum_{\ell\mid n}(\ell-1,a_\ell-\ell-1)\mathbb{Z}_p$ and define the *Kurihara number*
$$\tilde\delta_n:=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times}\Big[\frac an\Big]^+\prod_{\ell\mid n}\log_{\eta_\ell}(a)\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}_p/I_n\qquad(\tilde\delta_1=L(E,1)/\Omega^+),$$
and $\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta}):=\min\{\nu(n):\tilde\delta_n\neq0\}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}\cup\{\infty\}$. Then:
(1) $\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})<\infty$ if the main conjecture 2.3 localised at the augmentation ideal $(T)$ holds — in particular if $E$ is good ordinary at $p$ (Kato, Skinner–Urban, Wan), or $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$, or $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ and $E$ is semistable at $p$;
(2) if $\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})<\infty$ then $\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})$, and the higher Fitting ideals of $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})^\vee$ — hence its full isomorphism type — are given by the $p$-adic valuations $\partial^{(i)}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})$ of the $\tilde\delta_n$ with $\nu(n)=i$ [Thm. 1.8]; if $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite, $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})$ and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is determined explicitly.
The Kurihara numbers are Kolyvagin-derivative images of the Mazur–Tate elements: $\tilde\delta_n$ is the image of $\theta_n$ under $\sigma_a\mapsto\prod_{\ell\mid n}\log_{\eta_\ell}(a)\in\mathbb{Z}_p/I_n$, the "$\nu(n)$-th mixed discrete derivative" of $\theta_n$ in the $\ell$-directions (cf. [Ku14b], [Kim26, §1.4]); by Fourier inversion on $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ they are explicit $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$-linear combinations of the values $\tau(\chi)L(E,\bar\chi,1)/\Omega^+$, $\chi$ even of conductor dividing $n$. Kim realises them as the image of Kato's Kolyvagin system under a refined dual exponential. Related: Burungale–Castella–Grossi–Skinner [BCGS23] (non-vanishing of Kato's Kolyvagin system and Zhang's Tamagawa-refinement, good ordinary $p$, without surjectivity of $\bar\rho$; to appear in Camb. J. Math.), Castella–Sano [CS26] (all reduction types), Kurihara [Ku14a, Ku14b], Kim–Kim–Sun [KKS20], Sakamoto [Sa22].

**Anticyclotomic and function-field analogues (for orientation only).** Darmon [Da92] (refined conjecture for Heegner points; order of vanishing via Kolyvagin's derivatives), Bertolini–Darmon [BD94] (derived heights and generalised Mazur–Tate regulators), [BD96] (anticyclotomic $\theta\in I^{r}$ via Heegner points on Shimura curves), C.-H. ### 4.6 Answers to the two questions of the brief

**(a) Is $\theta\in I^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$ a theorem, and by what method?** Yes, in the following generality:
– $p$-power conductors, $p$ odd good ordinary with (Int$_p$): $\theta_{p^n}\in\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes I^{s_p}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_p\otimes I^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$ for all $n$ [Theorem 4.7; Kato's divisibility $+$ Proposition 4.6]; supersingular $a_p=0$ or non-anomalous ordinary: [KK21] via Fitting ideals and Theorem 4.4; non-ordinary of any weight: [EPW24].
– Tame conductors: [Ota18] (square-free $S$ of good primes with cyclic $E(\mathbb{F}_\ell)[p]$; $\theta_S\in I^{\min\{s_p,p\}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p$); [Kat21] ($M=mp^{n+1}$ under equivariant divisibility); [BKS25] ($r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$, tame $F$).
– All abelian $K$ ($p>3$, big image, mild local conditions): [BH25] gives $\theta_K\in I^{s_p+\operatorname{sp}(m)+2c^{(p)}(K)}$ and the Fitting-ideal containment, *preprint*.
The uniform method is *Kato's Euler system* — either through the Iwasawa main conjecture divisibility and Fitting ideals (Kurihara's machinery: [Ku02], [Ku03], [KK21], [Kat21], [EPW24]), or through Kolyvagin/Darmon derivatives and (equivariant) Kolyvagin–Stark systems ([Ota18], [BKS25], [BH25], [Kim26]). In every case the exponent obtained is the *Selmer corank* $s_p$ (plus trivial zeros), never $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ itself: the method cannot distinguish rational points from $p^\infty$-divisible elements of $\operatorname{Sha}$.

**(b) Does the augmentation order of $\theta$ relate to $r_{\mathrm{an}}$?** Not directly, and provably not in the following sense. $\theta_m$ depends only on the finitely many values $L(E,\chi,1)$, $\chi$ of conductor dividing $m$; $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is the order of a zero of the *untwisted* function in the variable $s$. Three exact statements delimit the relation:
1. At $p$-power conductors, $\operatorname{ord}_{I}(\theta_{p^n})=r_p$ for $n\gg0$ (Proposition 4.6); so the relation to $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is Conjecture $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$, $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$. What is known (see `A-iwasawa-padic.md`, Thm. 4.2 there): $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0\iff r_p=0$ (interpolation); $r_p\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod2$; for $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$, $r_p=1$ is equivalent (by the $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula) to the non-vanishing of the $p$-adic height of a generator — known for CM curves and for supersingular $p$, open for ordinary non-CM $p$; nothing for $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$.
2. At Kolyvagin-prime conductors, $\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})=s_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}+\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ exactly (Theorem 4.14(2)). Hence, under the hypotheses of 4.14: "$r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite" is equivalent to "$\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite", and the single inequality $\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})\le r_{\mathrm{an}}$ would give $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_p\le r_{\mathrm{an}}$, i.e. one half of BSD-rank together with a bound on the corank of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$. No known method produces such an upper bound ([GAP G4]); all known results bound $\operatorname{ord}(\tilde{\boldsymbol\delta})$ *from below* by algebraic data.
3. The parity of $\operatorname{ord}_I(\theta)$ *is* controlled analytically: $\operatorname{ord}_{I}(\theta_K)\equiv s_p\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod2$ (functional equation of $\theta$ $+$ $p$-parity theorem) [BH25, Rem. 1.5(b)], [Ota18, §1.3]. This is the only place where the sign of the functional equation, i.e. $r_{\mathrm{an}}\bmod2$, enters.
The complex derivatives $L^{(r)}(E,1)$ enter the Mazur–Tate world only through the *leading term* conjecture (d), and there only via the BSD formula itself: [BKS25, Thm. 1.1] shows that (d) is a consequence of BSD plus the Generalized Perrin-Riou conjecture 2.7, whose content is precisely the identification of a $p$-adic (Darmon) derivative of a zeta element with a complex derivative. Higher-derivative *interpolation* is the subject of the equivariant refinements of §3 (elements interpolating $L^{(r_\psi)}(E,\psi,1)/\text{regulators}$, whose augmentation order is predicted by the Galois module structure of $E(F)_p$, [BMC17, Cor. 2.11]), not of Mazur–Tate's $\theta$.

## 5. The gaps, and the attempts made on each

Each gap is stated so that it can be handed to a mathematician as a problem. Standing hypotheses: $E/\mathbb{Q}$, $p$ odd, $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ surjective (or $\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\supseteq SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$), $E$ good (ordinary unless said otherwise) at $p$, $E(\mathbb{Q})[p]=0$.

**[GAP G1] (Generalized Perrin-Riou / $p$-adic Beilinson formula in rank $r\ge2$).** Let $r=r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ and assume $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite and $R_p\ne0$. Prove
$$(1-\alpha^{-1})^{-1}(1-\beta^{-1})\cdot L^{(r)}_{S,p}\ =\ \frac{L^{(r)}_S(E,1)}{\Omega^+R_\infty}\cdot R_p ,$$
where $L^{(r)}_{S,p}$ and $L^{(r)}_S(E,1)$ are the $r$-th Taylor coefficients (at the trivial character, resp. at $s=1$) of the $S$-truncated $p$-adic and complex $L$-functions ($S\ni p$ any finite set of primes containing the bad ones; the $S$-truncation multiplies both sides by the same Euler factors), for a single pair $(E,p)$ with $r\ge2$. By [BKS24, Cor. 6.7] this is equivalent to Conjecture 2.7 for $E$ and $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$.

*Attempt.* (1) Reduction: by Theorem 2.8(iii)–(iv), under the main conjecture 2.5 and $R_p\neq0$, G1 is equivalent to "$\mathrm{BSD}_p(E)$ holds *and* the unit $u$ of Theorem 2.8(iii) equals $1$", i.e. to the exact MTT $p$-adic BSD formula divided by the exact complex BSD formula. So G1 cannot be attacked through algebraic (Iwasawa-theoretic) means alone: every algebraic statement about $\kappa_\infty$ is invariant under $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and the complex number $L^{(r)}(E,1)/(\Omega^+R_\infty)$ never appears in the Euler-system/main-conjecture formalism, whose only analytic input is Theorem 2.1 (critical values). (2) Rank-one template: all proofs of 2.9 express *both* sides through a Heegner point: $L'(E,1)$ by Gross–Zagier, $L'_p(E,0)$ by $p$-adic Gross–Zagier, $z_{\mathbb{Q}}$ by a Λ-adic comparison (Bertolini–Darmon–Venerucci) or by critical-slope $p$-adic GZ (BPS). For $r\ge2$ there is no known algebraic-cycle or automorphic construction whose height is $L^{(r)}(E,1)$ (see `../03-obstructions-rank-ge-2.md`, `C-higher-rank-euler-systems-diagonal-cycles.md`; diagonal-cycle methods control ranks $\le1$ of components). (3) Numerical relevance: for individual rank-$2$ curves one can verify $r_p=2$ by computing $L_p''(E,0)\ne0$, which by Theorem 2.6 proves $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite for that $(E,p)$ [Co04, Rem. 0.16(ii)], [SW13]; G1 for such $(E,p)$ is then a well-posed identity between two computable $p$-adic numbers and one real number, checkable to any precision but not provable by present methods. *Outcome:* precise reformulation only; no reduction of difficulty.

**[GAP G2] (non-degeneracy of the cyclotomic $p$-adic height).** For $E$ good ordinary at $p$ with $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite, prove $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$; equivalently (Theorem 4.7) that the canonical $p$-adic height pairing on $E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ is non-degenerate, equivalently $R^{\mathrm{Boc}}_\omega\neq0$ [BKS24, Thm. 5.6]. Known: $r_p\ge r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ (Theorem 2.6); $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}=0$ when $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$; for $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ the equality $r_p=1$ is the non-vanishing of the $p$-adic height of a generator (known for CM curves and supersingular $p$, open for ordinary non-CM $p$; `A-iwasawa-padic.md`, Thm. 4.2); verifiable numerically for each $(E,p)$. *Attempt:* none beyond the observation that G2 is a $p$-adic transcendence statement of Leopoldt type ([Co04, Rem. 0.13(ii)]) and that Theorem 2.8(iv) needs it. Not attempted further.

**[GAP G3] (Mazur–Tate leading term in rank $\ge2$).** Prove Conjecture 4.2(d) for one $(E,F)$ with $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge2$, $F$ real abelian, tame. *Reduction achieved:* by [BKS25, Thm. 1.1], under hypotheses (a)–(c) there, (d) follows from BSD for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and Conjecture 2.7 at the primes $p\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]$; by [BH25, Rem. 1.8(b)] the split-multiplicative refinement should combine with [BKS25]. So G3 $\Leftarrow$ BSD $+$ G1. Conversely, (d) for a single $F$ with $p\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]$ determines $\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\bmod$ the exponent of $I^r/I^{r+1}$ only if $R_F\ne0$ in $I^r/I^{r+1}$ — which can fail (Ota's Rem. 2.5(1)); Bertolini–Darmon's derived-height lifts [BD94] are the tool to extract more. *Outcome:* G3 is not independent of G1.

**[GAP G4] (analytic upper bound for the discrete order of vanishing).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem 4.14 with $E$ good ordinary at $p$: prove that some Kurihara number $\tilde\delta_n$ with $\nu(n)=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is non-zero. By Theorem 4.14(2) this gives $s_p\le r_{\mathrm{an}}$, hence $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le r_{\mathrm{an}}$ and $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\le r_{\mathrm{an}}-r_{\mathrm{alg}}$; if in addition $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ then $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite. *Attempt.* Fourier inversion on $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ and Theorem 4.1(i) give, as an identity in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$,
$$\tilde\delta_n\equiv\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{\chi}w(\chi)\,\tau(\chi)\,\frac{L^{(n)}(E,\bar\chi,1)}{\Omega^+},\qquad w(\chi)=\prod_{\ell\mid n}\ \sum_{j=0}^{\ell-2}j\,\bar\chi_\ell(\eta_\ell)^j=\prod_{\ell\mid n}\begin{cases}\frac{\ell-1}{\bar\chi_\ell(\eta_\ell)-1}&\chi_\ell\ne1\\[2pt] \frac{(\ell-1)(\ell-2)}2&\chi_\ell=1,\end{cases}$$
the sum over even characters $\chi=\prod_\ell\chi_\ell$ of $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ and $L^{(n)}$ the $n$-imprimitive twist. So $\tilde\delta_n$ is a finite difference of the function $\chi\mapsto L(E,\chi,1)$ in the "$\ell$-directions" (the weights $w(\chi)$ are the discrete derivatives of $\chi_\ell$ at the trivial character), while $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ concerns the $s$-derivative of $L(E,\cdot,s)$ at the trivial character. No identity relates the two; the only known bridges are (i) for $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$: $\tilde\delta_1=L(E,1)/\Omega^+\neq0$ trivially; (ii) for $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$: non-vanishing of $\tilde\delta_\ell$ for some $\ell$ follows from $s_p=1$ (Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin) via 4.14, i.e. *algebraically*, not analytically. A direct analytic proof even for $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ would be new and would reprove $s_p\le1$ without Heegner points. *Outcome:* the gap is sharpened to a concrete non-vanishing statement about finite sums of twisted $L$-values; no method to prove it.

**[GAP G5] (main conjecture $\Rightarrow$ integral TNC: the $\mu$ and unit issues in the descent).** Prove Conjecture 2.3 *in $\Lambda$* (not $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$) for good supersingular $p$ with $a_p\neq0$, for additive $p$, and for Eisenstein $p$ ($\bar\rho$ reducible) — cf. `A-iwasawa-padic.md` for the state of the art (Kobayashi/Sprung/Wan supersingular; Castella–Grossi–Lee–Skinner and Burungale–Castella–Skinner for Eisenstein and base change [BCS25]). Only the good-ordinary big-image case is settled (Theorem 2.5). *Not attempted here* (belongs to A).

**[GAP G6] (equivariant TNC in positive rank with $p\mid\#G$ beyond rank one).** Prove $\mathrm{eTNC}_p(h^1(E_F)(1),\mathbb{Z}[G])$ for one $(E,F/\mathbb{Q},p)$ with $p\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]$ and $\operatorname{rank}E(F)\ge2$; by [BMC17, Thm. 2.9] for cyclic $F$ this is an explicit congruence between leading terms $L^{(r_\psi)}(E,\psi,1)$ normalised by equivariant regulators. *Attempt:* since $\mathrm{eTNC}_p$ implies $\mathrm{BSD}_p$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and over $F$, G6 with $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})\ge2$ contains G1; with $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})\le1$ but $\operatorname{rank}E(F)\ge2$ it contains $\mathrm{BSD}_p$ over $F$ in rank $\ge2$, unknown. *Outcome:* reduces to G1-type statements over $F$; Bullach–Burns' "one inclusion" [BB25] is the only unconditional progress.

**What was proved here.** Proposition 4.6 [NEW] (equivalence of the $p$-power Mazur–Tate order of vanishing with $r_p$, and eventual exactness), Theorem 4.4 (proof on the page of Mazur–Tate's Prop. 3), Theorem 4.7 (the resulting unconditional order-of-vanishing theorem and its conditional sharpening). No progress on G1–G6 beyond the reductions recorded.

Relevant to `B`, and to the "Kurihara/Kolyvagin non-vanishing" side of 4.14.
4. **Burns–Kurihara–Sano, IMRN 2025 (arXiv:2103.11535)** and **J. Math. Soc. Japan 76 (2024) (arXiv:1910.07404).** The Generalized Perrin-Riou conjecture, the Generalized Rubin formula, "main conjecture $\Rightarrow$ MTT up to units in all reduction types", and Mazur–Tate in analytic rank one (Theorems 2.8, 4.12).
5. **Macias Castillo–Sano, arXiv:2603.23978 (Mar. 2026).** Nekovář–Selmer complexes vs Poitou–Tate complexes; determinant of a Selmer complex $\cong$ module of Stark systems; a "Heegner point Stark system"; Bertolini–Darmon derived heights $=$ Nekovář's higher heights. Technical infrastructure for the higher-rank leading-term problems (G1, G3). Also Sano, arXiv:2308.08875 (derived Bockstein regulators; anticyclotomic $p$-adic BSD from Heegner-point main conjectures up to units).
6. **Llerena-Córdova, arXiv:2412.17703 (Dec. 2024).** Numerical failure of [MT87, Conj. 4, 6] unless $\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is inverted; corrected statements (Conj. 3.4–3.6 there). Any use of Mazur–Tate in the synthesis must adopt the corrected ring.
7. **Burungale–Castella–Skinner, IMRN 2025 ("Base change and Iwasawa main conjectures for $GL_2$").** New cases of the main conjecture via base change — relevant to G5 and to `A`.
8. **Iwasawa invariants of Mazur–Tate elements:** Lei–Pollack–Pratap (arXiv:2412.16629, 2512.00525), Pratap–Ray (Math. Nachr. 299 (2026) 2652–2679, DOI 10.1002/mana.70211, Kida-type formula), Gajek-Leonard–Lei (arXiv:2508.11007), Doyon–Lei (Res. Number Theory 10 (2024), DOI 10.1007/s40993-024-00540-7). These concern $\lambda,\mu$ of $\theta_{p^n}$ (i.e. of the $p$-adic $L$-function), consistent with Proposition 4.6's "eventual stabilisation", not the BSD leading term.
9. **Anticyclotomic Mazur–Tate:** Kim (Springer Proc. 2026, arXiv:2403.01498), Shii (arXiv:2503.09034): weak main conjectures at supersingular/inert primes.
10. **Longo–Vigni, arXiv:2211.04907:** $p$-TNC for modular motives of weight $\ge4$ in analytic rank $\le1$, conditional on regulator/Abel–Jacobi conjectures — the only higher-weight analogue of the rank-one route.
