## 0. Scope, notation, and the shape of the conclusion

This document collects what the *analytic* theory of $L(E,s)$ can and cannot deliver for BSD, and pushes the "higher derivative" question to precisely stated gaps.

**Notation** (in addition to the charter). $E/\mathbb{Q}$ has conductor $N$ and newform $f=f_E=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nq^n\in S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ with $a_1=1$. The completed $L$-function is
$$\Lambda(E,s):=N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\,L(E,s).$$
$w=w(E)\in\{\pm1\}$ is the root number. For $p\nmid N$ write $1-a_pX+pX^2=(1-\alpha_pX)(1-\beta_pX)$, $|\alpha_p|=|\beta_p|=\sqrt p$; for $p\mid N$ put $\alpha_p=a_p\in\{0,\pm1\}$, $\beta_p=0$. $r_{\mathrm{an}}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$, $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})$. GRH$(E)$ means: every zero of $\Lambda(E,s)$ has real part $1$.

$K$ denotes an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant $D_K<0$, with quadratic character $\chi_K$; $E^K$ is the quadratic twist of $E$ by $\chi_K$, so that $L(E/K,s)=L(E,s)L(E^K,s)$. The **Heegner hypothesis** is

$$\text{(Heeg)}\qquad \text{every prime } \ell\mid N \text{ splits in } K \quad(\Rightarrow (D_K,N)=1).$$

$u_K=|\mathcal O_K^\times|/2$, $H_K$ the Hilbert class field, $\varphi:X_0(N)\to E$ a fixed modular parametrisation with $\varphi(\infty)=O$, $y_K=\operatorname{Tr}_{H_K/K}\varphi(x_1)\in E(K)$ the Heegner point of conductor $1$ (for $x_1=[\mathbb{C}/\mathcal O_K\to\mathbb{C}/\mathfrak n^{-1}]$, $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak n\simeq\mathbb{Z}/N$).

**Summary of the conclusions of this document.**

1. Exact arithmetic control of Taylor coefficients of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$ stops at order one: $L(E,1)/\Omega_E\in\mathbb{Q}$ (Manin, §1.3), $L'(E,1)$ is a Heegner height (Gross–Zagier, §1.4). For $L''(E,1)$ nothing is known—not even its sign (Lemma F.1.8, [NEW]; [GAP F.2]).
2. Every known "derivative formula" (Gross–Zagier and all its generalisations, the $p$-adic and rigid-analytic variants, the triple-product formula) is a formula for a *first* derivative of an $L$-function with a sign-forced simple zero, or a square of such an object. Function fields have all derivatives (Yun–Zhang) via moduli of shtukas with $r$ legs, for which no number-field analogue exists (§4). [GAP F.1].
3. Analytic rank is *certifiable* exactly when it is $\le 3$; no curve is known with $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge4$, because no method certifies $L''(E,1)=0$ (Proposition F.3.4). [GAP F.3].
4. The analytic inputs that *are* available for a rank-$2$ curve are: (i) infinitely many Heegner fields $K$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^K)=1$ (hence $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^K)=1$, $\operatorname{Sha}(E^K)$ finite, $\operatorname{rank}E(K)=r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)+1$; Proposition F.5.1), (ii) Rohrlich/Chinta nonvanishing of $L(E,\chi,1)$ for Dirichlet twists feeding Kato's Euler system, (iii) Cornut–Vatsal nonvanishing of $L'(E/K,\chi,1)$ for anticyclotomic $\chi$ (§5).

Gaps isolated: [GAP F.1]–[GAP F.5] (§6). New claims: Lemma F.1.8 [NEW] only; everything else is cited or assembled from cited results with proofs given on the page.

## 1. Properties of $L(E,s)$ and the arithmetic of $L(E,1)$, $L'(E,1)$

### 1.1 Modularity, continuation, functional equation

**Theorem F.1.1** [THEOREM]. For every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ there is a unique newform $f_E\in S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ with $L(E,s)=L(f_E,s)$. Consequently $\Lambda(E,s)$ is entire and
$$\Lambda(E,s)=w(E)\,\Lambda(E,2-s),\qquad w(E)\in\{\pm1\}.$$
*Sources.* Wiles, Ann. of Math. (2) 141 (1995) 443–551; Taylor–Wiles, Ann. of Math. (2) 141 (1995) 553–572 (semistable case); Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 14 (2001) 843–939 (all $E/\mathbb{Q}$). The functional equation for newforms is Hecke's, via the Fricke involution.

Standard consequences [THEOREM]: the Euler product converges absolutely for $\Re s>3/2$ (Hasse bound), so $L(E,s)\ne0$ there; $L(E,s)\ne0$ on $\Re s=3/2$ (Jacquet–Shalika, Invent. Math. 38 (1976) 1–16, applied to the unitary normalisation $L(s-\tfrac12,f)$); by the functional equation the only zeros with $\Re s<\tfrac12$ are the trivial zeros $s=0,-1,-2,\dots$ (poles of $\Gamma(s)$). All zeros of $\Lambda(E,s)$ lie in $\tfrac12\le\Re s\le\tfrac32$, symmetric about $s=1$ and about the real axis. Since $f$ has real Fourier coefficients, $\Lambda(E,s)$ is real on $\mathbb{R}$. The functional equation forces
$$ (-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}}=w(E)\qquad\text{and}\qquad L^{(k)}(E,1)=0\ \text{ whenever } (-1)^k\neq w(E), $$
because $\Lambda(E,1+t)=w\,\Lambda(E,1-t)$ has Taylor coefficients of one parity only, and $\Lambda^{(k)}(E,1)$ is a nonzero multiple of $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ plus lower derivatives.

### 1.2 Root number: local factorisation and parity

**Theorem F.1.2** [THEOREM]. $w(E)=\prod_{v}w_v(E)$ over all places of $\mathbb{Q}$, with $w_\infty(E)=-1$, $w_p(E)=1$ if $p\nmid N$, $w_p(E)=-a_p$ if $E$ has multiplicative reduction at $p$ (i.e. $-1$ split, $+1$ non-split). For additive reduction at $p\ge5$ (Rohrlich, Compositio Math. 87 (1993) 119–151, §2): $w_p(E)=\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)$ if $E$ is potentially multiplicative; if $E$ is potentially good with $e=12/\gcd(12,\operatorname{ord}_p\Delta_{\min})$, then $w_p(E)=\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)$ for $e\in\{2,6\}$, $\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)$ for $e=3$, $\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)$ for $e=4$. For $p\in\{2,3\}$ the complete tables are due to Halberstadt, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 326 (1998) 1047–1052.

For a quadratic twist by a fundamental discriminant $D$ with $(D,N)=1$ one has $w(E^D)=w(E)\chi_D(-N)$ (the twisted completed $L$-function $\Lambda(s,f,\chi_D)$ is $w(E)\chi_D(-N)$-invariant under $s\mapsto2-s$; this is the classical computation recorded e.g. in the statement of the Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein theorem [BFH90b], see §2.2). In particular, under (Heeg), $\chi_K(-N)=\chi_K(-1)\chi_K(N)=-1$, so
$$ w(E^K)=-w(E),\qquad w(E/K):=w(E)w(E^K)=-1 .\tag{1.1}$$

**Theorem F.1.3** ($p$-parity) [THEOREM]. For every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and every prime $p$, $(-1)^{\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})}=w(E)$. (Dokchitser–Dokchitser, Ann. of Math. (2) 172 (2010) 567–596, Theorem 1.4; building on Monsky ($p=2$), Nekovář (potentially ordinary/multiplicative), Kim (supersingular).) Hence if $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite for one $p$, then $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod 2$.

### 1.3 Manin's theorem: rationality of $L(E,1)/\Omega_E$, and the Manin constant

Let $E_0$ be the $X_0(N)$-optimal curve in the isogeny class of $E$, $\varphi_0:X_0(N)\to E_0$ the optimal parametrisation, $\omega_{E_0}$ a Néron differential. Then $\varphi_0^*\omega_{E_0}=c\cdot 2\pi i f(z)\,dz$ with $c=c_{E_0}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ the **Manin constant** (integrality: Edixhoven, in *Arithmetic algebraic geometry (Texel 1989)*, Progr. Math. 89 (1991) 25–39, attributed there to Gabber). Let $\Lambda_f=\{\int_\gamma 2\pi i f\,dz:\gamma\in H_1(X_0(N),\mathbb{Z})\}$; since $\varphi_{0*}$ is surjective on $H_1$, the period lattice of $\omega_{E_0}$ is $\Lambda_{E_0}=c\Lambda_f$.

**Theorem F.1.4** (Manin) [THEOREM]. $L(E,1)/\Omega_E\in\mathbb{Q}$ for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$. More precisely, let $n_N$ be the order of the class of the cuspidal divisor $(0)-(\infty)$ in $J_0(N)(\mathbb{Q})$ (finite by the Manin–Drinfeld theorem). Then $n_N\cdot L(E_0,1)\in\Lambda_f\cap\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{Z}\,\Omega_f^+$, where $\Omega_f^+$ is the least positive real period in $\Lambda_f$; consequently
$$ c\,n_N\,\frac{L(E_0,1)}{\Omega^+_{E_0}}\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad \Omega^+_{E_0}=c\,\Omega_f^+ , $$
and $\Omega_{E_0}=\int_{E_0(\mathbb{R})}|\omega_{E_0}|$ equals $\Omega^+_{E_0}$ or $2\Omega^+_{E_0}$ according as $E_0(\mathbb{R})$ is connected or not. For $E$ isogenous to $E_0$, $L(E,1)=L(E_0,1)$ and $\Omega_E/\Omega_{E_0}\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$.

*Source and proof.* Manin, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser. Mat. 36 (1972) 19–66 (= Math. USSR-Izv. 6 (1972) 19–64). Proof: $L(E,1)=2\pi\int_0^\infty f(iy)\,dy=-\int_0^{i\infty}2\pi i f(z)\,dz$ is the integral of $\omega_f$ over the modular symbol $\{0,\infty\}\in H_1(X_0(N),\text{cusps};\mathbb{Z})$. The Abel–Jacobi image of $(0)-(\infty)$ is the functional $\omega\mapsto\int_\infty^0\omega$ modulo periods; its $n_N$-torsion means $n_N\{0,\infty\}$ is homologous to a closed cycle, so $n_N L(E,1)\in\Lambda_f$; reality of $L(E,1)$ gives the rest. $\square$

For prime $N$, $n_N=\operatorname{num}\big((N-1)/12\big)$ (Ogg's conjecture, proved by Mazur, Modular curves and the Eisenstein ideal, Publ. Math. IHÉS 47 (1977) 33–186). Two consequences used below: (i) **exact vanishing of $L(E,1)$ is certifiable**: computing $L(E_0,1)/\Omega^+_{E_0}$ to error $<1/(2cn_N)$ decides whether it is $0$; (ii) $L(E,1)$ has a denominator bounded in terms of $N$ alone.

**Status of the Manin constant** [THEOREM]s (all for the optimal $\varphi_0$):
- $p\nmid c$ for every prime $p$ at which $E_0$ has semistable reduction: for $p>2$ this is Mazur, Invent. Math. 44 (1978) 129–162, sharpened by Abbes–Ullmo, Compositio Math. 103 (1996) 269–286 and Agashe–Ribet–Stein, Pure Appl. Math. Q. 2 (2006) 617–636; for all semistable $p$ (including $p=2$) Česnavičius, Compositio Math. 154 (2018) 1889–1920. In particular **$c=\pm1$ whenever $N$ is squarefree**, and in general $c$ is supported on the primes of additive reduction.
- Edixhoven (1991, Thm. 3): $p\nmid c$ for $p\ge11$ unless $E$ has additive, potentially ordinary reduction of Kodaira type II, III or IV at $p$.
- Česnavičius–Neururer–Saha, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 26 (2024) 573–637 (arXiv:1911.09446): $c_\varphi\mid 6\deg\varphi$ for every parametrisation $\varphi$ from an intermediate curve $X_\Gamma$, $\Gamma_1(N)\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_0(N)$, and $c_\varphi\mid\deg\varphi$ if $N$ is cube-free or $\Gamma=\Gamma_1(N)$.
- Computationally, $c=\pm1$ for all $N\le 500000$ (Cremona 2022, as reported in [CNS24, §1]).

[CONJECTURE] (Manin): $c=\pm1$ for every optimal $\varphi_0$.

**Positivity of $L(E,1)$** [THEOREM]. $L(E,1)\ge0$ for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$: this is the nonnegativity of $L(\tfrac12,\pi)$ for cuspidal automorphic representations of $\mathrm{GL}_2$ with trivial central character, Guo, Duke Math. J. 83 (1996) 157–190 (for level $1$ it is a consequence of Kohnen–Zagier, Invent. Math. 64 (1981) 175–198). Note that under BSD the sign is forced by $\Omega_E>0$; unconditionally it is a theorem only because of these period/relative-trace-formula identities (this is the "zeroth-order arithmetic content" of the central value; cf. Lemma F.1.8).

### 1.4 First derivative: Gross–Zagier

**Theorem F.1.5** (Gross–Zagier) [THEOREM]. Assume (Heeg). Then
$$ L'(E/K,1)=\frac{8\pi^2\,(f,f)_{\Gamma_0(N)}}{u_K^2\,\sqrt{|D_K|}\,\deg\varphi}\;\hat h_K(y_K), \qquad (f,f)_{\Gamma_0(N)}=\int_{\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathfrak H}|f(z)|^2\,dx\,dy , $$
where $\hat h_K$ is the Néron–Tate height on $E(K)$ normalised as in [GZ86, §V.2]. Equivalently, writing $\varphi^*\omega_E=c_\varphi\cdot2\pi i f\,dz$ and $\|\omega_E\|^2=\int_{E(\mathbb{C})}|\omega_E\wedge\overline{\omega_E}|$ (so that $\deg\varphi\cdot\|\omega_E\|^2=c_\varphi^2\cdot 8\pi^2(f,f)$),
$$ L'(E/K,1)=\frac{\|\omega_E\|^2}{c_\varphi^2\,u_K^2\,\sqrt{|D_K|}}\;\hat h_K(y_K). $$
In particular $L'(E/K,1)\ne0\iff y_K\notin E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, and $L'(E/K,1)\ge0$.

*Sources.* Gross–Zagier, Invent. Math. 84 (1986) 225–320, Thm. I.6.3 and §V.2 (the case $(D_K,N)=1$); the general explicit version with the extra factor $2^{-\mu(N,D_K)}$ when $(N,D_K)\neq1$, and with ring class characters, is Cai–Shu–Tian, Algebra & Number Theory 8 (2014) 2523–2572, Thm. 1.1, deduced from Yuan–Zhang–Zhang, *The Gross–Zagier formula on Shimura curves*, Ann. of Math. Studies 184 (2013).

Complex conjugation acts on $y_K$ by $-w(E)$ modulo torsion (Gross, in *L-functions and arithmetic (Durham 1989)*, LMS Lecture Notes 153 (1991) 235–256 [sign convention as recalled from Gross's notes]), consistently with $L'(E/K,1)=L'(E,1)L(E^K,1)+L(E,1)L'(E^K,1)$ and $E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\oplus E^K(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}$.

**Theorem F.1.6** (Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin, with the nonvanishing input) [THEOREM]. If $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\le1$ then $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite. Inputs: Kolyvagin, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR 52 (1988) 522–540 and *Grothendieck Festschrift II*, Progr. Math. 87 (1990) 435–483 ("if $y_K$ is non-torsion then $\operatorname{rank}E(K)=1$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite"); Kolyvagin–Logachev, Leningrad Math. J. 1 (1990) 1229–1253 and Bertolini–Darmon, J. reine angew. Math. 412 (1990) 63–74 for the removal of technical hypotheses on $K$; Theorem F.1.5; and the existence of a Heegner $K$ with $L(E^K,1)\neq0$ (case $w=-1$) resp. $L'(E^K,1)\ne0$ (case $w=+1$): Waldspurger, Compositio Math. 54 (1985) 173–242; Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein, Ann. of Math. 131 (1990) 53–127 and Invent. Math. 102 (1990) 543–618; Murty–Murty, Ann. of Math. 133 (1991) 447–475 (see §2.2). Kato's Euler system gives an independent proof of "$L(E,1)\neq0\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{alg}}=0$, $\operatorname{Sha}$ finite" (Kato, Astérisque 295 (2004) 117–290, Thm. 14.2).

What Theorem F.1.5 gives for $L'(E,1)$ itself: for $w(E)=-1$, choosing $K$ with $L(E^K,1)\ne0$,
$$ L'(E,1)=\frac{\|\omega_E\|^2\,\hat h_K(y_K)}{c_\varphi^2u_K^2\sqrt{|D_K|}\;L(E^K,1)},\qquad L(E^K,1)\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\cdot\Omega_{E^K}\ \ (\text{Thm. F.1.4}), $$
so $L'(E,1)$ is a rational multiple of $\|\omega_E\|^2\,\hat h_K(y_K)/(\sqrt{|D_K|}\,\Omega_{E^K})$. No algebraicity statement for $L'(E,1)$ (or for $\hat h_K(y_K)$) is known or expected; the *exact vanishing* of $L'(E,1)$ is however an algebraic statement ($y_K\in E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}$).

### 1.5 Higher derivatives: what is known (nothing), and the sign

**Fact F.1.7** [summary of the literature; see §4 for the complete list]. For $k\ge2$ there is no known identity expressing $L^{(k)}(E,1)$—for any $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=k\ge2$, let alone for all such $E$—in terms of heights, periods, regulators, or any other arithmetic invariant of $E$; nor any statement of the form "$L^{(k)}(E,1)=0$ iff [arithmetic condition]"; nor any unconditional statement about the *sign* of $L^{(k)}(E,1)$. The only known exact statements about $L^{(k)}(E,1)$, $k\ge2$, are the parity vanishing of §1.1.

**Lemma F.1.8** (sign of the leading coefficient) [NEW — elementary; a full proof follows; adversarial review requested per charter]. Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$, $r=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)$, and let
$$ m_E:=\#\{\sigma\in(1,\tfrac32):\ L(E,\sigma)=0\}\quad(\text{counted with multiplicity}). $$
Then $m_E<\infty$ and
$$ \operatorname{sign}L^{(r)}(E,1)=(-1)^{m_E}. $$
Consequently: (i) if $L(E,s)$ has no real zeros in $(1,\tfrac32)$ (in particular under GRH$(E)$), then $L^{(r)}(E,1)>0$; (ii) unconditionally, if $r\le1$ then $m_E$ is even, i.e. $L(E,1)>0$ when $r=0$ and $L'(E,1)>0$ when $r=1$; (iii) for $r\ge2$ the parity of $m_E$ is not known for any curve by theoretical means; but for an individual $E$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=2$ a rigorous numerical evaluation showing $L''(E,1)>0$ *proves* that $L(E,s)$ has an even number of zeros in $(1,\tfrac32)$.

*Proof.* $\Lambda(E,s)$ is real-valued on $\mathbb{R}$ and $\Lambda(E,s)>0$ for $s>\tfrac32$: indeed $N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)>0$, and for real $s>\tfrac32$ the Euler product converges absolutely with every factor positive—for $p\nmid N$, $a_p^2<4p$ (Hasse, and $2\sqrt p\notin\mathbb{Z}$), so $\alpha_p,\beta_p$ are complex conjugates and $(1-\alpha_pp^{-s})(1-\beta_pp^{-s})=|1-\alpha_pp^{-s}|^2>0$; for $p\mid N$, $1-a_pp^{-s}>0$. By Jacquet–Shalika, $\Lambda(E,\tfrac32)\neq0$, hence $\Lambda(E,\tfrac32)>0$ by continuity. Zeros of $\Lambda$ in the compact segment $[1+\varepsilon,\tfrac32]$ are finite in number; $m_E$ is finite because zeros cannot accumulate at $1$. On $(1,\tfrac32]$ the real-analytic function $\Lambda(E,\cdot)$ changes sign exactly at its zeros of odd multiplicity, so for $1<s<1+\delta$ with $\delta$ small, $\operatorname{sign}\Lambda(E,s)=(-1)^{m_E}$. As $s\to1^+$, $\Lambda(E,s)=\frac{\Lambda^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}(s-1)^r(1+o(1))$ and $\Lambda^{(r)}(E,1)=N^{1/2}(2\pi)^{-1}L^{(r)}(E,1)$ because $L^{(j)}(E,1)=0$ for $j<r$. This proves the displayed identity and (i).
(ii) For $r=0$, $L(E,1)\ge0$ by Guo (§1.3) and $L(E,1)\ne0$, so $L(E,1)>0$. For $r=1$: $w(E)=-1$; by Waldspurger/Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein/Murty–Murty there is $K$ satisfying (Heeg) with $L(E^K,1)\neq0$, hence $L(E^K,1)>0$ by Guo. Since $L(E,1)=0$, Theorem F.1.5 gives $L'(E,1)L(E^K,1)=L'(E/K,1)\ge0$, so $L'(E,1)\ge0$, and $L'(E,1)\ne0$ by assumption. (iii) is the content of [GAP F.2] below; the last sentence is (i) read backwards. $\square$

*Remarks.* (a) Hoffstein–Ramakrishnan, Int. Math. Res. Not. 1995, no. 6, 279–308, show that non-dihedral $\mathrm{GL}_2$ $L$-functions have no Landau–Siegel zero: for non-CM $E$ there is an absolute $c>0$ with $L(E,\sigma)\ne0$ for $\sigma\in(\tfrac32-c/\log N,\tfrac32)$. The interval $(1,\tfrac32-c/\log N)$ is unconstrained unconditionally. (b) Lemma F.1.8 shows that *positivity*, the weakest conceivable arithmetic content of the leading coefficient, is available exactly in the range where an arithmetic formula exists ($r\le1$). This is the first precise sense in which "$L''(E,1)$ has no known arithmetic content".

## 2. Nonvanishing theorems

### 2.1 Families of forms of level $N$ (level aspect)

Throughout, $S_2(q)^*$ is the set of newforms of weight $2$ and prime level $q$, $\varepsilon_f=w(f)$, $S_2(q)^{\pm}$ the subsets with $\varepsilon_f=\pm1$ (asymptotically each half of $S_2(q)^*$).

**Theorem F.2.1** [THEOREM].
1. (Duke, Invent. Math. 119 (1995) 165–174) $\#\{f\in S_2(q)^*:L(f,1)\neq0\}\gg q/\log^2q$.
2. (Iwaniec–Sarnak, Israel J. Math. 120 (2000) 155–177) For fixed even weight $k$ and $N\to\infty$, at least $50\%$ of the $f\in H_k^{+}(\Gamma_0(N))$ (newforms with even functional equation) satisfy $L(f,\tfrac k2)>0$; moreover, improving $50\%$ to $50\%+\delta$ for some $\delta>0$ would rule out Landau–Siegel zeros of real Dirichlet $L$-functions.
3. (Kowalski–Michel, Acta Arith. 94 (2000) 303–343; VanderKam) For $q$ prime large, at least $7/8$ of $f\in S_2(q)^-$ have $L'(f,1)\ne0$, i.e. a simple zero at the centre. Hence $\operatorname{rank}J_0(q)\ge(\tfrac7{16}-o(1))\dim J_0(q)$ unconditionally (via Theorem F.1.6 applied to the corresponding quotients).
4. (Kowalski–Michel, Duke Math. J. 100 (1999) 503–542; Israel J. Math. 120 (2000) 179–204) Unconditionally, $\frac1{|S_2(q)^*|}\sum_{f}\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(f,s)\le C$ for $q$ prime large, with $C=6.5$ admissible.
5. (Kowalski–Michel–VanderKam, J. reine angew. Math. 526 (2000) 1–34) Let $p_k:=\liminf_{q}\#\{f\in S_2(q)^*:\Lambda^{(k)}(f,1)\ne0\}/|S_2(q)^*|$ (so $p_k\le\tfrac12$ by parity). Then $p_k\ge\pi_k$ with $\pi_k=\tfrac12-\tfrac1{32}k^{-2}+O(k^{-3})$; numerically $p_2>0.48254$, $p_3>0.49478$, $p_4>0.49758$, $p_5>0.49856$; the best bounds for $k=0,1$ are $p_0\ge\tfrac14$ (Iwaniec–Sarnak) and $p_1\ge\tfrac7{16}$ (Kowalski–Michel). Consequences (loc. cit., Thm. 1.4, Cor. 1.3, 1.5): for $q$ prime large, $\frac1{|S_2(q)^*|}\sum_f\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(f,s)\le1.1891+o(1)$; at least $99\%$ of $f\in S_2(q)^*$ have $r_{\mathrm{an}}(f)\le4$; under BSD for $J_0(q)$, $\operatorname{rank}J_0(q)\le(1.1891+o(1))\dim J_0(q)$.
6. [CONDITIONAL: GRH for $\mathrm{GL}_2$ and Dirichlet $L$-functions] (Iwaniec–Luo–Sarnak, Publ. Math. IHÉS 91 (2000) 55–131, Cor. 2) $\liminf_N\#\{f\in H_k^+(\Gamma_0(N)):L(f,\tfrac k2)\ne0\}/|H_k^+(\Gamma_0(N))|>\tfrac9{16}$ and $\liminf_N\#\{f\in H_k^-(\Gamma_0(N)):L'(f,\tfrac k2)\ne0\}/|H_k^-(\Gamma_0(N))|>\tfrac{15}{16}$; the Density Conjecture would give $1$ in both cases.

The expected truth (Brumer, Murty; see [KMV00, §1]) is $p_0=p_1=\tfrac12$, $p_k=0$ for $k\ge2$: in the level aspect, analytic rank $\ge2$ should have density zero.

### 2.2 Quadratic twists of a fixed curve

Fix $E$ (equivalently $f$) and let $D$ run over fundamental discriminants. Recall $w(E^D)=w(E)\chi_D(-N)$ for $(D,N)=1$.

**Theorem F.2.2** (existence of nonvanishing twists, values) [THEOREM]. Let $S$ be a finite set of primes and prescribe, for each $p\in S$, the local behaviour (split/inert/ramified) of $p$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$, in a way compatible with $w(E^D)=+1$ (i.e. $\chi_D(-N)=w(E)$). Then there exist infinitely many such $D$ with $L(E^D,1)\ne0$. (Waldspurger, Compositio Math. 54 (1985) 173–242, via half-integral weight forms and the theta correspondence; Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein, Ann. of Math. 131 (1990) 53–127 via metaplectic Eisenstein series; over general number fields and with the local conditions as stated, Friedberg–Hoffstein, Ann. of Math. 142 (1995) 385–423 [statement in the form commonly cited; the exact list of admissible local conditions is in loc. cit.].) In particular, for $w(E)=-1$ there are infinitely many $K$ satisfying (Heeg) with $L(E^K,1)\neq0$; this is the analytic input of Kolyvagin's rank-one theorem.

**Theorem F.2.3** (existence of simple-zero twists, derivatives) [THEOREM]. Let $f$ be a newform of even weight $k$ on $\Gamma_0(M)$ and $S$ a finite set of primes containing all $p\mid M$. Then there exists a fundamental discriminant $D$ such that every $p\in S$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$, $w(f)\chi_D(-1)=-1$ (so that $L(s,f,\chi_D)$ has odd functional equation), and $L(s,f,\chi_D)$ has a *simple* zero at $s=k/2$. Enlarging $S$ produces infinitely many such $D$. For $w(f)=+1$ this forces $D<0$ (Heegner discriminants); for $w(f)=-1$ it forces $D>0$.
*Sources.* Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein, Invent. Math. 102 (1990) 543–618 (statement as in the zbMATH review of that paper, consulted via MaRDI item Q2640641); Murty–Murty, Ann. of Math. 133 (1991) 447–475 (weight $2$, by a mean-value theorem for $\sum_D L'(1,f\otimes\chi_D)$ over $D$ in a Heegner class, whose main term is of size $Y\log Y$ [precise error term not reproduced here]); Iwaniec, Sém. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 2 (1990) 365–376 (analytic method).

**Theorem F.2.4** (counting) [THEOREM]. Let $N_r(E,X)=\#\{|D|\le X: r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^D)=r\}$.

2. Ono–Skinner, Invent. Math. 134 (1998) 651–660: $N_0(E,X)\gg X/\log X$ for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (Waldspurger + Sturm-type congruences + Friedberg–Hoffstein).
3. Kriz–Li, Forum Math. Sigma 7 (2019) e15: if $E$ has a rational $3$-isogeny then $N_0(E,X)\gg X$ and $N_1(E,X)\gg X$ ("weak Goldfeld"); for $E$ with $E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0$ satisfying an explicit $2$-adic Heegner-point condition (loc. cit. Thm. 1.12), $N_r(E,X)\gg X/\log^{5/6}X$ for $r=0,1$. These are genuinely *analytic-rank* statements, obtained from congruences between $p$-adic logarithms of Heegner points and Theorem F.1.5.
4. Smith, arXiv:2503.17619 (2025; the underlying machinery is J. Amer. Math. Soc. 39 (2026) 1–72 and 453–514): for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$, $50\%$ of quadratic twists have $\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_2}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}=0$ and $50\%$ have corank $1$; hence BSD implies Goldfeld's conjecture, and $100\%$ of twists with $w=+1$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=0$. This is a Selmer statement; passing to $L(E^D,1)\ne0$ requires a rank-zero $2$-converse, which is now a theorem for CM curves: Burungale–Tian, Ann. of Math. 203 (2026) 1–13 ($\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0\Rightarrow L(E,1)\neq0$ for CM $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and every $p$), giving the first case of the even-parity Goldfeld conjecture: $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E^{(n)},s)=0$ for $50\%$ of squarefree $n>0$, $E^{(n)}:ny^2=x^3-x$.

[CONJECTURE] (Goldfeld, in *Number theory, Carbondale 1979*, LNM 751, 108–118): $N_0(E,X)\sim N_1(E,X)\sim\tfrac12\#\{|D|\le X\}$; in particular the average analytic rank over twists is $\tfrac12$.

**Theorem F.2.5** [CONDITIONAL: GRH for the $L(E^D,s)$] (Heath-Brown, Duke Math. J. 122 (2004) 591–623). For every $E$, the average of $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^D)$ over any family of quadratic twists is $\le\tfrac32$ (improving Goldfeld's $3.25$). Applied separately to the even and odd subfamilies (one-level density with test functions of Fourier support in $(-1,1)$; symmetry $\mathrm{SO}(\mathrm{even})$ resp. $\mathrm{SO}(\mathrm{odd})$), this gives: at least $\tfrac14$ of the twists with $w(E^D)=+1$ have $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$, and at least $\tfrac34$ of those with $w(E^D)=-1$ have $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ (as stated in David–Devin–Fazzari–Waxman, arXiv:2608.06286, §1, with reference to Heath-Brown). *Derivation of the by-sign proportions:* with $\nu=1$ the one-level density bound gives average rank $\le\tfrac32$ on each sign class; on the even class ranks are $\ge2$ when nonzero, so the proportion of nonzero ranks is $\le\tfrac34$; on the odd class ranks are $\ge3$ when $\ne1$, so that proportion is $\le\tfrac14$.

### 2.3 The universal family, ordered by height

**Theorem F.2.6** [CONDITIONAL: GRH for elliptic curve $L$-functions]. The average analytic rank of all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ ordered by naive height is $\le2.3$ (Brumer, Invent. ### 2.4 Random matrix predictions and heuristics [HEURISTIC]

- Katz–Sarnak (AMS Colloq. Publ. 45 (1999); Bull. AMS 36 (1999) 1–26): low-lying zeros of quadratic-twist families of a fixed $E$ follow the orthogonal ensemble $\mathrm{O}$; the even (odd) twists follow $\mathrm{SO}(2n)$ ($\mathrm{SO}(2n+1)$). The Density Conjecture for these families implies average analytic rank $\tfrac12$, i.e. Goldfeld's conjecture, and implies that $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ has density $0$. The same symmetry type is predicted for the universal family (average rank $\tfrac12$, versus the proven $\le25/14$ under GRH).
- Conrey–Keating–Rubinstein–Snaith, in *Number theory for the millennium I* (2002) 301–315 (arXiv math/0012043): combining the $\mathrm{SO}(2n)$ value distribution of $L(E^D,1)$ near $0$ (density $\propto x^{-1/2}$, from the rightmost pole at $s=-\tfrac12$ of the moments of characteristic polynomials) with the Waldspurger–Kohnen–Zagier discretisation $L(E^D,1)=\kappa_E\,c(|D|)^2/\sqrt{|D|}$, $c(|D|)\in\mathbb{Z}$: for prime twists $\#\{p\le T:\ w(E^p)=+1,\ L(E^p,1)=0\}\sim c_E\,T^{3/4}(\log T)^{-5/8}$ (their Conjecture 1); for all fundamental discriminants the prediction is $c_E\,X^{3/4}(\log X)^{b_E+3/8}$ with $b_E\in\{1,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\tfrac13,\tfrac{\sqrt2}2-\tfrac13\}$ according to the rational $2$-torsion and the squareness of the discriminant (Delaunay–Watkins, in *Ranks of elliptic curves and random matrix theory*, LMS Lecture Notes 341 (2007) 189–193).
- Watkins, Experiment. Math. 17 (2008) 105–125 (arXiv math/0608766): for the universal family ordered by $|\Delta|<X$ (and analogously by conductor), $\#\{E:\ w(E)=+1,\ L(E,1)=0\}\sim c_1X^{19/24}(\log X)^{3/8}$, against $cX^{5/6}$ curves in total (Brumer–McGuinness heuristic); so rank $\ge2$ curves should have density $0$, but with a very slow decay ($X^{-1/24}$ up to logs), consistent with the observed average analytic ranks $0.978$ (Brumer–McGuinness, prime conductor $<10^8$), $0.964$ (Stein–Watkins, $<10^{10}$), $0.937$ resp. $0.869$ (Watkins' samples at conductor $\approx10^{14}$, positive resp. negative discriminant).
- Bhargava–Kane–Lenstra–Poonen–Rains, Camb. J. Math. 3 (2015) 275–321, and Park–Poonen–Voight–Wood, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 21 (2019) 2859–2903: a random-alternating-matrix model for Selmer groups reproducing Delaunay's heuristics for $\operatorname{Sha}$ in ranks $0,1$ and predicting that all but finitely many $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le21$.
- *Murmurations* (2022–2026). He–Lee–Oliver–Pozdnyakov, arXiv:2204.10140, observed empirically that the average of $a_p(E)$ over elliptic curves of fixed rank (equivalently, fixed root number) and conductor in a dyadic range oscillates in $p/N$ with a rank-dependent shape; this is a correlation between Frobenius traces and root numbers [empirical for elliptic curves]. [THEOREM] Zubrilina, Invent. Math. 241 (2025) 627–680: for holomorphic newforms of fixed weight $k$ and squarefree level $N\to\infty$, the root-number-weighted averages of $a_p$ over $p\asymp N$ converge to an explicit "murmuration density" (via the Eichler–Selberg trace formula and class-number sums). [CONDITIONAL: GRH] Bober–Booker–Lee–Lowry-Duda, Algebra & Number Theory 20 (2026), no. 2: the weight aspect (level $1$, $k\to\infty$). These results quantify how root numbers (hence parities of $r_{\mathrm{an}}$) are encoded in $a_p$-statistics on average; they say nothing about individual $L$-values.
- [CONDITIONAL: GRH] David–Devin–Fazzari–Waxman, arXiv:2608.06286 (Aug 2026): for the CM family $E_d:y^2=x^3-dx$ ($d$ fourth-power-free odd), one-level density with support $(-\tfrac35,\tfrac35)$ gives average analytic rank $\le\tfrac{13}6$; under a quartic analogue of Patterson's conjecture on Gauss sums the support extends to $(-1,1)$ and the bound to $\tfrac32$, yielding positive proportions of $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ (resp. $0$) twists.

None of these heuristics has any bearing on the *value* $L''(E,1)$ for an individual rank-$2$ curve; see §4.4(d).

## 3. Rank bounds and certification of analytic rank

### 3.1 Unconditional bounds

**Proposition F.3.1** [THEOREM]. (a) $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ll\log N$ (Mestre, Compositio Math. 58 (1986) 209–232, via the explicit formula; also from Jensen's formula applied to $\Lambda(E,s)$ on a disc of radius $1$ about $s=1$, using the convexity bound). (b) (Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer, Invent. Math. 25 (1974) 1–61) Let $m_f$ be the number of sign changes of the real function $y\mapsto f(iy)$ on $(0,\infty)$ ("fundamental critical points of odd order"). Then $m_f<\infty$, $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\le m_f$ and $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\equiv m_f\pmod2$.

*Proof of (b).* Put $\psi(u)=f(iu/\sqrt N)$, so $\Lambda(E,s)=\int_0^\infty\psi(u)u^{s-1}\,du$, $\psi$ is real, $\psi(u)=e^{-2\pi u/\sqrt N}(1+o(1))>0$ for $u\to\infty$ (since $a_1=1$), and the functional equation $\Lambda(E,s)=w\Lambda(E,2-s)$ is equivalent, by Mellin inversion (substitute $u\mapsto1/u$), to $\psi(1/u)=w\,u^2\psi(u)$. Hence sign changes of $\psi$ are finite in number and $\operatorname{sign}\psi(0^+)=w$, so $(-1)^{m_f}=w=(-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}}$. If $r_{\mathrm{an}}=r$ then $\Lambda^{(j)}(E,1)=\int_0^\infty\psi(u)(\log u)^j\,du=0$ for $j<r$, so $\int\psi(u)P(\log u)\,du=0$ for every polynomial $P$ of degree $<r$. If $m_f<r$, choose $P(t)=\pm\prod_{i=1}^{m_f}(t-\log u_i)$, $u_i$ the sign-change points, so that $\psi(u)P(\log u)\ge0$ and $\not\equiv0$; the integral is $>0$, contradiction. $\square$

Mestre (loc. cit.) reports that among curves of conductor $\le430$ only $17$ have $r_{\mathrm{an}}<m_f$; the bound is computable but not sharp.

### 3.2 The explicit formula and GRH-conditional bounds

**Proposition F.3.2** (explicit formula) [THEOREM]. Let $h$ be even, holomorphic in $|\Im z|<\tfrac12+\delta$ with $h(z)\ll(1+|z|)^{-2-\delta}$ there, and $g(u)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(t)e^{-itu}\,dt$. Writing the zeros of $\Lambda(E,s)$ as $\rho=1+i\gamma_\rho$ (so $\gamma_\rho\in\mathbb{R}$ under GRH$(E)$),
$$ \sum_{\rho}h(\gamma_\rho)=g(0)\big(\log N-2\log2\pi-2\gamma_{\mathrm{Eu}}\big)+2\int_0^\infty\frac{g(0)-g(x)}{1-e^{-x}}e^{-x}\,dx-2\sum_{p}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(\alpha_p^k+\beta_p^k)\log p}{p^{k}}\,g(k\log p), $$
where $\gamma_{\mathrm{Eu}}$ is Euler's constant; equivalently the archimedean terms are $g(0)(\log N-2\log2\pi)+\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(t)\Re\psi(1+it)\,dt$ with $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$. (Iwaniec–Kowalski, *Analytic Number Theory*, AMS Colloq. Publ. 53 (2004), Thm. 5.12, specialised; the form with $\Re\psi$ is Lemma 2.1 of Bober, ANTS X, Open Book Ser. 1 (2013) 135–144, arXiv:1112.1503, whose eq. (2) is the case $h(t)=(\sin\pi\Delta t/\pi\Delta t)^2$; the rewriting of the $\psi$-integral uses $\psi(1+it)=-\gamma_{\mathrm{Eu}}+\int_0^\infty e^{-x}(1-e^{-ixt})(1-e^{-x})^{-1}dx$.)

**Theorem F.3.3** (Mestre) [CONDITIONAL: GRH$(E)$]. (Mestre, loc. cit., Prop. II.1; stated there for newforms of weight $k$ and level $N$ with $k^2N$ in place of $N$.) (i) There is an absolute constant $A$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\le A\log N/\log\log N$. (ii) The first zero $1+it_0$, $t_0>0$, of $\Lambda(E,s)$ satisfies $t_0\le B/\log\log N$ for an absolute $B$. (iii) Explicitly, with Odlyzko's test function $F(x)=((1-|x|)\cos\pi x+\pi^{-1}\sin\pi|x|)\mathbf 1_{|x|\le1}$ scaled by $\lambda=\log100$ and the trivial bound $|a_p|\le2\sqrt p$, $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\le0.268\log N+1.03$. Under BSD these become bounds for $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$; this is the "Mestre(–Oesterlé) bound" of the surveys.

*Derivation of (i) with an explicit constant (on the page).* Take $g_X(u)=(1-|u|/X)^+$, so $h_X(t)=X\big(\tfrac{\sin(Xt/2)}{Xt/2}\big)^2\ge0$, $h_X(0)=X$, $g_X(0)=1$. Under GRH$(E)$ every term $h_X(\gamma_\rho)$ is $\ge0$ and the $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ zeros at $\rho=1$ contribute $r_{\mathrm{an}}X$. Hence
$$ r_{\mathrm{an}}\le\frac1X\Big[\log N-2\log2\pi-2\gamma_{\mathrm{Eu}}+2\int_0^\infty\frac{\min(1,x/X)e^{-x}}{1-e^{-x}}dx+2\sum_{p^k\le e^X}\frac{|\alpha_p^k+\beta_p^k|\log p}{p^k}\Big]. $$
Since $|\alpha_p^k+\beta_p^k|\le2p^{k/2}$, the prime sum is $\le2\sum_{n\le e^X}\Lambda(n)n^{-1/2}=(4+o(1))e^{X/2}$; the integral is $O(1)$. Choosing $X=2\log\log N-2\log\log\log N$ gives
$$ r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\le\Big(\tfrac12+o(1)\Big)\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\qquad\text{under GRH}(E). $$
The constant $\tfrac12$ comes from the Fejér kernel and the trivial bound on $a_p$; it is not claimed to be optimal. $\square$

**Computations.** Bober (loc. cit.) implements the bound with $h(t)=(\sin\pi\Delta t/\pi\Delta t)^2$ (Sage: `E.analytic_rank_upper_bound()`, "Bober's rank bounding method"): for the record curves $E_{20},\dots,E_{24}$ of rank $\ge20,\dots,24$ he proves, under GRH+BSD, rank exactly $20,\dots,24$, and for Elkies' curve $E_{28}$ the bound $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le30$; Booker–Dwyer (unpublished, Bober's Remark 1.2) obtain $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{28})\le28$ under GRH$(E_{28})$ with an optimised test function. Klagsbrun–Sherman–Weigandt, Math. Comp. 88 (2019) 837–846, prove $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E_{28})=28$ under GRH for number fields alone (2-ranks of class groups of cubic fields). Elkies–Klagsbrun (announcement, NMBRTHRY list, 29 Aug 2024; see also MathOverflow question 477849) exhibit $E_{29}:y^2+xy=x^3-27006183241630922218434652145297453784768054621836357954737385x+55258058551342376475736699591118191821521067032535079608372404779149413277716173425636721497$ with $29$ independent rational points (unconditional lower bound $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge29$, verified by the height-pairing determinant), $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=29$ under GRH for number fields, and $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le29$ under GRH$(E_{29})$ [announcement; not yet refereed at the time of writing].

### 3.3 Certification of the analytic rank of an individual curve

**Proposition F.3.4** [THEOREM — folklore, assembled from the cited results; proof on the page]. Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$.
(a) *Upper bounds are certifiable unconditionally.* For each $k\ge0$, the assertion $L^{(k)}(E,1)\ne0$ (hence $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le k$) can be verified by a finite rigorous computation. Indeed, with $x_n=2\pi n/\sqrt N$ and $G_k(x):=\int_1^\infty e^{-xu}(\log u)^k\,du$,
$$ \Lambda^{(k)}(E,1)=\big(1+w(-1)^k\big)\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\,G_k(x_n), $$
a series with explicit exponentially small tails ($|a_n|\le d(n)\sqrt n$ by Deligne, and $G_k(x)\le\int_1^\infty e^{-xu}(u-1)^k\,du=k!\,e^{-x}x^{-k-1}$ for $x>0$, using $\log u\le u-1$), so interval arithmetic yields an enclosure of $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ of any prescribed width. (This is the Buhler–Gross–Zagier method, Math. Comp. 44 (1985) 473–481; in the form $\Lambda(E,s)=\int_1^\infty\psi(u)(u^{s-1}+wu^{1-s})du$, $\psi$ as in Prop. F.3.1.)
(b) *Exact vanishing certificates exist only in the following cases:* (1) $L(E,1)=0$: by Theorem F.1.4 (rationality with bounded denominator), or from $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge1$ via Theorem F.1.6. (2) $L^{(k)}(E,1)=0$ for $(-1)^k\ne w(E)$: parity, §1.1. (3) $L'(E,1)=0$ when $w(E)=-1$ and $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge2$ is certified (two independent rational points): otherwise $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ and Theorem F.1.6 would force $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=1$. Equivalently: pick $K$ with (Heeg) and $L(E^K,1)\ne0$ (certifiable by (a)); then $L'(E,1)\ne0$ would make $y_K$ non-torsion and Kolyvagin gives $\operatorname{rank}E(K)=1$, contradicting $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})\ge2$.
(c) Consequently $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=k$ is certifiable for $k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ whenever it is true: for $k=2$ ($w=+1$) by (b1)+(b2)+(a); for $k=3$ ($w=-1$) by (b3)+(b2)+(a), *provided* $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge2$ holds (which BSD predicts). Certifying $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge4$ requires certifying $L''(E,1)=0$ (if $w=+1$) or $L'''(E,1)=0$ (if $w=-1$), for which no method is known ([GAP F.3](c)). To our knowledge no elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is proven to have $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge4$; e.g. for $E_{29}$ only $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ (from $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge2$ and parity; $\ge3$ if $w(E_{29})=-1$) is known unconditionally.
(d) Under GRH$(E)$, Proposition F.3.2 gives certified upper bounds $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le\lfloor\text{RHS}/h(0)\rfloor$ computable from $a_p$, $p\le e^X$ (this is what Bober's code certifies); without GRH the same computation still bounds $\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho)$ but the terms from off-line zeros need not be $\ge0$, so no unconditional rank bound results from it beyond Proposition F.3.1.

*Proof.* (a): derivatives in $s$ of $\int_1^\infty\psi(u)(u^{s-1}+wu^{1-s})du$ at $s=1$; the factor $(1+w(-1)^k)$ is the parity of §1.1. (b),(c),(d) are immediate from the cited theorems. $\square$

*Remark (the analytic rank is computable but not provably so).* The procedure "increase precision and $k$ until some enclosure of $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ excludes $0$" terminates and outputs exactly $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)$; but the proof that the output is a *lower* bound uses the vanishing of the lower derivatives, available only through (b). This is precisely the asymmetry that BSD-rank statements for $r\ge2$ would remove.

Yun–Zhang, Ann. of Math. 189 (2019) 393–526 (arXiv:1712.08026): with squarefree level (Iwahori structures) and $r$ of either parity matching the root number of $\pi_{F'}$, each individual product $\mathscr L^{(a)}(\pi,\tfrac12)\mathscr L^{(r-a)}(\pi\otimes\eta,\tfrac12)$, $0\le a\le r$, is the self-intersection of an explicit linear combination of Heegner–Drinfeld cycles; in particular odd vanishing orders occur. Feng–Yun–Zhang, Invent. Math. 235 (2024) 569–668 and Ann. Sci. ENS 58 (2025) 275–388, extend this to *higher Siegel–Weil* (all derivatives of Siegel–Eisenstein series on unitary groups) and higher theta series; their modularity conjecture is announced proved in arXiv:2608.07173 (Aug 2026) [preprint].

**The structural obstruction** [quoted from Feng–Yun–Zhang, Invent. Math. 235, Introduction]: "When $r>1$, no analogue of the spaces $\operatorname{Sht}^r_{U(n)}$ is presently known in the number field setting." The $r$ legs are $r$ points of the base curve $X/\mathbb{F}_q$; the analogue would be a space over "$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}\times_{\mathbb{F}_1}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{F}_1}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$", which does not exist. For $r=1$ the moduli of shtukas with one leg is the function-field counterpart of a Shimura curve and the $r=1$ identity is the function-field Gross–Zagier formula; the number-field analogue exists only for $r\le1$. This is why number-field technology stops at the first derivative. See `docs/approaches/D-function-field-analogy.md`.

### 4.3 The gap

**[GAP F.1] (Second-derivative formula).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $w(E)=+1$ and $L(E,1)=0$. Construct, unconditionally and functorially in $E$, a finitely generated abelian group (or Chow/Selmer-type group) $M_E$, a bilinear pairing $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_E:M_E\times M_E\to\mathbb{R}$ (positive semi-definite on $M_E\otimes\mathbb{R}$), an element $z_E\in M_E\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ (or a pair $z_1,z_2$), and an explicit period $C_E>0$ such that
$$ L''(E,1)=C_E\,\langle z_E,z_E\rangle_E \qquad(\text{resp. } =C_E\det(\langle z_i,z_j\rangle_E)_{i,j\le2}), $$
with $z_E$ (resp. $z_1\wedge z_2$) nonzero if and only if $L''(E,1)\ne0$. BSD predicts $C_E\langle z_E,z_E\rangle_E=2\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}(E/\mathbb{Q})|\operatorname{Sha}|\prod c_p/|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2$ when $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=2$, so the target is known; what is missing is any construction of $z_E$ from $f_E$.

Sub-gaps that any solution would settle, and which are therefore honest tests of partial progress:

**[GAP F.2] (Sign).** Prove $L''(E,1)>0$ for every $E$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=2$; equivalently (Lemma F.1.8) prove that $L(E,s)$ has an even number of zeros in $(1,\tfrac32)$ whenever $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=2$.

**[GAP F.3] (Vanishing from rank; rank-2 converse).** (a) $w(E)=+1$ and $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge3\Rightarrow L''(E,1)=0$. (b) $w(E)=-1$ and $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)\ge4\Rightarrow L'''(E,1)=0$. (c) Give *any* algorithm that, on input $E$ with $L''(E,1)=0$, terminates with a proof of that fact. The contrapositive of (a) is a "rank-$2$ converse theorem": $L''(E,1)\ne0\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le2$.

### 4.4 Candidate arithmetic objects for $L''(E,1)$: the attempts

**(a) Factorisation over a quadratic field.** Let $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=2$ (so $w(E)=+1$) and $K$ satisfy (Heeg). By (1.1), $r':=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^K)$ is odd, and
$$ L(E/K,s)=\frac{L''(E,1)}{2}\cdot\frac{L^{(r')}(E^K,1)}{r'!}\,(s-1)^{2+r'}\big(1+O(s-1)\big),\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=2+r'\ge3. $$
Hence $L'(E/K,1)=L(E,1)L'(E^K,1)+L'(E,1)L(E^K,1)=0$ and $L''(E/K,1)=0$; by Theorem F.1.5, **$y_K$ is torsion for every Heegner field $K$**, and Kolyvagin's Euler system built from $y_K$ has trivial bottom class (the derived classes may still be nonzero: this is the subject of `docs/approaches/B-euler-systems-heegner-kolyvagin.md` §4 and of Kolyvagin's conjecture, W. Zhang, Camb. J. Math. 2 (2014) 191–253). If $r'=1$ (Proposition F.5.1 provides infinitely many such $K$):
$$ \frac{L'''(E/K,1)}{3!}=\frac{L''(E,1)}{2}\,L'(E^K,1),\qquad L'(E^K,1)=\frac{\|\omega_{E^K}\|^2\,\hat h_{K'}(y_{K'}(E^K))}{c^2u_{K'}^2\sqrt{|D_{K'}|}\,L(E^{KK'},1)} $$
for any auxiliary $K'$ satisfying (Heeg) for $E^K$ (conductor $N D_K^2$) with $L(E^{KK'},1)\neq0$ (Theorem F.2.2). Thus the third Taylor coefficient of $L(E/K,s)$ is $L''(E,1)$ times a Heegner height; **the factorisation transports the unknown factor unchanged.** Moreover, since the BSD conjectures for $E$, $E^K$ and $E/K$ are equivalent given finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ (Dokchitser–Dokchitser 2010, compatibility of BSD quotients under $L$-function identities; $E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\oplus E^K(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}$), no information is gained by passing to $A=\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}E=E\times E^K$: $L(A,s)=L(E/K,s)$ and BSD$(A)$ is BSD$(E)\wedge$BSD$(E^K)$. Ratios over two Heegner fields, $L'''(E/K_1,1)/L'''(E/K_2,1)=L'(E^{K_1},1)/L'(E^{K_2},1)$, cancel $L''(E,1)$ altogether. *Outcome:* negative; the only new exact information is the (well-known) torsion-ness of all $y_K$.

Real quadratic $K$ with $w(E^K)=+1$, $L(E^K,1)\ne0$: then $L(E/K,s)$ has a double zero with leading coefficient $\tfrac12L''(E,1)L(E^K,1)$, $L(E^K,1)\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\Omega_{E^K}$; again a restatement.

**(b) Bertolini–Darmon: derived heights and derivatives of anticyclotomic $p$-adic $L$-functions.** Bibliographic correction first: the Bertolini–Darmon papers are *Derived heights and generalized Mazur–Tate regulators*, Duke Math. J. 76 (1994) 75–111; *Derived $p$-adic heights*, Amer. J. Math. 117 (1995) 1517–1554; *Heegner points on Mumford–Tate curves*, Invent. Math. 126 (1996) 413–456; *A rigid analytic Gross–Zagier formula and arithmetic applications*, Ann. of Math. 146 (1997) 111–147; *Heegner points, $p$-adic $L$-functions, and the Cerednik–Drinfeld uniformization*, Invent. Math. 131 (1998) 453–491; *Iwasawa's main conjecture for elliptic curves over anticyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions*, Ann. of Math. 162 (2005) 1–64. (There is no paper titled "Heegner points and derivatives of $p$-adic $L$-functions" in Invent. Math. 1996; that title belongs to Perrin-Riou, Invent. Math. 89 (1987) 455–510.)

*Which derivative of which function they interpret.* Setting of [BD96, BD98]: $N=N^+N^-p$ with $p\,\|\,N$, $p$ inert in $K$, the primes of $N^-$ inert in $K$ and $N^-$ squarefree with an *odd* number of prime factors, the primes of $N^+$ split. Then $w(E/K)=-1$, $L(E/K,1)=0$ (classical Gross–Zagier–Zhang applies on the Shimura curve of discriminant $N^-p$ and level $N^+$). The **anticyclotomic $p$-adic $L$-function** $\mathscr L_p(E/K)\in\mathbb{Z}_p[[G_\infty]]$ ($G_\infty=\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/K)$, $K_\infty$ the anticyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension) is built from Gross points on the *definite* quaternion algebra of discriminant $N^-$ and interpolates $L(E/K,\chi,1)$ for anticyclotomic $\chi$ of $p$-power conductor; at the trivial character its interpolation factor vanishes ("exceptional zero", because $E/K_p$ has split multiplicative reduction). [BD98, Thm. A, B]: the image $\mathscr L'_p(E/K)\in I/I^2\cong G_\infty\otimes\dots$ of the first derivative, viewed through the Tate parametrisation $\Phi_{\mathrm{Tate}}:K_p^\times\to E(K_p)$, satisfies
$$ \Phi_{\mathrm{Tate}}\big(\mathscr L'_p(E/K)\big)=\alpha_K-w_p\,\bar\alpha_K\in E(K), $$
where $\alpha_K$ is the Heegner point coming from the Shimura curve parametrisation and $w_p=\pm1$ according as $E/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is split or non-split multiplicative. Corollary C/D: $\mathscr L_p'(E/K)\ne0\iff\alpha_K-w_p\bar\alpha_K$ has infinite order $\Rightarrow\operatorname{rank}E(K)=1$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ finite. So the derivative interpreted is the **first derivative, in the anticyclotomic variable at the trivial character, of the anticyclotomic $p$-adic $L$-function in the exceptional-zero case**, and it is *a point* (not a height). The Annals 1997 paper concerns *values* $L(E/K,\chi,1)$ and component groups, "not derivatives or heights" (its introduction). [BD05] proves one divisibility of the anticyclotomic main conjecture (definite case, $N^-$ with an odd number of primes) for $p\nmid N$ ordinary with $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ surjective and further ramification hypotheses [exact hypothesis list: see the paper; not reproduced].

*Derived heights.* In [BD94] a finite abelian $p$-extension $L/K$ with group $G$, augmentation ideal $I\subset\mathbb{Z}_p[G]$ (or $\mathbb{F}_p[G]$) is fixed and the $p$-Selmer group $S=\operatorname{Sel}_p(E/K)$ carries a decreasing filtration $S=S^{(1)}\supset S^{(2)}\supset\cdots$ by universal norms from the intermediate fields, together with **derived height pairings** $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_k:S^{(k)}\times S^{(k)}\to I^k/I^{k+1}$, with $S^{(k+1)}$ the null-space of $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_k$; $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_1$ is the Mazur–Tate $p$-adic height. The "generalised Mazur–Tate regulator" is the product of the discriminants of the $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_k$ on the graded pieces, an element of $I^{\rho}/I^{\rho+1}$ with $\rho=\sum_k k\dim(S^{(k)}/S^{(k+1)})$, and [BD94] conjecture that the Mazur–Tate element $\theta_L$ (Mazur–Tate, Duke Math. J. 54 (1987) 711–750) lies in $I^\rho$ with leading term this regulator (times the usual BSD factors). [BD95] passes to $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions: the derived heights measure the non-semisimplicity of the Selmer Iwasawa module (elementary divisors $\Lambda/T^k$ with $k\ge2$), and Howard, Amer. J. Math. 126 (2004) 1315–1340, relates them to higher derivatives of Perrin-Riou/Rubin-type *algebraic* $p$-adic $L$-functions. In the anticyclotomic setting the $p$-adic height pairing is alternating (Mazur–Rubin, ICM 2002 Proceedings, 185–195), hence degenerate in odd rank, and derived heights are unavoidable.

*Assessment for [GAP F.1].* Derived heights are (i) $p$-adic, with values in $I^k/I^{k+1}$, (ii) defined only when a first-order pairing is *degenerate*, and (iii) conjecturally compute leading terms of $\theta$-elements and $p$-adic $L$-functions—objects that interpolate *values* $L(E,\chi,1)$, never complex derivatives. The archimedean analogue of the mechanism does not exist: the Néron–Tate pairing is positive definite, so there is no filtration by null-spaces and no "archimedean derived height". The complex $L''(E,1)$ is (conjecturally) the determinant of a *nondegenerate* pairing on a rank-$2$ lattice, a structurally different object. Hence derived heights define a candidate rank-$2$ regulator for the **$p$-adic** leading term (cf. (c)) but not for $L''(E,1)$. *Outcome:* clarifies the question; no progress on [GAP F.1].

**(c) $p$-adic heights, $p$-adic Gross–Zagier, $p$-adic BSD** (details in `docs/approaches/A-iwasawa-padic.md`). [THEOREM]s: Perrin-Riou, Invent. Math. 89 (1987) 455–510 ($p$ good ordinary, split in $K$; the derivative of the two-variable $p$-adic $L$-function of $E/K$ in the cyclotomic direction at the central point equals an explicit Euler-factor multiple of the cyclotomic $p$-adic height of $y_K$); Kobayashi, Invent. Math. 191 (2013) 527–629 (supersingular $p$); Disegni, Compositio Math. 153 (2017) 1987–2074 and J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 22 (2023) 2199–2240 (Shimura curves, totally real fields, nonsplit primes). [CONJECTURE] Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum, Invent. Math. 84 (1986) 1–48: $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ ($+1$ if $E$ is split multiplicative at $p$) with leading term $\Omega_E^{-1}$-normalised $\prod_p(\text{Euler factors})\cdot\operatorname{Reg}_p(E)\,|\operatorname{Sha}|\prod c_\ell/|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2$ (times the $\mathscr L$-invariant in the exceptional case, proved for the derivative at $T=0$ by Greenberg–Stevens, Invent. Math. 111 (1993) 407–447), where $\operatorname{Reg}_p$ is the determinant of the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing. [THEOREM] Kato, Astérisque 295, Thm. 18.4 (hypotheses as in his §18.1): for $p$ with a root $\alpha$ of $X^2-a_pX+p$ of $p$-adic valuation $<1$ (all $p\nmid N$; for $p\,\|\,N$, $\alpha=a_p$; no such $\alpha$ for additive $p$), $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})\le\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_{p,\alpha}(E,s)$ if $E$ is not a Tate curve at $p$, and $\le\operatorname{ord}-1$ if it is. [CONDITIONAL] Perrin-Riou, Invent. Math. 109 (1992) 137–185 and Schneider, Invent. Math. 79 (1985) 329–374: if the Iwasawa main conjecture holds (Skinner–Urban, Invent. Math. 195 (2014) 1–277, plus Kato; hypotheses in `A`), the cyclotomic $p$-adic height is nondegenerate on $E(\mathbb{Q})$ (Schneider's conjecture, open in general) and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite, then $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ and the leading coefficient is the $p$-adic BSD expression up to a $p$-adic unit. *Assessment:* the $p$-adic Taylor coefficients at $T=0$ are (conditionally) arithmetic because $L_p$ is an element of the Iwasawa algebra whose algebraic counterpart is a characteristic ideal; no such algebraic incarnation of the complex $\Lambda(E,s)$ exists, and $L_p$ carries no information about $L''(E,1)$ (interpolation concerns only values at finite-order characters; already $L_p'(E,0)$ is unrelated to $L'(E,1)$—one is a $p$-adic, the other an archimedean height of the same point). *Outcome:* no route to [GAP F.1]; it does show that the "algebraic side" of a rank-$2$ leading term is well-posed ($\operatorname{Reg}_p$ of a rank-$2$ lattice).

**(d) Random-matrix and Cohen–Lenstra predictions for $L''$ and analytic $\operatorname{Sha}$ in rank-2 families** [HEURISTIC]. For odd twists, the distribution of $L'(E^D,1)$ is modelled by moments of $\Lambda_A'(1)$, $A\in\mathrm{SO}(2n+1)$ (Snaith, J. Phys. A 38 (2005) 10345–10360), and compared with regulators of rank-1 twists by Delaunay–Roblot, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 20 (2008) 601–624; Conrey–Rubinstein–Snaith–Watkins, LMS Lecture Notes 341 (2007) 201–214, treat the discretisation for odd twists. For the rank-$2$ subfamily $\{D: w(E^D)=+1,\ L(E^D,1)=0\}$ the natural model is the distribution of $\Lambda_A''(1)$ for $A\in\mathrm{SO}(2n)$ *conditioned on* $\Lambda_A(1)=0$; we found no published treatment of this conditional distribution or of the resulting prediction for $L''(E^D,1)/(2\Omega_{E^D}\operatorname{Reg}(E^D))$ ("analytic $\operatorname{Sha}$") [our search; see §7]. What the heuristics do say: Delaunay, Experiment. Math. 10 (2001) 191–196 (Cohen–Lenstra-type heuristics for $\operatorname{Sha}$; reproduced for $u\in\{0,1\}$ by the BKLPR model), predicts for curves of rank $u$ that $\Pr(p\mid|\operatorname{Sha}|)=1-\prod_{j\ge1}(1-p^{-(2j+2u-1)})$, i.e. $\approx p^{-1}$ for $u=0$, $\approx p^{-3}$ for $u=1$, $\approx p^{-5}$ for $u=2$: rank-$2$ curves should almost always have trivial $\operatorname{Sha}$, so $L''(E,1)/(2\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}(E)\prod c_p)$ should almost always equal $|E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^{-2}$. This is testable numerically to high precision and is consistent with the rank-2 data in Watkins (2008) [data not re-verified here]; it is evidence for BSD, not a route to a formula.

**[GAP F.5] (RMT model for rank-2 leading terms).** Derive, within the $\mathrm{SO}(2n)$ model, the conditional distribution of $\Lambda''_A(1)$ given $\Lambda_A(1)=0$, and formulate the corresponding conjecture for the distribution of $L''(E^D,1)/\Omega_{E^D}$ over rank-2 twists (with the Delaunay discretisation for $|\operatorname{Sha}|$ and a regulator model). Not needed for BSD, but it would make "analytic $\operatorname{Sha}$" statistics in rank $2$ precise.

**(e) Rankin–Selberg and triple-product specialisations.** (i) $L(E/K,s)=L(f\otimes\theta_K,s)$ with $\theta_K$ the weight-1 theta series of the trivial class group character; more generally $L(f\otimes\theta_\psi,s)=L(E/K,\psi,s)$ for ring class characters $\psi$: first derivatives are Heegner heights (item 3 of §4.1), never second derivatives. (ii) Triple products: for $g$ a weight-2 newform, $\rho_g\otimes\rho_g=\operatorname{Sym}^2\rho_g\oplus\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-1}$ gives $L(f\otimes g\otimes g,s)=L(f\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2g,s)\,L(f,s-1)$, so if $L(E,1)=L'(E,1)=0$ then
$$ L''(f\otimes g\otimes g,2)=L(f\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2g,2)\cdot L''(E,1), $$
i.e. $L''(E,1)$ is the leading Taylor coefficient of a triple-product $L$-function at its centre (whenever $L(f\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2 g,2)\ne0$)—but the Gross–Kudla/Yuan–Zhang–Zhang formula is again for the *first* derivative, and for $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ge2$ it only says that the $F$-component of the Gross–Kudla–Schoen cycle has height $0$. (iii) $L(E,s)^2$ is not a Rankin–Selberg $L$-function ($L(f\times f,s)=\zeta(s-1)L(\operatorname{Sym}^2f,s)$). (iv) "Second moments" $\sum_f|L(f,1)|^2$, $\sum_D L'(1,f\otimes\chi_D)$ (Iwaniec–Sarnak, Kowalski–Michel–VanderKam, Murty–Murty) are averages; they yield nonvanishing proportions and never an individual identity. *Outcome:* every specialisation reduces to a first-derivative object or to an average.

**Summary of the attempts (recorded per charter).** (a) yields the exact statement "all Heegner points of $E$ are torsion when $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ge2$" and the identity $L'''(E/K,1)/3!=\tfrac12L''(E,1)L'(E^K,1)$—nothing on $L''(E,1)$; (b) shows that the only existing "higher-order" height theory is $p$-adic and predicated on degeneracy, with no archimedean analogue; (c) shows the $p$-adic leading term is well-posed but disconnected from $L''(E,1)$; (d) yields only heuristics; (e) shows $L''(E,1)$ appears as a leading coefficient of triple products, where again only first derivatives are understood. The gap [GAP F.1] stands, with the structural reason of §4.2.

## 5. Analytic constraints usable as input to arithmetic arguments

### 5.1 Rank-one Heegner twists of a rank-two curve (input for `B` §4)

**Proposition F.5.1** [THEOREM — assembled from Theorems F.1.5, F.1.6, F.2.3; proof on the page]. Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $w(E)=+1$ and $L(E,1)=0$ (equivalently $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ge2$; e.g. $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=2$). Then:
1. For every $K$ satisfying (Heeg): $w(E^K)=-1$, $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)\ge3$, $L'(E/K,1)=L''(E/K,1)=0$, and $y_K\in E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}$.
2. For every finite set $S$ of primes containing all $p\mid N$ there exists $K$ satisfying (Heeg) in which all $p\in S$ split and $L'(E^K,1)\neq0$; there are infinitely many such $K$.
3. For each $K$ as in 2: $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^K)=r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^K)=1$, $\operatorname{Sha}(E^K/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite, $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=3$, $\operatorname{rank}E(K)=r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)+1$, and $L'''(E/K,1)/3!=\tfrac12L''(E,1)\,L'(E^K,1)$.

*Proof.* 1: (1.1) gives $w(E^K)=-1$, so $L(E^K,1)=0$; with $L(E,1)=0$ all terms of $L'(E/K,1)$ and $L''(E/K,1)$ vanish; Theorem F.1.5 gives $\hat h_K(y_K)=0$. 2: Theorem F.2.3 with $f=f_E$, $k=2$, $M=N$, $\varepsilon=+1$: the produced $D$ has $\chi_D(-1)=-1$, so $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)$ is imaginary, all $p\in S\supseteq\{p\mid N\}$ split (this is (Heeg)), and $L(s,f,\chi_D)=L(E^K,s)$ has a simple zero at $s=1$. Given finitely many such $K_1,\dots,K_m$, add to $S$ a prime inert in each $K_i$ to obtain a new $K$. 3: $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^K)=1$ by 2; Theorem F.1.6 for $E^K$ gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E^K)=1$ and finite $\operatorname{Sha}$; the rank of $E(K)$ is $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})+\operatorname{rank}E^K(\mathbb{Q})$; the Taylor identity is (a) of §4.4. $\square$

*Exact hypotheses:* no condition on $N$ (any conductor, any reduction types); $K$ is imaginary quadratic with all $p\mid N$ split (hence $(D_K,N)=1$); nothing is assumed about $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$. The "correct sign" is automatic: Heegner twists of a $w=+1$ curve are odd, and Theorem F.2.3 produces simple zeros among them. For $w(E)=-1$ the Heegner twists are even, and Theorem F.2.2 provides infinitely many Heegner $K$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E^K)=0$ (so $\operatorname{rank}E(K)=r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E)$); if moreover $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)\ge3$ then again all $y_K$ are torsion.

Quantitatively, the number of $|D|\le X$ with $w(E^D)=-1$ and $L'(E^D,1)\ne0$ is $\gg X^{1-\varepsilon}$ (Perelli–Pomykała), $\gg X/\log^{5/6}X$ under Kriz–Li's hypotheses, and at least $\tfrac34$ of the odd twists under GRH (Theorem F.2.5) [whether these counts restrict to the Heegner residue classes with the same strength was not checked here; Theorem F.2.3 gives infinitely many in every Heegner class]. A positive proportion unconditionally, for all $E$, is open:

**[GAP F.4] (analytic Goldfeld for derivatives).** For every $E/\mathbb{Q}$, show $\#\{|D|\le X:\ w(E^D)=-1,\ L'(E^D,1)\ne0\}\gg X$. Known for $E$ with a rational $3$-isogeny (Kriz–Li) and, on the Selmer side for $p=2$, for all $E$ (Smith 2025: $50\%$ of twists have $\operatorname{Sel}_{2^\infty}$-corank $1$); converting the latter into $L'(E^D,1)\neq0$ needs a rank-one $2$-converse theorem, available for CM curves at good ordinary $p$ (Burungale–Tian, Invent. Math. 220 (2020) 211–253) and for non-CM curves with hypotheses excluding $p=2$ (W. Zhang 2014; Skinner, Ann. of Math. 191 (2020) 329–354) [whether a $2$-adic rank-one converse is available for the congruent number curve: unverified].

### 5.2 Anticyclotomic twists: Cornut–Vatsal

**Theorem F.5.2** [THEOREM]. Let $K$ satisfy (Heeg) [more generally the configurations of Cornut–Vatsal], $p\nmid ND_K$ a prime, $K[p^n]$ the ring class field of conductor $p^n$, $y_n\in E(K[p^n])$ the Heegner points of conductor $p^n$. Then for $n\gg0$ the points $y_n$ have infinite order and, for all but finitely many anticyclotomic characters $\chi$ of $p$-power conductor, $L'(E/K,\chi,1)\neq0$ (indefinite case) resp. $L(E/K,\chi,1)\ne0$ (definite case). Cornut, Invent. Math. 148 (2002) 495–523 (Mazur's conjecture); Vatsal, Invent. Math. 148 (2002) 1–46 and Duke Math. J. 116 (2003) 219–261; Cornut–Vatsal, in *L-functions and Galois representations (Durham 2004)*, LMS Lecture Notes 320 (2007) 121–186; the passage to $L'(E/K,\chi,1)$ uses Zhang's Gross–Zagier formula with characters (§4.1 item 3). This holds regardless of $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)$: for a rank-2 curve, the $\chi$-components of $E(K[p^\infty])\otimes\mathbb{C}$ are nonzero for almost all $\chi$, each of "rank one" in the sense of $L'(E/K,\chi,1)\ne0$, while the trivial-character component ($E(K)$ itself) is invisible to Heegner points (Proposition F.5.1(1)).

### 5.3 Cyclotomic twists: Rohrlich, Chinta, Kato

**Theorem F.5.3** [THEOREM]. (a) (Rohrlich, Invent. Math. 75 (1984) 409–423) For each prime $p$, $L(E,\chi,1)\ne0$ for all but finitely many Dirichlet characters $\chi$ of $p$-power conductor. (Stated there for Weil curves; unconditional by Theorem F.1.1.) (b) (Rohrlich, Invent. Math. 97 (1989) 381–403) For any $s_0\in\mathbb{C}$ and any finite set $S$ of primes there are infinitely many primitive Dirichlet characters $\chi$, unramified at $S$, with $L(E,\chi,s_0)\ne0$ [the theorem is for cuspidal $\pi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_2$ over any number field and ray class characters]. (c) (Chinta, J. reine angew. Math. 544 (2002) 13–24) Thm. 1: for $q$ prime sufficiently large in terms of $N$ and $\varepsilon$, $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\prod_{\chi\bmod q}L(E,\chi,s)<q^{7/8+\varepsilon}$; Thm. 2: there is $\eta(p)\ll_E1$ such that $L(E,\chi,1)\ne0$ for all primitive $\chi$ of conductor $p^j$, $j>\eta(p)$; moreover $L(E,\chi,1)\neq0$ for a proportion $1+O_{E,\varepsilon}(q^{-1/8+\varepsilon})$ of $\chi\bmod q$. (d) (Kato, Astérisque 295, Thm. 14.2 and 14.4) If $\chi$ is a character of $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})$, $F/\mathbb{Q}$ finite abelian, and $L(E,\chi,1)\neq0$, then the $\chi$-part of $\operatorname{Sel}(F,T_pE)$ is finite for every $p$ (so $(E(F)\otimes\mathbb{C})^\chi=0$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/F)^\chi$ is finite); consequently (Thm. 14.4) $E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{m^\infty}))$ is finitely generated for every $m\ge1$.

*Use in Kato's argument and for a rank-2 curve.* Rohrlich's (a) is equivalent to $L_p(E,T)\ne0$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, which is the analytic input in Kato's proof that $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee$ is $\Lambda$-torsion and that $\operatorname{char}\mid p^n L_p$ (Thm. 17.4, good ordinary $p$). For $E$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)=2$ all nontrivial cyclotomic twists behave as rank $0$ (by (a),(c) for almost all $\chi$) and Kato's Euler system bounds the corresponding $\chi$-Selmer groups; the trivial-character component—$\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ itself—is exactly where the bottom Kato class is expected to vanish (Perrin-Riou's conjecture, `docs/approaches/C`). Thus both Euler systems (Heegner, Kato) see only "rank $\le1$" components; the rank-2 component is invisible to both. This is the Euler-system shadow of [GAP F.1].

For characters of higher order the situation is conjectural: David–Fearnley–Kisilevsky, Experiment. Math. 13 (2004) 185–198 [HEURISTIC], predict infinitely many $\chi$ of order $3$ or $5$ with $L(E,\chi,1)=0$ but only finitely many of any fixed prime order $\ge7$; Fearnley–Kisilevsky–Kuwata, J. London Math. Soc. 86 (2012) 539–557, give data and partial results; on the algebraic side Mazur–Rubin, Amer. J. Math. 140 (2018) 571–616 ("Diophantine stability") prove for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and all but finitely many primes $\ell$ that there are infinitely many cyclic degree-$\ell$ extensions $L/\mathbb{Q}$ with $E(L)=E(\mathbb{Q})$ [hypotheses as in loc. cit.].

### 5.4 Parity

By Theorem F.1.3, for every $E$ and $p$, $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod2$. For a curve with $r_{\mathrm{an}}=2$ this gives that $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is even for every $p$ (it is $\ge2$ if $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge1$, since then $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge2$ by Theorem F.1.6), and $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is even if $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite for some $p$. This is the only unconditional link between $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ and the arithmetic of $E$ besides $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge2\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ (Theorem F.1.6).
