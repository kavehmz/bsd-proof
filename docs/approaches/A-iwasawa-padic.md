## 0. Executive summary and dependency chart

The Iwasawa-theoretic attack produces, for an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and a prime $p$, the following
chain (all terms defined in §1; $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})$, $r_{\mathrm{an}}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$,
$r_p=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)$):

$$
r_{\mathrm{alg}}\;\le\;\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})
\;=\;\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty
\;\le\;\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)\;\le\; r_p .
\tag{0.1}
$$

* The first inequality is the Kummer sequence; equality iff $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite.
* The equality in the middle is Mazur's control theorem (§1.5) plus Kato's theorem that $X_\infty$ is $\Lambda$-torsion.
* The second inequality is the structure theorem for torsion $\Lambda$-modules (§3.1); equality iff $X_\infty$ is
  *semisimple at $T=0$*, which (Schneider, Perrin-Riou) is equivalent to the non-degeneracy of the cyclotomic
  $p$-adic height pairing on the compact Selmer group (§3.3).
* The last inequality is Kato's divisibility $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)\mid L_p(E,T)$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ (§2.1);
  equality of the orders of vanishing holds whenever the cyclotomic main conjecture is known (§2.2–2.6: essentially
  all good $p\ge5$ for non-CM $E$ with $E[p]$ irreducible, all good $p>2$ for semistable $E$ at supersingular $p$, CM curves, most Eisenstein primes; see Theorem 2.9 for the exact list).

**What the approach proves.** (i) The whole chain (0.1) is a theorem (Kato 2004, Thm. 18.4: $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\le r_p$ for every good or non-split multiplicative $p$; Prop. 3.4 below re-proves it in the good ordinary case).
(ii) If $r_p$ is *computed* to equal $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ for a single good ordinary prime $p$, then $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$
is finite, the $p$-adic height pairing is non-degenerate, and (when the main conjecture is known) the $p$-adic BSD leading
term holds up to a $p$-adic unit (Theorem 3.10, Theorem 6.1). This is what the computational team can *prove* for
389a1, 5077a1, etc.
(iii) The $p$-adic side of BSD (Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum) is a theorem up to a $p$-adic unit exactly under: main conjecture
+ Schneider's non-degeneracy + finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ (Theorem 3.10).

**Where it stops.** Nothing in (0.1) involves $r_{\mathrm{an}}$. The link between the $p$-adic and the complex
$L$-function is the interpolation formula, which sees only the *values* $L(E,\chi,1)$ at finite-order characters $\chi$
of $\Gamma$ and never a derivative $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ with $k\ge1$ (§4.5). Consequently the approach reduces the rank
conjecture to the *transfer conjecture* $\mathrm{T}(E,p):\ r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ (§4), together with Schneider's
conjecture (§5) and finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ (file `H-sha-and-descent.md`). Precisely (Theorem 4.3, `[NEW]` as an assembled equivalence):

> under the main conjecture for $(E,p)$: $\;$ BSD(rank) for $E$ $\iff$ $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ **and** $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite **and** Schneider$(E,p)$, in the sense that each side implies the other given BSD(rank) resp. given (Sha finite + Schneider).

What is known about $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ (Theorem 4.2): $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0\iff r_p=0$; $r_p\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod 2$; $r_p\le1\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}=r_p$ (converse theorems, under hypotheses); $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1\Rightarrow r_p=1$ is *equivalent* to the non-vanishing of the $p$-adic height of a generator, known for CM curves (Bertrand) and for supersingular $p$ (Kobayashi) but open for ordinary non-CM $p$; $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge3\Rightarrow r_p\ge3$ (`[NEW]`, Prop. 4.5, an elementary consequence of Gross–Zagier + $p$-adic Gross–Zagier + parity). Nothing is known relating $L''(E,1)$ to $L_p''(E,0)$; this is `[GAP A.1]`, isolated in §4.5 with the attempt in §4.6.

**2024–2026 developments the team must know** (details and exact hypotheses in §2):
Wan's 2014 preprint on Kobayashi's $\pm$ main conjecture (arXiv:1411.6352) was **withdrawn** on 2024-09-09 and superseded by Burungale–Skinner–Tian–Wan, arXiv:2409.01350 (Kobayashi's main conjecture for *semistable* $E$ at supersingular $p>2$; Perrin-Riou's conjecture for all good $p>2$; still a preprint); Burungale–Castella–Skinner (IMRN 2025) removed the "ramified multiplicative prime" hypothesis from Skinner–Urban for $p>3$; Castella–Grossi–Skinner (Math. Ann. 2025) proved Mazur's main conjecture at Eisenstein primes $p>2$ with $\varphi|_{G_p}\ne\mathbf 1,\omega$, and Keller–Yin (arXiv:2402.12781) removed that condition in the anticyclotomic setting; Burungale–Tian (Ann. of Math. 2026) proved the rank-zero $p$-converse for CM curves at every $p$; Yan–Zhu (J. Algebra 2026) and Ito Sprung (Adv. Math. 2024) gave further main-conjecture cases.

## 1. Setup and exact definitions

Throughout, $E/\mathbb{Q}$ has conductor $N$, $f_E=\sum a_nq^n\in S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ is the associated newform
(modularity: Wiles, Taylor–Wiles, Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor), $p$ is a prime, and unless stated otherwise $p\nmid N$.
Write $\omega_E$ for a Néron differential of a global minimal Weierstrass model.

### 1.1 Periods, modular symbols, the Manin constant

Let $\pi:X_0(N)\to E$ be a modular parametrisation and $c_\pi\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ the Manin constant, defined by
$c_\pi\,\pi^*\omega_E=2\pi i f_E(\tau)d\tau$. Let $\Omega_E^+=\int_{E(\mathbb{R})}|\omega_E|$ be the real (Néron) period and
$\Omega_E^-$ the corresponding imaginary period (so $\Omega_E^\pm$ generate, up to the number $c_\infty\in\{1,2\}$ of
connected components of $E(\mathbb{R})$, the $\pm$-parts of the period lattice of $\omega_E$).

**Definition 1.1 (modular symbols).** For $r\in\mathbb{Q}$ put
$$\lambda^+(r)=-\pi i\Big(\int_r^{i\infty}f_E(\tau)\,d\tau+\int_{-r}^{i\infty}f_E(\tau)\,d\tau\Big)\in\mathbb{R},\qquad [r]^+:=\lambda^+(r)/\Omega_E^+ .$$

`[THEOREM]` (Manin 1972, *Parabolic points and zeta functions of modular curves*, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR 36 (1972) 19–66; Drinfeld) $[r]^+\in\mathbb{Q}$ for all $r$, with bounded denominators; $[0]^+=L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+$. (Convention as in Stein–Wuthrich, Math. Comp. 82 (2013), §3, DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-2012-02649-4, whose text we fetched.)

`[THEOREM]` (Manin constant; survey Agashe–Ribet–Stein, Pure Appl. Math. Q. 2 (2006) 617–636, DOI 10.4310/pamq.2006.v2.n2.a11) $c_\pi\in\mathbb{Z}$ (Edixhoven, *On the Manin constants of modular elliptic curves*, 1991, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4612-0457-2_3), and a prime $\ell\mid c_\pi$ satisfies $\ell^2\mid 4N$ (Mazur). Hence for a **good prime $p\ge3$ the Manin constant is a $p$-adic unit** and plays no role in any $p$-adic statement below. For semistable $E$ and optimal $\pi$, $c_\pi=1$ (Česnavičius, Compos. Math. 154 (2018) 1889–1920, DOI 10.1112/S0010437X18007273 — abstract fetched; the precise scope of that theorem beyond the semistable case is `[unverified]` here and irrelevant for good $p\ge 3$).

*Dependence on the period.* Replacing $\Omega_E^+$ by any $\Omega\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\Omega_E^+$ (e.g. the period of $f_E$ or of a non-optimal curve in the isogeny class) multiplies $L_p(E,T)$ by a constant in $\mathbb{Q}^\times$. This does not affect $r_p$, nor any statement in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, but it affects the $\mu$-invariant and hence the *integral* form of the main conjecture; the $p$-adic BSD formula (§3.4) is stated with the Néron period $\Omega_E^+$ and the Néron differential.

### 1.2 The Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer $p$-adic $L$-function (good ordinary $p$)

Assume $p\nmid N$ and $p\nmid a_p$ (good ordinary). Let $\alpha,\beta\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be the roots of $X^2-a_pX+p$ with $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ the unit root; $\beta=p/\alpha$.

**Definition 1.2 (the measure).** For $k\ge1$ and $a\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$,
$$\mu_\alpha(a+p^k\mathbb{Z}_p)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}}\Big[\frac{a}{p^k}\Big]^+-\frac{1}{\alpha^{k+1}}\Big[\frac{a}{p^{k-1}}\Big]^+ .$$
The distribution relations of modular symbols make $\mu_\alpha$ a bounded $\mathbb{Z}_p$-valued measure on $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ up to a bounded denominator (Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer, Invent. Math. 25 (1974) 1–61, DOI 10.1007/BF01389997; Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum, Invent. Math. 84 (1986) 1–48, DOI 10.1007/BF01388731, §I.10).

Let $p$ be odd, $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times=\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p)$, $x\mapsto\langle x\rangle$ the projection to $1+p\mathbb{Z}_p$, $\omega$ the Teichmüller character. Let $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q})$, $\kappa:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\sim}1+p\mathbb{Z}_p$ the cyclotomic character, $\gamma$ a topological generator, $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ via $\gamma\mapsto1+T$. (For $p=2$ replace $1+p\mathbb{Z}_p$ by $1+4\mathbb{Z}_2$; we do not treat $p=2$ further.)

**Definition 1.3.**
$$L_p(E,T):=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times}(1+T)^{\log_p\langle x\rangle/\log_p\kappa(\gamma)}\,d\mu_\alpha(x)\ \in\ \Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p,\qquad L_p(E,s):=L_p\big(E,\kappa(\gamma)^{s-1}-1\big)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times}\langle x\rangle^{s-1}d\mu_\alpha(x).$$
$L_p(E,T)$ depends on $\gamma$; $L_p(E,s)$ does not. Put $r_p:=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_p(E,s)$.

**Theorem 1.4 (interpolation).** `[THEOREM]` (Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer 1974; MTT 1986 §I.14; as normalised in Stein–Wuthrich 2013 §3.2 and Greenberg, LNM 1716 (1999), Conj. 1.13 statement of the interpolation property, arXiv:math/9809206.)
1. $L_p(E,0)=\big(1-\tfrac1\alpha\big)^2\,\dfrac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E^+}$.
2. If $\chi:\Gamma\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p^\times$ has exact order $p^n\ge p$ (so $\chi$, viewed on $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ via $\langle\cdot\rangle$, is an even Dirichlet character of conductor $p^{n+1}$), then
$$L_p\big(E,\chi(\gamma)-1\big)=\frac{p^{\,n+1}}{\alpha^{\,n+1}\,G(\chi^{-1})}\cdot\frac{L(E,\chi^{-1},1)}{\Omega_E^+},$$
where $G(\chi^{-1})$ is the Gauss sum. (Other sources write the right side with $\chi$ in place of $\chi^{-1}$ and $\tau(\chi)$ for the Gauss sum; the two conventions differ by the substitution $T\mapsto(1+T)^{-1}-1$, which does not affect $r_p$.)

*The factor $(1-1/\alpha)^2$.* Since $\alpha\beta=p$ and $\alpha\equiv a_p\pmod p$, one has $v_p(1-\alpha^{-1})=v_p(\alpha-1)=v_p(1-a_p)$, and $\#\tilde E(\mathbb{F}_p)=p+1-a_p=(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)$ with $1-\beta$ a unit; hence
$$v_p\big((1-\alpha^{-1})^2\big)=2\,v_p\big(\#\tilde E(\mathbb{F}_p)\big),\tag{1.1}$$
the factor is non-zero always (for good $p$, $\alpha=1$ would force $a_p=p+1$, contradicting $|a_p|\le2\sqrt p$), and it is a $p$-adic unit iff $p$ is *non-anomalous* ($a_p\not\equiv1\bmod p$). Identity (1.1) is what matches the factor $(\#\tilde E(\mathbb{F}_p)[p^\infty])^2$ of Greenberg's algebraic leading-term formula (Theorem 3.7(3)) with the factor $\varepsilon_p=(1-\alpha^{-1})^2$ of the analytic one (Conjecture 3.9).

**Theorem 1.5 (functional equation and parity of $r_p$).** `[THEOREM]` (MTT 1986 §I.17; stated in Greenberg 1999, §1, whose text we fetched: $L_p(E,2-s)=w_E\,\langle N\rangle^{s-1}L_p(E,s)$ for $s\in\mathbb{Z}_p$, $w_E$ the root number of $L(E,s)$, $\langle N\rangle$ the projection of $N$ to $1+p\mathbb{Z}_p$.) Consequently
$$(-1)^{r_p}=w_E=(-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad r_p\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod 2 .\tag{1.2}$$
*Proof of (1.2).* Write $L_p(E,1+\varepsilon)=c\,\varepsilon^{r_p}+O(\varepsilon^{r_p+1})$ with $c\ne0$. The functional equation at $s=1-\varepsilon$ gives $c(-\varepsilon)^{r_p}+\dots=w_E\langle N\rangle^{-\varepsilon}(c\varepsilon^{r_p}+\dots)$, and $\langle N\rangle^{-\varepsilon}=1+O(\varepsilon)$; comparing leading terms, $(-1)^{r_p}=w_E$. The complex functional equation gives $(-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}}=w_E$. $\square$

**Integrality.** `[THEOREM]` If $p$ is odd and $E[p]$ is irreducible as a $G_\mathbb{Q}$-module, then $L_p(E,T)\in\Lambda$ (Greenberg–Vatsal, Invent. Math. 142 (2000) 17–63, DOI 10.1007/s002220000080, §3, via $p$-integrality of $[r]^+$; cited as "[20, Prop. 3.1]" in Burungale–Castella–Skinner 2025 whose text we fetched). If $E$ has a rational $p$-isogeny, $L_p(E,T)$ may have a negative $\mu$-invariant unless the period is renormalised (Greenberg–Vatsal §3; Wuthrich, Doc. Math. 19 (2014) 381–402, DOI 10.4171/dm/450). Example (Greenberg 1999 §1, text fetched): the three curves of conductor 11 at $p=5$ have $\mu\in\{0,1,2\}$.

### 1.3 Supersingular and multiplicative $p$

**Supersingular $p$ (Amice–Vélu, Vishik; Pollack; Kobayashi; Sprung).** If $p\nmid N$ and $p\mid a_p$, both roots $\alpha,\beta$ of $X^2-a_pX+p$ have $v_p=\tfrac12$ (Newton polygon; for $p\ge5$ the Hasse bound forces $a_p=0$), hence $\alpha,\beta\notin\mathbb{Q}_p$. Definition 1.2 still makes sense for either root and gives a distribution $\mu_\alpha$ of order $<1$ (not a measure); $L_{p,\alpha}(E,T)\in\mathcal H_{\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)}$ (power series with $O(\log^{1/2})$-growth; MTT §I.13, Kato 2004 §16). `[THEOREM]` (Pollack, Duke Math. J. 118 (2003) 523–558, DOI 10.1215/S0012-7094-03-11835-9) If $a_p=0$ there are $L_p^\pm(E,T)\in\Lambda$ and explicit half-logarithms $\log_p^\pm(T)$ with
$$L_{p,\alpha}(E,T)=\log_p^+(T)\,L_p^+(E,T)+\alpha\,\log_p^-(T)\,L_p^-(E,T),$$
and $L_p^\pm$ interpolate the twists $L(E,\chi,1)$ for $\chi$ of conductor $p^{n+1}$ with $n$ even ($+$) resp. odd ($-$), with the factors $e^\pm_p(\zeta)$ written out in Burungale–Skinner–Tian–Wan, arXiv:2409.01350, §1.1.1 (text fetched); at the trivial character $L_p^+(E,0)=2\,L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+$ and $L_p^-(E,0)=(p-1)L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+$. `[THEOREM]` (Kobayashi, Invent. Math. 152 (2003) 1–36, DOI 10.1007/s00222-002-0265-4) For $a_p=0$, $p>2$, there are signed Selmer groups $\operatorname{Sel}^\pm(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)\subset\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$, defined by the local conditions $E^\pm(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,p})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ at $p$ (trace-compatibility conditions on the formal group), whose Pontryagin duals $X^\pm_\infty$ are $\Lambda$-torsion, with $\operatorname{char}(X^\pm_\infty)\mid L^\pm_p(E,T)$ (via Kato). `[THEOREM]` (Sprung, J. Number Theory 132 (2012) 1483–1506, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2011.11.003; Adv. Math. 449 (2024) 109741, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2024.109741) For $a_p\neq0$ (so $p\in\{2,3\}$) there is an analogous pair $L_p^\sharp,L_p^\flat\in\Lambda$, Selmer groups $\operatorname{Sel}^{\sharp/\flat}$, and Kato's divisibility. `[CONJECTURE]` (Kobayashi's main conjecture) $\operatorname{char}(X^\pm_\infty)=(L^\pm_p(E,T))$. The $\pm$ $p$-adic $L$-functions are elements of $\Lambda$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_{p}^{\pm}(E,T)$ are the supersingular analogues of $r_p$; Kato's Theorem 18.4 (below, Theorem 2.1(4)) uses $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_{p,\alpha}$ directly.

**Multiplicative $p$.** If $p\,\|\,N$ put $\alpha=a_p\in\{\pm1\}$ and $\mu_\alpha(a+p^k\mathbb{Z}_p)=\alpha^{-k}[a/p^k]^+$. Then $L_p(E,0)=(1-\alpha^{-1})\,L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+$, which is $2L(E,1)/\Omega_E^+$ if $a_p=-1$ (non-split) and $\equiv0$ if $a_p=+1$ (split multiplicative: the *exceptional zero*, §3.5).

### 1.4 The Iwasawa module $X_\infty$ and the structure theorem

Let $\mathbb{Q}_n\subset\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ be the layers, $\Gamma_n=\Gamma^{p^n}$. Define
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)=\varinjlim_n\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_n),\qquad X_\infty:=\operatorname{Hom}\big(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty),\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\big),$$
a finitely generated $\Lambda$-module (Mazur 1972). Recall $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F)=\ker\big(H^1(F,E[p^\infty])\to\prod_v H^1(F_v,E)[p^\infty]\big)$.

`[THEOREM]` (structure theorem for $\Lambda$-modules; Iwasawa, Serre; e.g. Washington, *Introduction to cyclotomic fields*, Thm. 13.12) A finitely generated torsion $\Lambda$-module $X$ admits a $\Lambda$-homomorphism with finite kernel and cokernel (a *pseudo-isomorphism*)
$$X\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(f_i(T)^{e_i})\ \oplus\ \bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(p^{m_j}),$$
with $f_i$ irreducible distinguished polynomials. The characteristic ideal is $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X)=\big(p^{\mu}\prod_i f_i^{e_i}\big)$, $\mu=\sum m_j$, $\lambda=\sum e_i\deg f_i$. We write $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)=(f_E^{\mathrm{alg}}(T))$ when $X_\infty$ is torsion.

**Definition 1.6 (semisimplicity at $T=0$).** A torsion $\Lambda$-module $X$ is *semisimple at $T=0$* if $T$ acts semisimply on the $T$-primary part of $X\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, i.e. if no $f_i^{e_i}$ with $f_i(0)=0$ has $e_i\ge2$; equivalently $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X/TX$ (Lemma 3.1). The *semisimplicity defect* is $\delta_{ss}(X):=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X)-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X/TX\ \ge0$.

### 1.5 Mazur's control theorem

**Theorem 1.7.** `[THEOREM]` (Mazur, Invent. Math. 18 (1972) 183–266, DOI 10.1007/BF01389815; full proof for elliptic curves in Greenberg, LNM 1716 (1999), Thm. 1.2, text fetched.) Let $E/F$ be an elliptic curve over a number field with good ordinary reduction at all primes of $F$ above $p$, $F_\infty/F$ the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension. Then the restriction maps
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F_n)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F_\infty)^{\Gamma_n}$$
have finite kernel and cokernel, of order bounded independently of $n$. (Greenberg proves this in his §§2–3; his Thm. 1.14 gives the invariance of $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)$ under $\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}$ for good ordinary or multiplicative reduction whenever $X_\infty$ is torsion, and his Conj. 1.6/§§2–3 treat the modifications needed for multiplicative reduction at $p$.)

Dually: the natural map $X_\infty/TX_\infty\to\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})^\vee$ has finite kernel and cokernel, so
$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=r_{\mathrm{alg}}+\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty].\tag{1.3}$$

### 1.6 Kato's zeta element and the Coleman map (used in §4)

Let $T=T_pE$, $V=V_pE$. Following Kato (Astérisque 295 (2004) 117–290, numdam AST_2004__295__117_0, text fetched), $H^q(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],\cdot)$ denotes étale cohomology of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ with coefficients in $j_*$ of the Galois module (Kato §8.2) — so classes in $H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)$ are exactly the classes in $H^1(\mathbb{Q},T)$ unramified at every $\ell\ne p$. Put $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T):=\varprojlim_nH^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p,\zeta_{p^n}],T)$ (Kato's $\mathbf H^1$), a $\Lambda$-module. `[THEOREM]` (Kato Thm. 12.4, text fetched) $H^2_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)$ is $\Lambda$-torsion; $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)$ is $\Lambda$-torsion-free and $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ is free of rank one over $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ (no hypothesis); if $p\ne2$ and $E[p]$ is irreducible then $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)$ is free of rank one over $\Lambda$. Kato constructs the *zeta element* $z_\infty=\mathbf z_\gamma\in H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ (integral under (irr), Thm. 12.5(4)). Its specialisation $z_0:=z_\infty\bmod T\in H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)$ is the Beilinson–Kato element $z_E$ of BSTW §1.1.3. `[THEOREM]` (Kato Thm. 12.5(1), explicit reciprocity law; Thm. 16.6) with $\exp^*:H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)\to\operatorname{Fil}^0D_{dR}(V)=\mathbb{Q}_p\,\omega_E$ the Bloch–Kato dual exponential,
$$\exp^*(\operatorname{loc}_p z_0)=c_E\cdot\frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E^+}\,\omega_E,\qquad c_E\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\text{ explicit (an Euler factor at } p \text{ times a period ratio)},$$
so $\operatorname{loc}_pz_0\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)=\ker\exp^*=E(\mathbb{Q}_p)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\iff L(E,1)=0$ (BSTW §1.1.3 states exactly this consequence of Kato Thm. 12.5). In the good ordinary case there is a $\Lambda$-linear Coleman map $\operatorname{Col}:H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,T)\to\Lambda$ (projection to the unit-root quotient followed by Perrin-Riou's/Coleman's map) with
$$\operatorname{Col}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_\infty)=u\cdot L_p(E,T),\qquad u\in(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p)^\times \text{ (a unit in }\Lambda^\times\text{ under (irr))},$$
(Kato §16–17; summarised as "$\operatorname{Col}(Z_{\mathrm{Kato}})=(\mathscr L(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty))$" in Skinner's 2018 AWS notes §5.1.2, text fetched). The specialisation of $\operatorname{Col}$ at the trivial character is $\operatorname{Col}_0=c\cdot\exp^*_{\omega}$ on $H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$ with $c=(1-\alpha^{-1})(1-\beta p^{-1})^{-1}\cdot(\text{period ratio})\neq0$ (Perrin-Riou's interpolation of the big logarithm at the trivial character, Invent. Math. 115 (1994) 81–149, DOI 10.1007/BF01231755); the exact constant is irrelevant below, only $c\ne0$ is used, which follows from $\alpha\neq1$.

## 2. Theorems: what is proved about the main conjectures (status 2026-09-11)

Standing notation: $\bar\rho=\bar\rho_{E,p}:G_\mathbb{Q}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}_p}E[p]$, $\rho=\rho_{E,p}:G_\mathbb{Q}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}T$. Named hypotheses (as in Skinner–Urban and Burungale–Castella–Skinner):

* $(\mathrm{irr}_\mathbb{Q})$: $E[p]$ is an irreducible $G_\mathbb{Q}$-module.
* $(\mathrm{sur}_\mathbb{Q})$: $\bar\rho$ is surjective onto $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$.
* $(\mathrm{ram})$: there exists a prime $\ell\,\|\,N$, $\ell\ne p$, such that $E[p]$ is ramified at $\ell$ (for $\ell\|N$ this means $p\nmid\operatorname{ord}_\ell(\Delta_E)$).
* $(\mathrm{im})$: there exists $\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})}$ with $T/(\sigma-1)T\cong\mathbb{Z}_p$. (Implied by $(\mathrm{ram})$: take $\sigma$ in the inertia group at $\ell$, which acts on $T$ through a non-trivial unipotent matrix, and $\ell\neq p$ is unramified in $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})$. Implied by "$\rho(G_\mathbb{Q})\supseteq\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$", hence, for $p\ge5$, by $(\mathrm{sur}_\mathbb{Q})$ [Serre]. For non-CM $E$ it holds for all large $p$ by Serre's open image theorem.)

### 2.1 Kato

**Theorem 2.1 (Kato 2004).** `[THEOREM]` Kato, *$p$-adic Hodge theory and values of zeta functions of modular forms*, Astérisque 295 (2004) 117–290 (text fetched from numdam; theorem numbers below refer to it).
1. (Thm. 17.4(1)) If $E$ has good ordinary reduction at $p$ (any $p$), then $X_\infty$ is a torsion $\Lambda$-module. (Input: Rohrlich, Invent. 5. (Thm. 14.2) If $L(E,\chi,1)\ne0$ for a Dirichlet character $\chi$, then the $\chi$-part of $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\chi))$ is finite; in particular $L(E,1)\ne0\Rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ finite, for every $p$.

### 2.5 Supersingular primes: the 2024 change of status

**Theorem 2.6.** `[THEOREM]` (Kobayashi 2003, Thm. 1.3 as quoted in Pollack–Rubin's introduction and arXiv:2409.18021 §1, texts fetched) For $p>2$ good supersingular with $a_p=0$: $X_\infty^\pm$ is $\Lambda$-torsion and $(L^\pm_p(E,T))\subseteq\operatorname{char}(X^\pm_\infty)$ (i.e. $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty^\pm)\mid L_p^\pm$), integrally under the Euler-system hypothesis $(\mathrm{im})$. ## 3. Consequences at $T=0$: the chain $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}\le\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)\le r_p$

In this section $p$ is a good ordinary prime unless said otherwise. All proofs are on the page.

### 3.1 Λ-module lemmas

**Lemma 3.1.** Let $X$ be a finitely generated torsion $\Lambda$-module with $\operatorname{char}(X)=(f)$. Then $X/TX$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module and
$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(X/TX)\ \le\ \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f,$$
with equality if and only if $X$ is semisimple at $T=0$ (Definition 1.6). More precisely, if $X$ is pseudo-isomorphic to $Y=\bigoplus_i\Lambda/(f_i^{e_i})\oplus\bigoplus_j\Lambda/(p^{m_j})$, then $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(X/TX)=\#\{i: f_i=T\}$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f=\sum_{i:\,f_i=T}e_i$, so $\delta_{ss}(X)=\sum_{i:\,f_i=T}(e_i-1)$.

*Proof.* (a) *Elementary modules.* $\Lambda/(g)\otimes_\Lambda\Lambda/T=\mathbb{Z}_p/(g(0))$, which is $\mathbb{Z}_p$ if $g(0)=0$ and finite otherwise; $\Lambda/(p^m)\otimes\Lambda/T=\mathbb{Z}/p^m$. For $g=f_i^{e_i}$ with $f_i$ irreducible distinguished, $g(0)=0\iff f_i=T$, and then $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}g=e_i\ge1$. Summing gives the formulas for $Y$.

(b) *Pseudo-isomorphism invariance.* Let $\phi:X\to Y$ have finite kernel $K$ and cokernel $C$, and let $I=\phi(X)$. From $0\to K\to X\to I\to0$ we get the exact sequence $K/TK\to X/TX\to I/TI\to0$, so $X/TX\to I/TI$ is surjective with finite kernel. From $0\to I\to Y\to C\to0$ we get $\operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(C,\Lambda/T)=C[T]\to I/TI\to Y/TY\to C/TC\to0$, so $I/TI\to Y/TY$ has finite kernel and cokernel. Hence $X/TX\to Y/TY$ has finite kernel and cokernel and $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X/TX=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}Y/TY$; $\operatorname{char}$ is invariant under pseudo-isomorphism by definition. Combine with (a). $\square$

**Lemma 3.2.** Let $g,h\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ be non-zero with $g\mid h$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$. Then $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}g\le\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}h$.

*Proof.* $h=gq$ with $q\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p=\bigcup_kp^{-k}\Lambda$, a power series with bounded denominators; $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}$ is additive and $\ge0$ on non-zero elements. $\square$

### 3.2 The chain

**Proposition 3.3 (Kummer).** `[THEOREM]` (standard) $0\to E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\to\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]\to0$ is exact, so $\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=r_{\mathrm{alg}}+\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$, with equality $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}$ iff $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite ($\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is a cofinitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module, hence $\cong(\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)^{c}\oplus(\text{finite})$, finite iff $c=0$).

**Proposition 3.4.** `[THEOREM]` Let $p$ be a prime of good ordinary reduction. Then
$$r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ \le\ \operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\ =\ \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty\ \le\ \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E(T)\ \le\ r_p,$$
and, writing $\delta_{ss}=\delta_{ss}(X_\infty)$ and $c_{\mathrm{Sha}}=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$,
$$r_p-r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ =\ c_{\mathrm{Sha}}+\delta_{ss}+\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\big(L_p/f_E^{\mathrm{alg}}\big),\tag{3.1}$$
where the last term is $\ge0$ and vanishes whenever the main conjecture holds in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Theorem 2.9). In particular, under the main conjecture,
$$r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}\iff\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]\text{ is finite and }X_\infty\text{ is semisimple at }T=0 .\tag{3.2}$$

*Proof.* First inequality and $c_{\mathrm{Sha}}$: Proposition 3.3. Equality in the middle: by Theorem 2.1(1) $X_\infty$ is torsion, so $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)=(f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E)$ is defined; the dual of the control theorem (1.3) gives $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}X_\infty/TX_\infty=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$. Second inequality and $\delta_{ss}$: Lemma 3.1. Last inequality: Theorem 2.1(2) says $f_E^{\mathrm{alg}}\mid L_p(E,T)$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, and $L_p\ne0$ by Rohrlich's theorem (Theorem 1.4(2) with Rohrlich's non-vanishing), so Lemma 3.2 applies. (3.1) is the bookkeeping of the three defects; (3.2) follows. $\square$

*Remark 3.5.* Kato's Theorem 2.1(4) gives $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}\le r_p$ also for supersingular $p$ (with $L_{p,\alpha}$) and multiplicative $p$ (with the shift by one in the split case), where $X_\infty$ is not torsion (supersingular) or the local condition changes. For supersingular $p$ the role of $f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E$ is played by $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty^\pm)$ and the analogue of Proposition 3.4 holds with Kobayashi's control theorem (Kobayashi 2003, §9) and $L_p^\pm$ in place of $L_p$; we do not reproduce the proof.

### 3.3 The equality conditions: $p$-adic heights (Schneider, Perrin-Riou)

Let $h_p=\hat h_p:E(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{Q}_p$ be the canonical cyclotomic $p$-adic height (good ordinary $p$): with $\sigma_p\in\mathbb{Z}_p[[t]]$ the Mazur–Tate canonical $p$-adic sigma function (Mazur–Tate, Duke Math. J. 62 (1991) 663–688) and $P\in E(\mathbb{Q})$ with good reduction everywhere and $P\in\hat E(p\mathbb{Z}_p)$, $\hat h_p(P)=2\log_p\big(e(P)/\sigma_p(P)\big)$, $e(P)^2$ the denominator of $x(P)$, extended to $E(\mathbb{Q})$ by $\hat h_p(P)=m^{-2}\hat h_p(mP)$ (normalisation of Stein–Wuthrich §4.1, which is $2\times$ that of Mazur–Stein–Tate, Doc. Math. Extra Vol. (2006) 577–614, DOI 10.4171/dms/4/17). It is a quadratic form; $\langle P,Q\rangle_p=\tfrac12(\hat h_p(P+Q)-\hat h_p(P)-\hat h_p(Q))$ is the height pairing; $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})=\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_p)$ on a basis of $E(\mathbb{Q})/\mathrm{tors}$ and $\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma:=\operatorname{Reg}_p/(\log_p\kappa(\gamma))^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$. This pairing coincides with Schneider's (Invent. Math. 69 (1982) 401–409, DOI 10.1007/BF01389362; 79 (1985) 329–374, DOI 10.1007/BF01388978) and Perrin-Riou's height attached to the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension and the unit-root splitting of the Hodge filtration; it extends to the compact Selmer group $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)=\big(\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Sel}_{p^n}(E/\mathbb{Q})\big)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\supseteq E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Nekovář, *Selmer complexes*, Astérisque 310 (2006), DOI 10.24033/ast.717; Perrin-Riou 1992), with equality iff $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite.

**Conjecture 3.6 (Schneider).** `[CONJECTURE]` (Schneider 1982, as formulated in Stein–Wuthrich Conj. 4.1.) The pairing $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_p$ is non-degenerate on $E(\mathbb{Q})/\mathrm{tors}$, i.e. $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\ne0$. The strong form: non-degenerate on $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$.

**Theorem 3.7 (Schneider, Perrin-Riou; Jones for multiplicative $p$).** `[THEOREM]` (Stein–Wuthrich 2013, Thm. 6.1, text fetched, attribute the statement to Schneider, Invent. Math. 79 (1985), and Perrin-Riou, Invent. Math. 70 (1982) 369–398 ("Descente infinie et hauteur $p$-adique sur les courbes elliptiques à multiplication complexe"), with the multiplicative case due to Jones, Duke Math. J. 59 (1989) 399–420; the general framework for $p$-adic representations is Perrin-Riou, Invent. Math. 109 (1992) 137–185 and Ann. Inst. Fourier 43 (1993) 945–995, to which Kato attributes the argument of his Thm. 18.4. We use the statement exactly as formulated by Stein–Wuthrich.) Let $p$ be a prime of good ordinary or multiplicative reduction, $p>2$. Then:
1. $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E\ge r_{\mathrm{alg}}$;
2. $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ **if and only if** $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_p$ is non-degenerate on $E(\mathbb{Q})/\mathrm{tors}$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite;
3. in that case the leading coefficient $f^{\mathrm{alg},*}_E(0)$ of $f_E^{\mathrm{alg}}$ at $T=0$ satisfies
$$v_p\big(f^{\mathrm{alg},*}_E(0)\big)=v_p\Big(\varepsilon_p\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid N}c_\ell\cdot\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]\cdot\frac{\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma(E/\mathbb{Q})}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty])^2}\Big),$$
   where $\varepsilon_p=(1-\alpha^{-1})^2$ for good ordinary $p$ (so $v_p(\varepsilon_p)=2v_p(\#\tilde E(\mathbb{F}_p))$ by (1.1)), $\varepsilon_p=2$ for non-split multiplicative $p$, and $\varepsilon_p$ is replaced by $\mathscr L_p(E)/\log_p\kappa(\gamma)$ for split multiplicative $p$ (with the corrected height of §3.5 and Stein–Wuthrich §4.2). For $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=0$ and good ordinary $p$ this is Greenberg 1999, Thm. 4.1 (text fetched): $f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E(0)\sim\prod_\ell c_\ell^{(p)}\cdot|\tilde E(\mathbb{F}_p)_p|^2\cdot|\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})|/|E(\mathbb{Q})_p|^2$ when $\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite.

**Corollary 3.8 (dictionary).** `[THEOREM]` For good ordinary $p>2$: $X_\infty$ is semisimple at $T=0$ **and** $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite $\iff$ $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_p$ is non-degenerate on $E(\mathbb{Q})/\mathrm{tors}$ and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite. (Combine Lemma 3.1, (1.3) and Theorem 3.7(2): both sides say $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$.) The finer statement "semisimple at $T=0\iff\langle\ ,\ \rangle_p$ non-degenerate on $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$" (no finiteness assumption) is Perrin-Riou's `[THEOREM]` (Invent. Math. 109 (1992)) as we understand it from the secondary sources fetched; since we could not access the 1992 paper itself, we mark this finer form `[unverified]` and do **not** use it below. What we use is only Theorem 3.7 as stated.

*What happens when the height degenerates.* `[THEOREM]` (Bertolini–Darmon, Duke Math. J. 76 (1994), DOI 10.1215/S0012-7094-94-07604-7; Amer. J. Math. 117 (1995) 1517–1554, DOI 10.2307/2375029.) There is a filtration of $E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ by "universal norms" with *derived* $p$-adic height pairings $h^{(k)}$ on the graded pieces, and the leading coefficient of $f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E$ is always given by a formula of the shape of Theorem 3.7(3) with $\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma$ replaced by a product of derived regulators; the ordinary height is $h^{(1)}$. Thus $\delta_{ss}>0$ means exactly that some derived height of order $\ge2$ is needed. (Recent: Castella–Hsu–Kundu–Lee, Trans. AMS Ser. B 12 (2025) 748–788, DOI 10.1090/btran/227, for the BDP $p$-adic $L$-function.)

### 3.4 $p$-adic BSD (Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum) and what is a theorem

**Conjecture 3.9 (MTT, good ordinary and non-split multiplicative $p$).** `[CONJECTURE]` (MTT 1986 §II; normalised as in Stein–Wuthrich Conj. 5.1, text fetched.)
1. $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ (and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite);
2. the leading coefficient $L_p^*(E,0):=\lim_{T\to0}L_p(E,T)/T^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$ satisfies
$$L_p^*(E,0)=\varepsilon_p\cdot\frac{\prod_\ell c_\ell\cdot\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}})^2}\cdot\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma(E/\mathbb{Q}),\qquad \varepsilon_p=\begin{cases}(1-\alpha^{-1})^2&p\text{ good ordinary},\\ 1-\alpha^{-1}=2&p\text{ non-split multiplicative}.\end{cases}$$
   (Exact equality, not merely up to a unit; for $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=0$ this is the interpolation formula plus BSD.)
3. (split multiplicative $p$) $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}+1$ and $L_p^*(E,0)=\dfrac{\mathscr L_p(E)}{\log_p\kappa(\gamma)}\cdot\dfrac{\prod_\ell c_\ell\,\#\operatorname{Sha}}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}})^2}\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma$, $\mathscr L_p(E)=\log_p(q_E)/\operatorname{ord}_p(q_E)$ the $\mathscr L$-invariant.
   (Supersingular version: Bernardi–Perrin-Riou, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 317 (1993) 227–232; Perrin-Riou, Experiment. Math. 12 (2003) 155–186; $D_{dR}$-valued regulator, Stein–Wuthrich Conj. 5.2.)

**Theorem 3.10 (what is a theorem).** `[THEOREM]` Let $p>2$ be a prime of good ordinary reduction. Assume
  (MC) $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=(L_p(E,T))$ in $\Lambda$ (Theorem 2.9, rows (b),(d),(e), or (a) with $(\mathrm{im})$);
  (ND) $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\ne0$ (Conjecture 3.6);
  (Sha) $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite.
Then $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ and
$$L_p^*(E,0)\ \sim\ \varepsilon_p\cdot\frac{\prod_\ell c_\ell\cdot\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty])^2}\cdot\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma(E/\mathbb{Q})\qquad(\sim:\ \text{equality up to }\mathbb{Z}_p^\times),$$
i.e. Conjecture 3.9(1)–(2) holds up to a $p$-adic unit. If (MC) is only known in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, the same holds up to $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$ (the orders of vanishing agree; the leading terms agree up to a power of $p$). Conversely, under (MC) alone, $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ *implies* (ND) and (Sha).

*Proof.* Under (MC), $L_p=u\,f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E$ with $u\in\Lambda^\times$, so $r_p=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E$ and $L_p^*(E,0)=u(0)f^{\mathrm{alg},*}_E(0)$ with $u(0)\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. (ND)+(Sha) and Theorem 3.7(2) give $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$, and Theorem 3.7(3) gives the valuation of $f^{\mathrm{alg},*}_E(0)$, which is exactly that of the right-hand side. If (MC) holds only in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$, then $u\in(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p)^\times=p^{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda^\times$ (if $g,h\in\Lambda$, $p\nmid g$, $gh=p^m$ then $m=0$ since $p$ is prime in the UFD $\Lambda$), which gives the weaker conclusion. The converse is Theorem 3.7(2) read backwards. $\square$

*Attribution.* Theorem 3.10 is the standard consequence "main conjecture $\Rightarrow$ $p$-adic BSD up to a unit", due to Schneider (1985) and Perrin-Riou (1992, 1993) for the algebraic side and combined with the main conjecture wherever the latter is known; it is stated in this form (as an equivalence of the two BSD formulas in rank 1) in Kobayashi's 2013 work (Oxford 2012 slides fetched: "Perrin-Riou and Schneider's result on the $p$-adic BSD up to a $p$-adic unit"). We have not found in the literature an *exact* (not up-to-unit) proof of Conjecture 3.9(2) in any case of positive rank; the up-to-unit form is the best available because $\operatorname{char}$ is only defined up to $\Lambda^\times$.

### 3.5 The exceptional zero (split multiplicative $p$)

`[THEOREM]` (Greenberg–Stevens, Invent. Math. 111 (1993) 407–447, DOI 10.1007/BF01231294.) If $E$ has split multiplicative reduction at $p$ then $L_p(E,1)=0$ and
$$\frac{d}{ds}L_p(E,s)\Big|_{s=1}=\mathscr L_p(E)\cdot\frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E^+},\qquad\mathscr L_p(E)=\frac{\log_p q_E}{\operatorname{ord}_pq_E},$$
$q_E\in p\mathbb{Z}_p$ the Tate period. (Elementary proof: Kobayashi, Doc. Math. Extra Vol. (2006) 567–575, DOI 10.4171/dms/4/16.)

`[THEOREM]` $\mathscr L_p(E)\ne0$. *Proof.* $\log_p(q_E)=0$ iff $q_E=p^{v}\zeta$ with $\zeta$ a root of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Iwasawa's branch $\log_pp=0$; $\ker(\log_p|_{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times})=\mu(\mathbb{Q}_p)$), in which case $q_E$ is algebraic. But $j(E)=J(q_E)$ is rational, and by the $p$-adic Mahler–Manin theorem (Barré-Sirieix–Diaz–Gramain–Philibert, Invent. Math. 124 (1996) 1–9, DOI 10.1007/s002220050044: for $q\in\mathbb{C}_p$, $0<|q|_p<1$ algebraic, $J(q)$ is transcendental) $q_E$ is transcendental. $\square$

Hence for split multiplicative $p$: $r_p\ge1$ always, and $r_p=1\iff L(E,1)\ne0\iff r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$. Kato's Theorem 2.1(4) gives $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}\le r_p-1$, consistent with Conjecture 3.9(3). The Iwasawa-theoretic formulation is Skinner 2016 and Greenberg's Theorem 1.14/§§2–3 (Greenberg Selmer group with the trivial-quotient local condition).

## 4. The transfer problem: $r_p$ versus $r_{\mathrm{an}}$

### 4.1 Statement and the trivial cases

**Conjecture 4.1 ($\mathrm{T}(E,p)$, transfer).** `[CONJECTURE]` (This is the conjunction of Conjecture 3.9(1) and BSD(rank); it is stated as such in MTT 1986 and as "$\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_p$" in Kobayashi's 2012 Oxford lecture, slides fetched.) Let $p$ be a prime of good reduction and $\alpha$ an admissible root ($\operatorname{ord}_p\alpha<1$). Then
$$\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_{p,\alpha}(E,T)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\qquad(\text{i.e. }r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}).$$
For split multiplicative $p$ the statement is $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}+1$; for non-split multiplicative $p$, $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$.

**Theorem 4.2 (what is known about $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$).** Let $p$ be an odd prime of good reduction, $\alpha$ admissible.
1. `[THEOREM]` $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0\iff r_p=0$. *Proof.* Theorem 1.4(1) (or its supersingular analogue, Kato §16 / PARI's normalisation $(1-\alpha^{-1})^{-2}L_{p,\alpha}(E,\chi_0)=L(E,1)/\Omega$, documentation fetched) and $\alpha\ne1$. $\square$ For split multiplicative $p$: $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0\iff r_p=1$ (§3.5, using $\mathscr L_p(E)\ne0$); for non-split multiplicative $p$: $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0\iff r_p=0$.
2. `[THEOREM]` $r_p\equiv r_{\mathrm{an}}\pmod2$ (Theorem 1.5; for supersingular $p$ the same argument applies to $L_{p,\alpha}$, whose functional equation is in MTT §I.17). Hence $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2\Rightarrow r_p\ge2$ and $r_p\ge2\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$.
3. `[THEOREM]` ($p$-converse direction) Suppose $r_p=1$. Then $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=1$; if moreover one of the following holds, then $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$:
   (a) $E$ non-CM, $p>3$ good ordinary, $(\mathrm{irr}_\mathbb{Q})$ [Burungale–Castella–Skinner 2025, consequence of Thm. 1.2.2(a) stated in §1.2 there; earlier: Skinner, Ann. of Math. 191 (2020), DOI 10.4007/annals.2020.191.2.1, and W. Zhang, Camb. J. Math. 2 (2014) 191–253, DOI 10.4310/cjm.2014.v2.n2.a2, under $(\mathrm{sur}_\mathbb{Q})$ and ramification hypotheses; Burungale–Skinner–Tian–Wan Thm. 1.10 under $(\mathrm{sur}_\mathbb{Q})+(\mathrm{ram})$];
   (b) $E$ CM, $p$ good ordinary [Burungale–Tian, Invent. Math. 220 (2020) 211–253, DOI 10.1007/s00222-019-00929-7];
   (c) $E$ semistable, $p>2$ supersingular ($a_3=0$ and $(\mathrm{ram})$ if $p=3$), and $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite [BSTW 2024 Thm. 12.3, preprint; the same theorem also gives the ordinary case $p\nmid a_p$, $(\mathrm{irr}_\mathbb{Q})$, under finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$];
   (d) $p$ Eisenstein with the hypotheses of Castella–Grossi–Lee–Skinner 2022 / Keller–Yin 2024.
   *Proof.* Kato's Theorem 2.1(4) gives $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}\le r_p=1$. The $p$-parity theorem `[THEOREM]` (Dokchitser–Dokchitser, Ann. of Math. 172 (2010) 567–596, DOI 10.4007/annals.2010.172.567, Thm. 1.4, text fetched: for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and every $p$, $(-1)^{\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})}=w_E$) and (2) give $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}\equiv r_p\equiv1$, so $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}=1$; the cited $p$-converse theorems give $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$. $\square$
4. `[THEOREM]` (rank one, the height criterion) Suppose $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$, so $E(\mathbb{Q})$ has rank one (Gross–Zagier, Invent. Math. 84 (1986) 225–320; Kolyvagin) with generator $P$ modulo torsion, and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite. Let $p\nmid 2N$ be good ordinary. Then
$$r_p=1\iff\hat h_p(P)\neq0 .$$
   *Proof.* Choose an imaginary quadratic field $K$ with: every $\ell\mid N$ split in $K$ (Heegner hypothesis), $p$ split in $K$, $D_K$ odd, $D_K\ne-3$, and $L(E^K,1)\ne0$ for the quadratic twist $E^K$. Such $K$ exist: the root number of $E^K$ is $w_E\chi_K(-N)=-w_E=+1$ under the Heegner hypothesis (as $\chi_K(-1)=-1$, $\chi_K(N)=1$), and the non-vanishing theorem with prescribed local conditions at finitely many places `[THEOREM]` (Friedberg–Hoffstein, Ann. of Math. 142 (1995) 385–423, DOI 10.2307/2118638; earlier Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein, Invent. Math. 102 (1990) 543–618, and Murty–Murty, Ann. of Math. 133 (1991) 447–475; used in exactly this way in BSTW, proof of Thm. 12.3, text fetched) produces infinitely many such $K$. Then $L(E/K,s)=L(E,s)L(E^K,s)$ has a simple zero at $s=1$, and $\operatorname{rank}E^K(\mathbb{Q})=0$ (Kolyvagin, or Kato Thm. 2.1(5)), so $E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ has rank one, and the Heegner point $P_K\in E(K)$ is non-torsion by Gross–Zagier; hence $P_K=mP+(\text{torsion})$ with $m\ne0$. The $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula `[THEOREM]` (Perrin-Riou, Invent. Math. 89 (1987) 455–510, DOI 10.1007/BF01388982: $E/\mathbb{Q}$ good ordinary at $p$, $K$ with Heegner hypothesis, $(2N,D_K)=1$, $p$ split in $K$ — hypotheses as recorded in Disegni, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 22 (2023) 2199–2240, DOI 10.1017/S1474748021000608, introduction fetched, and in Kobayashi's slides; Disegni removes the splitting hypothesis) reads
$$\frac{d}{ds}L_p(E/K,\alpha,s)\Big|_{s=1}=u^{-2}\Big(1-\frac1\alpha\Big)^2\Big(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_K(p)\alpha}\Big)^2\,\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{p,K},$$
   where $L_p(E/K,\alpha,s):=L_p(E,\alpha,s)L_p(E^K,\alpha,s)$ (Kobayashi's construction of the two-variable function is the product of the one-variable function and its twist; MathSciNet review MR3020170 fetched), $u=\#\mathcal O_K^\times/2$, $\varepsilon_K$ the quadratic character, and $\langle\,,\rangle_{p,K}$ the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing of $E/K$, which restricted to $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is $[K:\mathbb{Q}]\langle\,,\rangle_p=2\langle\,,\rangle_p$ (functoriality of the height under base change — standard `[THEOREM]`, cf. Schneider 1982; the constant is immaterial). Since $r_p(E^K)=0$ by (1), $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_p(E/K,\alpha,s)=r_p(E)$, and the Euler factors are non-zero ($\alpha\ne\pm1$ for $p$ good). Hence $r_p(E)=1\iff L_p'(E/K,\alpha,1)\ne0\iff\hat h_p(P_K)\ne0\iff m^2\hat h_p(P)\ne0\iff\hat h_p(P)\ne0$. $\square$

   The same argument with Kobayashi's supersingular $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula `[THEOREM]` (Invent. Math. 191 (2013) 527–629, DOI 10.1007/s00222-012-0400-9; $p$ good supersingular, $(2N,D_K)=1$, Heegner hypothesis, $p$ split in $K$) gives, for good supersingular $p\nmid 2N$: $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_{p,\alpha}(E,s)=1\iff h_{p,\alpha}(P)\ne0$, where $h_{p,\alpha}$ is the $\alpha$-height (§5.2).

5. `[THEOREM]` (rank one: the cases where $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ is known) If $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ and either (i) $E$ has CM, $p$ good ordinary, or (ii) $p\nmid 2N$ is good supersingular, then $r_p=1$ (resp. $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_{p,\alpha}=1$ for both roots $\alpha$). *Proof.* By (4) it suffices to know $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$: for CM this is Bertrand's theorem (§5.3, Theorem 5.2); for supersingular $p$ it is Lemma 5.3 below (the $\alpha$-height of a non-torsion point is automatically non-zero because $\alpha\notin\mathbb{Q}_p$). This is the theorem announced in Kobayashi's 2012 lecture ("if the analytic rank is 1, the complex and $p$-adic BSD are equivalent" for CM or supersingular $p$). $\square$

6. `[THEOREM]` (rank one, ordinary non-CM: the exact obstruction) If $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$, $E$ non-CM, $p\nmid2N$ good ordinary, then $r_p=1$ **if and only if** Schneider's Conjecture 3.6 holds for $(E,p)$ (which here reads $\hat h_p(P)\neq0$). This is (4). No general theorem gives $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$ (§5). Under the main conjecture (Theorem 2.9), the same equivalence also follows from Theorem 3.7(2) and (3.2) without Heegner points.

**Theorem 4.3 (reduction of BSD(rank) to the transfer conjecture).** `[NEW]` *(assembly of Proposition 3.4, Corollary 3.8 and the main conjecture; we did not find this equivalence stated in the literature, and flag it for review.)* Let $p>2$ be a prime of good ordinary reduction such that $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)=(L_p(E,T))$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Theorem 2.9). Then
$$r_p-r_{\mathrm{an}}=(r_{\mathrm{alg}}-r_{\mathrm{an}})+\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]+\delta_{ss}(X_\infty),\tag{4.1}$$
and consequently:
1. If $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ holds, $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite and $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\ne0$, then BSD(rank) holds for $E$.
2. If BSD(rank) holds for $E$, then $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ holds **if and only if** $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is finite and $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\ne0$.
3. In particular, BSD(rank) for $E$ plus finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ imply: $\mathrm{T}(E,p)\iff$ Schneider$(E,p)$, for every good ordinary $p>2$ in Theorem 2.9. Conversely, if for **one** such $p$ one knows $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite and $\operatorname{Reg}_p\ne0$ (both computable in examples, §6), then BSD(rank) for $E$ is *equivalent* to $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$.

*Proof.* (4.1) is (3.1) with $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}(L_p/f^{\mathrm{alg}}_E)=0$ under the main conjecture. (1): the two hypotheses force $c_{\mathrm{Sha}}=0$ and, by Corollary 3.8, $\delta_{ss}=0$; then $\mathrm{T}$ gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$. (2): if $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ then $\mathrm{T}\iff c_{\mathrm{Sha}}+\delta_{ss}=0\iff(\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite and $X_\infty$ semisimple at $T=0)\iff(\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite and $\operatorname{Reg}_p\ne0)$ by Corollary 3.8. (3) restates (1),(2). $\square$

*Interpretation.* Everything on the right of (4.1) except $r_{\mathrm{alg}}-r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is a $p$-adic/Galois-cohomological quantity that Iwasawa theory controls, and is expected to vanish. The approach therefore isolates BSD(rank) as *exactly* the transfer statement $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ — a statement comparing two analytic functions, one complex and one $p$-adic, whose only known relation is Theorem 1.4.

### 4.2 The case $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$: lower bounds for $r_p$

**Proposition 4.4.** `[THEOREM]` If $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ then $r_p\ge2$. *Proof.* Theorem 4.2(1),(2). $\square$

**Proposition 4.5 (rank $\ge3$).** `[NEW]` *(An elementary corollary of Gross–Zagier, $p$-adic Gross–Zagier and the parity of $r_p$; we have not found it in the literature and flag it as folklore-level for review.)* Let $p\nmid2N$ be a prime of good ordinary reduction (or good supersingular, using Kobayashi's formula). If $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge3$, then $r_p\ge3$ (resp. $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_{p,\alpha}(E,s)\ge3$ for both roots).

*Proof.* Since $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is odd, $w_E=-1$. Choose $K$ as in the proof of Theorem 4.2(4) (Heegner hypothesis, $p$ split, $D_K$ odd $\ne-3$, $L(E^K,1)\ne0$). Then $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E)+0\ge3$, so $L'(E/K,1)=0$, so by Gross–Zagier the Heegner point $P_K\in E(K)$ is torsion, so $\langle P_K,P_K\rangle_{p,K}=0$ (the height is a quadratic form, hence kills torsion), so by the $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula $L_p'(E/K,\alpha,1)=0$; also $L_p(E/K,\alpha,1)=0$ by Theorem 4.2(1). Hence $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_p(E,\alpha,s)+\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_p(E^K,\alpha,s)\ge2$, and the second term is $0$ by Theorem 4.2(1) as $L(E^K,1)\ne0$. So $r_p(E)\ge2$, and $r_p(E)$ is odd by Theorem 4.2(2), so $r_p(E)\ge3$. $\square$

*Remarks.* (i) The argument uses only the *vanishing* of the Heegner point, i.e. only that $L'(E/K,1)=0$; it cannot be iterated: no formula expresses $L''(E/K,1)$ or $L_p''(E/K,1)$ through any known algebraic object. (ii) The converse implications: $r_p\ge3\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge3$ holds if Schneider's conjecture holds in rank one for $(E,p)$ (else $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ and $\hat h_p(P)=0$ by Theorem 4.2(4)); $r_p\ge 2\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ is Theorem 4.2(2). (iii) Table of the status of $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$, $p\nmid2N$ good:

| | $r_p=0$ | $r_p=1$ | $r_p=2$ | $r_p\ge3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$ | always | impossible | impossible | impossible |
| $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ | impossible | $\iff\hat h_p(P)\ne0$ (Thm 4.2(4)); known for CM and supersingular $p$ | impossible | possible only if $\hat h_p(P)=0$ |
| $r_{\mathrm{an}}=2$ | impossible | impossible | **open** ($\mathrm{T}$) | **open** |
| $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge3$ | impossible | impossible (Prop. 4.5) | impossible (Prop. 4.5) | $\mathrm{T}$ open beyond $r_p\ge3$ |

"Impossible" entries are theorems (4.2(1),(2), 4.5); the $r_p=1$ column also uses the $p$-converse theorems of 4.2(3) under their hypotheses.

### 4.3 Perrin-Riou's conjecture on Kato's zeta element (rank one, now a theorem)

**Conjecture 4.6 (Perrin-Riou 1993, §3.3.2).** `[CONJECTURE → THEOREM]` (Statement as in BSTW Conj. 1.12, text fetched.) Let $z_E=z_0\in H^1(\mathbb{Q},V)$ be the Beilinson–Kato element (§1.6) and suppose $L(E,1)=0$, so $\operatorname{loc}_pz_E\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)=E(\mathbb{Q}_p)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$. Then there is $P\in E(\mathbb{Q})$ with (a) $\log_{\omega_E}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_E)\doteq\log_{\omega_E}(P)^2$ (equality up to $\mathbb{Q}^\times$) and (b) $P$ non-torsion $\iff r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$. In particular $\operatorname{loc}_pz_E\neq0\iff r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$.

`[THEOREM]` Conjecture 4.6 holds: (i) for $E$ with semistable reduction at an odd prime $p$ (good or multiplicative) — Bertolini–Darmon–Venerucci, Adv. Math. 398 (2022) 108172, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2021.108172, Thm. A (text fetched); (ii) for every $p\nmid2N$ (with $a_3=0$ if $p=3$ is supersingular) — BSTW 2024, Thm. 1.13 (preprint; a $p$-integral version, (7.4) there); (iii) good ordinary $p$ via the critical-slope $p$-adic Gross–Zagier formula — Büyükboduk–Pollack–Sasaki, arXiv:1811.08216 (abstract fetched; published status `[unverified]`); (iv) the exceptional-zero (split multiplicative) case — Venerucci, Invent. Math. 203 (2016) 923–972, DOI 10.1007/s00222-015-0606-8; (v) CM case — Rubin, Invent. Math. 107 (1992) 323–350. The proofs of (i) compare Beilinson–Kato elements with Heegner points through $p$-adic families of Beilinson–Flach elements and use Gross–Zagier plus Waldspurger; the complex derivative $L'(E,1)$ enters (b) only through Gross–Zagier.

**What Conjecture 4.6 does and does not give for the transfer problem.** It identifies the *value* at the trivial character of Kato's Euler system with the *square of the logarithm* of a Heegner point; it says nothing about $L_p'(E,0)$. Indeed $\log_{\omega}(P)$ can be non-zero while $\hat h_p(P)=0$, and the two are unrelated invariants of $P$ (one is the value of a locally analytic function of a local point, the other a global height). Perrin-Riou's conjecture is thus *orthogonal* to $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ in rank one: it is a "rank $\le1$ criterion" ($\operatorname{loc}_pz_E\ne0\iff r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$) coming from the complex side via Gross–Zagier.

### 4.4 Kato's zeta element in rank $\ge2$: derivative classes

**Proposition 4.7.** `[THEOREM]` (Kato 2004 Thm. 12.5 + Rubin, *Euler Systems*, Thm. II.2.3 and Remark II.2.9, text fetched.) Let $E$ be non-CM and $p$ any prime (so $V=V_pE$ is an irreducible $G_\mathbb{Q}$-representation and Rubin's hypothesis $\mathrm{Hyp}(\mathbb{Q},V)$ holds: the image of $\rho$ is open in $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ by Serre, so it contains a unipotent $\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&p^n\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\rho(G_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})})$ with $\dim V/(\tau-1)V=1$). If $z_0\ne0$ in $H^1(\mathbb{Q},V)$, then the fine (strict) Selmer group $\operatorname{Sel}^0_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q}):=\ker\big(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,E[p^\infty])\big)$ is finite. Consequently
$$z_0\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\le1,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})\ge2\ \Longrightarrow\ z_0=0 .$$
*Proof.* Rubin's Theorem II.2.3: for an Euler system $\mathbf c$ for $T$ with $c_\mathbb{Q}\notin H^1(\mathbb{Q},T)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ and $\mathrm{Hyp}(\mathbb{Q},V)$, the Selmer group $\mathcal S^{\Sigma_p}(\mathbb{Q},W^*)$ with the strict local condition at $p$ is finite; here $W^*=\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mu_{p^\infty})\cong E[p^\infty]$ (Weil pairing) and $\mathcal S^{\Sigma_p}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^\infty])$ differs from $\operatorname{Sel}^0_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ by finite groups (local conditions at $\ell\ne p$ differ by finite groups). Kato's Euler system has $c_\mathbb{Q}=z_0$ (Kato Thm. 12.5, §13). Finally $\operatorname{Sel}/\operatorname{Sel}^0\hookrightarrow H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,E[p^\infty])=\operatorname{im}\big(E(\mathbb{Q}_p)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\big)$, which has corank one. $\square$

So in rank $\ge2$ (or whenever $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}\ge2$) the bottom class of Kato's Euler system **vanishes identically**, and all information is carried by $z_\infty$ modulo higher powers of $T$.

**Proposition 4.8 (derivative classes and the Taylor coefficients of $L_p$).** `[THEOREM]` (assembled from Kato Thm. 12.4(3), 12.5, 16.6 and the $\Lambda$-linearity of $\operatorname{Col}$.) Assume $E$ non-CM, $p>2$ good ordinary, $(\mathrm{irr}_\mathbb{Q})$, so that $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)$ is free of rank one over $\Lambda$ (Kato Thm. 12.4(3): $p\ne2$ and $T/\mathfrak mT$ irreducible); fix a generator $z_{\mathrm{gen}}$ and write $z_\infty=g\cdot z_{\mathrm{gen}}$ with $g\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ ($g\in\Lambda$ under $(\mathrm{im})$). Then:
1. $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)/T\hookrightarrow H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)$ (from $H^0(\mathbb{Q},T)=0$), so $z_{\mathrm{gen}}(0)\neq0$ and $z_0=g(0)\,z_{\mathrm{gen}}(0)$; hence $z_0=0\iff T\mid g$. More generally put $k:=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}g$ and $z_k:=z_\infty/T^k\in H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ (well defined, $H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}$ being torsion-free); its specialisation $z_k(0)=(g/T^k)(0)\,z_{\mathrm{gen}}(0)$ is a **non-zero** element of $H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)$ — the *$k$-th derivative class*.
2. $r_p=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}g+\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{Col}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_{\mathrm{gen}})$.
3. (Poitou–Tate) The image $\mathcal L:=\operatorname{loc}_p\big(H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)\big)\subset H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$ is a line (exactly one-dimensional), and $\operatorname{Col}_0$ vanishes on $\mathcal L$ iff $\mathcal L=H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$. With $k=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}g$ as in (1):
$$r_p\ge k+1\iff z_k(0)\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)\ (\text{the compact Selmer group}),$$
and $z_k(0)$ is a non-zero multiple of $z_{\mathrm{gen}}(0)$; if $\operatorname{loc}_pz_{\mathrm{gen}}(0)\ne0$ (so that it spans $\mathcal L$) this is equivalent to $\mathcal L=H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$, while if $\operatorname{loc}_pz_{\mathrm{gen}}(0)=0$ then $z_k(0)$ lies in the fine Selmer group $Ш^1(V)$ and $r_p\ge k+1$ automatically.

*Proof.* (1) From the exact sequence of $\Lambda$-modules $0\to T\otimes\Lambda\xrightarrow{T}T\otimes\Lambda\to T\to0$ (Galois acting on $\Lambda$ through $\Gamma$ inverted) and $H^0(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)=E(\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$-free part $=0$, the connecting map gives $0\to H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(T)/T\to H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)$. Torsion-freeness gives the well-definedness of $z_k$, and $(g/T^k)(0)\ne0$ by definition of $k$. (2) $\operatorname{Col}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_\infty)=uL_p$ with $u$ a unit (§1.6) and $\operatorname{Col}$ is $\Lambda$-linear, so $uL_p=g\cdot\operatorname{Col}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_{\mathrm{gen}})$; take $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}$. (3) Poitou–Tate for $V$ over $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ ($S=\{p,\infty\}$; $H^1(\mathbb{R},V)=0$ for $p$ odd, $H^2(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)=H^0(\mathbb{Q}_p,V^*(1))^\vee=0$, $\dim H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)=2$ by the local Euler characteristic, $V^*(1)\cong V$ by the Weil pairing) gives the exact sequence $0\to Ш^1\to H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)\to H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)\to H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)^\vee\to H^2(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)\to0$ with $Ш^1=\ker\operatorname{loc}_p$. Writing $h^i=\dim H^i(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)$ and $i:=\dim\mathcal L$, exactness at $H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$ and at $H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)^\vee$ gives $h^1=(2-i)+h^2$, while Tate's global Euler characteristic formula gives $h^0-h^1+h^2=\dim H^0(\mathbb{R},V)-\dim V=1-2=-1$ (complex conjugation has eigenvalues $+1,-1$ on $V$), i.e. $h^2=h^1-1$ since $h^0=0$. Hence $i=1$. $\operatorname{Col}_0=c\exp^*_\omega$ with $c\ne0$ (§1.6), and $\ker\exp^*=H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$ is a line, so $\operatorname{Col}_0|_{\mathcal L}=0\iff\mathcal L=H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$. Finally $\operatorname{Col}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_k)=uL_p/T^k$ and $(uL_p/T^k)(0)=u(0)\cdot(\text{coefficient of }T^k\text{ in }L_p)$, so $r_p\ge k+1\iff\operatorname{Col}_0(\operatorname{loc}_pz_k(0))=0\iff\operatorname{loc}_pz_k(0)\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$; since $z_k(0)$ lies in $H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],V)$, its localisation lies in $\mathcal L$, and it is unramified at all $\ell\ne p$ (Kato's $j_*$-convention, §1.6), so $\operatorname{loc}_pz_k(0)\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$ iff $z_k(0)\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$. $\square$

*Reading of Proposition 4.8.* Both summands in (2) are $p$-adic-height-type quantities:
* $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}g$ is, under Kato's main conjecture (Conj. 12.10, equivalent to the ordinary main conjecture, Kato §17.13), $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}H^2_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p],T)$, which by Poitou–Tate over $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ is governed by the fine Selmer group over $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ (Coates–Sujatha, Math. Ann. 331 (2005) 809–839, DOI 10.1007/s00208-004-0609-z; Wuthrich, J. Algebraic Geom. 16 (2007) 83–108) — by the control theorem for fine Selmer groups (Wuthrich 2007, `[section unverified]`) and Lemma 3.1, its $T$-adic valuation is $\ge\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}^0_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\max(\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}-1,0)$, with equality iff the fine Selmer Iwasawa module is semisimple at $T=0$; `[HEURISTIC]` this semisimplicity should be governed by the $p$-adic height restricted to the fine Mordell–Weil group $M(E/\mathbb{Q})=\ker(E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p\to\hat E(\mathbb{Q}_p))$ (Wuthrich's "fine regulator", Stein–Wuthrich §4.3);
* $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{Col}(\operatorname{loc}_pz_{\mathrm{gen}})$ is $\ge1$ iff $\mathcal L=H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)$, and its exact value is the order of vanishing along the cyclotomic direction of the Coleman image of a *global* Iwasawa class that is finite at $p$; the first derivative of the Coleman map at a finite class is the $p$-adic height pairing (this is the mechanism behind Perrin-Riou's Theorem 3.7 and Rubin's formula; Nekovář, *Selmer complexes*, Ch. 11 `[chapter number unverified]`).

In particular, in rank $2$ with $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite and the main conjecture, $r_p=2$ iff both terms equal $1$, i.e. iff the height is non-degenerate — consistent with (3.2). **Nothing in this analysis involves $L''(E,1)$.**

### 4.5 Why the interpolation property cannot see derivatives at $s=1$

We make precise the sense in which $L_p$ "knows only $L(E,1)$ and its twists".

1. **What determines $L_p$.** `[THEOREM]` The measure $\mu_\alpha$ is determined by its values on the cosets $a+p^k\mathbb{Z}_p$, which are $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of modular symbols $[a/p^k]^+\in\mathbb{Q}$ (Definition 1.2). Equivalently, by Rohrlich's non-vanishing, $L_p(E,T)$ is the unique element of $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ interpolating the *algebraic numbers* $L(E,\chi^{-1},1)/\Omega^+_E$ (times the explicit factors of Theorem 1.4) for all finite-order characters $\chi$ of $\Gamma$: these numbers lie in $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})$ and can be embedded into $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. The Taylor coefficients of $L_p$ at $T=0$ are the *$p$-adic moments*
$$\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^k}{ds^k}L_p(E,s)\Big|_{s=1}=\frac1{k!}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times}\big(\log_p\langle x\rangle\big)^k\,d\mu_\alpha(x),$$
$p$-adic limits of $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of modular symbols weighted by the $p$-adic logarithm.
2. **What $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ is.** With $\Lambda(E,s)=N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(E,s)=\int_0^\infty f_E(iy/\sqrt N)\,y^{s}\,\frac{dy}{y}$, the derivatives at $s=1$ are the *archimedean moments* $\int_0^\infty f_E(iy/\sqrt N)\,(\log y)^k\,y\,\frac{dy}y$ of the real-analytic function $f_E(iy)$ on $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ (formally, $L^{(k)}(E,1)=\sum_n a_n(-\log n)^k/n$ regularised). The modular symbols are the values $\int_{r}^{i\infty}f_E$ of the *antiderivative* at the cusps $r\in\mathbb{Q}$; they determine $f_E$ (the cusps are dense), hence in principle $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ — but only through the full $\mathbb{R}$-analytic structure, not through any $p$-adic limit of finite $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of the $[a/p^k]^+$.
3. **The two weight spaces meet at one point.** The interpolation formula identifies the value of the complex Mellin transform at the *finite-order twists* $\chi$ (points of the $p$-adic weight space $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times,\mathbb{C}_p^\times)$) with values of the $p$-adic Mellin transform. The complex variable $s$ near $1$ corresponds to the archimedean characters $n\mapsto n^{1-s}$, which are not $p$-adic characters and are not limits of finite-order characters in any $p$-adic sense. The only point common to "$\{\chi\text{ finite order}\}$" and "$\{s\in\mathbb{C}\}$" is $\chi=\mathbf 1$, $s=1$: the value $L(E,1)$. For $s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge2}$ the $p$-adic values $L_p(E,s)$ are conjecturally $p$-adic regulators of Beilinson elements (Perrin-Riou's $p$-adic Beilinson conjecture), not related to $L(E,s)$ by a formula; for weight $2$ the only critical value is $s=1$.
4. **Heights do not transfer.** The only known bridge beyond values is Gross–Zagier: $L'(E/K,1)\leftrightarrow\hat h_{NT}(P_K)$ and $L_p'(E/K,1)\leftrightarrow\hat h_p(P_K)$ (Theorem 4.2(4)). The two heights of the *same* point are a real and a $p$-adic number with no known relation (the Néron–Tate height is conjecturally transcendental; the $p$-adic height is conjecturally non-zero). Hence even in rank one the transfer is *exactly* Schneider's conjecture, and in rank $\ge2$ there is no Heegner-type element whose complex and $p$-adic heights are both computed by derivatives.
5. **$\Lambda$-adic zeta element.** $z_\infty$ encodes all twisted values $L(E,\chi,1)$ and, via Proposition 4.8, all Taylor coefficients of $L_p$ at $T=0$ (through the derivative classes and the Coleman map). It does **not** encode $L^{(k)}(E,1)$ for $k\ge1$: the Bloch–Kato dual exponential of any specialisation of $z_\infty$ at a finite-order character $\chi$ is the value $L(E,\chi^{-1},1)$ (Kato Thm. 12.5(1)), and the classes $z_k(0)$ of Proposition 4.8 are $p$-adic derivatives, whose Bloch–Kato logarithms/heights are $p$-adic invariants.

`[HEURISTIC]` Points 1–5 are theorems about what *is* encoded; the assertion "no formula expresses $L''(E,1)$ in terms of $L_p$" is not a theorem (a proof of BSD would provide such a formula via the two leading-term formulas). The precise open statement is:

**[GAP A.1] (Transfer in rank two).** *Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be a non-CM elliptic curve and $p>3$ a prime of good ordinary reduction with $E[p]$ irreducible. Assume $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=2$, $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ finite and $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\neq0$ (so that $r_p=2$ by Theorems 2.3 and 3.10, and $r_{\mathrm{an}}\in\{2,4,6,\dots\}$ by Theorem 4.2). Prove that $L''(E,1)\ne0$.* Equivalently: prove the "rank-two $p$-converse" $\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=2\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}=2$ under these hypotheses. By Theorem 4.3, under the stated hypotheses this is *equivalent* to BSD(rank) for $E$. The same gap with $2$ replaced by $r\ge3$ (with the additional input Proposition 4.5 giving $r_p\ge3$) is [GAP A.1$_r$].

### 4.6 Attempt on [GAP A.1]

We record what was tried and where each attempt stops.

*(a) Reduce to rank one by twisting.* For $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=2$ one may try to find a quadratic twist $E^K$ or a base change to $K$ over which the relevant rank is $1$ and apply Theorem 4.2(4). Over $K$, $E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\oplus E^K(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ has rank $\ge2$; Gross–Zagier requires $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=1$, impossible. Twisting by higher-order characters (base change to cyclic extensions) gives Rankin–Selberg or Artin-twisted $L$-functions $L(E,\rho,s)$; the only rank-two results there are the Darmon–Rotger–Bertolini–Seveso–Venerucci "generalised Kato classes" `[THEOREM]` (Darmon–Rotger, Res. Math. Sci. 3 (2016), DOI 10.1186/s40687-016-0074-9; Castella–Hsieh, Forum Math. Sigma 10 (2022), DOI 10.1017/fms.2021.85 — both located via Crossref; statements not independently verified here), which express a class $\kappa(f,g,h)\in\operatorname{Sel}(E/\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$ built from a triple product through $p$-adic regulators $\log_p(P)\log_p(Q)-\log_p(P')\log_p(Q')$ of rank-two Mordell–Weil groups and relate its non-vanishing to $L(E,\rho_{gh},1)\neq0$ — again a *value* of a complex $L$-function (rank $0$ for the twisted motive), not $L''(E,1)$. This is approach C (`C-higher-rank-euler-systems-diagonal-cycles.md`). **Stops:** no complex derivative appears.

*(b) Use the derivative classes.* Proposition 4.8 shows $r_p=2$ (under MC, rank 2, Sha finite, $\operatorname{Reg}_p\ne0$) and identifies $z_2(0)\in H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},V)$ as a canonical non-zero Selmer class. To get $r_{\mathrm{an}}=2$ one would need an Euler system whose bottom class is $z_2(0)$ (a "rank-two Euler system" in the sense of Perrin-Riou/Rubin: $\wedge^2$ of Selmer classes); Kato's Euler system has core rank one and its derivatives are not Euler systems. The Mazur–Rubin/Büyükboduk theory of Kolyvagin systems of higher core rank requires an $r$-fold exterior power of classes (Stark systems), none of which is constructed for $V_pE$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $r=2$. **Stops:** no construction.

*(c) Compare leading terms.* Under MC + Sha finite + $\operatorname{Reg}_p\neq0$, Theorem 3.10 gives $L_p^*(E,0)\sim\varepsilon_p\prod c_\ell\,\#\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma/\#E(\mathbb{Q})^2_{\mathrm{tors}}$. BSD(leading term) predicts $L''(E,1)/2=\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_{NT}\prod c_\ell\#\operatorname{Sha}/\#E(\mathbb{Q})^2_{\mathrm{tors}}$. The ratio $L''(E,1)/(2\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_{NT})$ should thus be a rational number whose $p$-adic valuation is computable from $L^*_p(E,0)/\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma$; but no theorem asserts $L''(E,1)/(\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_{NT})\in\mathbb{Q}$ (this is the "algebraicity" part of BSD in rank 2, unknown — cf. `F-analytic-side.md`), and even algebraicity would not give non-vanishing. **Stops:** algebraicity of $L^{(r)}(E,1)/(\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_{NT})$ is unknown for $r\ge2$.

*(d) Prove a weaker transfer: $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le r_p$.* This would follow from a formula $L^{(k)}(E,1)=\Phi_k(\text{Taylor coefficients of }L_p)$; by §4.5 none exists at the level of the interpolation property. Conversely $r_p\le r_{\mathrm{an}}$ would follow from a "$p$-adic analytic continuation" of $L(E,\chi,1)$ in the character $\chi$ through the archimedean characters, which is meaningless. **Stops:** the two variables are not the same variable.

*(e) What would suffice.* Any one of: (i) a rank-two Euler/Kolyvagin system with bottom class $z_2(0)$ and an explicit reciprocity law expressing $L''(E,1)$ (approach C); (ii) an "archimedean–$p$-adic" Gross–Zagier formula for a rank-two cycle (none known); (iii) the analytic non-vanishing $L''(E,1)\ne0$ under the arithmetic hypotheses of [GAP A.1] by analytic means (approach F; hopeless at present since it would be BSD in rank 2 for these curves). We record [GAP A.1] as *not reduced* by this approach.

## 5. Schneider's conjecture: non-degeneracy of the cyclotomic $p$-adic height

### 5.1 Statement and reformulations

**Conjecture 5.1 (Schneider).** `[CONJECTURE]` (Schneider, Invent. Math. 69 (1982) 401–409 and 79 (1985) 329–374; general $p$-adic representations: Perrin-Riou, *Fonctions $L$ $p$-adiques des représentations $p$-adiques*, Astérisque 229 (1995); statement for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ as in Stein–Wuthrich Conj. 4.1.) For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $p$ a prime of good ordinary (or multiplicative) reduction, the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_p$ is non-degenerate on $E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p$; equivalently $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\ne0$.

Equivalent forms (good ordinary $p>2$; proofs in §3):
* (algebraic) $X_\infty$ is semisimple at $T=0$ *and* $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite $\iff$ Conjecture 5.1 *and* $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite (Corollary 3.8);
* (analytic, under the main conjecture) $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ $\iff$ Conjecture 5.1 *and* $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite ((3.2));
* (rank one, via Heegner points, no main conjecture needed) if $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$: Conjecture 5.1 $\iff r_p=1\iff L_p'(E/K,\alpha,1)\neq0$ for any auxiliary $K$ as in Theorem 4.2(4).

**Relation to Leopoldt.** `[THEOREM]` (classical; e.g. Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg, *Cohomology of Number Fields*, Ch. X §3, and Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, §5.5 and Thm. 13.31 — standard references, not re-fetched.) For the multiplicative group over a number field $F$ and the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension $F_\infty$, the analogue of $X_\infty$ is the Galois group $\mathfrak X_\infty$ of the maximal abelian pro-$p$ extension of $F_\infty$ unramified outside $p$. Its $\Lambda$-rank equals $r_2(F)$ ("weak Leopoldt" for the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension, a theorem of Iwasawa), and $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\mathfrak X_\infty/T\mathfrak X_\infty=r_2(F)+1+\delta_F$, where $\delta_F$ is the Leopoldt defect: the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-rank of the kernel of $\mathcal O_F^\times\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p\to\prod_{v\mid p}\mathcal O_{F_v}^\times$, i.e. the corank of the "$p$-adic height" $u\mapsto(\log_p\iota_v(u))_{v\mid p}$ on units. Leopoldt's conjecture ($\delta_F=0$) is thus the exact analogue of Conjecture 5.1 (non-degeneracy of the $p$-adic logarithmic pairing on the "Mordell–Weil group" $\mathcal O_F^\times$), and the torsion/semisimplicity dichotomy of Lemma 3.1 is the analogue of "weak Leopoldt versus Leopoldt". Schneider (Invent. Math. 71 (1983) 251–293, DOI 10.1007/BF01389099) formulated the conjecture $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)=\operatorname{rank}$ in this spirit `[content of that paper as reported in secondary sources; not re-fetched]`. Two consequences of the analogy: (i) Leopoldt's conjecture is known for abelian extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$ (Brumer, via Baker's transcendence theorem) and open in general — non-degeneracy statements of this type have so far only been proved by **transcendence** methods; (ii) the CM case of Conjecture 5.1 is precisely the "abelian" case where such methods work (Theorem 5.2).

### 5.2 Known cases

**Theorem 5.2 (CM curves, any non-torsion point).** `[THEOREM]` (Bertrand, *Valeurs de fonctions thêta et hauteurs $p$-adiques*, Séminaire de Théorie des Nombres Paris 1980–81, Progr. Math. 22, Birkhäuser 1982, pp. 1–11; cited for exactly this statement in Stein–Wuthrich §4.1 ("the special case of curves with complex multiplication of rank 1") and by Silverman on MathOverflow, and in Burungale–Disegni, Ann. Inst. Fourier 70 (2020) 2077–2101, DOI 10.5802/aif.3381: "for CM elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$, the result was previously known as a consequence of works of Bertrand, Gross–Zagier and Rohrlich"; the original could not be fetched, so the exact hypotheses on $p$ are `[unverified]` — presumably $p$ a prime of good ordinary reduction, i.e. split in the CM field.) If $E$ has CM and $P\in E(\mathbb{Q})$ is non-torsion, then $\hat h_p(P)$ is a transcendental number, in particular $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$. Hence Conjecture 5.1 holds for CM curves of rank one. For CM curves of rank $\ge2$ the non-vanishing of $\operatorname{Reg}_p$ (a determinant of transcendental numbers) is **open**; the generic non-vanishing along anticyclotomic families for CM abelian varieties with root number $-1$ is Burungale–Disegni 2020 (abstract fetched).

**Lemma 5.3 (supersingular $p$: single points are automatic).** `[THEOREM]` (Kobayashi, Invent. Math. 191 (2013), §4, as summarised in the MathSciNet review MR3020170 and in Kobayashi's 2012 Oxford slides, both fetched: the $\alpha$-height pairings are "equivalent and nontrivial"; we give the proof.) Let $p\nmid 2N$ be good supersingular, $\alpha$ a root of $X^2-a_pX+p$, and let $h_\alpha:=h_{\nu_\alpha}$ be the $p$-adic height attached to the $\varphi$-eigenvector $\nu_\alpha\in D_{dR}(V)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)$ of eigenvalue $\alpha^{-1}$ (Bernardi–Perrin-Riou normalisation, Stein–Wuthrich §4: $h_{a\omega+b\eta}=a\,h_\omega+b\,h_\eta$ with $h_\omega(P)=\log_E(P)^2$). Then $h_\alpha(P)\ne0$ for every non-torsion $P\in E(\mathbb{Q})$.

*Proof.* Since $p\mid a_p$, both roots have $v_p(\alpha)=\tfrac12$ (Newton polygon of $X^2-a_pX+p$), so $\alpha\notin\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)$ is a quadratic extension. The Frobenius $\varphi$ acts $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linearly on the two-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_p$-space $D=D_{dR}(V)=\mathbb{Q}_p\omega\oplus\mathbb{Q}_p\eta$ with eigenvalues $\alpha^{-1},\beta^{-1}\notin\mathbb{Q}_p$; an eigenvector $\nu_\alpha=a\omega+b\eta$ ($a,b\in\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)$) cannot be a $\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)^\times$-multiple of a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-rational vector (such a vector would be a $\varphi$-eigenvector with eigenvalue in $\mathbb{Q}_p$), so $b\ne0$ and $a/b\notin\mathbb{Q}_p$. Now $h_\alpha(P)=a\log_E(P)^2+b\,h_\eta(P)$ with $\log_E(P)^2,h_\eta(P)\in\mathbb{Q}_p$. If $h_\alpha(P)=0$ then $(a/b)\log_E(P)^2=-h_\eta(P)\in\mathbb{Q}_p$, forcing $\log_E(P)=0$; but the formal-group logarithm is injective on $\hat E(p\mathbb{Z}_p)$, which contains a non-torsion multiple of any non-torsion $P$, so $\log_E(P)\ne0$. Contradiction. $\square$

Thus in the supersingular case the rank-one form of Schneider's conjecture is trivially true, which is why Theorem 4.2(5) holds there; for rank $\ge2$ the non-vanishing of the $D_{dR}$-valued regulator (Perrin-Riou's Conjecture 3.3.7 in Ann. Inst. Fourier 43 (1993); Stein–Wuthrich Conj. 4.4: "the fine regulator is non-zero") is **open**.

**Ordinary non-CM curves.** `[THEOREM]` The literature contains **no** theorem asserting $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$ for a single non-torsion point $P$ on a non-CM elliptic curve at a good ordinary prime $p$, for any $(E,p)$, other than by direct computation to finite precision (which proves non-vanishing for that $(E,p,P)$: a $p$-adic number computed to precision $O(p^n)$ with a non-zero digit is non-zero). Stein–Wuthrich write (§4.1, text fetched): "Apart from the special case treated in [Ber82] of curves with complex multiplication of rank 1, there are hardly any results on this conjecture." We searched for later results (2013–2026: "Schneider conjecture $p$-adic height non-degenerate", "$p$-adic height non-vanishing", "$p$-adic regulator nonzero") and found only: Burungale–Disegni 2020 (CM, generic in anticyclotomic families); Burungale–Skinner–Wan, *A refined non-vanishing of the $p$-adic logarithm of a rational point on an abelian variety*, Ann. Math. Québec (2026), DOI 10.1007/s40316-026-00278-x (located via Crossref; this concerns the $p$-adic **logarithm**, not the height; content `[unverified]`); Castella–Hsu–Kundu–Lee 2025 (derived heights). Numerical evidence: Mazur–Stein–Tate 2006; Stein–Wuthrich 2013 (Theorem 1.1: $1{,}534{,}422$ pairs $(E,p)$ of rank $\ge2$ curves with $N\le30{,}000$, $5\le p<1000$ good ordinary, $\bar\rho_p$ surjective, for which $\operatorname{Reg}_p\ne0$ was verified and $\operatorname{Sha}[p]=0$ deduced); Balakrishnan–Çiperiani–Stein, Math. Comp. 84 (2015) 923–954 (Heegner points and anticyclotomic $\Lambda$-adic regulators); Wuthrich, J. London Math. Soc. 70 (2004) 23–40 (heights in families; main statement not re-verified here `[unverified]`).

**Bertolini–Darmon and anticyclotomic heights.** The brief asks about Bertolini–Darmon's results on $p$-adic heights of Heegner points. Our understanding (bibliographic data verified via Crossref; the papers themselves were not fetched in this session, so the descriptions are `[unverified]` in detail): Bertolini–Darmon 1994/1995 (derived heights, §3.3) study $p$-adic height pairings attached to a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension — in the *anticyclotomic* case over an imaginary quadratic $K$ the pairing is forced to be degenerate in many situations by the action of complex conjugation on the anticyclotomic Galois group — and replace the height by derived heights whose regulators compute the leading term of the characteristic series; Howard 2005 proves the $\Lambda$-adic Gross–Zagier formula (Theorem 2.8(2), abstract fetched) expressing the $\Lambda^{ac}$-adic height of the Heegner tower as a derivative of the two-variable $p$-adic $L$-function — a formula, not a non-vanishing theorem. The Bertolini–Darmon "rigid analytic Gross–Zagier formula" (Ann. of Math. 146 (1997) 111–147 `[not re-verified]`) computes a derivative of the anticyclotomic $p$-adic $L$-function at an exceptional zero as a formal-group *logarithm* of a Heegner point, not a height. None of these results proves the non-vanishing of a cyclotomic $p$-adic height of a Heegner point on a non-CM curve; we found no paper claiming this (searches recorded in §9).

**[GAP A.2] (Schneider, rank one, ordinary non-CM).** *Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be a non-CM elliptic curve with $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})=1$, $P$ a generator modulo torsion, and $p\nmid2N$ a prime of good ordinary reduction. Prove $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$.* Equivalent (Theorem 4.2(4)): $r_p=1$ whenever $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$; equivalent (under the main conjecture): $X_\infty$ has no summand $\Lambda/(T^e)$ with $e\ge2$. Weaker useful forms: (A.2$'$) for fixed $(E,P)$, $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$ for infinitely many $p$; (A.2$''$) for fixed $(E,P)$, $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$ for all but finitely many $p$.

**[GAP A.3] (Schneider, general rank).** *For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of rank $r\ge2$ and $p$ good ordinary, prove $\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_p)\ne0$.* Even for CM curves and even for supersingular $p$ (where the relevant statement is the non-vanishing of the fine regulator) this is open.

### 5.4 Attempt on [GAP A.2]

*(a) Transcendence (Bertrand's route).* For CM $E$ and ordinary $p$, $\hat h_p(P)=2\log_p(e(P)/\sigma_p(P))$ where the canonical sigma function $\sigma_p$ satisfies $x(t)+\tfrac{1}{12}e_2=-\big(\tfrac{d}{\omega}\big)^2\log\sigma_p$ with $e_2=E_2(E,\omega_E)$ the $p$-adic weight-two Eisenstein value (Mazur–Tate 1991; Stein–Wuthrich (4.1)). In the CM case $e_2$ is algebraic (Katz's theory of $p$-adic Eisenstein series at CM points `[standard; not re-verified here]`), so $\sigma_p$ differs from a $p$-adic theta function with algebraic data by an algebraic exponential factor and a $p$-adic Schneider–Lang-type theorem applies; this is our reading of Bertrand's mechanism `[unverified against the original]`. For non-CM $E$, $e_2$ is (conjecturally) transcendental and $\sigma_p\in\mathbb{Z}_p[[t]]$ has no known algebraicity property beyond $p$-integrality; the method gives nothing. `[HEURISTIC]` as regards the transcendence of $e_2$; the failure of the method is a `[THEOREM]`-level observation only in the sense that no transcendence statement for $\sigma_p$ is available. **Stops:** algebraic differential equation for $\sigma_p$ has a transcendental coefficient.

*(b) Iwasawa-theoretic route.* By Corollary 3.8 and Theorem 2.9, [GAP A.2] (for $(E,p)$ with the main conjecture and $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$) is equivalent to $X_\infty\simeq_{\text{ps}}\Lambda/(T)\oplus(\text{$T$-coprime})$, i.e. to $r_p=1$. Iwasawa modules are only accessible through their characteristic ideals (main conjectures) and through control theorems; no known technique proves semisimplicity of an Iwasawa module at a given point except by evaluating a $p$-adic $L$-function there — which is circular here. The Leopoldt analogue confirms the difficulty: semisimplicity statements for $\mathfrak X_\infty$ at $T=0$ *are* Leopoldt's conjecture, open outside abelian (transcendence-accessible) cases. **Stops:** no non-circular handle on semisimplicity.

*(c) Variation in $p$ (forms A.2$'$, A.2$''$).* $\hat h_p(P)$ for varying $p$ are values of different $p$-adic functions with no common algebraic structure; there is no analogue of "the $\ell$-adic realisations of a motive" for $p$-adic heights (the height is not a motivic invariant: it depends on the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension). We found no theorem of the form "for fixed $E,P$, $\hat h_p(P)\ne0$ for infinitely many $p$". One conditional remark: `[NEW]` *(trivial consequence of Theorem 4.2(3),(4))* if $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ and $\hat h_p(P)=0$ for some good ordinary $p\nmid2N$, then $r_p\ge3$ (parity), so $L_p(E,T)$ has a zero of order $\ge3$ at $T=0$ although $\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb{Q})=1$; under the main conjecture $X_\infty$ then contains $\Lambda/(T^e)$ with $e\ge3$ — a "$p$-adic zero of order $\ge3$ with no arithmetic cause". No contradiction is available. **Stops:** no constraint links the $p$-adic heights at different $p$.

*(d) Heegner points and $\Lambda$-adic heights.* Howard's $\Lambda$-adic Gross–Zagier formula and the Heegner point main conjecture (Theorem 2.8(3),(4)) determine the structure of $X^{ac}_\infty$ in terms of Heegner points; the *cyclotomic* height of the base Heegner point is not among the quantities so determined (the anticyclotomic tower and the cyclotomic direction are transverse: the two-variable $p$-adic $L$-function of $E/K$ has the cyclotomic derivative of Perrin-Riou along the anticyclotomic line, and the Heegner point main conjecture concerns the anticyclotomic variable). Non-vanishing of $L_p'(E/K,1)$ is not implied by any known nonvanishing theorem for two-variable $p$-adic $L$-functions (Hsieh's $\mu$-invariant results concern the BDP/anticyclotomic function, i.e. *values*, cf. Yan–Zhu 2026 abstract fetched). **Stops:** transversality of the two variables.

We record [GAP A.2] as *not reduced*.

### 6.3 What each outcome proves

**Theorem 6.1 (certificate).** `[THEOREM]` Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $p\ge3$ good ordinary. Suppose (C1) certifies that the coefficient of $T^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$ in $L_p(E,T)$ is non-zero (PARI `r` $=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$; the rank $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ itself certified by descent/saturation). Then:
1. $r_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ (Proposition 3.4 gives $r_p\ge r_{\mathrm{alg}}$; the certificate gives $\le$).
2. $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]$ is **finite** (Proposition 3.4: all inequalities in the chain are equalities, so $c_{\mathrm{Sha}}=0$). This uses only Kato (Theorem 2.1(1),(2)) and Mazur's control theorem — no main conjecture, no hypothesis on $\bar\rho$.
3. $X_\infty$ is semisimple at $T=0$ and **Schneider's conjecture holds for $(E,p)$**, $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E/\mathbb{Q})\ne0$ (Proposition 3.4 gives $\delta_{ss}=0$; Theorem 3.7(2) converts). *The regulator need not be computed to prove this.*
4. If moreover the main conjecture holds in $\Lambda$ for $(E,p)$ (Theorem 2.9; automatic for 389a1 and 5077a1 at good ordinary $p\ge3$), and (C2),(C3) give $v_p(\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma)$ and $v_p(L_p^*(E,0))$, then
$$\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^\infty]=p^{\,b_p},\qquad b_p=v_p\big(L_p^*(E,0)\big)-v_p(\varepsilon_p)-\sum_\ell v_p(c_\ell)-v_p\big(\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma(E/\mathbb{Q})\big)+2v_p\big(\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\big)$$
   (Theorem 3.10); with only Kato's divisibility one gets $\le$ (Stein–Wuthrich Prop. 11.2). If $b_p=0$ the computation has also *verified the main conjecture* for $(E,p)$ directly (Stein–Wuthrich §11 remark), independently of Theorem 2.2.
5. $p$-adic BSD (Conjecture 3.9(1),(2)) holds for $(E,p)$ up to a $p$-adic unit; the exact equality is *supported* to precision $O(p^n)$, not proved.
6. If $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is known (389a1, 5077a1), $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ is verified trivially. If $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is unknown (rank $\ge4$), the computation proves **nothing** about $r_{\mathrm{an}}$; by Theorem 4.3(3) it proves exactly: BSD(rank) for $E$ $\iff$ $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$, i.e. it reduces BSD(rank) for that curve to the single statement $r_{\mathrm{an}}=r_p$.

(ii) The exact (not up-to-unit) $p$-adic BSD formula. (iii) Anything about $L^{(k)}(E,1)$: no $p$-adic quantity is related to it (§4.5). (iv) Finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ (all $p$ at once): one $p$ at a time only.

**Summary of the attack.** Iwasawa theory proves the chain (0.1) unconditionally and, where the main conjecture is known (Theorem 2.9), converts each of $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite, Schneider, and the $p$-adic leading term into the *single computable invariant* $r_p$ (Theorem 6.1). It reduces BSD(rank) to $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ (Theorem 4.3) and settles $\mathrm{T}(E,p)$ exactly in the cases $\min(r_{\mathrm{an}},r_p)=0$, in the case $r_p=1$ (under $p$-converse hypotheses), in the case $r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ for CM curves and supersingular $p$, and gives $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge3\Rightarrow r_p\ge3$. It cannot, by its nature, produce a relation between $L^{(k)}(E,1)$, $k\ge1$, and any $p$-adic quantity; that relation — [GAP A.1] — is where the approach ends.
