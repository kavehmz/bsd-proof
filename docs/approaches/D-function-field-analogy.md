## D.0 Purpose, notation, and the results of this file in one page

**Purpose.** Over a global function field the analogue of BSD is a theorem *modulo finiteness of one
$\ell$-primary part of Sha* (Tate, Milne, Schneider, Bauer, Kato–Trihan), and the Yun–Zhang formula gives a
geometric meaning to *all* central Taylor coefficients of $L(E,s)$. This file (i) states these results with exact
hypotheses and verified references, (ii) isolates the mechanism of the proofs, (iii) identifies, statement by
statement, which ingredient has no analogue over $\mathbb Q$, (iv) states each missing analogue as a self-contained
`[GAP]`, and (v) records what an attempt on each gap achieves. Everything tagged `[NEW]` is proved in full on the
page; all of it is elementary linear algebra/Iwasawa theory assembled from cited theorems, and none of it proves
anything about BSD over $\mathbb Q$.

**Standing notation (this file).**

- $k=\mathbb F_q$, $p=\operatorname{char} k$; $C/k$ a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus
  $g_C$; $K=k(C)$; $|C|$ the closed points; for $v\in|C|$, $k_v$ the residue field, $q_v=|k_v|=q^{\deg v}$.
  $\bar k$ an algebraic closure, $\bar C=C\times_k\bar k$, $K\bar k$ the field of constants extension.
  $\mathrm{Fr}\in G_k=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)$ denotes the **geometric** Frobenius (inverse of $x\mapsto x^q$).
- $E/K$ an elliptic curve. $E$ is *constant* if $E\cong E_0\times_kK$; *isotrivial* if $j(E)\in k$ (equivalently,
  constant after a finite separable extension of $K$); *non-isotrivial* otherwise. $\mathfrak n$ is the conductor
  (an effective divisor on $C$).
- $\pi:\mathcal E\to C$ the minimal regular model (a smooth projective surface over $k$, relatively minimal, with
  zero section $O$). For $v\in|C|$: $f_v$ = number of irreducible components of the fibre $\mathcal E_v$ as a
  $k_v$-scheme; $\Phi_v$ the component group of the Néron model; $c_v=|\Phi_v(k_v)|$ the Tamagawa number.
- $L(E,T)=\prod_{v\ \mathrm{good}}(1-a_vT^{\deg v}+q_vT^{2\deg v})^{-1}\prod_{v\ \mathrm{bad}}(1-a_vT^{\deg v})^{-1}$
  with $a_v=q_v+1-|\bar E_v(k_v)|$ at good $v$ and $a_v\in\{1,-1,0\}$ for split multiplicative, non-split
  multiplicative, additive $v$; $L(E,s):=L(E,q^{-s})$.
- For a prime $\ell\neq p$: $j:U\hookrightarrow C$ the good-reduction locus, $\mathcal F=\mathcal F_\ell:=j_*j^*R^1\pi_*\mathbb Z_\ell$
  (the middle extension of the lisse sheaf $R^1\pi_*\mathbb Z_\ell|_U=(T_\ell E)^\vee$; by the Weil pairing
  $\mathcal F(1)|_U\cong T_\ell E$, so $\mathcal F(1)\cong j_*T_\ell E$), $V:=H^1(\bar C,\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)$ and
  $M:=V(1)=H^1(\bar C,j_*V_\ell E)$ (so $\mathrm{Fr}$ acts on $M$ as $q^{-1}\mathrm{Fr}|_V$). $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_N$ the eigenvalues of
  $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $V$; $M^\circ\subset M$ denotes the image of $H^1(\bar C,\mathcal F(1))$ (a $\mathrm{Fr}$-stable lattice).
- $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{rank}E(K)$, $r_{\mathrm{an}}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$; $m_q$, resp. $g_q$, the
  algebraic, resp. geometric, multiplicity of the eigenvalue $q$ of $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $V$ (equivalently of $1$ on $M$);
  the **Frobenius defect** $\delta_\ell(E/K):=m_q-g_q\ge0$.
- $\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell^\infty}(E/K)=\ker\big(H^1(K,E[\ell^\infty])\to\prod_vH^1(K_v,E)\big)$ (flat cohomology
  if $\ell=p$), $s_\ell:=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell^\infty}(E/K)$;
  $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)=\ker(H^1(K,E)\to\prod_vH^1(K_v,E))$; $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)=H^2_{\text{ét}}(\mathcal E,\mathbb G_m)$;
  $\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)$ the Néron–Severi group, $\rho=\operatorname{rank}\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)$; $\mathbb T\subset\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)$
  the *trivial sublattice* generated by $O$, a fibre $F$ and the fibre components not meeting $O$
  ($\operatorname{rank}\mathbb T=2+\sum_v(f_v-1)$).
- $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$ the Néron–Tate height on $E(K)$, normalised to take values in $\mathbb Q\cdot\log q$;
  $R(E/K)=|\det\langle P_i,P_j\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}|$ for a basis $(P_i)$ of $E(K)/E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}$.

## D.1 BSD over global function fields: statements and theorems

### D.1.1 The $L$-function

**[THEOREM] (Grothendieck, Deligne).** Let $E/K$ be non-constant. The Euler factor at $v$ is
$\det(1-\mathrm{Fr}_vT^{\deg v}\mid\mathcal F_{\bar v}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)^{-1}$ for a geometric Frobenius $\mathrm{Fr}_v$ acting on the stalk
$\mathcal F_{\bar v}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=H^1(\bar E_{\bar\eta},\mathbb Q_\ell)^{I_v}$, so $L(E,T)$ is the $L$-function of the sheaf $\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$;
moreover $H^0(\bar C,\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)=H^2(\bar C,\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)=0$ and
$$L(E,T)=\det\big(1-T\,\mathrm{Fr}\ \big|\ V\big)=\prod_{i=1}^N(1-\alpha_iT)\in\mathbb Z[T],\qquad N=4g_C-4+\deg\mathfrak n,$$
where each $\alpha_i$ is an algebraic integer with $|\iota(\alpha_i)|=q$ for every complex embedding $\iota$, and the
multiset $\{\alpha_i\}$ is stable under $\alpha\mapsto q^2/\alpha$. Consequently $L(E,s)$ is a polynomial in $q^{-s}$,
satisfies the functional equation $L(E,2-s)=\varepsilon_E\,q^{N(s-1)}L(E,s)$ with $\varepsilon_E:=q^{-N}\prod_i(-\alpha_i)\in\{\pm1\}$
(substitute $T\mapsto1/(q^2T)$ in the product), its zeros lie on $\Re s=1$, and
$$r_{\mathrm{an}}=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\#\{i:\alpha_i=q\}=m_q .$$
*References.* Cohomological formula and rationality: Grothendieck, *Formule de Lefschetz et rationalité des fonctions L*,
Sém. Bourbaki 279 (1964/66), and SGA 4½, *Rapport*; purity/Riemann hypothesis: Deligne, *La conjecture de Weil II*,
Publ. Math. IHÉS 52 (1980), 137–252. The vanishing of $H^0,H^2$ for non-constant $E$ is the Lang–Néron theorem
($E(K\bar k)$ finitely generated) plus duality; see Ulmer, *Park City lectures on elliptic curves over function fields*,
arXiv:1101.1939, Lecture 4, and Ulmer, *CRM lectures on curves and Jacobians over function fields*, arXiv:1203.5573,
§"$L$-functions" (Lemma there). For constant $E=E_0\times_kK$, $L(E,s)$ is a rational function with poles on
$\Re s\in\{1/2,3/2\}$ and zeros on $\Re s=1$ (Ulmer, arXiv:1101.1939, Exercise in Lecture 1), and
$r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is still the multiplicity of the eigenvalue $q$ on $H^1(\bar C,\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)$.

Note the tautology that will matter in §D.5: $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is an *algebraic* multiplicity of a Frobenius eigenvalue.

### D.1.2 The conjecture in Tate's normalisation

**[CONJECTURE] BSD$(E/K)$ (rank).** $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$.

**[CONJECTURE] BSD$(E/K)$ (leading term; Tate 1966, Conj. (B), function-field case).** $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$,
$\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite, and
$$L^*(E,1):=\lim_{s\to1}\frac{L(E,s)}{(s-1)^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}}=\frac{|\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)|\cdot R(E/K)\cdot\tau(E/K)}{|E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2},
\qquad
\tau(E/K):=q^{\,1-g_C}\prod_{v\in|C|}c_v\,q_v^{a_v},$$
where $\omega$ is any non-zero invariant differential on $E/K$ and $a_v\in\mathbb Z$ is defined by requiring
$\varpi_v^{a_v}\omega$ to be a Néron differential at $v$ ($\varpi_v$ a uniformiser). Equivalently
$\tau(E/K)=q^{1-g_C}\prod_v L_v(E,1)\int_{E(K_v)}|\omega|_v$, because
$\int_{E(K_v)}|\omega|_v=c_v\,q_v^{a_v}\,|\bar E^0_v(k_v)|/q_v$ and $|\bar E^0_v(k_v)|/q_v=L_v(E,1)^{-1}$ for every $v$
(good: $(q_v+1-a_v)/q_v$; multiplicative: $(q_v\mp1)/q_v$; additive: $1$). The product formula makes $\tau$ independent
of $\omega$; the factor $q^{1-g_C}$ is $\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb A_K/K)^{-1}$ for the standard Haar measure. This is
Tate's Conjecture (B) of *On the conjectures of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer and a geometric analog*, Sém. Bourbaki 9
(1966), Exp. 306, 415–440 (numdam SB_1964-1966__9__415_0), specialised to elliptic curves over function fields, in the
form used by Ulmer, arXiv:1203.5573, §"The refined BSD conjecture" (Ulmer writes the Tamagawa factor as
$q^{g_X(1-g_C)}\prod_v q^{g_Xa_v}c_v$; the exponent must be read with $q_v$ for places of degree $>1$, as the local-volume
computation above shows). For abelian varieties the statement involves the dual $A^\vee$ and
$|A(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}||A^\vee(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}|$; see Kato–Trihan (below), §1.

Both sides are homogeneous in $(\log q)^{r_{\mathrm{alg}}}$: $1-q^{1-s}=(s-1)\log q+O((s-1)^2)$, and
$\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}=(\log q)\cdot(\text{a }\mathbb Q\text{-valued pairing})$ (Proposition D.2.3 below).

### D.1.3 The Artin–Tate conjecture for the surface

For a smooth projective geometrically irreducible surface $\mathcal X/k$ put
$P_2(\mathcal X,T)=\det(1-T\,\mathrm{Fr}\mid H^2(\bar{\mathcal X},\mathbb Q_\ell))$,
$\zeta(\mathcal X,s)=\prod_{i=0}^4P_i(\mathcal X,q^{-s})^{(-1)^{i+1}}$, $R(\mathcal X)=|\det(D_i\cdot D_j)|$ for a basis
$(D_i)$ of $\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal X)/\mathrm{tors}$, and $\alpha(\mathcal X)=\chi(\mathcal X,\mathcal O_{\mathcal X})-1+\dim\operatorname{PicVar}(\mathcal X)$.

**[CONJECTURE] $T_1(\mathcal X,\ell)$, $T_2(\mathcal X)$, $AT(\mathcal X)$ (Tate 1965/1966; Artin–Tate).**
$T_1(\mathcal X,\ell)$: the cycle class map $\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal X)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\to H^2(\bar{\mathcal X},\mathbb Q_\ell(1))^{G_k}$
is surjective (it is always injective). $T_2(\mathcal X)$: $\rho(\mathcal X)=-\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\zeta(\mathcal X,s)$
$(=$ multiplicity of the eigenvalue $q$ of $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $H^2(\bar{\mathcal X},\mathbb Q_\ell))$.
$AT(\mathcal X)$: $T_2(\mathcal X)$ holds, $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)$ is finite, and
$$\zeta(\mathcal X,s)\sim\frac{R(\mathcal X)\,|\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)|\,q^{-\alpha(\mathcal X)}}{|\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal X)_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}\,(1-q^{1-s})^{\rho(\mathcal X)}\qquad(s\to1).$$
*References.* Tate, *Algebraic cycles and poles of zeta functions*, in: Arithmetical Algebraic Geometry (Purdue 1963),
Harper & Row 1965, 93–110 (conjectures $T_1,T_2$); Tate 1966 loc. cit. Conj. (C) and §4 (Artin–Tate formula, formulated
jointly with Artin); Milne, *On a conjecture of Artin and Tate*, Ann. of Math. 102 (1975), 517–533, DOI 10.2307/1971042,
Introduction (statement (A–T) as displayed above). *[The brief for this file cites "Milne (1975 Invent. Math.)"; the
Artin–Tate paper is in the Annals. Milne's Inventiones paper is the 1968 one on constant abelian varieties, cited below.]*

### D.1.4 The theorems

**[THEOREM] D.1.4.1 (Tate 1966; Milne 1975; Tate–Milne "$T_1\Leftrightarrow T_2$").** For $\mathcal X$ as above:
(i) $\rho(\mathcal X)\le-\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\zeta(\mathcal X,s)$;
(ii) $T_2(\mathcal X)\Rightarrow T_1(\mathcal X,\ell)$ for all $\ell$ (including $\ell=p$), hence $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)[\ell^\infty]$ finite for all $\ell$;
(iii) $T_1(\mathcal X,\ell)$ for **one** $\ell$ ($\ell=p$ allowed) $\Rightarrow T_2(\mathcal X)$;
(iv) $T_2(\mathcal X)\Rightarrow AT(\mathcal X)$.
For $\ell\ne p$, (i)–(iv) are Tate 1966, §4–5 (Thm. 5.1 there, as cited by Liu–Lorenzini–Raynaud, Invent. Math. 159
(2005), 673–676); the $p$-parts of (iii) and (iv) are Milne 1975, Thms. 4.1 and 6.1, proved there for $p\ne2$; the
restriction $p\neq 2$ was removed by the de Rham–Witt theory of Illusie and Milne, *Values of zeta functions of varieties
over finite fields*, Amer. J. Math. 108 (1986), 297–360, DOI 10.2307/2374676 (see Ulmer arXiv:1203.5573,
§"The case $p=2$ and de Rham–Witt cohomology"). $T_1(\mathcal X,\ell)\Leftrightarrow\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)[\ell^\infty]$
finite $\Leftrightarrow T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)=0$ is elementary from the Kummer sequence (Proposition D.2.1 below).

**[THEOREM] D.1.4.2 (Grothendieck; Milne 1981; Geisser 2020).** For $E/K$ with minimal regular model $\mathcal E$
(which has a section), $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)\cong\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ canonically
(Grothendieck, *Le groupe de Brauer III*, in: Dix exposés sur la cohomologie des schémas, North-Holland 1968, §4;
Ulmer arXiv:1101.1939, Lecture 3, Thm. "Sha–Br"). For a general fibred surface $\mathcal X\to C$ with generic fibre $X$ of
index $\delta$, if the groups are finite then
$|\operatorname{Sha}(J_X)|\prod_v\delta_v\delta_v'=|\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)|\,\delta^2$ ($\delta_v,\delta_v'$ local index and
period), and $|\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal X)|$ is a square: Liu–Lorenzini–Raynaud, Invent. Math. 159 (2005), 673–676, DOI
10.1007/s00222-004-0403-2, with the formula corrected in Geisser, *Comparing the Brauer group to the Tate–Shafarevich
group*, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 19 (2020), 965–970, DOI 10.1017/S1474748018000294 (arXiv:1712.06249), and
Liu–Lorenzini–Raynaud, *Corrigendum*, Invent. Math. 214 (2018), 593–604 (arXiv:1804.11158); the error came from an
omission in Gordon's 1979 formula and does not affect the squareness conclusion. Milne's earlier comparison for
Jacobians: *Comparison of the Brauer group with the Tate–Šafarevič group*, J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo Sect. IA 28 (1981),
735–743 [journal data taken from the zbMATH review of Kato–Trihan; not independently fetched].

**[THEOREM] D.1.4.3 (Tate 1966; Milne 1975; Kato–Trihan 2003).** Let $E/K$ be an elliptic curve. Then:

1. $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le r_{\mathrm{an}}$.
2. The following are equivalent: (a) $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$; (b) $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite;
   (c) $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell^\infty]$ is finite for **one** prime $\ell$ ($\ell=p$ allowed);
   (d) $T_2(\mathcal E)$; (e) $T_1(\mathcal E,\ell)$ for one $\ell$; (f) $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)[\ell^\infty]$ finite for one $\ell$.
3. If these hold, then the leading-term formula of D.1.2 holds, and so does $AT(\mathcal E)$.
4. If $K'/K$ is a finite extension and BSD holds for $E/K'$, then it holds for $E/K$.

*Attribution with exact scope.* Items 1, 2 (for $\ell\ne p$) and 3 up to $p$-powers: Tate 1966, §4–5. Item 2 with
$\ell=p$ and the $p$-part of item 3 for $\mathcal E$ when $p>2$: Milne 1975 (via D.1.4.1 and D.1.4.2; the $p=2$ case
completed as in D.1.4.1). Items 2–3 for arbitrary abelian varieties $A/K$ and all $\ell$ including $\ell=p$ and $p=2$,
by a direct comparison of flat cohomology with log-crystalline/syntomic cohomology and without passing through a
surface: Kato–Trihan, *On the conjectures of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer in characteristic $p>0$*, Invent. Math. 153
(2003), 537–592, DOI 10.1007/s00222-003-0299-2, main theorem: *if $\operatorname{Sha}(A/K)[\ell^\infty]$ is finite for
some prime $\ell$ then BSD (rank and Tate's leading-term formula) holds for $A/K$.* Intermediate results: Schneider,
*Zur Vermutung von Birch und Swinnerton-Dyer über globalen Funktionenkörpern*, Math. Ann. 260 (1982), 495–510, DOI
10.1007/BF01457028 (abelian varieties, prime-to-$p$ part: $\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$ finite for one $\ell\ne p$
implies the prime-to-$p$ part of the formula); Bauer, *On the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer for abelian
varieties over function fields in characteristic $p>0$*, Invent. Math. 108 (1992), 263–287, DOI 10.1007/BF02100606
($p$-part for $A$ with everywhere good reduction). Item 4: Tate 1966 via $T_2$ (Ulmer arXiv:1101.1939, Lecture 3,
Thm. "BSD–Tate"(4)). The equivalence $AT(\mathcal E)\Leftrightarrow$ BSD$(E/K)$ *including leading terms* is
Gordon (Compos. Math. 38 (1979)) with Geisser's correction, and, unconditionally and with two proofs,
Lichtenbaum–Ramachandran–Suzuki, *The conjectures of Artin–Tate and Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer*, Épijournal Géom.
Algébrique 6 (2022), DOI 10.46298/epiga.2022.7482 (arXiv:2101.10222).

**Two consequences that have no analogue over $\mathbb Q$ (proved on the page from D.1.4.3).**

**[THEOREM] D.1.4.4 (analytic rank $0$).** If $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$ then $E(K)$ is finite, $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite,
and the leading-term formula holds. *Proof.* $0\le r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$ forces equality in D.1.4.3(1),
hence D.1.4.3(2)(b) and (3). $\square$ Over $\mathbb Q$ the corresponding statement ($L(E,1)\ne0\Rightarrow E(\mathbb Q)$
finite and $\operatorname{Sha}$ finite) is a theorem of Kolyvagin (Math. USSR-Izv. 32 (1989), 523–541) and of Kato
(Astérisque 295 (2004)) by an entirely different mechanism (Euler systems), and the leading term is known only in
$p$-parts under hypotheses (see `B-…`, `A-…`).

**[THEOREM] D.1.4.5 (Frobenius semisimplicity from finiteness of Sha).** If $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell_0^\infty]$ is finite
for one prime $\ell_0$, then for every $\ell\ne p$ the Frobenius acts semisimply on the generalised $q$-eigenspace of
$V=H^1(\bar C,\mathcal F_\ell\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)$, i.e. $\delta_\ell(E/K)=0$; equivalently $s_\ell=r_{\mathrm{an}}$.
*Proof.* Proposition D.5.1 below gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_\ell=g_q\le m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ for every $\ell\ne p$;
D.1.4.3 gives $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$. $\square$

### D.1.5 Known cases

**[THEOREM] D.1.5.1 (constant and isotrivial curves).** BSD holds for every isotrivial $E/K$.
*Source.* Constant $A=A_0\times_kK$: Milne, *The Tate–Šafarevič group of a constant abelian variety*, Invent. Math. 6
(1968), 91–105, DOI 10.1007/BF01389836 (finiteness of Sha; then D.1.4.3). Equivalently: $\mathcal E$ is birational to
$E_0\times C$ and $T_1$ for a product of curves follows from Tate's theorem on endomorphisms of abelian varieties over
finite fields (Tate, Invent. Math. 2 (1966), 134–144). Isotrivial: reduce to constant by D.1.4.3(4).

**[THEOREM] D.1.5.2 (low height over $k(t)$).** If $K=k(t)$ and $E$ has height $h\le2$ (i.e. $\mathcal E$ is a rational or
a K3 surface), BSD holds. Rational surfaces: $T_2$ is elementary. K3: Artin–Swinnerton-Dyer, *The Shafarevich–Tate
conjecture for pencils of elliptic curves on K3 surfaces*, Invent. Math. 20 (1973), 249–266, DOI 10.1007/BF01394097;
also the Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces over finite fields: Charles, Invent. Math. 194 (2013), 119–145 ($p\ge5$),
Madapusi Pera, Invent. Math. 201 (2015), 625–668 ($p$ odd), Kim–Madapusi Pera, Forum Math. Sigma 4 (2016) ($p=2$,
with erratum 2020), Ito–Ito–Koshikawa, Forum Math. Sigma 9 (2021) (via CM liftings).

**[THEOREM] D.1.5.3 (domination by products of curves; unbounded rank with BSD).** If $\mathcal E$ is dominated by a
product of curves then BSD holds for $E$ ($T_1$ descends along dominant maps and holds for products of curves). Cases:
Shioda's "four monomials" (Shioda 1986; Ulmer arXiv:1101.1939, Lecture 1, Thm. "4-monos": $E$ birational to
$V(f)$, $f\in k[t,x,y]$ a sum of exactly four monomials satisfying Shioda's determinant condition); Ulmer, *Elliptic
curves with large rank over function fields*, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), 295–315 (arXiv:math/0109163): the curves
$y^2+xy=x^3-t^d$ over $\mathbb F_p(t)$ satisfy BSD (their surfaces are dominated by Fermat surfaces) and have
$\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb F_p(t))$ unbounded as $d$ varies, in fact asymptotically maximal for the conductor; Berger,
*Towers of surfaces dominated by products of curves and elliptic curves of large rank over function fields*, J. Number
Theory 128 (2008): $E$ birational to $f(x)=t^dg(y)$, $(d,p)=1$; Ulmer, *L-functions with large analytic rank and abelian
varieties with large algebraic rank over function fields*, Invent. Math. 167 (2007), DOI 10.1007/s00222-006-0018-x
(arXiv:math/0609664): for every $p$ and $g$, absolutely simple $g$-dimensional abelian varieties over $\mathbb F_p(t)$
satisfying BSD with unbounded rank. Surveys: Ulmer arXiv:1101.1939 (Park City 2009), arXiv:1203.5573 (CRM 2010).

**[THEOREM]/[CONDITIONAL] D.1.5.4 (analytic rank $\le1$).** $r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$: D.1.4.4, unconditional.
$r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$: Ulmer, *Geometric non-vanishing*, Invent. Math. 159 (2005), 133–186, DOI 10.1007/s00222-004-0386-z
(arXiv:math/0305321), Introduction, second theorem (label "intro-elliptic" in the arXiv source): if $p>3$ and $E/K$ is non-isotrivial there are a finite separable
$F'/K$ and a quadratic $F''/F'$ such that $E/F'$ is semistable with a place $\infty$ of split multiplicative reduction,
$\infty$ is not split in $F''$, all other bad places are split in $F''$, $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/F',s)=r_{\mathrm{an}}$, and
$\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/F'',s)$ is odd and $\le r_{\mathrm{an}}+1$. Hence, *given a Gross–Zagier formula for Heegner
points on the Drinfeld modular curve attached to $(E/F',F''/F')$*, $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1\Rightarrow$ BSD for $E/K$
(Ulmer loc. cit., discussion after the theorem, and Ulmer arXiv:math/0305320, §3.8). Such a formula is proved by
Rück–Tipp, *Heegner points and $L$-series of automorphic cusp forms of Drinfeld type*, Doc. Math. 5 (2000), 365–444, DOI
10.4171/DM/85, for $K=\mathbb F_q(t)$ (odd $q$) with hypotheses on $(N,\infty)$ and on the quadratic field; Brown,
*Heegner Modules and Elliptic Curves*, LNM 1849, Springer 2004, DOI 10.1007/b98488, proves finiteness of Sha (hence BSD)
for a class of elliptic curves with split multiplicative reduction at $\infty$ by a Kolyvagin-type argument; Yun–Zhang II
(§D.3.2, Remark after Thm. 1.3) give the $r=1$ case of their formula as "a direct analogue of the Gross–Zagier formula"
and record that "D. Ulmer has an unpublished proof of a formula similar to" it. **Status of record:** *"$r_{\mathrm{an}}=1
\Rightarrow$ BSD" for all non-isotrivial $E$ over all $K$ with $p>3$ is `[CONDITIONAL]` on a Gross–Zagier formula in the
generality of Ulmer's theorem, which is not in print in that generality as of 2026-09-11 (searches in §D.7).* This is
the exact function-field counterpart of Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin, with the roles reversed: the "Kolyvagin" half is
automatic (D.1.4.3), the "Gross–Zagier" half is the missing piece.

## D.2 Why the function-field proof works, step by step, and what fails over $\mathbb Q$

Throughout §D.2, $E/K$ is non-constant and $\ell\ne p$ unless stated. The proof of D.1.4.3 has six steps.

**Step 1 (the $L$-function is a characteristic polynomial).** D.1.1: $L(E,T)=\det(1-T\,\mathrm{Fr}\mid V)$; the
Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula $\sum_{x\in C(\mathbb F_{q^n})}\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Fr}_x\mid\mathcal F_{\bar x})=\sum_i(-1)^i\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Fr}^n\mid H^i(\bar C,\mathcal F))$
turns the Euler product into an alternating product of characteristic polynomials, and Deligne's purity puts all zeros
on $\Re s=1$. Thus $r_{\mathrm{an}}=m_q$ (algebraic multiplicity).

**Step 2 (the $L$-function sits in the middle cohomology of a surface).** For the minimal regular model, the Leray
spectral sequence for $\pi:\mathcal E\to C$ degenerates and
$$H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Q_\ell(1))\;=\;H^2(\bar C,\mathbb Q_\ell(1))\ \oplus\ H^1(\bar C,R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell(1))\ \oplus\ H^0(\bar C,R^2\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell(1)),
\qquad R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell\cong j_*j^*R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell=\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell,$$
$G_k$-equivariantly, the outer two summands (the *trivial part* $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}$) having $G_k$-invariants
of dimensions $1$ and $1+\sum_v(f_v-1)$, spanned by the classes of $O$, $F$ and the fibre components (Ulmer
arXiv:1203.5573, §"Leray spectral sequence", Cor. "R1H1-cohom", and §"Comparison of the Selmer group and
$H^1(\bar C,R^1\pi_*\mathbb Z_\ell(1))^{G_k}$"). Hence
$$\zeta(\mathcal E,s)^{-1}\ \text{has at }s=1\text{ the order }\ \rho^{\mathrm{an}}(\mathcal E):=\dim\text{gen.eigsp}_{q}\,H^2=2+\sum_v(f_v-1)+r_{\mathrm{an}},$$
which is the cohomological form of the elementary identity
$L(E,T)=\frac{Z(C,T)Z(C,qT)}{Z(\mathcal E,T)}\prod_{v\ \mathrm{bad}}(\cdots)$ (Ulmer arXiv:1101.1939, Lecture 3, eq. (Z–L)).

**Step 3 (points are divisors: Shioda–Tate).** **[THEOREM]** $D\mapsto D\cdot E$ induces
$L^1\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)/L^2\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\cong E(K)$ (non-constant case), where $L^1$ = classes of divisors of
degree $0$ on the generic fibre and $L^2$ = classes of divisors supported in fibres; hence
$\rho=2+\sum_v(f_v-1)+r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ (Tate 1966; Shioda, *On elliptic modular surfaces*, J. Math. Soc. Japan 24
(1972); Shioda, Comment. Math. Univ. St. Pauli 39 (1990) [journal data unverified]; Ulmer arXiv:1101.1939,
Lecture 3, Thm. "Shioda–Tate").

**Step 4 (cycle classes, Kummer, and the Brauer group).**

**Proposition D.2.1 [THEOREM, proof on the page].** For every prime $\ell$ (including $\ell=p$, with flat cohomology)
there is an exact sequence
$$0\to\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\to H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Z_\ell(1))^{G_k}\to T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)\to0 .$$
*Proof.* The Kummer sequence on $\bar{\mathcal E}$ and $\operatorname{Pic}(\bar{\mathcal E})/\ell^n=\mathrm{NS}(\bar{\mathcal E})/\ell^n$
($\operatorname{Pic}^0$ is divisible) give $0\to\mathrm{NS}(\bar{\mathcal E})/\ell^n\to H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mu_{\ell^n})\to\operatorname{Br}(\bar{\mathcal E})[\ell^n]\to0$;
pass to the inverse limit ($T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\bar{\mathcal E})$ is torsion-free since $\operatorname{Br}(\bar{\mathcal E})[\ell]$ is finite), take
$G_k$-invariants using $H^1(G_k,\mathrm{NS}(\bar{\mathcal E})\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell)$ finite and
$(\mathrm{NS}(\bar{\mathcal E})\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell)^{G_k}=\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell$ (Lang: $H^1(G_k,\operatorname{Pic}^0(\bar{\mathcal E}))=0$),
and identify $T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\bar{\mathcal E})^{G_k}=T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)$ via Hochschild–Serre (the kernel of
$H^2(\mathcal E,\mathbb Z_\ell(1))\to H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Z_\ell(1))^{G_k}$ is finite and $T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)$ is torsion-free).
This is Tate 1966 §5 / Ulmer arXiv:1203.5573 §"Basic exact sequences", eq. (cycleclass). $\square$

Combined with D.1.4.2 ($\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)\cong\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$) and the descent sequence
$0\to E(K)\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\to\operatorname{Sel}(E/K,\mathbb Z_\ell)\to T_\ell\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)\to0$
(finiteness of the groups $\operatorname{Sel}(E/K,\ell^n)$: classical for $\ell\ne p$, Milne, Bull. LMS 2 (1970) for $\ell=p$),
Step 4 says: *algebraic classes $\subset$ Tate classes, with quotient $T_\ell\operatorname{Br}=T_\ell\operatorname{Sha}$*.

**Step 5 (the inequality chain).** Steps 2–4 give (Proposition D.5.1 for details)
$$r_{\mathrm{alg}}\ \le\ s_\ell\ =\ g_q\ \le\ m_q\ =\ r_{\mathrm{an}},$$
with $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=s_\ell\iff\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$ finite, and $g_q=m_q\iff$ Frobenius semisimple at $q$.
Tate's theorem that $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=s_\ell$ forces $g_q=m_q$ is a statement about *pairings*: the intersection form
on $\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathbb Q$ is non-degenerate (Hodge index theorem), it is the restriction of the cup product,
and non-degeneracy of the cup product on the full eigenspace $H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Q_\ell(1))^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$ is
equivalent to semisimplicity (Lemma D.5.2). This is the content of Tate's diagram
$e=g^*\circ j\circ f\circ h$ (Ulmer arXiv:1203.5573, §"Artin–Tate conjecture").

**Step 6 (the leading term).** With $T_1$ in hand, Tate computes the "$z$-values" ($\#\ker/\#\operatorname{coker}$) of the
quasi-isomorphisms in his diagram: the intersection form contributes $|\mathrm{NS}_{\mathrm{tors}}|/R(\mathcal E)$, Poincaré
duality and the Hochschild–Serre edge map contribute $|\mathrm{NS}_{\mathrm{tors}}|\cdot q^{(\cdots)}$ ($p$-adic cohomology
enters only here), the Kummer map contributes $|\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)|$, and the map invariants$\to$coinvariants contributes
the leading coefficient of $P_2(\mathcal E,q^{-s})/(1-q^{1-s})^\rho$. Milne 1975 and Kato–Trihan supply the $p$-adic part
(crystalline/log-crystalline/syntomic cohomology vs. flat cohomology of $\mu_{p^n}$); Kato–Trihan bypass the surface
by working directly with the Néron model and its log Dieudonné crystal.

### D.2.2 What each step needs and what $\mathbb Q$ lacks

### D.2.3 The pairing coincidence (why Step 5 is a theorem over $K$)

**Proposition D.2.3 [THEOREM] (Manin, Shioda; Ulmer arXiv:1203.5573, §"Heights", Prop.).** Let $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q}\subset\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathbb Q$
be the trivial sublattice. The intersection form is non-degenerate on $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q}$; for $P\in E(K)$ let
$\phi(P)\in\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q}^\perp\subset\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathbb Q$ be the orthogonal projection of the class of the
closure $\bar P$. Then $(P,Q)\mapsto-\phi(P)\cdot\phi(Q)$ is a positive-definite $\mathbb Q$-valued form on
$E(K)\otimes\mathbb Q$ and
$$\langle P,Q\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}=-(\log q)\,\phi(P)\cdot\phi(Q).$$
(Manin, Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR 28 (1964) = AMS Transl. (2) 59 (1966), 82–110; Shioda 1990; Ulmer loc. cit. via Gross's local
heights.) Since the cycle class map is compatible with cup product and intersection product (SGA 4½, *Cycle*), the
Néron–Tate height on $E(K)$ *is* the cup product on $H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Q_\ell(1))^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}\cap\mathbb T^\perp\cong M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$
restricted to algebraic classes, for every $\ell$. Over $\mathbb Q$ the Néron–Tate height is real-valued and cannot be
restricted to a $\mathbb Q_p$-vector space of Selmer classes; the $p$-adic height pairing is a different object
(D.5.3). This is the exact point where Tate's proof of Step 5 stops transferring.

### D.2.4 What Arakelov geometry, Deninger's programme and Connes–Consani would have to supply

**Arakelov geometry.** Supplies Step 3 completely (Faltings–Hriljac) and an intersection theory on
$\overline{\mathcal E}$; supplies no cohomology theory, no Frobenius, and no interpretation of $L(E,s)$. **[THEOREM]** as
far as Step 3; nothing beyond.

**Deninger's programme.** **[CONJECTURE] (Deninger).** For an arithmetic scheme $\mathcal X$ (with compactification
$\overline{\mathcal X}$) there should be complex vector spaces $H^n(\overline{\mathcal X},\mathcal C)$, possibly infinite-dimensional,
with an endomorphism $\theta$ acting as a derivation for cup product, such that
$$\text{(for a number field }k)\quad H^0=\mathbb C\ (\theta=0),\quad H^2\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{tr}\ }\mathbb C(-1)\ (\theta=\mathrm{id}),\quad
\text{eigenvalues of }\theta\text{ on }H^1(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_k},\mathcal C)=\text{non-trivial zeros of }\zeta_k\text{ with algebraic multiplicities},$$
the cup product $H^{1,\theta\sim\alpha}\times H^{1,\theta\sim1-\alpha}\to\mathbb C$ being perfect; and for motives (e.g. $H^1$
of $E/\mathbb Q$) $L(E,s)=\det_\infty\big(\tfrac1{2\pi}(s-\theta)\mid H^1_{\mathrm{ar}}\big)$ as a regularised determinant, with
$E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C$ identified via an Abel–Jacobi map with the $1$-eigenspace and the Néron–Tate height with the
restriction of the scalar product $\mathrm{tr}(h\cup\ast h')$ (Deninger, *Motivic $L$-functions and regularized determinants*,
Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 55 (1994), 707–743; *Some analogies between number theory and dynamical systems on foliated
spaces*, Doc. Math. Extra Vol. ICM I (1998), 163–186, DOI 10.4171/dms/1-1/2; *The Hilbert–Polya strategy and height
pairings*, arXiv:1001.1621; the list of properties 2.1–2.7 is quoted from Deninger arXiv:2204.02714, §2). This is
exactly a "Step 1–2" for $\mathbb Q$: it would make $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ an algebraic multiplicity and $E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C$ an
eigenspace, so that BSD(rank) would become the pair "semisimplicity of $\theta$ at $1$" + "Abel–Jacobi surjectivity",
precisely as over $K$.

**[THEOREM] D.2.4.1 (Deninger's no-go theorem).** *For a number field $k$ there is no functorial real-valued cohomology
theory $H^\bullet(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_k},\mathcal R)$ with endomorphism $\theta_{\mathbb R}$ such that
$H^\bullet(\ ,\mathcal C):=H^\bullet(\ ,\mathcal R)\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$, $\theta=\theta_{\mathbb R}\otimes\mathrm{id}$, satisfies
$\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,H^1(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_k},\mathcal C)^{\theta\sim1/2})=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}L(V_\pi,s)$
for every irreducible representation $V_\pi$ of $G=\operatorname{Gal}(k/\mathbb Q)$ ($k/\mathbb Q$ Galois).* Proof idea: take $k$ with a
symplectic irreducible $V_\pi$ of root number $-1$ (quaternion fields), so $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}L(V_\pi,s)$ is odd, while a
real structure forces symplectic constituents to occur with even multiplicity. Deninger, *There is no "Weil-"cohomology
theory with real coefficients for arithmetic curves*, Ann. Sc. Norm. Super. Pisa Cl. Sci. (5) (2024), 1717–1725, DOI
10.2422/2036-2145.202204_005 (arXiv:2204.02714), Thm. 2.13. Consequence for `[GAP D.1]`: any solution must have
genuinely complex (or $K_{\mathbb R}$-valued, quaternionic) coefficients without a real structure, so the "leafwise
cohomology of a foliated dynamical system" model (which always has a real structure) must be twisted. Deninger's
constructions of dynamical systems for arithmetic schemes via sheafified rational Witt vectors (arXiv:1807.06400,
v4 2024) have "some (but not all) the expected properties" (his words). Deninger, arXiv:2504.15767 (to appear in J.
Number Theory, 2026): assuming Serre's vanishing conjecture at $s=1/2$, the functor $k\mapsto V_k=H^{1,\theta\sim1/2}$
is determined up to a 2-torsion abelian automorphism group; no construction. **[HEURISTIC]** overall.

**Connes–Consani.** Their programme (arithmetic site, scaling site, $\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}$ as a curve over
$\mathbb F_1$/$\mathbb S$) aims at Weil's proof of RH for $\zeta$; verified status: Riemann–Roch theorems for
$\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}$ (arXiv:2205.01391, 2022) and for $\mathbb Z$ over $\mathbb S$ (arXiv:2306.00456, 2023); Weil
positivity at the archimedean place via the semi-local trace formula (Selecta Math. 2021, arXiv:2006.13771); a
"Jacobian of $\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}$" (arXiv:2602.15941, Feb. 2026); "absolute geometry of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$" realising
Scholze's untilt heuristic and a real analogue of the Fargues–Fontaine curve (arXiv:2606.06604, June 2026); the
"absolute twistor line" (arXiv:2609.00299, 31 Aug. 2026). None of these papers treats $L(E,s)$ for an elliptic curve
or produces a cohomology with a Frobenius whose characteristic polynomial is an $L$-function; all are **[HEURISTIC]** for
the purposes of this file.

**Scholze's "shtukas for $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$".** See §D.4; it aims at Step 2 (a category $\mathrm{Kt}_{\mathbb Q}$ replacing
"$\bar{\mathbb F}_p$-vector spaces with Frobenius") rather than at Step 1.

**[GAP D.1] (Cohomological realisation of $L(E,s)$ over $\mathbb Q$).** *Construct a $\mathbb C$-linear (or $K_{\mathbb R}$-valued)
abelian tensor category $\mathcal T$ with a functorial endomorphism $\theta$ of each object, a functor
$\mathcal H^1:\{\text{elliptic curves over }\mathbb Q\}\to\mathcal T$, and a regularised determinant $\det_\infty$, such that for every
$E/\mathbb Q$: (a) $L(E,s)=\det_\infty(\tfrac1{2\pi}(s-\theta)\mid\mathcal H^1(E))$; (b) $\dim\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta\sim1}<\infty$ and there
is a functorial injection $E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C\hookrightarrow\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta=1}$; (c) there is a $\theta$-compatible
non-degenerate pairing on $\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta\sim1}$ restricting to the Néron–Tate height on $E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C$.
Deninger's theorem D.2.4.1 shows (a)–(c) cannot hold with real coefficients if the analogous statements are required
functorially for Artin motives.* Given (a)–(c), BSD(rank) is equivalent to: [$\theta$ semisimple at $1$ on
$\mathcal H^1(E)$] and [the injection in (b) is onto $\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta=1}$]; by Lemma D.5.2 the first is implied by the second
together with (c). No approach to `[GAP D.1]` beyond the heuristic programmes above is known to this author.

## D.3 Yun–Zhang: the higher Gross–Zagier formula

Sources: Z. Yun, W. Zhang, *Shtukas and the Taylor expansion of $L$-functions*, Ann. of Math. (2) 186 (2017), no. 3,
767–911, DOI 10.4007/annals.2017.186.3.2, arXiv:1512.02683 (v3, April 2017) — "YZ I"; *Shtukas and the Taylor
expansion of $L$-functions (II)*, Ann. of Math. (2) 189 (2019), no. 2, 393–526, DOI 10.4007/annals.2019.189.2.2,
arXiv:1712.08026 (v2, June 2020) — "YZ II". All statements below were checked against the arXiv TeX sources
(§D.7); theorem numbers refer to the arXiv versions' introductions.

### D.3.1 YZ I: exact setting and statement

**Setting (YZ I §1.1–1.3).** $k=\mathbb F_q$ with $p=\operatorname{char}k>2$. $X/k$ smooth projective geometrically
connected of genus $g$, $F=k(X)$. $\nu:X'\to X$ a finite **étale** double cover with $X'$ geometrically connected
($g'=2g-1$), $F'=k(X')$, $\eta=\eta_{F'/F}$ the associated quadratic idèle class character, $\sigma$ the involution.
$G=\mathrm{PGL}_2$, $K=\prod_xG(\mathcal O_x)$, $\mathscr H=C_c^\infty(G(\mathbb A)/\!/K,\mathbb Q)$ the spherical Hecke algebra.
An *everywhere unramified cuspidal automorphic representation* $\pi$ is an irreducible $\mathscr H$-submodule
$\mathcal A_\pi\subset C_c^\infty(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)/K,\mathbb Q)$; $E_\pi:=\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr H}(\mathcal A_\pi)$ is a
(totally real) number field, the *coefficient field*, and $\lambda_\pi:\mathscr H\to E_\pi$ the character.
$L(\pi,s)$ is the complete standard $L$-function, a polynomial of degree $4(g-1)$ in $q^{-s-1/2}$ with coefficients in
$\mathcal O_{E_\pi}$; $L(\pi_{F'},s)=L(\pi,s)L(\pi\otimes\eta,s)$ (degree $8(g-1)$), with functional equation
$L(\pi_{F'},s)=\epsilon(\pi_{F'},s)L(\pi_{F'},1-s)$, $\epsilon(\pi_{F'},s)=q^{-8(g-1)(s-1/2)}$. Put
$$\mathscr L(\pi_{F'},s):=\epsilon(\pi_{F'},s)^{-1/2}\frac{L(\pi_{F'},s)}{L(\pi,\mathrm{Ad},1)},\qquad
\epsilon(\pi_{F'},s)^{-1/2}:=q^{4(g-1)(s-1/2)},\qquad \mathscr L(\pi_{F'},s)=\mathscr L(\pi_{F'},1-s),$$
$$\mathscr L^{(r)}(\pi_{F'},\tfrac12):=\frac{d^r}{ds^r}\Big|_{s=1/2}\mathscr L(\pi_{F'},s)\ \in\ E_\pi\cdot(\log q)^r,\qquad
\mathscr L^{(r)}(\pi_{F'},\tfrac12)=0\ \text{for }r\text{ odd}.$$

**Geometry (YZ I §1.4).** $r\ge0$ **even**; $\mu\in\{\pm\}^r$ with exactly $r/2$ plus signs (YZ I §5.1.2, "parity
condition"). $\mathrm{Sht}^r_G$ is the moduli stack of $G$-shtukas with $r$ modifications (legs): a Deligne–Mumford stack,
locally of finite type, with $\pi_G:\mathrm{Sht}^r_G\to X^r$ smooth of relative dimension $r$ (so $\dim\mathrm{Sht}^r_G=2r$).
$T=(\operatorname{Res}_{F'/F}\mathbb G_m)/\mathbb G_m$; $\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T\to X'^r$ is a torsor under the finite Picard stack
$\operatorname{Pic}_{X'}(k)/\operatorname{Pic}_X(k)$, so $\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T$ is a proper smooth DM stack of dimension $r$ over $k$. There
is a finite morphism $\theta^\mu:\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T\to\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G:=\mathrm{Sht}^r_G\times_{X^r}X'^r$ and the **Heegner–Drinfeld
cycle** is $\theta^\mu_*[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]\in\mathrm{Ch}_{c,r}(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G)_{\mathbb Q}$ (proper cycles of dimension $r$ = half the
dimension). Let $\widetilde W\subset\mathrm{Ch}_{c,r}(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G)_{\mathbb Q}$ be the $\mathscr H$-submodule generated by it,
$W=\widetilde W/(\text{kernel of the intersection pairing})$.

**[THEOREM] D.3.1.1 (YZ I, Thm. 1.5).** $W=W_{\mathrm{Eis}}\oplus\bigoplus_\pi W_\pi$ (orthogonal, $\mathscr H$-stable), $\pi$ over the
finitely many everywhere unramified cuspidal $\pi$, with $W_\pi=W[\mathfrak m_\pi]$ an $E_\pi$-vector space of dimension
$\le1$.

**[THEOREM] D.3.1.2 (YZ I, Thm. 1.6, cycle version).** Let $[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]_\pi\in W_\pi$ be the projection of the
Heegner–Drinfeld cycle. Then, in $E_\pi$,
$$\boxed{\ \frac{1}{2(\log q)^r}\,|\omega_X|\,\mathscr L^{(r)}(\pi_{F'},\tfrac12)\;=\;\big([\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]_\pi,\ [\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]_\pi\big)_\pi\ },
\qquad |\omega_X|=q^{-\deg\omega_X}=q^{-(2g-2)},$$
where $(\ ,\ )_\pi$ is the $E_\pi$-bilinear lift of the intersection pairing.

**[THEOREM] D.3.1.3 (YZ I, Thm. 1.10, cohomological version).** For $\ell\ne p$, $V'_{\mathbb Q_\ell}:=H^{2r}_c(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G\otimes_k\bar k,\mathbb Q_\ell)(r)$
decomposes (Thm. 1.8) as $V'_{\mathrm{Eis}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathfrak m}V'_{\mathfrak m}$ over finitely many maximal ideals of
$\mathscr H_{\mathbb Q_\ell}$, the $V'_{\mathfrak m}$ finite-dimensional generalised eigenspaces; for $\lambda\mid\ell$ a place of $E_\pi$
and $[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]_{\pi,\lambda}$ the projection of the cycle class to $V'_{\pi,\lambda}$, the same identity holds in
$E_{\pi,\lambda}$ with the $E_{\pi,\lambda}$-bilinear cup product; in particular the right side lies in $E_\pi$.

**[THEOREM] D.3.1.4 (YZ I, Thms. 1.11, 1.12).** The intersection pairing is positive definite on
$W_{\mathrm{cusp}}=W_{\mathrm{Eis}}^\perp$ (evidence for the Hodge standard conjecture on $\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G$); and, for $r>0$
even, $\langle\theta^\mu_*[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T],\theta^\mu_*[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]\rangle=\frac{2^{r+2}}{(\log q)^r}L^{(r)}(\eta,0)$.
For $r=0$, D.3.1.2 is Waldspurger's formula (YZ I, Remark after Thm. 1.6).

*Method (YZ I §1.5).* Comparison of two relative trace formulae: Jacquet's RTF for the $A\times A$-period with
$|h_1h_2|^s\eta(h_2)$, whose spectral side is $\tfrac12|\omega_X|\mathscr L(\pi_{F'},s+\tfrac12)\lambda_\pi(f)$, differentiated $r$
times; and the intersection number $\mathbb I_r(f)=\langle\theta^\mu_*[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T],f*\theta^\mu_*[\mathrm{Sht}^\mu_T]\rangle$, expanded
via the cohomological spectral decomposition. The key identity $\mathbb I_r(f)=(\log q)^{-r}\mathbb J_r(f)$ is proved for enough
Hecke functions $f=h_D$ ($D$ effective of degree $\ge\max(2g'-1,2g)$) by interpreting both sides as traces of Frobenius
compositions on the cohomology of Hitchin-type moduli stacks $\mathcal M_d,\mathcal N_d\to\mathcal A_d$ and applying Ngô's
perverse continuation principle. The proof is *global* (no local orbital-integral comparison) — YZ I, Remark in §1.5.

**Quotation (YZ I §1, verbatim from the TeX).** "The very existence of such moduli stacks presents a striking difference
between a function field and a number field. In the number field case, the analogous spaces only exist (at least for
the time being) when $r\le1$." and "This leads us to speculate that, contrary to the usual belief, central derivatives of
arbitrary order of motivic $L$-functions (for instance, those associated to elliptic curves) should bear some geometric
meaning in the number field case. However, due to the lack of the analog of $\mathrm{Sht}^r_G$ for arbitrary $r$ in the number
field case, we could not formulate a precise conjecture."

### D.3.2 YZ II: ramification, odd $r$, products of derivatives

**Setting (YZ II §1.1).** $p\ne2$. $\pi$ a cuspidal automorphic representation of $G(\mathbb A_F)$ which is an unramified
twist of the Steinberg representation at each $x$ in a finite set $\Sigma\subset|X|$ and unramified elsewhere
("square-free level"), $N=\deg\Sigma$. $\nu:X'\to X$ a double cover, $X'$ geometrically connected, **possibly ramified**,
with ramification locus $R$, $\rho:=\deg R$, $g'-1=2(g-1)+\rho/2$. **Hypothesis:** $R\cap\Sigma=\emptyset$.
Normalised $L$-functions
$\mathscr L(\pi,s+\tfrac12)=q^{(2g-2+N/2)s}L(\pi,s+\tfrac12)$, $\mathscr L(\pi\otimes\eta,s+\tfrac12)=q^{(2g-2+\rho+N/2)s}L(\pi\otimes\eta,s+\tfrac12)$
(note: unlike YZ I, $L(\pi,\mathrm{Ad},1)$ is *not* absorbed), and the two-variable function
$\mathscr L_{F'/F}(\pi,s_1,s_2):=\mathscr L(\pi,s_1+s_2+\tfrac12)\mathscr L(\pi\otimes\eta,s_1-s_2+\tfrac12)$, with
$\mathscr L_{F'/F}(\pi,s_1,s_2)=(-1)^{r(\pi_{F'})}\mathscr L_{F'/F}(\pi,-s_1,-s_2)$, $r(\pi_{F'}):=\#\{x\in\Sigma\text{ inert in }X'\}$,
so $(-1)^{r(\pi_{F'})}$ is the root number of $\pi_{F'}$. Put
$\mathscr L^{(r_+,r_-)}_{F'/F}(\pi):=\partial_{s_1}^{r_+}\partial_{s_2}^{r_-}\mathscr L_{F'/F}(\pi,s_1,s_2)|_{s_1=s_2=0}$; it vanishes unless
$r_++r_-\equiv r(\pi_{F'})\pmod 2$.

**Heegner hypothesis and the parity of $r$.** Write $\Sigma=\Sigma_f\sqcup\Sigma_\infty$ with **all places of $\Sigma_f$
split** and **all places of $\Sigma_\infty$ inert** in $X'$ (this decomposition exists uniquely because $R\cap\Sigma=\emptyset$).
The integer $r\ge0$ must satisfy $r\equiv\#\Sigma_\infty\pmod2$; the places of $\Sigma_\infty$ play the role of the
archimedean place of $\mathbb Q$ (Atkin–Lehner-type modification of the shtuka there), the places of $\Sigma_f$ that of primes
dividing the level. **This is how odd $r$ — and in particular the Gross–Zagier case $r=1$ — is reached: $r\equiv\#\Sigma_\infty$,
and $\#\Sigma_\infty=r(\pi_{F'})$ is the parity of the root number.** The moduli stack $\mathrm{Sht}^r_G(\Sigma;\Sigma_\infty)$
of $G$-shtukas with $r$ legs and Iwahori level at $\Sigma$ is a smooth DM stack of dimension $2r$, locally of finite type,
with $\Pi^r_G:\mathrm{Sht}^r_G(\Sigma;\Sigma_\infty)\to X^r\times\mathfrak S_\infty$, $\mathfrak S_\infty=\prod_{x\in\Sigma_\infty}\operatorname{Spec}k(x)$
(YZ II, §3, Proposition on $\mathrm{Sht}^r_G(\Sigma;\Sigma_\infty)$). Its base change $\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\Sigma_\infty)$ to $X'^r\times\mathfrak S'_\infty$ splits over $\bar k$
into components $\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi)$ indexed by choices $\xi$ of geometric points over $\Sigma'_\infty$. The Heegner–Drinfeld
cycle $\mathcal Z^\mu(\xi)\in\mathrm{Ch}_{c,r}(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi))_{\mathbb Q}$ is the pushforward of $[\mathrm{Sht}^{\underline\mu}_T(\mu_\infty\cdot\xi)]$
(rank-one shtukas on $X'$) along $\theta'^\mu_\xi$, for a datum $\mu=(\underline\mu,\mu_f,\mu_\infty)\in\mathfrak T_{r,\Sigma}:=\{\pm1\}^r\times\operatorname{Sect}(\Sigma'_f/\Sigma_f)\times\{\pm1\}^{\Sigma_\infty}$;
$Z^\mu(\xi)\in H^{2r}_c(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi),\mathbb Q_\ell)$ its class, $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)$ its projection to the generalised eigenspace
$V'(\xi)_\pi$ for $\mathfrak m_\pi=\ker\lambda_\pi\subset\mathscr H^\Sigma_G$ (spectral decomposition: YZ II Thm. 1.1).

**[THEOREM] D.3.2.1 (YZ II, Thm. 1.2).** Under the hypotheses just listed, for $\mu,\mu'\in\mathfrak T_{r,\Sigma}$, with
$r_+=\#\{i:\mu_i=\mu'_i\}$, $r_-=\#\{i:\mu_i\ne\mu'_i\}$:
$$\boxed{\ \frac{|\omega_X|\,q^{\rho/2-N}\,\epsilon_-(\pi\otimes\eta)}{2(-\log q)^r\,L(\pi,\mathrm{Ad},1)}\;\mathscr L^{(r_+,r_-)}_{F'/F}(\pi)
\;=\;\big(Z^\mu_\pi(\xi),\,Z^{\mu'}_\pi(\xi)\big)_{\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi)}\ },$$
$|\omega_X|=q^{-(2g-2)}$, $\epsilon_-(\pi\otimes\eta)\in\{\pm1\}$ the product of the Atkin–Lehner eigenvalues of $\pi\otimes\eta$ at
the places of $\Sigma_-(\mu,\mu')\subset\Sigma$ (YZ II, §1.1, the set denoted $\Sigma_-(\mu,\mu')$), and $(\ ,\ )$ the cup product on $V'(\xi)_\pi$.

**[THEOREM] D.3.2.2 (YZ II, Thm. 1.3 and Corollary).** With $\epsilon_{r_1}=\prod_{i\le r_1}\frac{1+\sigma_i}2\prod_{i>r_1}\frac{1-\sigma_i}2$
($\sigma_i$ the Galois involution in the $i$-th $X'$-factor):
$$\frac{|\omega_X|\,q^{\rho/2-N}}{2(-\log q)^rL(\pi,\mathrm{Ad},1)}\,\mathscr L^{(r_1)}(\pi,\tfrac12)\,\mathscr L^{(r-r_1)}(\pi\otimes\eta,\tfrac12)
=\big(\epsilon_{r_1}Z^\mu_\pi(\xi),\epsilon_{r_1}Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)\big);$$
for $r_1=r$, with $Y^\mu_\pi(\xi)$ the pushforward of $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)$ to $\mathrm{Sht}^r_G(\Sigma;\xi)$:
$\frac{2^{r-1}|\omega_X|q^{\rho/2-N}}{(-\log q)^rL(\pi,\mathrm{Ad},1)}\mathscr L^{(r)}(\pi,\tfrac12)\mathscr L(\pi\otimes\eta,\tfrac12)=(Y^\mu_\pi(\xi),Y^\mu_\pi(\xi))$.
Thus products of *separate* derivatives of $L(\pi,s)$ and $L(\pi\otimes\eta,s)$ are reached — new even in the unramified case.

**The case $r=1$ (YZ II, Remark after Thm. 1.3, and §3, subsection "The case $r=1$ and Drinfeld modules").** If $\Sigma_\infty=\{\infty\}$,
$r=1$, $\mu=\mu'$, then
$$-\frac{|\omega_X|q^{\rho/2-N}}{2\log q\cdot L(\pi,\mathrm{Ad},1)}\,\mathscr L'(\pi_{F'},\tfrac12)=\big(Z^\mu_\pi(\xi),Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)\big)_{\mathrm{Sht}'^1_G(\Sigma;\xi)},$$
and $\mathrm{Sht}^1_G(\Sigma;\Sigma_\infty)|_{X-\{\infty\}}\cong\mathrm{DrMod}_2(\Sigma_f)/\operatorname{Pic}^0_X(k)$, the moduli of Drinfeld
$A$-modules of rank 2 with Iwahori level at $\Sigma_f$, $A=\Gamma(X-\{\infty\},\mathcal O_X)$ (via Blum–Stuhler). YZ II call this
"a direct analogue of the Gross–Zagier formula for modular curves" and add: "We understand that D. Ulmer has an
unpublished proof of a formula similar to (1.4). The method of our proof is quite different from that in [GZ] in that we
do not need to explicitly compute either side of the formula." *The translation of this identity into a statement about
Néron–Tate heights of Heegner points on $E(F')$ is not carried out in YZ II.*

### D.3.3 What is deduced about BSD, exactly

Let $E/F$ be a **non-isotrivial semistable** elliptic curve (so $\pi=\pi_E$ has square-free level $\Sigma=$ the
multiplicative places, and $\pi_x$ is an unramified twist of Steinberg at each $x\in\Sigma$). By Drinfeld's global
Langlands correspondence for $\mathrm{GL}_2$ over function fields (Drinfeld, Proc. ICM Helsinki 1978; *Varieties of modules of
$F$-sheaves*, Funct. Anal. Appl. 21 (1987), 107–122, DOI 10.1007/BF01078024; for all $\mathrm{GL}_n$: L. Lafforgue, Invent. Math.
147 (2002), 1–241, DOI 10.1007/s002220100174) there is a cuspidal $\pi$ with
$\rho_\pi\cong V_\ell(E)^*\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, $L(E,s)=L(\pi,s-\tfrac12)$, $L(E_{F'},s)=L(\pi_{F'},s-\tfrac12)$ (YZ II §1.3.2).
With $W'_\pi:=H^1(\bar X',j'_{!*}\nu^*\rho_\pi)$ one has $L(\pi_{F'},s-\tfrac12)=\det(1-q^{-s}\mathrm{Fr}\mid W'_\pi)$, and $W'_\pi$ is a
subquotient of $H^2(\bar{\mathcal E}',\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$ for a semistable model $\mathcal E'\to X'$ — "the $\ell$-adic Selmer group of
$E$" in YZ II's words; in the notation of §D.0 applied to $E_{F'}$, $W'_\pi(1)\cong M_{E/F'}\otimes\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell$.

**What YZ II prove (verbatim structure of YZ II §1.3.1).**

(a) **[THEOREM]** If $r\ge\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}L(\pi_{F'},s)$ and $L(\pi_{F'},s)$ is not constant (i.e. $2(4g-4+N+\rho)>0$),
then $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)\ne0$ in $H^{2r}_c(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi),\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$: by D.3.2.1 with $\mu=\mu'$ its
self-intersection is a non-zero multiple of $\mathscr L^{(r)}(\pi_{F'},\tfrac12)$, which is $\ne0$ for $r=\operatorname{ord}$ and, by the
*super-positivity* of YZ I, Thm. B.2 (all central Taylor coefficients of order $\ge\operatorname{ord}$ have the same sign, in fact
are $\ge0$ after the normalisation), also for larger $r$ of the right parity. So **the Heegner–Drinfeld classes of every
order $r\ge r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{F'})$ are non-zero cohomology classes** on the $2r$-dimensional stack $\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G$.

(b) **[CONJECTURE] (YZ II, eq. (1.9), "It is expected that…").** $(\mathbf R\Pi^r_{G,!}\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell)_\pi=\pi^K\otimes(j_{!*}\rho_\pi[-1])^{\boxtimes r}\boxtimes\bigotimes_{x\in\Sigma_\infty}\rho_{\pi,x}^{I_x}$
on $X^r\times\mathfrak S_\infty$, hence $V'(\xi)_\pi\cong\pi^K\otimes W_\pi'^{\otimes r}\otimes\ell_{\pi,\xi}$ ($\pi^K$ and
$\ell_{\pi,\xi}$ one-dimensional). This is Drinfeld's lemma plus the Langlands parametrisation of the cohomology of
$\mathrm{Sht}^r$; for the Hecke-finite cuspidal part it is in the spirit of Drinfeld ($r=2$), L. Lafforgue, V. Lafforgue
(*Chtoucas pour les groupes réductifs et paramétrisation de Langlands globale*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 31 (2018), 719–891,
DOI 10.1090/jams/897) and C. Xue (*Cuspidal cohomology of stacks of shtukas*, Compos. Math. 156 (2020), 1079–1151, DOI
10.1112/S0010437X20007058; Épijournal Géom. Algébrique 4 (2020)), but YZ II do not claim it for the full generalised
eigenspace $V'(\xi)_\pi$, and this file has not verified that the literature covers exactly (1.9) *[unverified]*.
Granting (1.9), $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)\in\pi^K\otimes W_\pi'^{\otimes r}\otimes\ell_{\pi,\xi}$ and (YZ II: "it can be shown")
$Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)$ is an eigenvector of $\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{Fr}^{\otimes r}\otimes\mathrm{id}$ with eigenvalue $q^r$.

(c) **[CONJECTURE] (YZ II, Conjecture 1.7).** *Let $r=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}L(\pi_{F'},s)$ $(=\dim$ of the generalised
$q$-eigenspace of $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $W'_\pi)$ and $\mu\in\mathfrak T_{r,\Sigma}$. Then
$Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)\in\pi^K\otimes\bigwedge^r\big(W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}\big)\otimes\ell_{\pi,\xi}$. In particular the generalised
$q$-eigenspace of $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $W'_\pi$ equals the eigenspace, and $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)$ is a basis of the line
$\pi^K\otimes\bigwedge^r(W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q})\otimes\ell_{\pi,\xi}$.* (Since $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)\ne0$ by (a), membership in
$\bigwedge^rW_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}$ forces $\dim W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}\ge r=\dim$ generalised eigenspace, i.e. Frobenius
semisimplicity at $q$ — in the notation of §D.0 for $E_{F'}$: $\delta_\ell(E/F')=0$, equivalently $s_\ell(E/F')=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E/F')$.)

(d) **Announced, not published (YZ II §1.3.1: "In a forthcoming work, the authors plan to prove (assuming that (1.9) holds)").**
(i) if $r_0$ is the least $r$ with $Z^\mu_\pi\ne0$ for some $\mu$, then $\dim W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}=r_0$ and $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)$ is a basis of
$\pi^K\otimes\bigwedge^{r_0}(W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q})\otimes\ell_{\pi,\xi}$; (ii) $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}L(\pi_{F'},s)=1\iff\dim W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}=1$,
and $\operatorname{ord}=3\Rightarrow\dim W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}=3$. *Searches (§D.7) found no paper containing these results as of
2026-09-11; there is no "Yun–Zhang III".* For elliptic curves, (ii) would give Frobenius semisimplicity at $q$ (hence
$s_\ell=r_{\mathrm{an}}$) for $E_{F'}$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{F'})=3$; note that for $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1$ semisimplicity is automatic
(a generalised eigenspace of dimension $\le1$ is an eigenspace).

(e) **What is *not* deduced (YZ II §1.3.2, last paragraph, and this file).** Nothing about $E(F')$ or $E(F)$: no point is
produced, no bound on $r_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is obtained, finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ is not addressed. YZ II: "While it is difficult to
construct algebraic cycles on $\mathcal E'$ spanning $W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q}$, it may be easier to construct a basis of the line
$\bigwedge^r(W_\pi'^{\mathrm{Fr}=q})$. Conjecture 1.7 proposes a candidate generator … It is not clear though how to relate the
ambient space of $Z^\mu_\pi(\xi)$, namely $\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi)$, to powers of $\mathcal E'$." In the terminology of §D.5: the
higher Gross–Zagier formula lives entirely on the **Tate-class side** of the chain $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_\ell=g_q\le m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$
(it produces, conjecturally, a generator of $\bigwedge^{m_q}$ of the eigenspace, thereby closing the gap $g_q\le m_q$);
it says nothing about the **algebraicity gap** $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_\ell$, which is $T_1(\mathcal E')$, i.e. finiteness of
$\operatorname{Sha}$. By D.1.4.3 that second gap is the whole of BSD.

**[GAP D.2] (From Heegner–Drinfeld classes to algebraic cycles).** *Let $E/F$ be non-isotrivial and semistable, $F'/F$ a
quadratic extension as in D.3.2 (Heegner hypothesis), $r:=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E/F')$, and $\mathcal E'\to X'$ a semistable model.
Construct a correspondence (or a motivic/Hecke-equivariant map) $\Xi:H^{2r}_c(\mathrm{Sht}'^r_G(\Sigma;\xi),\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell)_\pi\to
H^{2r}(\bar{\mathcal E}'^{\,r},\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell)(r)$, compatible with cycle classes, such that $\Xi(Z^\mu_\pi(\xi))$ is the class of an
algebraic cycle on $\mathcal E'^{\,r}$ whose Künneth components in $\bigwedge^r H^1(\bar X',R^1\pi'_*\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell)(r)$ are
generated by classes of sections of $\mathcal E'$. Any such $\Xi$ with $\Xi(Z^\mu_\pi(\xi))\ne0$ algebraic would give $r$ independent
algebraic classes in $M_{E/F'}^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$, hence $r_{\mathrm{alg}}(E/F')\ge r=r_{\mathrm{an}}(E/F')$, hence BSD for $E/F'$ and (D.1.4.3(4))
for $E/F$.* Sub-gaps: (D.2.i) establish (1.9); (D.2.ii) prove Conjecture 1.7; (D.2.iii) the "$r=1$ dictionary": prove that
under $\mathrm{Sht}^1_G(\Sigma;\{\infty\})|_{X-\infty}\cong\mathrm{DrMod}_2(\Sigma_f)/\operatorname{Pic}^0_X(k)$ and the Drinfeld modular parametrisation
$J(\mathrm{DrMod}_2(\Sigma_f))\to E$ (Drinfeld; Gekeler–Reversat, J. reine angew. Math. 476 (1996), 27–94, DOI 10.1515/crll.1996.476.27;
Zarhin's Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over function fields, Math. USSR-Izv. 9 (1975), 255–260), the cup product
$(Z^\mu_\pi(\xi),Z^\mu_\pi(\xi))$ equals an explicit multiple of $\langle P_{F''},P_{F''}\rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}$ for the Heegner point
$P_{F''}\in E(F'')$; this would make D.1.5.4 unconditional for $p>3$ (modulo matching Ulmer's hypotheses).

**[GAP D.3] (Frobenius semisimplicity at $q$).** *Let $E/K$ be non-isotrivial with $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ and $\ell\ne p$. Prove
$\delta_\ell(E/K)=0$, i.e. $\mathrm{Fr}$ acts semisimply on the generalised $q$-eigenspace of $H^1(\bar C,\mathcal F_\ell\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)$;
equivalently $\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell^\infty}(E/K)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$.* Known: implied by finiteness
of $\operatorname{Sha}[\ell_0^\infty]$ for one $\ell_0$ (D.1.4.5); automatic for $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1$; implied for $E_{F'}$ by YZ II
Conjecture 1.7; equivalent to non-degeneracy of the cup product on the transcendental Tate classes (Corollary D.5.4).
This is the function-field shadow of the "rank $\ge2$ wall" of `../03-obstructions-rank-ge-2.md`.

### D.3.4 Follow-ups 2017–2026 (verified)

- **[THEOREM]** Howard–Shnidman, *A Gross–Kohnen–Zagier formula for Heegner–Drinfeld cycles*, Adv. Math. 351 (2019),
  117–194, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2019.05.005 (arXiv:1707.00213): for unramified cuspidal $\pi$ and even $r$, the intersection
  of Heegner–Drinfeld cycles from two *different* étale double covers equals $C_r(\pi)$, the $r$-th central derivative of a
  product of two toric periods; $\langle[\mathrm{Sht}^r_{T_1}]_\pi,[\mathrm{Sht}^r_{T_2}]_\pi\rangle=0\iff
  L^{(r)}(\pi,\tfrac12)L(\pi\otimes\chi_1,\tfrac12)L(\pi\otimes\chi_2,\tfrac12)=0$. Iwahori-level version: H. Li, arXiv:1904.11479 (2019).
- **[THEOREM]** Shnidman, *Manin–Drinfeld cycles and derivatives of $L$-functions*, arXiv:2004.03365 (v2 2022; "to appear in
  JEMS" per arXiv comment): pairing of cycles from the *split* torus with the Heegner–Drinfeld cycle equals
  $L^{(r)}(\pi,\tfrac12)\times$ (Waldspurger's toric period); the Yun–Zhang correspondence "differentiating the $L$-function"
  is realised as the action of $e+f\in\mathfrak{sl}_2$ on $(\mathbb Q_\ell^2)^{\otimes2d}$ (Schur–Weyl duality).
- **[THEOREM]** Feng–Yun–Zhang, *Higher Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups: the non-singular terms*, Invent. Math. 235
  (2024), 569–668, DOI 10.1007/s00222-023-01228-y (arXiv:2103.11514): $r$-th central derivatives of non-singular Fourier
  coefficients of Siegel–Eisenstein series $=$ degrees of special cycles of virtual dimension $0$ on moduli of Hermitian
  shtukas with $r$ legs — the function-field Kudla–Rapoport conjecture for *all* $r$.
- **[THEOREM]/[CONJECTURE]** Feng–Yun–Zhang, *Higher theta series for unitary groups over function fields*, Ann. Sci. Éc.
  Norm. Supér. (2025) (arXiv:2110.07001, v3 Jan. 2024): construction of higher theta series with values in Chow groups
  of $\mathrm{Sht}^r_{U(n)}$ via derived algebraic geometry; **Modularity Conjecture 4.15**. Feng–Yun–Zhang, *Modularity of higher
  theta series I: cohomology of the generic fiber*, arXiv:2308.10979 (v3, 16 June 2026): generic modularity of the
  $\ell$-adic realisation ("arithmetic Fourier transform", "sheaf–cycle correspondence"). Feng–Khan, *Modularity II: Chow
  group of the generic fiber*, arXiv:2403.19711 (2024): generic modularity in Chow groups via motivic homotopy theory.
  **Feng–Yun–Zhang, *Modularity of Higher Theta Series III: Proof of the Modularity Conjecture*, arXiv:2608.07173
  (7 Aug. 2026): "We prove the Modularity Conjecture for higher theta series on moduli stacks of Hermitian shtukas. For
  general linear shtukas, we establish a more refined phenomenon that we call supermodularity. As a key input, we prove the
  Trace Conjecture for Hitchin stacks of low corank".** (abstract, arXiv API, fetched 2026-09-11). This opens "higher
  theta lifting" and a putative "higher arithmetic inner product formula" (Feng–Khan §1.1) — the unitary-group, all-$r$
  analogue of Li–Liu (Ann. of Math. 194 (2021), DOI 10.4007/annals.2021.194.3.6) over function fields.
- **[THEOREM]** Bieker, *A higher Gross–Zagier type formula for moduli of shtukas at deeper level*, arXiv:2607.07531

## D.4 The transfer problem: shtukas with $r$ legs over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$

### D.4.1 What would be needed

A function-field shtuka with $r$ legs over $S/\mathbb F_p$ relative to $X$ is a $G$-bundle $\mathcal E$ on $X\times_{\mathbb F_p}S$ with
an isomorphism $(\mathrm{Frob}_S^*\mathcal E)|_{X\times S\setminus\bigcup\Gamma_{x_i}}\cong\mathcal E|_{X\times S\setminus\bigcup\Gamma_{x_i}}$
of prescribed type along the graphs of legs $x_i:S\to X$ (Scholze, ICM 2018, Def. in §4, quoted from the TeX). The two
ingredients used in D.3 are: **(L)** the product $X\times_{\mathbb F_p}S$, so that legs vary in $X^r$ and $\mathrm{Sht}^r_G\to X^r$
exists; **(F)** the Frobenius of $S$, absent from $X$, making the shtuka condition non-trivial; and, for the cohomology,
**(D)** Drinfeld's lemma ($\pi_1$ of $(X\times\cdots\times X)/\text{partial Frobenii}$ is $\pi_1(X)^r$), which is what makes
$H^*(\mathrm{Sht}^r_G)_\pi$ a representation of $G_F^r$ and yields (1.9). For $\mathbb Q$: (L) would require
"$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\times_{\mathbb F_1}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$"; (F) a Frobenius on the base; (D) a Drinfeld lemma for it.

### D.4.2 Scholze's statements (ICM 2018), quoted from the TeX of arXiv:1712.03708

P. Scholze, *$p$-adic geometry*, Proc. ICM 2018 (Rio de Janeiro), Vol. I, 899–933, DOI 10.1142/9789813272880_0032:

- (§1) "What we are proposing here is that, despite extreme difficulties in making sense of this, there should exist a
  theory of shtukas in the number field case, and that the cohomology of an algebraic variety, i.e. a motive, should be an
  example of such a shtuka."
- (§3, after Thm. on local Shimura varieties) "Such spaces live over the base space $\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb Q_p\times\ldots\times\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb Q_p$
  parametrizing the points of modification: These products give good meaning to the non-existent products
  $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q_p\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_1}\ldots\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_1}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q_p$. The resulting spaces are now in
  general just diamonds." With $\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb Q_p\times\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb Q_p=(\widetilde{\mathbb D}_{\mathbb Q_p}\setminus\{0\})^\diamond/\underline{\mathbb Z_p^\times}$,
  and Weinstein's theorem that $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}((\widetilde{\mathbb D}_C\setminus\{0\})/\underline{\mathbb Q_p^\times})=G_{\mathbb Q_p}$.
- "A search for a hypothetical space $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\times\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ thus seems closely related to a realization of the
  absolute Galois group of $\mathbb Q$ as a geometric fundamental group." Partial step: Kucharczyk–Scholze, *Topological

**Reading for BSD.** Scholze's conjecture concerns *varieties over $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$* and would supply a substitute for

### D.4.3 Mixed-characteristic local shtukas and why they do not give global $r$-leg cycles

**[THEOREM] (Scholze–Weinstein, *Berkeley Lectures on $p$-adic Geometry*, Ann. of Math. Studies 207, Princeton 2020, DOI
10.23943/princeton/9780691202082.001.0001, Lectures 11–25).** For $S$ perfectoid of characteristic $p$, a $p$-adic shtuka
over $S$ with legs at untilts $S^\sharp_1,\dots,S^\sharp_n$ is a $G$-bundle on "$S\dot\times\operatorname{Spa}\mathbb Z_p$" with a
Frobenius-isomorphism away from the graphs of the untilts; the moduli $\mathrm{Sht}_{(G,b,\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n)}$ are locally spatial
diamonds over $\operatorname{Spd}\breve E\times\cdots$; with one leg and minuscule $\mu$ they are the local Shimura varieties
(Rapoport–Zink spaces when of PEL type), with integral models given by moduli of shtukas bounded by local models.
Fargues–Scholze, *Geometrization of the local Langlands correspondence*, arXiv:2102.13459 (v4, Nov. 2024, accepted version):
$\mathrm{Bun}_G$ on the Fargues–Fontaine curve, Hecke stacks $\mathrm{Hck}_{G,I}$ with $|I|$ legs over $(\operatorname{Div}^1)^I$, the spectral action.

**Why this is not (L)+(F) for $\mathbb Q$.** (i) All legs are untilts of the *same* characteristic-$p$ base, i.e. all sit
over the single place $p$: $\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb Q_p\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Spd}\mathbb Q_p$ is a local object; there is no product over
$\mathbb F_1$ of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ with itself, and the legs cannot be moved to a place $\ell\ne p$ or to $\infty$. (ii) The
Frobenius used is that of the characteristic-$p$ perfectoid test object $S$, i.e. the "$S$-direction"; the arithmetic
$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$-direction has no Frobenius. (iii) Consequently there is no global cohomology with a $G_{\mathbb Q}^r$-action and no
Drinfeld lemma; the cohomology of $\mathrm{Sht}_{(G,b,\mu_\bullet)}$ carries $W_E^{\,n}$-actions (local Weil groups) — the local
analogue of (D) — which is exactly what geometrises *local* Langlands. **In the number-field case the only global
$\mathrm{Sht}^r$ that exist are $r=0$ (the adelic double coset $G(\mathbb Q)\backslash G(\mathbb A)/K$; Waldspurger's formula) and $r=1$
(Shimura varieties: "one leg at $\infty$"; Gross–Zagier), exactly as YZ I say.** The mod $p$ fibre of a Shimura variety
can be viewed as having a second leg at $p$ (Frobenius twist): this is the viewpoint of Xiao–Zhu, *Cycles on Shimura
varieties via geometric Satake*, arXiv:1707.05700 (2017), who construct cohomological correspondences between mod $p$ fibres
of different Shimura varieties and show that irreducible components of basic Newton strata generate all Tate classes in the
middle cohomology under a genericity condition. This is the only number-field incarnation of a "two-leg" picture known to
this author, and it lives in characteristic $p$ (special fibres), not over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$.

**[GAP D.4] (Global shtukas with $r\ge2$ legs over $\mathbb Z$).** *For $E/\mathbb Q$ with associated $\pi$ on $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ and an imaginary
quadratic $F'$ satisfying a Heegner hypothesis, construct for each $r\ge2$ (of the right parity) an object $\mathcal S_r$ (a
scheme, stack, diamond, or object of a category to be specified) with: (α) a Hecke action of $\mathscr H^\Sigma$ and a
"middle-degree cohomology" $H^{2r}(\mathcal S_r)$ with a Hecke-equivariant $G_{\mathbb Q}$-action such that
$H^{2r}(\mathcal S_r)_\pi\cong\pi^K\otimes(V_\ell E)^{\otimes r}(\cdot)$ functorially in $r$; (β) a class $Z_r\in H^{2r}(\mathcal S_r)_\pi$, image
of a "cycle", with $(Z_r,Z_r)=c_r\cdot L^{(r)}(E_{F'},1)$ for an explicit non-zero $c_r$; (γ) for $r=0,1$ the pair
$(\mathcal S_r,Z_r)$ recovers $(G(\mathbb Q)\backslash G(\mathbb A)/K,\ \text{toric period})$ and $(X_0(N),\ \text{Heegner point})$ with
Waldspurger's and Gross–Zagier's formulas.* Constraints: by D.2.4.1, if (α)–(γ) are required functorially for Artin motives
with $\mathbb R$-coefficients, no such theory exists; by D.4.3 the diamond-theoretic constructions are local; by D.2.2 even a
solution of (α)–(γ) would only close the Tate-class half of BSD (D.3.3(e)) unless $\mathcal S_r$ is related to $\mathcal E^r$.
Cross-reference: this is the same wall as the "diagonal cycles for $r\ge2$" problem of `C-higher-rank-euler-systems-diagonal-cycles.md`;
what exists over $\mathbb Q$ for higher-rank *groups* at $r=1$ is the Kudla programme (Kudla–Rapoport; Li–Zhang, J. Amer. Math.
Soc. 35 (2022), 705–797, DOI 10.1090/jams/988; Li–Liu, Ann. of Math. 194 (2021), DOI 10.4007/annals.2021.194.3.6),
whose function-field all-$r$ version is Feng–Yun–Zhang (D.3.4).

## D.5 The analogue over $\mathbb Q$ of "$\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$ finite $\Rightarrow$ BSD", and the transfer conjecture over function fields

Throughout §D.5, $E/K$ is **non-constant** and $\ell\ne p$ unless stated. Recall $V=H^1(\bar C,\mathcal F\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell)$,
$M=V(1)$, $m_q,g_q$ the algebraic/geometric multiplicities of the eigenvalue $q$ of $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $V$ (= of $1$ on $M$),
$\delta_\ell=m_q-g_q$, $s_\ell=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell^\infty}(E/K)$.

### D.5.1 The chain of inequalities

**Proposition D.5.1 [THEOREM] (assembled from Tate 1966, Grothendieck 1968, Shioda–Tate; proof on the page).**

1. $s_\ell=g_q$.
2. $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_\ell$, with equality iff $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell^\infty]$ is finite.
3. $g_q\le m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$, with equality iff $\mathrm{Fr}$ acts semisimply on the generalised $1$-eigenspace $M_1$ of $M$.
4. (Tate–Milne) $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=s_\ell\Rightarrow g_q=m_q$. Hence $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_\ell=g_q\le m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ and
   $\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$ finite $\Rightarrow$ all four numbers coincide.

*Proof.* (2) is the descent sequence $0\to E(K)\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\to\operatorname{Sel}(E/K,\mathbb Z_\ell)\to T_\ell\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)\to0$
tensored with $\mathbb Q_\ell$ ($\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$ is cofinitely generated because $\operatorname{Sel}(E/K,\ell)$ is finite, so
$\operatorname{rank}T_\ell\operatorname{Sha}=0$ iff $\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$ finite). (3) is D.1.1 and linear algebra. (4) is D.1.4.3(2)$\Rightarrow$(1)
combined with (1)–(3). For (1): let $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\subset H^2:=H^2(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Q_\ell(1))$ be the span of the classes of
$O$, $F$ and the fibre components. These are classes of $k$-rational divisors, hence $\mathrm{Fr}$-invariant, and their
intersection matrix is non-degenerate (the trivial lattice is $U\oplus(\text{negative definite root lattices})$, Shioda),
so $\dim\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}=\operatorname{rank}\mathbb T=2+\sum_v(f_v-1)$ and $H^2=\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\oplus\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}^\perp$,
$\mathrm{Fr}$-equivariantly (the cup product is $\mathrm{Fr}$-invariant because $\mathrm{Fr}$ acts trivially on $H^4(\bar{\mathcal E},\mathbb Q_\ell(2))$).
In the Leray filtration $F^0\supset F^1\supset F^2$ of $H^2$ (Step 2 of §D.2) one has $F^2=\mathbb Q_\ell F$,
$F^1/F^2\cong H^1(\bar C,R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell(1))\cong M$, and $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\to F^0/F^1=H^0(\bar C,R^2\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell(1))$ is surjective
with kernel $\mathbb Q_\ell F$ (the class of $O$ maps to the generic section $1$, the fibre components to the local vectors;
Ulmer arXiv:1203.5573, §"Comparison of the Selmer group and $H^1(\bar C,R^1\pi_*\mathbb Z_\ell(1))^{G_k}$"). Hence
$H^2=\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}+F^1$, $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\cap F^1=F^2$, and $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}^\perp\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2/\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\cong F^1/F^2\cong M$
as $\mathrm{Fr}$-modules. Therefore
$$\dim H^{2,\mathrm{Fr}=1}=\big(2+\textstyle\sum_v(f_v-1)\big)+g_q .$$
By Proposition D.2.1 $\otimes\mathbb Q$, $\dim H^{2,\mathrm{Fr}=1}=\rho+\operatorname{rank}T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)$; by D.1.4.2 and (2),
$\operatorname{rank}T_\ell\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)=\operatorname{rank}T_\ell\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)=s_\ell-r_{\mathrm{alg}}$; by Shioda–Tate (Step 3),
$\rho=2+\sum_v(f_v-1)+r_{\mathrm{alg}}$. Comparing gives $s_\ell=g_q$. $\square$

**Lemma D.5.2 [THEOREM] (classical linear algebra behind Tate's "$T_1\Rightarrow T_2$"; proof on the page).** Let $M$ be a
finite-dimensional vector space over a field of characteristic $0$ with a non-degenerate bilinear form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$
and an automorphism $\varphi$ with $\langle\varphi x,\varphi y\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle$. Let $M_1$ be the generalised
$1$-eigenspace, $N:=\varphi-1$ on $M_1$, $M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}:=\ker N$. Then:
(a) $\langle M_\lambda,M_\mu\rangle=0$ unless $\lambda\mu=1$; in particular $\langle\ ,\ \rangle|_{M_1}$ is non-degenerate;
(b) the radical of $\langle\ ,\ \rangle|_{M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}}$ equals $\ker N\cap\operatorname{im}N$; hence
$\varphi|_{M_1}$ is semisimple $\iff$ $\langle\ ,\ \rangle|_{M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}}$ is non-degenerate.

*Proof.* (a) Over an algebraic closure, for $x\in M_\lambda$, $y\in M_\mu$ write $\varphi=\lambda+N_\lambda$, $\varphi=\mu+N_\mu$;
then $\langle x,y\rangle=\langle\varphi x,\varphi y\rangle=\lambda\mu\langle x,y\rangle+\lambda\langle x,N_\mu y\rangle+\mu\langle N_\lambda x,y\rangle+\langle N_\lambda x,N_\mu y\rangle$,
and induction on the nilpotency indices of $x,y$ gives $(1-\lambda\mu)\langle x,y\rangle=0$. Non-degeneracy on $M_1$ follows
from $M=\bigoplus_\lambda M_\lambda$. (b) For $x,y\in M_1$: $\langle Nx,y\rangle+\langle x,Ny\rangle+\langle Nx,Ny\rangle=0$; taking
$y=z\in\ker N$ gives $\langle Nx,z\rangle=0$, so $\operatorname{im}N\subset(\ker N)^\perp$ (orthogonal inside $M_1$); by
non-degeneracy on $M_1$, $\dim(\ker N)^\perp=\dim M_1-\dim\ker N=\dim\operatorname{im}N$, so $\operatorname{im}N=(\ker N)^\perp$ and the
radical of $\langle\ ,\ \rangle|_{\ker N}$ is $\ker N\cap(\ker N)^\perp=\ker N\cap\operatorname{im}N$. Finally $\ker N\cap\operatorname{im}N=0$
iff $N=0$ (if $N\ne0$ and $j\ge1$ is maximal with $N^j\ne0$, then $0\ne N^jx\in\ker N\cap\operatorname{im}N$). $\square$

**Corollary D.5.3 [THEOREM] (pairing coincidence).** Let $A\subset M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$ be the image of $E(K)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$ under
$P\mapsto$ (orthogonal projection of $[\bar P]$ to $\mathbb T^\perp$). The cup product on $M\cong\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}^\perp$ restricted to
$A$ is $-(\log q)^{-1}\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{\mathrm{NT}}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$, hence non-degenerate. *Proof.* Proposition D.2.3 and the
compatibility of cycle classes with intersection and cup products. $\square$

**Corollary D.5.4 [NEW] (the Frobenius defect is the degeneracy of the cup product on transcendental Tate classes).**
Let $B:=A^\perp\cap M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$. Then $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}=A\oplus B$, $B\cong M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}/A\cong V_\ell\operatorname{Sha}(E/K):=T_\ell\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$
(so $\dim B=s_\ell-r_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell^\infty]$), and
$$\delta_\ell(E/K)=0\iff\text{the cup product restricted to }B\text{ is non-degenerate.}$$
In particular $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell^\infty]$ finite $\Rightarrow B=0\Rightarrow\delta_\ell=0$.
*Proof.* $M$ with the cup product is non-degenerate (Poincaré duality on $\mathcal E$ and non-degeneracy of $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}$), and
$\mathrm{Fr}$ preserves the form, so Lemma D.5.2 applies with $\varphi=\mathrm{Fr}$: $\delta_\ell=0\iff\langle\ ,\ \rangle|_{M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}}$
non-degenerate. Since $\langle\ ,\ \rangle|_A$ is non-degenerate (D.5.3), $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}=A\oplus B$ orthogonally and
non-degeneracy on $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$ is equivalent to non-degeneracy on $B$. The identification $B\cong M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}/A\cong V_\ell\operatorname{Sha}$
is Proposition D.2.1 $\otimes\mathbb Q$ together with $H^{2,\mathrm{Fr}=1}=\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\oplus M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$,
$\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=\mathbb T_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\oplus A$ and D.1.4.2. $\square$

*Reading.* Over $K$, BSD splits into two independent-looking statements about the same Tate-class space $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$:
**(algebraicity)** $B=0$, and **(semisimplicity)** the cup product on $B$ is non-degenerate; Tate's theorem says the first
implies the second trivially (there is nothing to be degenerate), and the higher Gross–Zagier formula (D.3.3) aims at the
second. The Néron–Tate height enters only through Corollary D.5.3, i.e. through the fact that *the archimedean height is a
cup product in $\ell$-adic cohomology* — for every $\ell$ simultaneously. This is what fails over $\mathbb Q$ (D.5.3 below).

### D.5.2 The exact analogue over $\mathbb Q$ and its decomposition

Over $\mathbb Q$ (notation of the charter; $p$ a prime of good ordinary reduction, $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$,
$X_\infty=\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb Q_\infty)^\vee$, $L_p(E,T)$, $r_p=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_p(E,T)$, $s_p=\operatorname{corank}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbb Q)$),
the statement of D.1.4.3(2)(c)$\Rightarrow$(a) reads:

**[GAP D.5] (Tate–Milne over $\mathbb Q$).** *Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be an elliptic curve and $p$ a prime such that $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)[p^\infty]$
is finite. Prove $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$.* Equivalently (given the finiteness hypothesis):
$s_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$, i.e. *the Selmer corank equals the analytic rank*.

**Decomposition of [GAP D.5] along the Iwasawa-theoretic chain.**

- **(A) [THEOREM]** $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le s_p$, with equality iff $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite (descent sequence; identical to D.5.1(2)).
- **(B) [CONDITIONAL]** $s_p\le\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)$, with equality iff the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing
  on $E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb Q_p$ is non-degenerate, when $X_\infty$ is $\Lambda$-torsion (Kato, Astérisque 295 (2004)) and
  $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite: Schneider, *$p$-adic height pairings II*, Invent. Math. 79 (1985), 329–374, DOI
  10.1007/BF01388978 (good ordinary $p$; see `A-iwasawa-padic.md` for the exact form and for the non-ordinary case). The
  non-degeneracy is **Schneider's conjecture** `[CONJECTURE]`. And $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=(L_p(E,T))$ is the Iwasawa
  main conjecture: Kato (one divisibility), Skinner–Urban, Invent. Math. 195 (2014), 1–277, DOI 10.1007/s00222-013-0448-1
  (equality under their hypotheses: $p\ge3$ good ordinary, $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ irreducible, a ramification condition), with the
  later extensions listed in `A-…`. Under these, $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=r_p$.
- **(C) [CONJECTURE]** $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ (Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum, Invent. Math. 84 (1986), 1–48, DOI 10.1007/BF01388731,
  in the good ordinary case; for split multiplicative $p$ the correct statement is $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}+1$, Greenberg–Stevens,
  Invent. Math. 111 (1993), 407–447, DOI 10.1007/BF01231294 for the first-derivative case).

So [GAP D.5] $\Leftarrow$ (IMC) + (Schneider non-degeneracy) + (transfer $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$). Over $K$ (constant
$\mathbb Z_\ell$-extension, $\ell\ne p$): (IMC) is a theorem (D.5.7(4)); (transfer) is a tautology (D.5.7(3)); and (Schneider
non-degeneracy) is exactly Frobenius semisimplicity $\delta_\ell=0$ (D.5.6(5)), which is a theorem *under the hypothesis of
[GAP D.5]*, finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}[\ell^\infty]$, by Tate–Milne (D.1.4.5) via the pairing coincidence D.5.3. **Weaker analogues provable over $\mathbb Q$:** $r_{\mathrm{an}}\le1\Rightarrow s_p=r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$
(Gross–Zagier, Invent. Math. 84 (1986), 225–320; Kolyvagin 1989; Kato 2004); $s_p=0\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}=0$ and
$s_p=1\Rightarrow r_{\mathrm{an}}=1$ under hypotheses (Skinner–Urban; W. Zhang; Skinner — see `B-…`, where the exact hypotheses
are recorded). Nothing for $r_{\mathrm{an}}\ge2$ or $s_p\ge2$.

### D.5.3 Why Tate's proof of (B)+(C) does not transfer: two pairings versus one

Over $K$, by Lemma D.5.2, "semisimplicity" of the operator whose generalised eigenspace has dimension $r_{\mathrm{an}}$ is equivalent
to non-degeneracy of a pairing on the Selmer space, and that pairing *is* the Néron–Tate height on the algebraic part
(D.5.3): one pairing, known non-degenerate, in one cohomology. Over $\mathbb Q$ the same linear algebra holds for the operator
$\gamma-1=T$ on $X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_p$: $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)=\dim_{\mathbb Q_p}(X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_p)_{T\text{-gen}}$ equals
$\dim(X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_p)/T$ iff $T$ acts semisimply on the $T$-primary part, and (Schneider, Perrin-Riou, Nekovář,
*Selmer complexes*, Astérisque 310 (2006)) the $p$-adic height pairing is the Bockstein pairing measuring exactly this. But the
pairing whose non-degeneracy is *known* — the archimedean Néron–Tate height — takes values in $\mathbb R$ and is not the
restriction of any $\mathbb Q_p$-bilinear form on Selmer classes. Over $K$ both heights are the same rational number times
$\log q$ (D.2.3). This is a complete diagnosis of the failure of Step 5 of §D.2 over $\mathbb Q$; it does not suggest a repair.

### D.5.4 The transfer conjecture over function fields: constant ($=$ "arithmetic") $\mathbb Z_\ell$-extensions

Iwasawa's original analogy (Iwasawa, *On $\mathbb Z_\ell$-extensions of algebraic number fields*, Ann. of Math. 98 (1973),
246–326, DOI 10.2307/1970784) matches the cyclotomic $\mathbb Z_p$-extension of $\mathbb Q$ with the **constant-field** $\mathbb Z_\ell$-extension
$K_\infty:=K\cdot\mathbb F_{q^{\ell^\infty}}$ of $K$, and $\gamma$ with Frobenius. Lai–Longhi–Tan–Trihan call $K_\infty$ the
*arithmetic* $\mathbb Z_p$-extension; $\mathbb Z_p$-extensions with fixed constant field are *geometric*. Set
$K_n:=K\mathbb F_{q^{\ell^n}}$, $\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/K)\cong\mathbb Z_\ell$, $\gamma\in\Gamma$ the image of the geometric Frobenius
$\mathrm{Fr}$ (a topological generator), $\Lambda:=\mathbb Z_\ell[[\Gamma]]\cong\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]$, $T=\gamma-1$,
$S_n:=\operatorname{Sel}_{\ell^\infty}(E/K_n)$, $S_\infty:=\varinjlim S_n$, $X_\infty:=S_\infty^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}(S_\infty,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)$,
a compact $\Lambda$-module; $\gamma$ acts on $X_\infty$ contragrediently, $(\gamma f)(x)=f(\gamma^{-1}x)$.

**Theorem D.5.5 [THEOREM] (Lai–Longhi–Tan–Trihan, $\ell=p$).** Let $A/K$ be an abelian variety with semistable reduction and
$p$ any prime (p = 2 allowed), $K^{(p)}_\infty$ the arithmetic $\mathbb Z_p$-extension. There is a $p$-adic $L$-function
$\mathcal L_{A/K^{(p)}_\infty}\in Q(\Lambda)$ with $\omega(\mathcal L_{A/K^{(p)}_\infty})=L_Z(A,\omega,1)$ for every continuous character
$\omega:\Gamma\to\mathbb C^\times$ ($Z$ the bad places), defined as an alternating product of determinants of $1-$Frobenius on log

**Proposition D.5.6 [NEW] (structure of the constant-extension Iwasawa module, $\ell\ne p$).** Let $E/K$ be non-constant.

1. $X_\infty$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module; in particular it is $\Lambda$-torsion with $\mu$-invariant $0$
   (a special case of Ray 2026, loc. cit.).
2. $S_\infty=H^1(C_\infty,j_*E[\ell^\infty])=H^1(\bar C,j_*E[\ell^\infty])^{G_{k_\infty}}$, where $C_\infty=C\times_kk_\infty$, and there is a
   $\Gamma$-equivariant isomorphism $T_\ell S_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\cong M_{(1)}$ under which $\gamma$ acts as $\mathrm{Fr}|_{M_{(1)}}$. Hence
   $X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\cong M_{(1)}^{\vee}$ with $\gamma$ acting as the transpose of $\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}$, and
   $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}X_\infty=\dim M_{(1)}=\sum_{|u-1|_\ell<1}m(u)$ ($m(u)$ the algebraic multiplicity).
3. $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=\det\big(\gamma-\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}\mid M_{(1)}\big)\cdot\Lambda$, and
   $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$.
4. (Control) $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}X_\infty/TX_\infty=\dim M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}=g_q=s_\ell(E/K)$.
5. Hence $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)-\operatorname{rank}X_\infty/TX_\infty=\delta_\ell(E/K)$, and $T$ acts semisimply on the
   $T$-primary part of $X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$ iff $\delta_\ell(E/K)=0$ iff $\mathrm{Fr}$ is semisimple at $q$ iff (Lemma D.5.2,
   Corollary D.5.4) the cup product on $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$ is non-degenerate.

*Proof.* (1) $X_\infty/\ell X_\infty=(S_\infty[\ell])^\vee$ and $S_\infty[\ell]=\bigcup_n\operatorname{im}(S_n[\ell])$, so it suffices to bound
$|S_n[\ell]|$ independently of $n$. From $0\to E[\ell]\to E[\ell^\infty]\xrightarrow{\ell}E[\ell^\infty]\to0$ the map
$H^1(K_n,E[\ell])\to H^1(K_n,E[\ell^\infty])[\ell]$ is surjective, and a class of $H^1(K_n,E[\ell])$ mapping into $S_n$ lies in
the $\ell$-Selmer group $\operatorname{Sel}_\ell(E/K_n)$; hence $|S_n[\ell]|\le|\operatorname{Sel}_\ell(E/K_n)|$. Let $U\subset C$ be the good-reduction
locus and $U_n=U\times_kk_n$. At a good place $w$ of $K_n$ (recall $\ell\ne p$) the local Selmer condition is the unramified
condition, so $\operatorname{Sel}_\ell(E/K_n)\subset H^1(\pi_1(U_n),E[\ell])=H^1_{\text{ét}}(U_n,E[\ell])$, and by Hochschild–Serre
$|H^1(U_n,E[\ell])|\le|H^1(k_n,H^0(\bar U,E[\ell]))|\cdot|H^1(\bar U,E[\ell])|\le|E[\ell]|\cdot|H^1(\bar U,E[\ell])|=:B<\infty$
(finiteness of étale cohomology of a finite locally constant sheaf on a curve over $\bar k$, SGA 4½, *Arcata*). A union of
an increasing chain of subgroups of order $\le B$ has order $\le B$, so $X_\infty/\ell X_\infty$ is finite and, $X_\infty$ being a
compact $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module, $X_\infty$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ (topological Nakayama). A $\Lambda$-module finitely
generated over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ is $\Lambda$-torsion with $\mu=0$.

(2) *Step (a): the local conditions are trivial.* Let $w$ be any place of $K_n$. The group $E(K_{n,w})$ has a finite-index
subgroup $E^1(K_{n,w})$ (the formal group) on which multiplication by $\ell$ is bijective ($\ell\in\mathcal O_w^\times$), so
$E(K_{n,w})\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell=0$, and the local Kummer sequence
$0\to E(K_{n,w})\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\to H^1(K_{n,w},E[\ell^\infty])\to H^1(K_{n,w},E)[\ell^\infty]\to0$ shows that the Selmer
condition at $w$ is: *the class is $0$ in $H^1(K_{n,w},E[\ell^\infty])$.* At a good place $w$ every class unramified at $w$ is
$0$ locally, because $H^1(k_w,E[\ell^\infty])=E[\ell^\infty]/(\mathrm{Fr}_w-1)=0$ ($\mathrm{Fr}_w-1$ has non-zero determinant on the divisible group
$E[\ell^\infty]\cong(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)^2$, hence is surjective). Therefore
$S_n=\ker\big(H^1(U_n,E[\ell^\infty])\to\bigoplus_{w\ \mathrm{bad}}H^1(K_{n,w},E[\ell^\infty])\big)$, where $H^1(U_n,-)$ is the subgroup of
classes unramified on $U_n$.
*Step (b): passage to the limit.* Étale cohomology commutes with the filtered limit $U_\infty:=U\times_kk_\infty=\varprojlim U_n$
(SGA 4, VII 5.7), and above each bad place of $K$ there are finitely many places of $K_\infty$, so
$S_\infty=\varinjlim S_n=\ker\big(H^1(U_\infty,E[\ell^\infty])\to\bigoplus_{w\ \mathrm{bad}}H^1(K_{\infty,w},E[\ell^\infty])\big)$.
*Step (c): identification with $H^1(C_\infty,j_*E[\ell^\infty])$.* The residue fields of $C_\infty$ at closed points are algebraic
extensions of $k_\infty=\mathbb F_{q^{\ell^\infty}}$, whose absolute Galois groups $\cong\prod_{\ell'\ne\ell}\mathbb Z_{\ell'}$ have
$\ell$-cohomological dimension $0$. Hence for every bad point $x$ of $C_\infty$, inflation–restriction gives
$H^1(K_{\infty,x},E[\ell^\infty])=H^1(K^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\infty,x},E[\ell^\infty])^{G_{k_\infty(x)}}=H^0(x,R^1j_*E[\ell^\infty])$, and the low-degree
Leray sequence for $j:U_\infty\to C_\infty$, $0\to H^1(C_\infty,j_*E[\ell^\infty])\to H^1(U_\infty,E[\ell^\infty])\to H^0(C_\infty,R^1j_*E[\ell^\infty])$
(with $R^1j_*E[\ell^\infty]$ a skyscraper on the bad points), gives $S_\infty=H^1(C_\infty,j_*E[\ell^\infty])$. By Hochschild–Serre for
$\bar C\to C_\infty$ with group $G_{k_\infty}$ (cohomological dimension $0$ for $\ell$-primary torsion modules),
$S_\infty=H^1(\bar C,j_*E[\ell^\infty])^{G_{k_\infty}}$.
*Step (d): comparison with $M^\circ\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$.* The natural map $j_*T_\ell E\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\to j_*E[\ell^\infty]$ is
injective with cokernel a finite skyscraper on the bad points (stalk at $x$: the finite group $E[\ell^\infty]^{I_x}/\mathrm{div}$), and
$0\to H^1(\bar C,j_*T_\ell E)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\to H^1(\bar C,j_*T_\ell E\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)\to H^2(\bar C,j_*T_\ell E)[\ell^\infty]\to0$
with $H^2(\bar C,j_*T_\ell E)$ finite ($E$ non-constant, D.1.1). So there are $G_k$-equivariant maps with finite kernels and
cokernels between $H^1(\bar C,j_*E[\ell^\infty])$ and $M^\circ\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ (note $M^\circ=H^1(\bar C,j_*T_\ell E)/\mathrm{tors}$
and finite $\otimes\ \mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell=0$). Taking $G_{k_\infty}$-invariants preserves "finite kernel and cokernel" (the cokernel
on invariants injects into the invariants of a finite group or into an $H^1(G_{k_\infty},\text{finite})$), so
$S_\infty\approx(M^\circ\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)^{G_{k_\infty}}$ up to finite groups, $G_k$-equivariantly.
*Step (e): the invariants.* The image of $G_k=\hat{\mathbb Z}$ in $\mathrm{GL}(M^\circ)$ is generated by $\mathrm{Fr}$; write
$\hat{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z_\ell\times\hat{\mathbb Z}^{(\ell')}$ and $\mathrm{Fr}=\mathrm{Fr}_\ell\mathrm{Fr}_{\ell'}$ accordingly. The image of $\hat{\mathbb Z}^{(\ell')}$ is a
pro-(prime-to-$\ell$) subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(M^\circ)$, hence finite (the kernel of $\mathrm{GL}(M^\circ)\to\mathrm{GL}(M^\circ/\ell)$ is pro-$\ell$),
generated by $\mathrm{Fr}_{\ell'}$ of order $m$ prime to $\ell$; and $G_{k_\infty}=\overline{\mathrm{Fr}^{\hat{\mathbb Z}^{(\ell')}}}$ acts through this finite
group. Averaging over it is an idempotent in $\mathbb Z_\ell[\mathrm{Fr}_{\ell'}]$, so $(M^\circ\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)^{G_{k_\infty}}=(M^\circ)^{\mathrm{Fr}_{\ell'}}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$
and $(M^\circ)^{\mathrm{Fr}_{\ell'}}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=M_{(1)}$: on a generalised eigenspace $M_u$, $\mathrm{Fr}_{\ell'}$ acts by the prime-to-$\ell$
root of unity $\omega(u)$ in $u=\omega(u)\langle u\rangle$, which is $1$ iff $u$ is a principal unit. Hence
$T_\ell S_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\cong M_{(1)}$, $\Gamma$-equivariantly, $\gamma$ acting as $\mathrm{Fr}$ (the geometric Frobenius acts on
$H^1(\bar C,-)$ by transport of structure as the inverse of the arithmetic Frobenius, i.e. as the operator $\mathrm{Fr}$ of D.1.1). Dualising,
$X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=\operatorname{Hom}(T_\ell S_\infty,\mathbb Z_\ell)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\cong M_{(1)}^\vee$ with $\gamma$ acting contragrediently, i.e. as
$(\mathrm{Fr}^{-1})^{\vee}$.

(3) By (1) and (2), $X_\infty$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ with $X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\cong M_{(1)}^\vee$; the structure
theory of $\Lambda$-modules finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ gives $X_\infty\sim\bigoplus\Lambda/(f_j)$ with $\prod f_j$ the
characteristic polynomial of $T=\gamma-1$ on $X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$, made distinguished; this is
$\det(\gamma-\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}\mid M_{(1)})$ up to $\Lambda^\times$. Its order at $T=0$ is the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue $1$
of $\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}$ on $M_{(1)}$, i.e. of the eigenvalue $1$ of $\mathrm{Fr}$ on $M$, i.e. $m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ (D.1.1).

(4) $X_\infty/TX_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$ is the space of $\gamma$-coinvariants of $M_{(1)}^\vee$, dual to the $\mathrm{Fr}$-invariants of
$M_{(1)}$, which are $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$ of dimension $g_q=s_\ell(E/K)$ by Proposition D.5.1(1).

(5) is (3), (4), Lemma D.5.2 and Corollary D.5.4. $\square$

**Proposition D.5.7 [NEW] (the $\ell$-adic $L$-function along the constant extension; main conjecture and transfer theorem).**
Let $E/K$ be non-constant, $\ell\ne p$, and define
$$\mathcal L_\ell(E):=\det\big(1-\gamma\otimes\mathrm{Fr}\ \big|\ \Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}M^\circ\big)=\prod_{i=1}^N\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_i}{q}\,\gamma\Big)=L\big(E,\tfrac{\gamma}{q}\big)\ \in\ \mathbb Z[\tfrac1q][\gamma]\subset\Lambda .$$
Then:

1. (Interpolation) For every continuous character $\omega:\Gamma\to\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell^\times$, in particular every character of
   finite order, $\omega(\mathcal L_\ell(E))=L(E\otimes\omega,1):=L(E\otimes\omega,T)|_{T=q^{-1}}$, where
   $L(E\otimes\omega,T)=\prod_v\det\big(1-\omega(\mathrm{Fr}_v)\mathrm{Fr}_vT^{\deg v}\mid\mathcal F_{\bar v}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\big)^{-1}$, with $\mathrm{Fr}_v$ a
   geometric Frobenius at $v$ acting on the stalk $\mathcal F_{\bar v}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=H^1(\bar E_{\bar\eta},\mathbb Q_\ell)^{I_v}$ (equivalently, the
   arithmetic Frobenius on $(V_\ell E)^{I_v}$), so that for $\omega=1$ the factors are the Euler factors $1-a_vT^{\deg v}+q_vT^{2\deg v}$,
   resp. $1-a_vT^{\deg v}$, of D.0; for finite-order $\omega$ this is the value at $s=1$ of the complex twisted $L$-function.
2. (Uniqueness) $\mathcal L_\ell(E)$ is the unique element of $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$ with $\omega(\mathcal L)=L(E\otimes\omega,1)$ for all
   finite-order $\omega$.
3. (Transfer) $r_\ell(E):=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\mathcal L_\ell(E)=m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$.
4. (Main conjecture, unconditional) $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=\mathcal L_\ell(E)\cdot\Lambda$.
5. ($\ell$-adic BSD, rank part) $r_\ell(E)=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)=s_\ell+\delta_\ell(E/K)\ \ge\ s_\ell\ \ge\ r_{\mathrm{alg}}$;
   the first inequality is an equality iff $\mathrm{Fr}$ is semisimple at $q$, the second iff $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell^\infty]$ is finite; both are
   equalities if $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell_0^\infty]$ is finite for one prime $\ell_0$ (D.1.4.3, D.1.4.5).

*Proof.* Integrality: $L(E,T)=\prod(1-\alpha_iT)\in\mathbb Z[T]$ (D.1.1), so $L(E,\gamma/q)\in\mathbb Z[1/q][\gamma]\subset\mathbb Z_\ell[\gamma]$ as
$\ell\ne p$; its constant term (in $\gamma$) is $1$. (1) For $\omega=\chi\circ(G_K\to G_k\to\Gamma)$ one has
$\omega(\mathrm{Fr}_v)=\omega(\gamma)^{\deg v}$ because a geometric Frobenius at $v$ restricts to $\mathrm{Fr}^{\deg v}$ on $\bar k$; hence
each Euler factor of $L(E\otimes\omega,T)$ is the Euler factor of $L(E,T)$ with $T^{\deg v}$ replaced by $(\omega(\gamma)T)^{\deg v}$,
i.e. $L(E\otimes\omega,T)=L(E,\omega(\gamma)T)$ as rational functions, and $L(E\otimes\omega,q^{-1})=\prod_i(1-\alpha_i\omega(\gamma)/q)=\omega(\mathcal L_\ell(E))$.
(2) The characters of finite order correspond to the points $T=\zeta-1$, $\zeta\in\mu_{\ell^\infty}$, of the open unit disc,
infinitely many of them; a non-zero element of $\mathbb Q_\ell\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]$ has finitely many zeros in the open unit disc
(Weierstrass preparation). (3) $\mathcal L_\ell(E)=\prod_i\big((1-\tfrac{\alpha_i}q)-\tfrac{\alpha_i}{q}T\big)$; the $i$-th factor
vanishes at $T=0$ iff $\alpha_i=q$, in which case it equals $-T$. So $\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}=\#\{i:\alpha_i=q\}=m_q=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ by D.1.1.
(4) Put $u_i=\alpha_i/q$; each $u_i$ is an $\ell$-adic unit ($u_i\bar u_i=1$ at all complex places, $\ell\ne p$). Over a finite
extension $\mathcal O_\lambda$ containing all $u_i$: the factor $1-u_i\gamma=(1-u_i)-u_iT$ is a unit of $\mathcal O_\lambda[[T]]$ if $|u_i-1|_\ell=1$,
and equals $-u_i\,(\gamma-u_i^{-1})$ if $|u_i-1|_\ell<1$. The two index sets are stable under $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb Q}_\ell/\mathbb Q_\ell)$, so
$P_1:=\prod_{|u_i-1|_\ell=1}(1-u_i\gamma)$ and $D:=\prod_{|u_i-1|_\ell<1}(\gamma-u_i^{-1})$ lie in $\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]$; $P_1$ has unit constant
term, hence $P_1\in\Lambda^\times$; $c:=\prod_{|u_i-1|_\ell<1}(-u_i)\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$; and $D$ is a monic polynomial in $T$ with all roots
$u_i^{-1}-1$ in the open unit disc, hence a distinguished polynomial. Thus $\mathcal L_\ell(E)=cP_1\cdot D$ with $cP_1\in\Lambda^\times$, and
$D=\det(\gamma-\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}\mid M_{(1)})$ generates $\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(X_\infty)$ by Proposition D.5.6(3). (5) is (3), (4), Proposition D.5.6(4)–(5) and Proposition D.5.1. $\square$

**Remarks.** (i) *Shape of the dictionary.* With $\gamma\leftrightarrow$ geometric Frobenius the constant-extension Iwasawa
theory of $E/K$ for $\ell\ne p$ has exactly the shape of cyclotomic Iwasawa theory over $\mathbb Q$: an Iwasawa main conjecture
(D.5.7(4), a theorem, as is LLTT's for $\ell=p$), a control theorem (D.5.6(4)), and a "Schneider" statement
$\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)\ge\operatorname{rank}X_\infty/T$ with equality iff a pairing on the Selmer space is non-degenerate
(D.5.6(5): the pairing is the cup product on $M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}$, non-degenerate iff $\delta_\ell=0$). The three inputs of Tate–Milne
(D.1.4.3) are therefore: (main conjecture) + (Schneider non-degeneracy) + (algebraicity $B=0$), and Tate's proof supplies
Schneider non-degeneracy from algebraicity via the pairing coincidence D.5.3. (ii) *Where the "transfer" is hidden.* Statement
(3) is a tautology because the Iwasawa variable $\gamma$ *is* the Frobenius whose eigenvalue $q$ defines $r_{\mathrm{an}}$: the
constant-field direction is simultaneously the complex $s$-direction ($q^{-s}\mapsto\omega(\gamma)q^{-s}$) and the $\ell$-adic Iwasawa
direction, and algebraic multiplicities of an algebraic eigenvalue do not depend on the completion. There is nothing to
transfer. (iii) The statement for $\ell=p$ (Theorem D.5.5) is deep because $M$ must be replaced by log-crystalline cohomology
and the Selmer group by flat cohomology, but the shape is identical; LLTT's factor $\star$ carries the Euler-characteristic
corrections that vanish rationally for $\ell\ne p$. (iv) Geometric $\mathbb Z_\ell$-extensions and the Kummer towers
$\mathbb F_q(t^{1/d})$ of Ulmer (Invent. Math. 167 (2007)) behave differently: ranks can be unbounded there, the function-field
analogue of growth in anticyclotomic towers; no transfer statement is available or expected in that direction beyond
LLTT/Tan–Trihan–Tsoi. (v) *Correction of a tempting error.* One might expect $X_\infty\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$ to see only the
$\mathrm{Fr}$-*eigenvectors* (since each $S_n$ has corank $\dim\ker(\mathrm{Fr}^{\ell^n}-1)$); but the finite parts of the $S_n$ grow with $n$
and the direct limit acquires the full pro-$\ell$ generalised eigenspaces — this is exactly how the non-semisimple part of
$\mathrm{Fr}$ becomes $T$-torsion of $X_\infty$ of the Schneider type. The example $M^\circ=\mathbb Z_\ell^2$, $\mathrm{Fr}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$
gives $S_n\approx\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\oplus\mathbb Z/\ell^n$, $S_\infty\approx(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)^2$, $\operatorname{char}(X_\infty)=T^2$,
$\operatorname{rank}X_\infty/T=1$.

**Proposition D.5.8 [NEW] (Minkowski: there is no constant-field $\mathbb Z_\ell$-extension of $\mathbb Q$, hence no substitution rule
for twists).** Let $F$ be a global field and $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{ur}}(F)$ the group of finite-order idèle class characters of $F$
unramified at every place (trivial on $\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\mathcal O_v^\times\times\prod_{v\mid\infty}F_v^{\times,\circ}$).
(a) If $F=k(C)$, then $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{ur}}(F)=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Pic}(C),\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ contains $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ via $\deg$; a
character $\omega=\chi\circ\deg$ satisfies $L(E\otimes\omega,T)=L(E,\omega(\gamma)T)$, and the characters of $\Gamma$ are exactly
those with $\chi$ of $\ell$-power order. (b) If $F$ is a number field, $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{ur}}(F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{Cl}^+(F),\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$
is finite, and $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb Q)=1$. Consequently no $\mathbb Z_\ell$-extension of a number field is unramified at all places;
for every non-trivial finite-order character $\omega$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q_\infty/\mathbb Q)$ the Euler factor of $L(E\otimes\omega,s)$ at $p$
is not obtained from that of $L(E,s)$ by a substitution, and $L_p(E,T)$ is not a specialisation of $L(E,s)$.
*Proof.* (a) Class field theory for $F=k(C)$: $F^\times\backslash\mathbb A_F^\times/\prod_v\mathcal O_v^\times=\operatorname{Div}(C)/\operatorname{Prin}(C)=\operatorname{Pic}(C)$,
and $\deg:\operatorname{Pic}(C)\to\mathbb Z$; the substitution identity is the computation in the proof of D.5.7(1). (b)
$F^\times\backslash\mathbb A_F^\times/(\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\mathcal O_v^\times\times\prod_{v\mid\infty}F_v^{\times,\circ})\cong\mathrm{Cl}^+(F)$ (finiteness
of the narrow class group); for $F=\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb Q^\times\backslash\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q}^\times\cong\mathbb R_{>0}\times\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times$. A
$\mathbb Z_\ell$-extension unramified everywhere would yield infinitely many unramified characters. The last assertions follow since
$\mathbb Q_\infty/\mathbb Q$ is totally ramified at $p$: for $p\nmid N$, $\omega$ of conductor $p^n>1$ changes the Euler factor at $p$ to
$1$ (from $(1-a_pp^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}$) and multiplies the Frobenius at every other prime $v$ by $\omega(\mathrm{Fr}_v)$, a root of unity
that is not of the form $\zeta^{d(v)}$ for any additive "degree" function $d$ on primes (there is none on $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, as
$\mathcal X_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb Q)=1$). $\square$

**Reading of D.5.8.** The function-field transfer theorem D.5.7(3) rests on the existence of the constant-field extension,
which is unramified everywhere and whose characters twist $L(E,s)$ by a translation of $s$. Minkowski's theorem forbids this
over $\mathbb Q$: the cyclotomic $\mathbb Z_p$-extension is the only candidate for "$\mathbb Q\otimes_{\mathbb F_1}\overline{\mathbb F}_1$", it is
ramified at $p$, and its characters produce genuinely new $L$-functions. The conjecture $r_p=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ (MTT) is therefore
a statement with real content over $\mathbb Q$ and none over $K$. This is the precise sense in which "the transfer conjecture is a
theorem over function fields, and trivially so".

**Proposition D.6.1 [NEW] (formal; conditional on the hypothetical axioms (a)–(c) of [GAP D.1]).** Suppose a theory
$(\mathcal T,\theta,\mathcal H^1,\det_\infty)$ satisfies (a)–(c) of [GAP D.1] for $E/\mathbb Q$, with (c) strengthened to: the pairing is
$\theta$-invariant and non-degenerate on the whole generalised eigenspace $\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta\sim1}$. Then
$$\text{BSD(rank) for }E\iff E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C\to\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta=1}\text{ is surjective}.$$
*Proof.* By (a), $r_{\mathrm{an}}=\dim\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta\sim1}$ (algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue $1$ of $\theta$, i.e. order of
vanishing of the regularised determinant at $s=1$). By (b), $r_{\mathrm{alg}}\le\dim\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta=1}\le\dim\mathcal H^1(E)^{\theta\sim1}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$.
If the Abel–Jacobi map is onto the eigenspace, the pairing restricted to the eigenspace is the Néron–Tate height by (c), hence
non-degenerate, hence $\theta$ is semisimple at $1$ by the argument of Lemma D.5.2(b) applied to $N=\theta-1$ on the generalised
eigenspace: $\theta$-compatibility of the pairing (Leibniz rule with $\theta=2$ on the target, the weight of $H^1(E)$) means
$\langle Nx,y\rangle+\langle x,Ny\rangle=0$, so again $\operatorname{im}N\perp\ker N$, $\operatorname{im}N=(\ker N)^\perp$, and the radical of the pairing on
$\ker N$ is $\ker N\cap\operatorname{im}N$, which vanishes iff $N=0$; and $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$. Conversely
$r_{\mathrm{alg}}=r_{\mathrm{an}}$ forces both inequalities to be equalities. $\square$
So in any Deninger-type theory BSD(rank) would be *equivalent to an Abel–Jacobi surjectivity statement*, exactly as over $K$
(where it is $T_1(\mathcal E)$); the semisimplicity half would come for free from the height. This is the content of Deninger's
remark that the Néron–Tate pairing should be the restriction of the scalar product on the $1$-eigenspace (arXiv:2204.02714, §2).
Status: `[HEURISTIC]` as to existence; the implication itself is a proved formal statement.

**Corollary D.6.2 [NEW].** Let $N=\mathrm{Fr}-1$ on $M_1$ and let $b(E/K,\ell)$ be the number of Jordan blocks of size $\ge2$ of $N$.
Then $b(E/K,\ell)=\dim(\ker N\cap\operatorname{im}N)\le\dim B=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)[\ell^\infty]$. In particular:

$B$; hence $A=0$, $r_{\mathrm{alg}}=0$, and $\dim B=\dim M^{\mathrm{Fr}=1}=1$. $\square$
