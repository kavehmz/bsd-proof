Thus its two principal residue components give exactly Ψ,Ψ^c

## 2. Actual coefficient twist and unnormalized tame norm

\in H^1(K^{(2)}_{n,k},M_m). \tag{9}$$

## 4. Finite first jets and their unique corrected derivatives

Set
$$R_m=(\mathbb Z/p^m)[X,Y]/(X,Y)^2,\qquad J_m=(X,Y)R_m.
$$
For n,k≥m the exact finite group-ring map sends γ_π to1+X
and γ_barπ to1+Y. Its defining relations hold because
(1+X)^(p^n)=1+p^nX=1 and likewise for Y. In terms of ray
exponents this requires n+1,k+1≥m+1.

Use the norm-compatible Shapiro convention, with inverse Galois
action on the group ring, as in the earlier finite extraction.
The resulting action on M_m⊗R_m is
$$g(v\otimes1)=gv\otimes
(1-c_\pi(g)X-c_{\bar\pi}(g)Y). \tag{14}$$
Apply this finite group-ring map to the normalized actual class
from (9)–(13), after Shapiro over K. It gives
$$\mathfrak z_m\in H^1(G_{K,S},M_m\otimes R_m),
\qquad S=\{w:w\mid 2\cdot3\cdot13\cdot p\}\cup S_\infty.
\tag{15}$$
Norm compatibility proves independence of the sufficiently large
finite source and layer. Proposition3.1 proves that its augmentation
in H¹(G_{K,S},M_m) is zero.

The invariant group H⁰(G_{K,S},M_m) is zero. Indeed τ fixes K
and acts as −1 on T_π, and two is a p-unit. The same argument
works over every K^(2)_{n,k}, since τ belongs to Δ and fixes
those fields. It concerns the action of a lift of τ, not an
order-two element asserted in an absolute Galois group.

**[NEW] Proposition 4.1 (the actual two derivative classes).**
There is a unique pair
$$d_{\pi,m},d_{\bar\pi,m}\in H^1(G_{K,S},M_m)
\quad\text{with}\quad
\mathfrak z_m=X d_{\pi,m}+Y d_{\bar\pi,m}. \tag{16}$$
If a cocycle for (15) is f(g)=A(g)+XB(g)+YD(g), and
A(g)=gv−v, then the pair is represented by
$$d_{\pi,m}(g)=B(g)+c_\pi(g)gv,\qquad
d_{\bar\pi,m}(g)=D(g)+c_{\bar\pi}(g)gv. \tag{17}$$

*Proof.* The exact coefficient sequence
$$0\to M_m\otimes J_m\to M_m\otimes R_m\to M_m\to0 \tag{18}$$
has its usual M_m action on both submodule coordinates and on
the quotient. H⁰ of the quotient is zero, so the map on H¹
from the left is injective. Its image is the kernel of augmentation.
Since J_m has basis X,Y over Z/p^m, this proves existence and
uniqueness of (16).

The cocycle equations from (14) are
$$dA=0,\quad dB(g,h)=c_\pi(g)gA(h),\quad
dD(g,h)=c_{\bar\pi}(g)gA(h).$$
Subtract the deformed coboundary
gv−v−c_π(g)gvX−c_barπ(g)gvY from f. The result has
exactly the two coefficients in (17). Their differentials are
zero since d(c_π(g)gv)(g,h)=−c_π(g)gA(h), and similarly
in the other direction.

The vector v is unique by H⁰=0. If f changes by the coboundary
of u+Xs+Yt, then v changes to v+u, B to B+ds−c_π gu,
and D to D+dt−c_barπ gu. Thus (17) changes only by ds,dt.
This checks representative invariance directly. ∎

Reduction in m commutes with all maps constructing (15).
Uniqueness in (18) makes the two classes compatible in m.
There is no lim¹H⁰ term, since those invariant groups vanish.
Thus the actual unit tower supplies integral classes
$$d_\pi,d_{\bar\pi}\in H^1(G_{K,S},T_\pi). \tag{19}$$
Neither is defined by positing a free generator of two-variable
Iwasawa H¹.

## 5. Exact specialization and generator scalars

**[NEW] Proposition 5.1.** Let Sh denote the fixed Shapiro

$$\operatorname{Sh}(d_{\pi,m}+d_{\bar\pi,m})=d_m^{\rm cyc},\qquad

*Proof.* The coefficient map X,Y↦T takes (14) to

It takes (16) to T(d_π,m+d_barπ,m). The unique preimage

therefore is that sum. Shapiro introduces no extra factor two.
Compatibility and inverse limits prove the second equality. ∎

For explicit covariance under changing generators, replacing
γ_π by γ_π^u and γ_barπ by γ_barπ^v for units u,v∈Z_p×
gives X'=uX and Y'=vY to first order. The two new coefficient

The rational image of the SUM in (20) is in full Selmer by the

**[NEW] Proposition 6.1 (derived specialization, not H¹ flatness).**

$$\mathcal C_2\otimes_{\Lambda_2}^{\mathbf L}\mathbb Z_p

*Proof.* Use the finite free Koszul resolution on X,Y at the

Since H⁰ of the specialization is zero, its kernel is zero.

For the cyclotomic quotient itself, the same coefficient argument

## 7. Additional relations forced by the actual higher unit lift

The actual source gives more than a free-standing first-jet
cochain. It lifts to the full two-variable coefficient module.
Consequently its two first derivatives satisfy the following
global cohomological constraints.

**[NEW] Proposition 7.1.** In H²(G_{K,S},T_π),
$$c_\pi\cup d_\pi=0,\qquad
c_{\bar\pi}\cup d_{\bar\pi}=0,\qquad
c_\pi\cup d_{\bar\pi}+c_{\bar\pi}\cup d_\pi=0. \tag{25}$$

*Proof.* Represent z₂,∞ by a continuous one-cocycle with
coefficients T_π⊗Λ₂. Its constant coefficient is a coboundary
gv−v, since its actual augmentation class is zero integrally.
Subtract the coboundary of the constant lift v. The resulting
cocycle has zero constant coefficient. Write its first terms as
$$Xb+Yd+X^2u+XYv_2+Y^2w\pmod{I^3}.$$
The inverse action has linear part −c_πX−c_barπY.
Since the cocycle's constant term is zero, its quadratic action
terms do not contribute at degree two. Comparing coefficients
in its cocycle identity gives
$$du=c_\pi\cup b,\quad
dw=c_{\bar\pi}\cup d,\quad
dv_2=c_\pi\cup d+c_{\bar\pi}\cup b.$$
The classes of b,d are the unique derivatives already constructed,
so these coboundaries prove (25). ∎

These are global cup identities. The p-adic height uses a Selmer
cone with local terms; (25) does not say that the height or its
regulator is zero. It also does not prove individual Selmer
membership of d_π,d_barπ.
