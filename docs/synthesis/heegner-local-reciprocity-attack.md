$$
 R=\mathbb Z/p^k,\quad V=E[p^k],\quad
 P_m=S_GD_\ell D_qy_m,\quad
 c_m=\kappa_m^{\mathrm{raw}}\in H^1(K,V).
 \tag{1}
$$
The previous proof constructs

$c_m=0\Longleftrightarrow\theta_m=0$ at this full coefficient.

For the detection theorem in §2 we impose the additional hypothesis
$$
 \rho_{E,p^k}(G_{\mathbb Q})=\mathrm{GL}_2(R).
 \tag{2}
$$
One may instead assume the stronger uniform hypothesis
$\rho_{E,p^\infty}(G_{\mathbb Q})=\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_p)$
when applying the construction at every k. Neither hypothesis is

 H^1(\mathbb Q,V)\simeq H^1(K,V)^+,
 \tag{4}
$$
because cohomology of the order-two quotient with p-primary

## 2. Full-p-power detection by fresh Kolyvagin primes

**[NEW] Proposition 2.1.** Assume (2). If $c_m$ has exact order
$p^s$ with $1\le s\le k$, then infinitely many primes v, avoiding
any fixed finite set, satisfy
$$
 v\text{ inert in }K,\qquad
 v\equiv-1,\quad a_v\equiv0\pmod{p^k},
 \tag{5}
$$
and the finite localization of c_m at $\lambda=(v)$ has exact
order $p^s$. The primes can be chosen fresh, in particular
$v\nmid 6NpDm$.

*Proof.* Put $L=\mathbb Q(E[p^k])$ and $G=\mathrm{GL}_2(R)$.
The central scalar $2I$ acts on V by 2. For a cocycle b on G,
commuting $2I$ with g gives
$b(g)=(g-1)b(2I)$, so $H^1(G,V)=0$. Inflation-restriction
therefore injects $H^1(\mathbb Q,V)$ into
$H^1(L,V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_L,V)$.

Choose a cocycle c representing $c_{\mathbb Q}$. Its restriction
has image W, an R-submodule of V stable under the full G action:
for $h\in G_L$, $c(ghg^{-1})=\rho(g)c(h)$.
The algebra generated by G contains all two-by-two matrix units
(use differences of elementary unipotents and their products).
Thus W is $I\oplus I$ for an ideal I of R, hence $W=p^tV$.
Restriction is injective on cohomology and a homomorphism has
the same order as its image exponent. Consequently $t=k-s$.

Let $L_c$ be the finite Galois extension cut out by
$g\mapsto(c(g),\rho(g))\in V\rtimes G$. Then
$\operatorname{Gal}(L_c/L)=W$, a p-group. The quadratic
field K is not contained in L: it ramifies at a prime of D,
where the torsion field L is unramified because $(D,Np)=1$.
Nor can K lie in $L_c$, since $[L_c:L]$ is a p-power.
Hence K and $L_c$ are linearly disjoint over Q.

Choose a complex conjugation $\tau$. Its action on V has free
rank-one eigenspaces $V_\tau^+$ and $V_\tau^-$; p is odd.
Since $\tau^2=1$, the cocycle identity gives
$(1+\tau)c(\tau)=0$. Thus $c(\tau)\in V_\tau^-$.
Choose $w\in p^{k-s}V_\tau^+$ of exact order $p^s$.
The preceding field disjointness lets us find $h\in G_{LK}$
with $c(h)=w$. For $\gamma=h\tau$,
$$
 \rho(\gamma)=\tau,\qquad \gamma|_K\ne1,\qquad
 c(\gamma^2)=(1+\tau)(w+c(\tau))=2w.
 \tag{6}
$$
Chebotarev applied to $L_cK/\mathbb Q$ supplies infinitely many
unramified primes with Frobenius in this conjugacy class,
outside any specified finite set. Trace and determinant give
(5). Their K-Frobenius is the square of rational Frobenius.
Its coefficient action is trivial, so unramified evaluation
identifies the finite local cohomology with V.
The value in (6) has order $p^s$ since 2 is a unit.

Conjugating $\gamma$ in the semidirect product changes the
translation of $\gamma^2$, whose linear part is one, only by
the invertible coefficient action. Thus its order is unchanged.
This handles the conjugacy qualification in Chebotarev.
$\square$

For these primes let $F_v$ denote arithmetic Frobenius on V
under a chosen embedding at v. Then $F_v^2=1$, with both
eigenspaces $V_v^\pm$ free rank one over R. Fix an R-isomorphism
$\lambda_v:V_v^+\to R$ and define the actual local map
$$
 \operatorname{ev}_v:
 H^1_f(K_\lambda,V)^+\longrightarrow R,\qquad
 b\longmapsto\lambda_v\big(b(\operatorname{Frob}_\lambda)\big).
 \tag{7}
$$
The superscript refers to simultaneous conjugation on local
cohomology. On **finite** evaluation this action is $F_v$,
as proved in the previous note. On transverse evaluation
it is instead $v^{-1}F_v=-F_v$ modulo $p^k$; we retain
that distinction below. The map (7) is an isomorphism.

**[NEW] Corollary 2.2.** On the actual two-prime class, the
family of maps (7) at all fresh primes (5) detects zero,
and detects its exact additive order. In particular
$$
 \theta_m=0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{ev}_v(\operatorname{loc}_\lambda c_m)=0
       \text{ for every such fresh }v .
 \tag{8}
$$
Any finite subgroup of the plus cohomology unramified outside
a fixed finite set can be separated by finitely many of these
maps: repeatedly choose a nonzero element in the current
intersection of kernels and use Proposition 2.1.
This does not give a fixed prime-independent test matrix or
a rank-five bound.

## 3. An actual cyclic isogeny cover realizes the test

The rest of the local construction only needs a fresh prime
v satisfying (5); hypothesis (2) was used to obtain enough
such tests to detect the global class. All curves and groups
in this section are in characteristic v, which is distinct from p.

Let $\widetilde E/\mathbb F_v$ be the good reduction.
Finite-field Kummer evaluation is an isomorphism
$$
 \delta_v:\widetilde E(\mathbb F_{v^2})/
                  p^k\widetilde E(\mathbb F_{v^2})
             \longrightarrow V,\qquad
 U\longmapsto(F_v^2-1)V_U ,
 \quad p^kV_U=U .
 \tag{9}
$$
Here the torsion of the reduction is identified with V by
the prime-to-v specialization isomorphism.
The right side is independent of the division point because
$F_v^2=1$ on V. Surjectivity follows from finite-field
Kummer theory: $H^1(\mathbb F_{v^2},\widetilde E)=0$,
equivalently $F_v^2-1$ is a surjective isogeny on geometric
points. Its kernel is precisely the displayed p-multiples.

The subgroup scheme $V_v^-$ of $\widetilde E[p^k]$ is
defined over $\mathbb F_v$. Form the genuine quotient
$$
 \varphi_v:\widetilde E\longrightarrow A_v=\widetilde E/V_v^- .
$$
Multiplication by $p^k$ factors as
$$
 [p^k]=\eta_v\circ\varphi_v,\qquad
 \eta_v:A_v\longrightarrow\widetilde E,\qquad
 \ker(\eta_v)=V/V_v^-\simeq V_v^+.
 \tag{10}
$$
Both isogenies have degree $p^k$ and are étale.
The kernel of $\eta_v$ is constant over $\mathbb F_v$,
since Frobenius acts by +1. No square root of a determinant,
characteristic-zero isogeny, or global rational p-subgroup
of E is assumed.

**[NEW] Proposition 3.1 (geometric Frobenius value).**
The Kummer boundary for $\eta_v$, evaluated at arithmetic
$\mathbb F_{v^2}$-Frobenius, is
$$
 U\longmapsto\operatorname{pr}_+\delta_v(U)
        \in V_v^+,\qquad
 \operatorname{pr}_+=(1+F_v)/2 .
 \tag{11}
$$

*Proof.* Choose $V_U$ as in (9), and set
$Z_U=\varphi_v(V_U)$, so $\eta_v(Z_U)=U$.
The Frobenius translation of this lift is
$F_v^2Z_U-Z_U=\varphi_v((F_v^2-1)V_U)$.
The restriction of $\varphi_v$ to V identifies its quotient
by $V_v^-$ with $V_v^+$, proving (11). $\square$

The good-reduction modular parametrization extends to
$\pi_v:X_0(N)_{\mathbb F_v}\to\widetilde E$; this follows
from the Néron mapping property on the smooth model at
$v\nmid N$. It still sends infinity to O.
Pull (10) back along $\pi_v$:
$$
 \mathcal Z_v=X_0(N)_{\mathbb F_{v^2}}
                    \times_{\widetilde E}A_v
       \longrightarrow X_0(N)_{\mathbb F_{v^2}} .
 \tag{12}
$$
This is an actual finite étale torsor of degree $p^k$
under the constant group $V_v^+$. Its fiber at infinity
has the specified rational point O. We do not require
this cover to be geometrically connected.

Let $\Sigma_v$ be the supersingular points of
$X_0(N)_{\overline{\mathbb F}_v}$. They are rational over
$\mathbb F_{v^2}$. Their interpretation as enhanced
supersingular elliptic curves identifies them with the
definite quaternionic set for the algebra ramified at
v and infinity and Eichler level N. Orientations are
retained from the fixed Heegner ideal and the reduction
place. Define
$$
 \psi_{v,k}(x)=
 \lambda_v\operatorname{pr}_+\delta_v(\pi_v(x)),
       \qquad x\in\Sigma_v .
 \tag{13}
$$
It is precisely the Frobenius value of the fiber torsor
(12), in the declared basis. The same formula is defined
on all $\mathbb F_{v^2}$-points of the modular curve,
including infinity, where it is zero.

**[THEOREM, primary geometry used]** The supersingular
set, its definite quaternionic interpretation, CM
reduction and Frobenius/Hecke actions are described in
[Vatsal, *Special values of anticyclotomic L-functions*,
Duke Math. J. 116 (2003), 219–261, author §6.6–6.8
and §6.12–6.16](https://www.math.ubc.ca/~vatsal/research/mew3.pdf).
The source's isogeny-cover construction is modulo a
residual prime. Equations (9)–(13) supply the explicit
p-power version here. We use its geometric description
and equivariance, not its additional nonvanishing or
characteristic-zero comparison theorem.

In particular we have not used its Ihara argument
to claim $\psi_{v,k}$ is primitive for every v.
Such a claim needs extra hypotheses. The function
constructed here can be divisible by p or identically
zero; exact detection in Proposition 2.1 selects v
for which the particular derived sum is nonzero when
the global class is nonzero.

## 4. Hecke eigenfunction and exact derived toric evaluation

**[NEW] Proposition 4.1.** For every prime r away from Nv,
the function in (13), with Hecke action by summing the
r-isogeny targets with their actual multiplicities, satisfies
$$
 T_r\psi_{v,k}=a_r(E)\psi_{v,k}.
 \tag{14}
$$
It satisfies the corresponding $U_r$ relation at $r\mid N$.
On the degree-zero supersingular module, the new operator
$U_v$ is Frobenius permutation, so
$$
 \psi_{v,k}(U_v Z)=\psi_{v,k}(Z)
       \quad (Z\in R[\Sigma_v]^0).
 \tag{15}
$$

*Proof.* The relation
$\pi_*T_r=a_r(E)\pi_*$ on $J_0(N)$ can be applied to
the divisor $[x]-[\infty]$. The Hecke image of infinity
is its degree times infinity, so its image in E is zero.
Consequently summing $\pi_v$ on the actual isogeny
targets gives $a_r(E)\pi_v(x)$. Apply the group
homomorphism $\lambda_v\operatorname{pr}_+\delta_v$.
The same argument gives $U_r$ for $r\mid N$.

Since $\pi_v$ and $\delta_v$ commute with Frobenius,
$\psi_{v,k}(F_vx)=\lambda_v\operatorname{pr}_+F_v
\delta_v(\pi_vx)=\psi_{v,k}(x)$.
The supersingular character-module identification gives
$U_v=F_v$ on its degree-zero part; this is the action
recorded in Vatsal §6.16, citing Ribet Proposition 3.8.
This proves (15). $\square$

Thus (13) is a genuine finite-coefficient quaternionic
Hecke eigenfunction of level Nv, with $U_v=+1$ on the
new degree-zero module. It may be nonprimitive.
We do not assert that it is the reduction modulo $p^k$
of a single characteristic-zero newform.

For each $c\mid m$ and $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(H_c/K)$,
let
$$
 x_{v,c}(\sigma)=\operatorname{red}_{\mathfrak v}(\sigma x_c)
                         \in\Sigma_v ,
 \tag{16}
$$
where a prime $\mathfrak v$ of the ring class field
above v is fixed compatibly. CM reduction is
supersingular because v is inert and prime to cND.
The point carries the reduction of the
$\mathcal O_c$-embedding and the specified N-orientation;
it is the corresponding quaternionic toric point.
The conductor does not silently drop in this embedding.
At a prime t different from v, an element of K with
t-power denominator that became an endomorphism after
reduction would force its integral multiple to kill the
corresponding t-power torsion after reduction. Prime-to-v
étale specialization would give the same vanishing before
reduction, hence divisibility of that endomorphism by the
same t-power already in characteristic zero. At v the
order $\mathcal O_c$ is maximal because $v\nmid c$.
Thus the reduced embedding is optimal with the declared
conductor c. This also explains why no replacement of c
by a smaller conductor is made in (17).
The finite sum below counts each ring-class element
once. It does not divide by automorphism orders or
silently replace the CM orbit by distinct reduced points.

Define the actual derived toric divisor
$$
 \mathcal D_{v,m}
  =\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{q}
       ij\,[x_{v,m}(s\sigma_\ell^i\sigma_q^j)]
            \in\mathbb Z[\Sigma_v].
 \tag{17}
$$
This retains both derivative ranges and every trace
representative from the original point.

**[NEW] Proposition 4.2 (full-coefficient geometric reciprocity).**
$$
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \operatorname{ev}_v(\operatorname{loc}_\lambda c_m)
       =\psi_{v,k}(\mathcal D_{v,m})
       =\sum_{s,i,j}ij\,
          \psi_{v,k}(x_{v,m}(s\sigma_\ell^i\sigma_q^j))
          \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z/p^k .}
 \tag{18}
$$

*Proof.* The ideal $\lambda=(v)$ is principal, generated
by a rational integer prime to the conductor m, so its
ring-class Artin symbol in $H_m/K$ is one. Thus it
splits completely in $H_m/K$. Under the chosen
embedding the completion of $H_m$ is exactly
$K_\lambda$. The local restriction of the global
Kummer descent is therefore the Kummer class of the
actual point $P_m$ in $E(K_\lambda)/p^k$.

The good-reduction formal group is p-divisible
because v differs from p. Reducing that point and
using finite-field Kummer gives (9). Its image is
in the plus eigenspace by (3) and finite conjugation
equivariance, so projection onto that eigenspace
does not lose information. Finally reduction and
$\pi$ commute, and both Kummer and the group law
are additive. Inserting (1) gives exactly (17)–(18).
$\square$

There is a canonical way to put this same evaluation
on the degree-zero character module while displaying
the necessary unit. Choose a prime t away from Nv
with $a_t(E)-(t+1)\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$ and put
$$
 e_t=\frac{T_t-(t+1)}{a_t(E)-(t+1)}
       \quad\text{on }R[\Sigma_v].
 \tag{19}
$$
Under (2) such t can be found by Chebotarev: Frobenius
$2I$ modulo p gives trace 4, determinant 4 and
$a_t-(t+1)\equiv-1\pmod p$. Further finite exclusions
can be imposed. Degree of $T_t Z$ is $(t+1)\deg Z$,
so $e_tZ$ is in $R[\Sigma_v]^0$; (14) gives
$$
 \psi_{v,k}(e_t\mathcal D_{v,m})
                  =\psi_{v,k}(\mathcal D_{v,m}).
 \tag{20}
$$
The operator is only used as this map to degree
zero; no idempotence is claimed.

All equalities (18)–(20) are modulo the specified
$p^k$. For the signed normalized system, (18) is
multiplied by the known scalar $u_2=-1$ and the
chosen tensor generators. For the next transverse
class the reviewed local relation gives
$\kappa_{mv}^{\mathrm{raw}}(\sigma_v)
=\kappa_m^{\mathrm{raw}}(\operatorname{Frob}_\lambda)$,
since the raw two-prime sign is +1. The three-prime
raw sign is -1; the transverse conjugation action
is $-F_v$, exactly consistent with this equality.

## 5. Relation to the primary Jochnowitz congruence

The construction (12)–(18) is the finite isogeny-cover
mechanism of a Jochnowitz congruence, proved here at the
specified coefficient. Its target is a function on the
actual definite quaternionic set. It is useful to
distinguish it from a stronger characteristic-zero
newform identification.

**[THEOREM, precisely limited source comparison]**
[Bertolini-Darmon, *Euler systems and Jochnowitz
congruences*, Amer. J. Math. 121 (1999), 259–281,
Theorem 6.1, author PDF p. 24](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/darmon/pub/Articles/Research/21.Jochnowitz/paper.pdf)
assume that E is the strong Weil curve with minimal
parametrization, that E[p] is absolutely irreducible,
and $p\nmid2N\deg(\pi)$. The auxiliary prime is a
Kolyvagin prime relative to E,K,p. For their Hecke
maximal ideal at level Nv with $U_v=-w(E)$ and
each newform g on their v-new quotient, their
explicit isomorphism satisfies
$$
 \eta_{\mathrm{BD}}\big((P_K,0)\big)
     =u\,\deg(\pi)\,\mathcal L(g/K,1)
          \pmod{\mathfrak m}.
 \tag{21}
$$
Here the target is their rank-one supersingular
character-module quotient, $u=|\mathcal O_K^\times|/2$,
and $\mathcal L$ is their algebraic toric special-value
element. In our discriminant scope u=1. The
normalization of $\eta_{\mathrm{BD}}$ is not the
arbitrarily chosen basis $\lambda_v$ in (7);
the factor $\deg(\pi)$ in (21) is retained rather
than declared absent from every normalization.

This theorem is modulo the residual maximal ideal.
It is not being asserted modulo every $p^k$, nor
applied as stated to $D_\ell D_qy_{\ell q}$ instead
of its conductor-one trace. Its hypotheses on the
optimal curve and modular degree are only needed
when quoting (21); they were not used in the
direct geometric identity (18).

The same distinction applies to
[Vatsal, author §6.12–6.16](https://www.math.ubc.ca/~vatsal/research/mew3.pdf).
Its residual isogeny-cover functions become
characteristic-zero toric eigenfunctions modulo a
prime, up to a unit, after its nonvanishing and
multiplicity-one argument. For that argument the
source imposes additional hypotheses, including
the exclusion of primes dividing the Shimura
subgroup in §6.3. We use neither its assertion
that all such functions are nonzero nor its
global nonvanishing theorem. Equations (9)–(15)
prove the required cover and Hecke properties
without them.

In particular a function in (13) annihilated by
a power of p is still a valid eigenfunction as
an R-module element, but does not necessarily
define a faithful Hecke character with target R.
Even a primitive weak eigenfunction modulo
$p^k$ does not by definition lift to a single
characteristic-zero newform congruent to all
its coefficients at that precision. No such
lift is assumed here.

There are no omitted automorphism weights in
(18): it evaluates a function on a divisor,
counting all repeated reductions. If one
instead identifies functions with divisors by
the quaternionic monodromy pairing, that
pairing has weight $|\operatorname{Aut}(x)|/2$
at a supersingular point x. Likewise a
Waldspurger formula has its own period and
test-vector normalization. Neither operation
is necessary for (18), and neither is made
without those extra factors.

## 6. The exact effect of untwisted central vanishing

For a conductor c prime to vND, define the
unnormalized finite toric element
$$
 \Theta_{v,c}
  =\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(H_c/K)}
       \psi_{v,k}(x_{v,c}(\sigma))\,\sigma^{-1}
       \in R[\operatorname{Gal}(H_c/K)] .
 \tag{22}
$$
For c=m let $[1]$ denote coefficient at the
identity of the group ring. Then (18) is
exactly
$$
 \operatorname{ev}_v(\operatorname{loc}c_m)
       =[1]\big(S_GD_\ell D_q\Theta_{v,m}\big).
 \tag{23}
$$
This coefficient operation is not augmentation.
No division by $|\operatorname{Gal}(H_m/K)|$
or by p-power character denominators occurs.

**[NEW] Proposition 6.1 (the actual trace consequences).**
The analytic O5 hypotheses imply
$$
 \operatorname{aug}(\Theta_{v,1})=0\quad\text{in }R.
 \tag{24}
$$
For a fresh conductor prime r which is
inert in K and satisfies $r\nmid cNpvD$, the actual
CM orbit values satisfy
$$
 \sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(H_{cr}/H_c)}
   \psi_{v,k}\big(x_{v,cr}(\sigma\tau)\big)
       =a_r(E)\,
          \psi_{v,k}\big(x_{v,c}(\bar\sigma)\big).
 \tag{25}
$$
Thus at the two conductor primes in m the
corresponding fiber sums are zero modulo
$p^k$.

*Proof.* Gross-Zagier and the untwisted
first-derivative vanishing make $P_K$ torsion.
There is no p-primary torsion in E(K),
so its local point class and (7) are zero.
The unweighted conductor-one instance of
(18) is exactly (24).
Apply reduction, $\pi_v$ and the additive
finite-field boundary to the compatible
Heegner trace identity
$\operatorname{Tr}y_{cr}=a_ry_c$ to obtain
(25). Its final assertion uses
$p^k\mid a_\ell,a_q$. $\square$

The extra untwisted third and fourth
derivative vanishings have not entered
(24)–(25). These identities do not compute
the weighted coefficient (23). Replacing
that coefficient by the augmentation would
discard the exact derivative weights
which define the class.

There is also an actual symmetry vanishing,
but it stops at parity:

**[NEW] Proposition 6.2.** Let n be a squarefree
product of Heegner Kolyvagin primes. If n has
an odd number of prime factors, at a fresh prime v
with all needed common p-power congruences,
the analogue of the plus-valued derived
sum (18) is zero. For an even number of
conductor primes, conjugation permits the
plus-valued sum and does not force it
to vanish.

*Proof.* The reviewed raw conjugation sign
is $(-1)^{\nu(n)}$ when $w(E)=-1$.
Finite local evaluation has this same
$F_v$ eigenvalue. The plus projection in
(13) therefore kills the entire evaluation
for odd $\nu(n)$, proving the geometric
sum identity. For even $\nu(n)$ its image
is in $V_v^+$, on which that projection
is the identity. Thus this symmetry
calculation yields no even-index
vanishing. $\square$

This is an identity for the actual
isogeny-cover toric sums, not a model
module example. At two primes it is
consistent with the full detection in
Proposition 2.1 and leaves the needed
rank-five step unresolved.
