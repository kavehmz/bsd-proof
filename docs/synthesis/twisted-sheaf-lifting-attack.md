This attempt constructs an actual global object. For a class
$\beta\in\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)$ of period $n$, it produces a
locally free $\beta$-twisted bundle of rank $n$ on the fixed arithmetic
model of $389a1$. On the generic elliptic curve the bundle can be chosen

Brauer–Hasse–Noether. This is a genuine descent step that succeeds.

It does **not** split the original gerbe over $E$. Its minimal possible
twisted rank is exactly $n$. The remaining rank-one moduli is a torsor
under $E$, and local points do not supply its global point. A second,
Poincaré pushforward construction verifies the same period-dependent

The next Fourier–Mukai step also succeeds: a compatible stable twisted
bundle of numerical type $(n,1)$ transforms to an ordinary line bundle
of degree $-n$ on the corresponding genus-one torsor. Sections 9–10
calculate this target and its descent explicitly. It is not a twisted
line on $E$; transformations preserving the original torsor/twist
data retain rank divisibility by $n$.

## 1. The fixed surface and the class to lift

Use the already constructed regular proper flat surface
$$
\mathcal E:\quad Y^2Z+YZ^2=X^3+X^2Z-2XZ^2\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb Z}.
$$
Its generic fiber is $E=389a1$, with section $O=[0:1:0]$. The reviewed
[arithmetic-surface note](weil-etale-lattice-attack.md) proves
$$
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal E)\overset\sim\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(E),
\qquad\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)\simeq\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q).
\tag{1.1}
$$
The latter includes the real place and possible divisible parts. The
generic restriction further injects into $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb Q(E))$.
No finiteness assumption enters (1.1).

Let $\beta$ be any element of exact order $n$. Choose a Kummer lift
$$z\in H^2_{\rm fppf}(\mathcal E,\mu_n)$$
of $\beta$, and let $\mathscr G\to\mathcal E$ be its $\mu_n$-gerbe.
Subtract a multiple of $c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(O))$ so that the
restriction of $z$ to $E_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$ is zero. This is possible
because $H^2(E_{\overline{\mathbb Q}},\mu_n)=\mathbb Z/n$, generated by
the degree-one class. The restriction to the zero section is automatically
zero, since
$$H^2(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z,\mu_n)=0$$
by fppf Kummer theory, $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb Z)=0$, and
$\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb Z)=0$. Fix a neutralization there.

A *weight-one twisted sheaf* means that inertia acts by its standard
character. The determinant of a rank-$n$ such sheaf on a $\mu_n$-gerbe
has weight zero and descends to the base. Over the generic fiber,
the geometric triviality of $z$ makes its degree and slope the ordinary
degree and slope after untwisting by a degree-zero twisted line. These
definitions do not depend on the trivialization.

The generic local Selmer data say that $\beta|_{E_{\mathbb Q_v}}=0$ for
every place $v$. Hence
$$z|_{E_{\mathbb Q_v}}=c_1(M_v),\qquad M_v\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(E_{\mathbb Q_v}).
\tag{1.2}
$$
To obtain degree zero, first choose any Kummer line representing the
local lift; its degree is divisible by $n$, and tensor it with the
appropriate $n$th power of $\mathcal O_E(O)$.

**[THEOREM, framework]** The change between $\mu_n$- and $\mathbb G_m$-
gerbes preserves the category of weight-one twisted sheaves. A twisted
bundle of rank $r$ gives an Azumaya algebra of degree $r$ by descending
its endomorphism algebra. Coherent twisted sheaves extend from the
generic point, and on a regular surface their reflexive hulls are
locally free. These are
[Lieblich, arXiv:math/0511244v4, Lemma 3.1.1.12, Proposition 3.1.2.1,
Lemma 3.1.3.1 and Proposition 3.1.3.9](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0511244v4),
*Compositio Mathematica* **144** (2008), 1–31,
[DOI 10.1112/S0010437X07003144](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X07003144).
The mixed-characteristic extension below uses the $\mathbb G_m$-gerbe,
not a claim that a $\mu_n$-gerbe is étale at residue characteristics
dividing $n$.

## 2. An explicit degree-one stable bundle with any rank

The following elementary form of the elliptic bundle classification
is enough; it is proved here rather than applying a genus-at-least-two
moduli theorem to an elliptic curve.

**[NEW] Lemma 2.1.** Let $C/F$ be a smooth projective genus-one curve
with rational point and let $D$ be a degree-one line bundle. For every
$r\geq1$ there is a geometrically stable rank-$r$ bundle $V_r(D)$ with
determinant $D$. Over an algebraic closure it is unique up to isomorphism.
It has $h^0=1$ and $h^1=0$.

*Proof.* Set $V_1=D$. Suppose $V_{r-1}$ has been constructed. Riemann–Roch
and Serre duality give
$$\operatorname{Ext}^1(V_{r-1},\mathcal O_C)
 \simeq H^0(C,V_{r-1})^\vee\simeq F,$$
because the canonical bundle is trivial. Choose a nonzero extension
$$0\to\mathcal O_C\to V_r\to V_{r-1}\to0.\tag{2.1}$$
It remains nonzero after extending the field, so stability can be
checked over an algebraic closure.

If a proper subbundle $W\subset V_r$ were destabilizing, its integral
degree would be at least one. Its intersection with $\mathcal O_C$
has degree at most zero. If its image in $V_{r-1}$ has smaller rank
than $r-1$, stability of $V_{r-1}$ makes that image have degree at most
zero, a contradiction. In the remaining case $W$ has rank $r-1$,
maps injectively into $V_{r-1}$, and its degree is at least one.
Its image must then have degree exactly one and zero torsion cokernel.
It splits (2.1), again a contradiction. Thus $V_r$ is stable.

A positive-slope stable bundle has no nonzero map to $\mathcal O_C$;
Serre duality therefore gives $h^1(V_r)=0$, and Riemann–Roch gives
$h^0(V_r)=1$.

Conversely, let $V$ be any stable rank-$r$, degree-one bundle with
determinant $D$ over an algebraic closure. Its unique nonzero section
has no zeros: otherwise its saturation is a positive-degree line
subbundle, contradicting stability. The quotient by $\mathcal O_C$
has rank $r-1$, degree one, and is stable. Indeed a destabilizing
subbundle of that quotient would have degree at least one, and its
inverse image would destabilize $V$. By induction the quotient is
$V_{r-1}(D)$, and the extension is nonzero. All nonzero elements of
its one-dimensional extension space give isomorphic bundles. This
proves uniqueness. $\square$

This agrees with the coprime-rank/degree case of
[Atiyah, *Vector Bundles Over an Elliptic Curve*, Proc. London Math. Soc.
(3) **7** (1957), 414–452](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-7.1.414).
Only the degree-one case proved above will be used.

## 3. Fixed determinant: a base-field gerbe that really does descend

Let $\mathscr G_E$ be the generic fiber of $\mathscr G$. Consider the
stack $\mathscr M_z$ of pairs
$$
(V,\delta),\qquad
V\text{ geometrically stable, weight one, rank }n,
\quad\delta:\det V\overset\sim\longrightarrow\mathcal O_E(O).
\tag{3.1}
$$
Here the determinant is viewed as its descended weight-zero line.

**[NEW] Proposition 3.1 (global twisted object from local objects).**
$\mathscr M_z$ is a $\mu_n$-gerbe over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q$,
and this gerbe is neutral. In particular (3.1) has a global object.

*Proof.* Over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ the gerbe $\mathscr G_E$ is neutral.
Choose a weight-one trivializing line $T$ with $T^n\simeq\mathcal O$.
Tensoring with $T^{-1}$ identifies (3.1) with stable ordinary bundles
of rank $n$ and determinant $\mathcal O_E(O)$. Lemma 2.1 gives exactly
one geometric isomorphism class. Its automorphisms are scalars; preserving
the determinant identifies the automorphism band with $\mu_n$.

There are no hidden infinitesimal moduli. Stability gives
$H^0(E_{\overline{\mathbb Q}},\mathcal End(V))=\overline{\mathbb Q}$.
Serre duality identifies the trace map
$H^1(\mathcal End(V))\to H^1(\mathcal O_E)$ with the dual of the
scalar inclusion on $H^0$, so it is an isomorphism. In characteristic
zero the trace splitting therefore gives
$H^1(\mathcal End_0(V))=0$; also $H^2(\mathcal End_0(V))=0$
because the base is a curve. The fixed-determinant deformation theory
is consequently rigid and unobstructed. Thus the stable fixed-determinant
stack is étale locally this $\mu_n$-gerbe, not a thicker moduli space
with just one geometric point. Alternatively, the descent gerbe of
the chosen geometric object alone suffices for the argument below.

One may see the descent gerbe directly. Choose the geometric object
and determinant-preserving isomorphisms between it and its Galois
conjugates. The failure of their cocycle condition is a scalar
$2$-cocycle in $\mu_n$. Its class
$$\gamma_z\in H^2(\mathbb Q,\mu_n)\tag{3.2}$$
is exactly the obstruction to descending the chosen geometric object.
The data descend to a finite extension, so this is continuous Galois
cohomology. Equivalently, the fixed-determinant moduli has one coarse
point and is the indicated banded gerbe over it.

At a completion, (1.2) identifies $\mathscr G_E$ with the root gerbe
of $M_v$. Let $T_v$ be its tautological weight-one line, so
$T_v^n$ descends to $M_v$. Lemma 2.1 constructs the ordinary stable
bundle
$$W_v=V_n(\mathcal O_E(O)\otimes M_v^{-1}).$$
Then $T_v\otimes W_v$ has precisely the determinant in (3.1), is
geometrically stable, and is a local object of $\mathscr M_z$.
Thus every localization of $\gamma_z$ is zero.

Kummer theory over a field gives
$H^2(\mathbb Q,\mu_n)=\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb Q)[n]$.
Brauer–Hasse–Noether now implies $\gamma_z=0$. Modifying the isomorphisms
by a $1$-cochain makes their cocycle condition exact and descends $V$
with its determinant identification. $\square$

The distinction between the two gerbes is essential. The neutralized
$\mathscr M_z$ lives **over $\mathbb Q$** and parametrizes rank-$n$
objects. The original gerbe $\mathscr G_E$ lives **over $E$** and
has Brauer class $\beta$. Proposition 3.1 has not neutralized it.
In particular the descended endomorphism algebra is an Azumaya algebra
representing $\beta$, not an untwisted endomorphism algebra of a
bundle on $E$.

## 4. Extension with the same rank to the arithmetic surface

**[NEW] Proposition 4.1.** The generic twisted bundle from Proposition
3.1 extends to a locally free weight-one twisted bundle $\mathcal V$
on $\mathscr G$, of rank $n$, whose descended determinant is
$\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(O)$. Its endomorphism algebra descends to an
Azumaya algebra $\mathcal A_\beta$ of degree $n$ on $\mathcal E$.
The generic bundle is geometrically stable; stability at every special
fiber is not asserted.

*Proof.* Pass to the associated $\mathbb G_m$-gerbe using the cited
change-of-band equivalence. It is étale locally neutral over the regular
surface: take étale neighborhoods $U\to\mathcal E$ on which the gerbe
is neutral. Weight-one modules there identify with ordinary modules
on the regular surface $U$. This uses étale neighborhoods of the base,
not an étale scheme atlas for the Artin $\mathbb G_m$-gerbe.

Spread the generic bundle and its finite presentation to
$\mathcal E[1/S]$ for a finite set of primes $S$. Shrinking this open
if necessary makes the extension locally free there. Extend it
coherently over the surface and take its reflexive hull. These operations
can equally be performed on modules for any Azumaya representative of
$\beta$, and therefore retain the twisting datum. On regular local
rings of dimension at most two every reflexive module is locally free.
This proves local freeness on the gerbe without requiring a $\mu_n$-
gerbe to be étale at primes dividing $n$. The rank and the given bundle
on the open set are unchanged.

Return to the original $\mu_n$-gerbe. The determinant has weight zero
and descends to a line bundle on $\mathcal E$ whose generic class is
$\mathcal O_E(O)$. The isomorphism on Picard groups in (1.1) makes
this line $\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}(O)$. Choose an integral determinant
identification; it may replace the initially chosen generic identification
by a rational scalar, which has no effect on the statement about the
line bundle or the Azumaya algebra. Finally $\operatorname{End}(\mathcal V)$
has weight zero, descends, and is étale locally a matrix algebra of
degree $n$. $\square$

The zero-section neutralization also identifies $\mathcal V|_O$ with
a rank-$n$ vector bundle on $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, hence with
a free module. Choosing a basis gives an actual integral framing there.
This splits the Azumaya algebra on that section only; it is not a
section of its Severi–Brauer fibration over all of $\mathcal E$.

The cited extension argument is a regular-dimension-two theorem. A
reflexive hull need not be locally free in dimension three, and none
of the arithmetic-surface conclusions here is inferred from such a
higher-dimensional assertion.

## 5. A second construction using the actual torsor and Poincaré family

The coordinator suggested the following independent geometric construction.
It verifies the same period-dependent rank, and keeps the descent obstruction
in the universal Picard family explicit.

Let $C$ be the genus-one torsor corresponding to $\beta$ under the
normalized Hochschild–Serre identification, with its sign chosen as
described below. It is everywhere locally soluble and has period $n$.
The reviewed [genus-one note, Proposition 2.3](genus-one-finiteness-attack.md)
constructs a closed point $Z=\operatorname{Spec}L$ of degree $n$.
Briefly, $n[C]=0$ gives a rational degree-$n$ Picard class; local
solubility and Brauer–Hasse–Noether make it a line bundle; Riemann–Roch
makes it effective. Every closed-point degree is a multiple of $n$,
so this effective divisor is one degree-$n$ point. No finite-Sha
assumption is needed.

Let $\mathscr P_C^0\to\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)=E$ be the Picard gerbe
of degree-zero line bundles on $C$. By its moduli definition it has a
universal weight-one line $\mathcal P$ on $C\times\mathscr P_C^0$.
The object $\mathcal O_C$ neutralizes this gerbe over $O\in E$.

Here is the sign check rather than an implicit identification. Choose
$x\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q})$ and put $t_\sigma=\sigma x-x$.
Use $c\mapsto c-x$ to pull the normalized Poincaré line on $E\times E$
back to $C\times E$. Changing $x$ to $\sigma x$ multiplies the
family by the degree-zero line corresponding to $-t_\sigma$ on the
second factor. Thus its Picard-gerbe obstruction has Hochschild–Serre
class represented by $(-t_\sigma)$ under this convention. Take $C$
with this class equal to the class of $\beta$; equivalently replace
$C$ by its inverse torsor if the opposite convention was initially
used. Both the original Brauer class and the Picard-gerbe class are
normalized at $O$, so equality of their Hochschild–Serre images makes
them equal, with no undetermined constant Brauer term.

**[NEW] Proposition 5.1 (finite Poincaré pushforward).** Restrict the
universal line to $Z\times\mathscr P_C^0$ and push it along the second
projection:
$$\mathcal W_\beta=(p_2)_*(\mathcal P|_{Z\times\mathscr P_C^0}).\tag{5.1}$$
This is a weight-one twisted vector bundle of rank $n$ on $E$ for
the class $\beta$.

*Proof.* The projection is finite flat of degree $n$. Its direct image
of an invertible sheaf is locally free of rank $n$, and the scalar
inertia action remains weight one. The previous cocycle calculation
identifies the gerbe. $\square$

Over an algebraic closure, (5.1) is the sum of the $n$ degree-zero
Poincaré lines indexed by the geometric points of $Z$. Thus it is
not the stable degree-one bundle of §3, but its endomorphism algebra
has the same degree and Brauer class. The integral extension argument
of §4 applies to it as well without a prescribed determinant.

The same point $Z$ gives $C(L)\ne\varnothing$, so $\beta|_{E_L}=0$.
After base change to $\mathcal O_L$, normalization and resolution of
the resulting arithmetic surface produce a regular proper splitting
object of generic degree $n$ over $\mathcal E$: its pulled-back Brauer
class is generically zero, hence zero by regular Brauer injectivity.
No claim that an unmodified, possibly singular ramified base change
already has injective generic Brauer restriction is needed.

Thus an actual splitting object exists with degree $n$. Its construction
still uses the individual torsor's degree-$n$ point, not a fixed degree
independent of the class.

## 6. The rank obstruction survives determinants and ordinary K-theory

**[NEW] Lemma 6.1 (determinant obstruction).** If a weight-one twisted
vector bundle of rank $r$ exists for a Brauer class $\beta$ of period
$n$, then $n\mid r$.

*Proof.* In a local trivialization, its gluing matrices satisfy
$$g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki}=a_{ijk}I_r,$$
where $(a_{ijk})$ represents $\beta$. Taking determinants makes
$(a_{ijk}^r)$ a coboundary. Hence $r\beta=0$ and $n\mid r$.
Equivalently, its determinant is a weight-$r$ twisted line on the
$\mathbb G_m$-gerbe, which trivializes the $r$th multiple of the
Brauer class, not the original class. $\square$

**[NEW] Corollary 6.2 (optimal rank and actual twisted rank image).**
For every $\beta\in\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal E)$ of period $n$,
$$
\min\{\operatorname{rank}\mathcal V:\mathcal V\text{ a nonzero locally free
 weight-one twisted sheaf}}=n,
$$
$$\operatorname{ind}(\beta_{\mathbb Q(E)})=n,
\qquad\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{rank}:K_0^\beta(\mathcal E)
                                        \to\mathbb Z\bigr)=n\mathbb Z.
\tag{6.1}
$$
Here $K_0^\beta$ is the group of weight-one twisted bundles, not the
ordinary $K_0$ ring.

*Proof.* Proposition 4.1 attains rank $n$ and Lemma 6.1 gives the lower
bound. Generic Brauer injectivity preserves the period; a degree-$n$
Azumaya representative and period-divides-index give generic index $n$.
Ranks of differences of twisted bundles lie in $n\mathbb Z$, while
the constructed rank-$n$ bundle shows equality. $\square$

The ordinary Picard lattice does not remove this obstruction. The
determinant fixed in §3 is ordinary because the rank is already $n$:
it certifies the known relation $n\beta=0$. It does not manufacture
a weight-one line or a relation $\beta=0$.

Moreover, $K_0^\beta$ is naturally a module for ordinary $K_0$, not
a ring under its own tensor product: tensoring two weight-one bundles
changes the twist to $2\beta$, and an exterior power changes its weight.
Thus roots or cancellation identities in ordinary $K_0$ cannot be
applied as though these were untwisted line bundles.

The algebra $\mathcal A_\beta=\operatorname{End}(\mathcal V)$ does have
canonically trivial ordinary determinant. Locally, conjugation by $g$
on matrices has determinant
$\det(g)^n\det(g^{-1})^n=1$. The coordinator's
separate ordinary-$K_0$ calculation $K_0(\mathcal E)=\mathbb Z\oplus
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal E)$ with square-zero augmentation,
now independently [reviewed PASS](review-k-theory-lattice.md), implies
$$[\mathcal A_\beta]=n^2[\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}]\quad\text{in ordinary }K_0.
\tag{6.2}
$$
It is an equality after forgetting algebra multiplication. The Brauer
class is still $\beta$, and (6.1) still holds. The ordinary-$K_0$ input
and deduction (6.2) have both passed their separate independent reviews.
None of the twisted-bundle
construction depends on the ordinary-$K_0$ calculation.

## 7. The rational section that is still missing

Form the Severi–Brauer fibration
$$S_\beta=\operatorname{SB}(\mathcal A_\beta)\longrightarrow\mathcal E,$$
whose fibers are forms of $\mathbb P^{n-1}$. The chosen framing gives
a rational point over the zero-section fiber. At every completion the
generic fibration over $E_{\mathbb Q_v}$ has a rational section because
$\beta_v=0$. Over a regular curve such a generic section extends by
properness. Neither statement gives a section over $\mathbb Q(E)$.

**[NEW] Proposition 7.1 (the surviving descent step).** The following
are equivalent:

1. $S_\beta$ has a rational section over $E$.
2. Its generic Severi–Brauer variety has a $\mathbb Q(E)$-point.
3. The original Brauer class $\beta$ is zero.
4. Its $\mathbb G_m$-gerbe admits a weight-one line bundle.

*Proof.* The first two are the definition of a rational section. A
Severi–Brauer variety has a rational point exactly when its central
simple algebra splits. Generic Brauer injectivity makes this equivalent
to $\beta=0$ on $\mathcal E$. A weight-one line trivializes that gerbe,
and conversely a neutralization supplies such a line. $\square$

There is a direct moduli description of this remaining step. Over an
algebraic closure, weight-one twisted lines of degree zero form a
torsor under $\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)=E$. Framing at the neutralized
zero section removes their scalar automorphisms. Descent gives an
actual $E$-torsor $T_\beta$ over $\mathbb Q$, equipped with its
universal framed twisted line. The cocycle of changes between geometric
trivializations is the Hochschild–Serre class of $\beta$, up to the
same harmless sign convention as §5. Thus $T_\beta$ has local points
everywhere, while constructing a rational point on it is exactly the
remaining rank-one problem. If such a point is supplied, the framing
eliminates the subsequent universal-sheaf gerbe obstruction; it does
not supply the point.

This explains why §3's Brauer–Hasse–Noether argument cannot simply be
reused. There the coarse moduli was a single rational point and the
only obstruction was a gerbe over $\mathbb Q$. Here the coarse moduli
is the possibly nontrivial genus-one torsor $T_\beta$ itself.

## 8. Arithmetic boundedness and the exact unresolved target

The constructed stable generic bundle has fixed determinant of degree
one, but its rank is $n$. For the fixed plane-cubic polarization of
degree three its Hilbert polynomial is
$$P_n(t)=3nt+1.$$
Thus fixing the determinant and imposing stability have not made a
single bounded moduli problem as $\beta$ varies. Boundedness for one
rank and one Hilbert polynomial does not bound these slopes $3n$.

More decisively, any finite splitting field of $\beta$ and any finite
generically flat splitting cover has degree divisible by $n$, by
restriction followed by corestriction. Lemma 6.1 is the sheaf-theoretic
version of the same obstruction. The constructions in §§3–5 attain
the period-dependent rank or degree; no period-independent estimate
has appeared.

**[GAP TS-389]** For the fixed surface $\mathcal E$ and the explicitly

## 9. Fourier–Mukai really reduces the rank, on the torsor

Keep the Picard gerbe $\mathscr H=\mathscr P_C^0$ and its universal
weight-one line $\mathcal P$ from §5. The transform of an ordinary
object on $C$ with kernel $\mathcal P$ is $\beta$-twisted on $E$.
In the opposite direction the dual kernel cancels the twist:
$$
\Psi(V)=\mathbf Rp_{C,*}
  (\mathcal P^\vee\otimes p_E^*V)\in D^b(C).
\tag{9.1}
$$
The tensor inside (9.1) has weight zero and therefore descends from
$C\times\mathscr H$ to $C\times E$. The target in (9.1) is the
actual curve $C$ over $\mathbb Q$, not a chosen geometric identification
$C_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}\simeq E_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$.

**A necessary degree normalization.** An equivalence of two
$\mathbb G_m$-gerbes can tensor all objects by an ordinary line bundle.
For rank $n$ this shifts degree by a multiple of $n$. Thus the degree
normalization of §3 must be aligned with the kernel before substituting
$(n,1)$ into a Fourier–Mukai numerical formula.

Here is an actual alignment. Let $\mathcal W_\beta$ be (5.1). Its
determinant is a weight-$n$ line on $\mathscr H$. Define the reduction
$\mathscr G'\to E$ to have, as objects, objects of $\mathscr H$
together with a trivialization of this determinant. It is a $\mu_n$-
gerbe: locally a trivialization exists, and its preserving inertia is
$\mu_n$. Its associated $\mathbb G_m$-gerbe is $\mathscr H$.

After choosing $x\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q})$, the normalized Poincaré
family makes $\mathcal W_\beta$ a sum of degree-zero lines on
$E_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$. Its determinant therefore has degree zero.
To obtain a section of $\mathscr G'$ one only needs an $n$th root
of that degree-zero determinant, which exists geometrically and again
has degree zero. Consequently the numerical degree defined by
$\mathscr G'$ agrees with the normalized Poincaré degree.

The Kummer class of $\mathscr G'$ extends to $\mathcal E$: it differs
from the restriction of the original $z$ by $c_1(M)$ for some
$M\in\operatorname{Pic}(E)$, and (1.1) extends $M$. Its geometric
Kummer class is zero, and its restriction at $O$ is zero as in §1.
Repeat Proposition 3.1 for this reduction. It constructs a geometrically
stable twisted $V$ whose rank/degree in the Poincaré normalization is
exactly $(n,1)$. This choice changes the auxiliary Kummer lift, not the
Brauer class being split.

**[NEW] Proposition 9.1 (the rank-one transform).** For this $V$,
$\Psi(V)$ is an ordinary line bundle on $C$ of degree $-n$.

*Proof.* Work first geometrically, choose $x\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q})$,
and identify the Poincaré family with the normalized one on $E\times E$.
For any $c\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q})$, its restriction along
$\{c\}\times E$ is a degree-zero line. The fiber of (9.1) is therefore
the cohomology of a stable ordinary bundle of rank $n$, degree one.
Lemma 2.1, or its Serre-duality argument after tensoring by a degree-zero
line, gives $H^1=0$ and $h^0=1$. Cohomology and base change makes
(9.1) a locally free sheaf of rank one, with no higher direct image.
These properties descend to $C/\mathbb Q$.

For completeness compute the degree. On $E\times E$ write
$$D=\Delta-E\times\{O\}-\{O\}\times E=c_1(\mathcal P).$$
The three curves have square zero, each pair intersects once, and hence
$D^2=-2[\mathrm{point}]$. Both factor restrictions of $D$ have degree
zero. Since the genus-one Todd class is one, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch
gives, in numerical cohomology,
$$
\operatorname{ch}(\Psi(V))
 =p_{C,*}\bigl((1-D+D^2/2)(n+[\mathrm{point}]_E)\bigr)
 =1-n[\mathrm{point}]_C.
$$
Thus its degree is $-n$, as claimed. $\square$

The dual line has degree $n$ and yields, by Riemann–Roch, another
effective degree-$n$ divisor on $C$. This reproduces the individual
torsor's period-index degree; it supplies no rational point when $n>1$.
No numerical equality here states that $n>1$ actually occurs for389a1.

There is also a shift to retain. The Poincaré transform $\Phi$ from
$C$ to the twisted $E$ is an equivalence, and the opposite dual-kernel
transform (9.1) has right-adjoint correction
$(-\otimes\omega_C)[1]$. Trivialize $\omega_C$ by the invariant
differential induced on the torsor from the fixed Néron differential
of its Jacobian $E$. Then $\Psi\Phi\simeq[-1]$, and its shifted version
$\Psi[1]$ is the inverse. In particular an inverse-equivalence image
would be the above line shifted by $[1]$; silently ignoring this shift
would give a wrong numerical sign. These identities follow geometrically
from normalized Poincaré convolution and descend with the kernels:
the adjunction unit/counit are isomorphisms after faithfully flat
extension to an algebraic closure.

The primary construction is also given in Ramachandran–Rosenberg,
[*Derived categories of curves of genus one and torsors over abelian
varieties*, arXiv:2212.14497v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.14497v2),
Theorem2 and §§3.1,3.4–3.6. Their theorem writes the opposite Brauer
sign for its twisted-category convention. Here weights, the Picard gerbe,
and the kernel in (9.1) fix the sign directly. Their hypotheses are
a smooth projective geometrically connected genus-one curve over a
perfect field; $\mathbb Q$ qualifies. No finiteness of Sha is assumed.

### 9.2. An explicit elementary modification, not only a moduli point

The coordinator suggested an additional concrete construction of the
same stable numerical type. It makes the extension class visible.

Keep the closed point $D=\operatorname{Spec}L\subset C$ of degree $n$
and $\mathcal W_\beta$ from (5.1), now denoted $W$. Use the compatible
$\mu_n$ reduction $\mathscr G'$ obtained by trivializing $\det W$.
Then $\det W$ descends as the trivial line. At $O\in E$, the Picard
object $\mathcal O_C$ identifies the weight-one fiber of $W$ with
the ordinary vector space $H^0(D,\mathcal O_D)=L$.

**[NEW] Proposition 9.2 (the extension vector is the field unit).**
Choose a nonzero rational tangent vector $\tau\in T_OE$. There is
an actual twisted extension
$$
0\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow V_D\longrightarrow k(O)
\longrightarrow0
\tag{9.2}
$$
whose extension class is $1\otimes\tau\in L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}T_OE$.
Here $k(O)$ is weight one for the chosen neutralization at $O$.
The middle term is locally free, geometrically stable of rank $n$
and degree one, with descended determinant $\mathcal O_E(O)$.

*Proof.* For a locally free twisted $W$, the local resolution of the
skyscraper at the rational Cartier point $O$ gives
$$
\operatorname{Ext}^1(k(O),W)=W|_O\otimes T_OE.
$$
The sheaf $\mathcal Hom(k(O),W)$ is zero, so the local-to-global
spectral sequence introduces no additional obstruction to this extension.
The stated vector is rational and therefore defines (9.2).

Over an algebraic closure, $D$ is $n$ distinct points and
$W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^nL_i$ for distinct degree-zero Poincaré lines.
The conjugate coordinates of $1\in L$ are all one. Thus the extension
class has nonzero projection to every
$\operatorname{Ext}^1(k(O),L_i)$.
Over the discrete valuation ring at $O$, an extension by $k(O)$ is
free precisely when its vector in $W|_O$ is nonzero: if $t$ is a
uniformizer, adjoining a lift $v$ of the quotient gives the relation
$tv=w$ for a lift of that vector, and a unit coordinate of $w$
eliminates one generator. Hence $V_D$ is locally free. Away from $O$
it equals $W$. Its determinant is $\det W\otimes\mathcal O_E(O)$.

To check stability, let $U\subset V_D$ be a proper subbundle that
destabilizes it. Its degree is at least one. Its image in $k(O)$
must be all of $k(O)$, because $W$ is semistable of degree zero.
Set $U'=U\cap W$. It has
$\deg U'=\deg U-1\geq0$, while semistability of $W$ gives
$\deg U'\leq0$. Thus both inequalities are equalities.
Moreover $W/U'$ injects into the vector bundle $V_D/U$, so $U'$
is a subbundle.

A degree-zero subbundle of a direct sum of distinct stable degree-zero
lines is the sum of a subset of those lines. One can see this in the
abelian category of semistable bundles of slope zero: the $L_i$ are
pairwise nonisomorphic simple objects and their finite direct sum is
semisimple, so every subobject is such a sum. This use of that category
is justified directly by the slope inequality: kernels, images and
cokernels between slope-zero semistable bundles have slope zero and
are locally free whenever nonzero.

The subextension $0\to U'\to U\to k(O)\to0$ would make the class of
(9.2) map to zero in $\operatorname{Ext}^1(k(O),W/U')$. But a proper
subset leaves some $L_i$ in the quotient, and that component of the
extension class is nonzero. This contradiction proves stability.
$\square$

The same construction can be obtained directly from
$$0\to\mathcal O_C(-D)\to\mathcal O_C\to\mathcal O_D\to0.$$
The Poincaré transform gives
$\Phi(\mathcal O_D)=W$ and
$\Phi(\mathcal O_C)=k(O)[-1]$, after choosing the one-dimensional
cohomology/tangent identification. Since every degree-$-n$ Poincaré
twist of $\mathcal O_C(-D)$ has $H^0=0$ and $h^1=n$,
$\Phi(\mathcal O_C(-D))[1]$ is a rank-$n$ vector bundle. Transforming
the sequence gives (9.2); the restriction of the constant section
$1$ to $D$ explains its extension vector. Each geometric component
is nonzero also because it is the transform of the nonzero restriction
$\mathcal O_C\to k(d_i)$.

Thus, in compatible choices,
$$V_D\simeq\Phi(\mathcal O_C(-D))[1],
\qquad \Psi(V_D)\simeq\mathcal O_C(-D). \tag{9.3}$$
This is an explicit construction of the stable fixed-determinant
twisted bundle, separate from §3's successful base-field-gerbe descent.
Its arithmetic input remains the individual degree-$n$ point of $C$.
The transform returns exactly that degree-$n$ divisor class and supplies
neither a degree-one divisor nor a rational point of $C$.


## 10. Explicit descent and the transformations preserving the twist

Here are the cohomological coordinates, including the convention.
Over an algebraic closure put $L_y=\mathcal O_E([y]-[O])$ and
$$A(x,y)(W)=t_x^*W\otimes L_y.$$
The translation/tensor components of these functors compose by addition;
their functorial isomorphisms can have a scalar coherence factor. The
latter is retained below. Choose a geometric trivializing weight-one
line for the $\mathbb G_m$-gerbe. In the resulting ordinary category,
Galois descent for the twisted $E$ has the form
$$W\longmapsto L_{\xi_\sigma}\otimes\sigma W.\tag{10.1}$$
This defines $\xi\in H^1(\mathbb Q,E)$ as the *tensor-descent* class;
it is the Hochschild–Serre class with its sign fixed by (10.1).
The normalization at $O$ removes the constant Brauer term, so $\xi$
has exact order $n$. The translation/tensor cohomological pair of
(10.1) is $(0,\xi)$.

Let $F$ denote the normalized geometric Fourier–Mukai transform, with
numerical matrix $S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
on the column $(\operatorname{rank},\operatorname{degree})$. Let
$T$ be tensoring by $\mathcal O_E(O)$, with numerical matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The biextension identity of the Poincaré line gives
$$
F\,t_x^*\simeq(-\otimes L_{-x})F,
\qquad F(-\otimes L_y)\simeq t_y^*F.
\tag{10.2}
$$
For example the first identity follows by replacing the integration
variable $u$ by $u+x$: the kernel becomes
$\mathcal P(u-x,v)=\mathcal P(u,v)\otimes L_{-x}(v)$.
The second follows from
$\mathcal P(u,v)\otimes L_y(u)=\mathcal P(u,v+y)$.
Similarly $t_x^*\mathcal O(O)=\mathcal O([-x])$ gives
$$T A(x,y)T^{-1}\simeq A(x,y+x).\tag{10.3}$$
Thus conjugation by $F$ and $T$ acts on $(x,y)$ by the same matrices
$S$ and $T$. They are defined over $\mathbb Q$ on the untwisted
elliptic curve. Since these matrices generate $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$,
their standard-word representatives give on cohomological pairs
$$
g\binom{\alpha}{\xi}
 =\binom{a\alpha+b\xi}{c\alpha+d\xi},
\qquad g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10.4}
$$
Scalar coherence does not change (10.4).

In particular $S(0,\xi)=(\xi,0)$: the tensor part of (10.1)
has become a translation cocycle, the descent datum for a genus-one
torsor. Choosing its usual point-cocycle convention recovers $C$ or
its inverse as fixed in §5. This is the explicit descent reason for
the target in (9.1). The nonzero class has moved into the curve;
it has not disappeared. The dual Poincaré kernel changes the
corresponding signs, without changing its order or the target issue.

**[NEW] Proposition 10.1 (exact stabilizer of the cohomological pair).**
For the standard action (10.4), the stabilizer of $(0,\xi)$ is
$$
\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):
 b\equiv0,\quad a\equiv d\equiv1\pmod n\right\}.
\tag{10.5}
$$
Consequently a transformation in (10.5) sends $(n,1)$ to an object
whose numerical rank $an+b$ is divisible by $n$.

*Proof.* The transformed pair is $(b\xi,d\xi)$. Equality with
$(0,\xi)$ is exactly $n\mid b$ and $n\mid(d-1)$. The determinant
identity then gives $a\equiv1\pmod n$; conversely these conditions
suffice. The rank assertion is immediate. $\square$

Two limitations prevent overinterpreting (10.5). First it classifies
cohomological pairs, not all descended Fourier–Mukai kernels: matching
translation/tensor components can leave a scalar $2$-cocycle over
$\mathbb Q$. One must still neutralize that actual kernel-descent
gerbe, not declare it zero from matching pairs. This is the base-field
Brauer orbit in Antieau–Krashen–Ward,
[*Derived categories of torsors for abelian schemes*,
arXiv:1409.2580](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.2580), §4 and §5.
We do not require or assert that every matrix in (10.5) lifts here.

Second, the numerical matrix alone does not specify its action on
these pairs. Inversion $[-1]_E^*$ acts numerically as the identity
but negates $(x,y)$; a cohomological shift acts numerically by a sign
but commutes with every $A(x,y)$. If a standard representative is
composed with inversion, the condition becomes
$b\equiv0$, $a\equiv d\equiv-1\pmod n$. Both versions still force
$n\mid b$. In particular one cannot exclude the actual shift functor
by applying (10.5) to its numerical matrix alone. This distinction is
already visible from $F^2\simeq[-1]_E^*[-1]$.

There is an independent check covering every actual equivalence,
without assuming a classification of its kernels. In the compatible
normalization of §9 the geometric numerical image is exactly
$$
\operatorname{im}\bigl(K_0^\beta(E)\to\mathbb Z^2\bigr)
   =n\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z.
\tag{10.6}
$$
Ranks are divisible by $n$ by Lemma6.1. The two global bundles
$\mathcal W_\beta$ and $V$ have classes $(n,0)$ and $(n,1)$,
which generate the right side. Thus any actual self-equivalence with
geometric numerical matrix $g\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ must
preserve this lattice. Applying it to $(0,1)$ forces $n\mid b$.
Every image of $(n,1)$ then has rank divisible by $n$, even as a
complex with virtual rank, and cannot be a line or shifted line when
$n>1$.

This tests the proposed constructive next step on the actual twisted
category. Fourier–Mukai does construct a line, but on $C$; returning
to the fixed twisted $E$ restores the rank divisibility. To obtain an
HB-cycle splitting one still needs a point on the rank-one torsor of
§7, or a new period-independent splitting construction. The argument
does not rule out such a construction by other means.

### 10.2. The actual generic twisted lattice is finite, with period-dependent pairing

The equivalence constructs more than a numerical rank obstruction. It
identifies the entire Grothendieck group on the generic curve:
$$K_0^\beta(E)\simeq K_0(C)\simeq
\mathbb Z\oplus\operatorname{Pic}(C).\tag{10.7}$$
The second isomorphism is rank and determinant for a regular curve.
For example, a rational section of a line bundle expresses its class
by divisors, and the localization sequence expresses every vector-bundle
class by its rank and closed-point classes. Relations among the latter
are precisely principal divisors. This gives (10.7) without an
arithmetic-surface Gersten assumption.

Every rational Picard class on $C$ descends to a line bundle:
its obstruction lies in $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb Q)$, and each
$C(\mathbb Q_v)\ne\varnothing$ kills its localization by evaluation
at a local point. Brauer–Hasse–Noether then kills the obstruction.
In particular $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)=E(\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Z^2$ for389a1.
Period-index equality and the constructed $D$ give the split exact
sequence
$$
0\to E(\mathbb Q)\to\operatorname{Pic}(C)
 \xrightarrow{\deg} n\mathbb Z\to0,
$$
where choosing $\mathcal O_C(D)$ supplies the splitting. Consequently
$$K_0^\beta(E)\simeq\mathbb Z^4.\tag{10.8}$$
This concerns the generic curve, not an unproved calculation of the
twisted Grothendieck group of the arithmetic surface.

There is nevertheless a measured integral dependence on $n$.
Riemann–Roch gives the Euler form
$$\chi(A,B)=r_A d_B-d_A r_B$$
on a genus-one curve. Its radical on (10.7) is the rank-two
$\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$ subgroup. On the quotient, choose classes
with numerical types $(1,0)$ and $(0,n)$; its matrix is
$$\begin{pmatrix}0&n\\-n&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad\det=n^2.\tag{10.9}$$
Equivalence preserves this form, and (10.6) is its expression on the
twisted elliptic curve. Thus the actual finite free twisted group has
constant abstract rank, while its integral numerical embedding and
Euler pairing retain the individual period. No common unimodular
comparison that removes (10.9) has been constructed.


## 11. Source and circularity audit

The use of twisted sheaves, coherent extension, regular-surface reflexive
hulls, and the Azumaya correspondence is covered by the exact Lieblich
statements cited in §1. The genus-one stable fixed-determinant assertion
is proved separately in Lemma 2.1. In particular Lieblich's Corollary
3.2.2.4, which assumes genus at least two, is not being misapplied.

Lieblich's *Arithmetic aspects of moduli spaces of sheaves on curves*,
Clay Mathematics Proceedings **14** (2011), 95–119,
[§3, Theorem 3.4](https://www.claymath.org/library/proceedings/cmip014c.pdf),
has a conjectural Hasse-principle input for its general global-field
period–index result. It is not an unconditional source for our arithmetic
surface. The successful genus-one descent above uses a coarse point
and the ordinary Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem directly. The finite-field
and algebraically closed-field surface period–index theorems are likewise
not being applied to a surface over $\mathbb Z$.

The Poincaré pushforward uses the reviewed individual torsor period-index
argument, whose only global descent input is Brauer–Hasse–Noether after
local solubility. It assumes no finite Sha group and supplies no uniform
period bound. No prior numerical scripts were rerun. The completed
construction is a global period-$n$ twisted bundle and a period-dependent
splitting object; the missing uniform or rank-one descent is preserved
explicitly rather than absorbed into a moduli-existence assertion.
