# Trivial primary groups at 2, 3, and 389 for 389a1

Date: 2026-09-12. This note completes the primary-finiteness obligations
at the explicit set $S_0=\{2,3,389\}$ used in the
[uniform-witness argument](uniform-witness-attack.md). It does not prove
finiteness of the entire Tate–Shafarevich group.

Let
$$
E:\quad y^2+y=x^3+x^2-2x,
\qquad A_p=\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)[p^\infty].
$$

**[THEOREM, consequence of cited theorems and certified computations]**
$$
A_2=A_3=A_{389}=0.
\tag{1}
$$
The coordinator independently checked Lemma 1, the exceptional-zero factor, and the valuation comparison in §6.
The proof first establishes finiteness using orders of vanishing, then
uses certified regulator valuations to prove triviality. It also gives
the exact orders
$$
\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_3(E,T)=2,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}L_{389}(E,T)=3,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{T=0}f_{E,p}(T)=2\quad(p=3,389),
\tag{2}
$$
with the characteristic-series convention specified in §3 below.

## 1. Exact arithmetic inputs

**[THEOREM, exact calculation]** The model has
$$
\Delta=389,\qquad c_4=112,\qquad c_6=-856.
$$
It is minimal, good away from $389$, and has multiplicative type $I_1$
at $389$. The reduction there is split: the square criterion for a node
gives $-c_6\equiv78\equiv95^2\pmod{389}$. Its only Tamagawa number is
$c_{389}=1$. The valuation $v_{389}(j_E)=-1$ also excludes CM, since
a CM $j$-invariant is an algebraic integer.

The exact descent calculation returns
$$
\texttt{ellrank}(E)=[2,2,0,[[-2,0],[4,8]]],
$$
so $\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)=2$. This algebraic rank is the only
rank input needed below; no equality with complex analytic rank is used.

Direct finite-field point counts give
$$
\#E(\mathbb F_2)=5,\qquad\#E(\mathbb F_3)=6,
\qquad\#E(\mathbb F_5)=9.
\tag{3}
$$
Thus $a_3=-2$, and $3$ is good ordinary. Reduction at the good prime $5$
injects rational $2$-torsion into a group of odd order, proving
$E(\mathbb Q)[2]=0$. The script also verifies the stronger statement
$E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=0$.

## 2. The two Galois-image hypotheses

Write $\bar\rho_p:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$ for the
residual representation and $\rho_p:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_p)$
for the Tate-module representation. The Kato theorem used below requires
the latter to be surjective.

**[THEOREM, semistable character input]** If the residual representation
of a semistable rational elliptic curve is reducible, its two
semisimplified characters are $1$ and the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character.
This is Serre, *Propriétés galoisiennes des points d'ordre fini des courbes
elliptiques*, Invent. Math. **15** (1972), 259–331, p. 307; the scope
without any optimality assumption is explicitly restated in
[Agashe–Winters, arXiv:2510.04323v1, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04323v1#S2).
That restatement was directly read. Reducibility would therefore force
$p\mid\#E(\mathbb F_\ell)$ at every good $\ell\ne p$.

For $p=3$, the point count at $2$ rules this out. For $p=389$, both
counts at $2$ and $3$ rule it out. Hence $\bar\rho_3$ and
$\bar\rho_{389}$ are irreducible.

**[NEW, elementary deduction from Tate uniformization; coordinator-reviewed]
Lemma 1.** Both $\rho_3$ and $\rho_{389}$ are surjective.

*Proof at $389$.* Because the reduction is split multiplicative,
$E/\mathbb Q_{389}$ is a Tate curve with parameter $q_T$ satisfying
$v_{389}(q_T)=1$. In
$F=\mathbb Q_{389}(\mu_{389})$, with its normalized integer valuation,
the parameter has valuation $388$, which is prime to $389$. Consequently
adjoining $q_T^{1/389}$ gives an extension with ramification degree $389$.
The Tate description of $E[389]$, generated by $\mu_{389}$ and a
$389$th root of $q_T$, shows that inertia over $F$ contains a nonidentity
unipotent matrix in $\bar\rho_{389}$.

An irreducible subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$ containing a
nonidentity unipotent contains $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$: conjugate
its fixed line to a different line, use these two lines as a basis,
and obtain nontrivial upper and lower unipotents. Their powers give
the full two root groups over the prime field, which generate
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$. The determinant is the surjective
cyclotomic character, so the residual image is all of
$\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_{389})$. Since $389\ge5$, residual surjectivity
lifts to full $389$-adic surjectivity by
[Stein–Wuthrich, Proposition 7.2, p. 1778](https://wstein.org/papers/mcom2649-iwasawa-alg.pdf).
The published paragraph preceding that proposition also states directly
that, at a multiplicative prime $p$, nonsurjectivity can occur only if
there is a rational $p$-isogeny; the argument above checks the image
without using that shortcut.

*Proof at $3$.* The same multiplicative prime $389$ now supplies a
full integral root group. On $T_3E$, Tate uniformization gives an inertia
action
$$
\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&z\\0&1\end{pmatrix}:z\in\mathbb Z_3\right\}
\subseteq\rho_3(G_{\mathbb Q})
\tag{4}
$$
in a suitable $\mathbb Z_3$-basis. Indeed, at a residue-characteristic
$389$ place all $3$-power roots of unity are unramified, while the
$3$-adic tame character is surjective and its Tate-curve coefficient
is $v_{389}(q_T)=1$. Equivalently, over the maximal unramified extension,
adjoining each $3^n$th root of $q_T$ has ramification degree $3^n$.

Let $L$ be the primitive fixed line of (4). Irreducibility of
$\bar\rho_3$ gives an image element $g$ whose image of $L$ is a distinct
line modulo $3$. A primitive generator of $L$ and its image under $g$
therefore form a $\mathbb Z_3$-basis. In that basis (4) remains the full
upper root group: the change of off-diagonal coordinate multiplies its
parameter by a unit. Its conjugate by $g$ is the full lower root group,
again with a unit change of parameter. Elementary matrices over the
local ring $\mathbb Z_3$ generate $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z_3)$.
Thus $\rho_3(G_{\mathbb Q})$ contains $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z_3)$.
Its determinant is all of $\mathbb Z_3^\times$, so its image is
$\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_3)$. $\square$

The integral argument at $3$ matters: Proposition 7.2 is stated only
for $p\ge5$, and residual surjectivity by itself need not imply
$3$-adic surjectivity. The script's residual-image check at $389$ agrees
with the proof above, but is not substituted for the $3$-adic argument.

## 3. The exact Iwasawa implications

For $p=3,389$, let $f_{E,p}(T)$ be the characteristic series of the
Pontryagin dual of the ordinary cyclotomic Selmer group, in the
convention of Stein–Wuthrich §6.1, and let $L_p(E,T)$ use the variable
associated to $\gamma=1+p$. The letter $T$ in both series denotes
the same augmentation parameter. In particular, the split-multiplicative
factor below is retained explicitly.

The references are to Stein–Wuthrich, *Algorithms for the arithmetic
of elliptic curves using Iwasawa theory*, Math. Comp. **82** (2013),
1757–1792, DOI
[10.1090/S0025-5718-2012-02649-4](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2012-02649-4).
Both the author draft and
[published offprint](https://wstein.org/papers/mcom2649-iwasawa-alg.pdf)
were checked; their theorem statements used here agree.

**[THEOREM, Schneider–Perrin-Riou–Jones, SW Theorem 6.1, p. 1776]**
At an odd good ordinary or multiplicative prime,
$$
\operatorname{ord}_T f_{E,p}\ge \operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q).
\tag{5}
$$
Equality holds if and only if the corresponding $p$-adic height pairing
is nondegenerate and $A_p$ is finite. In particular, proving equality
in (5) proves finiteness; height nondegeneracy is not required as a
separate input to that direction.

**[THEOREM, Kato and the split-multiplicative extension, SW Theorem 7.3,
p. 1778]** If $E$ is semistable at the odd prime $p$ and $\rho_p$ is
surjective, then
$$
L_p(E,T)=f_{E,p}(T)d_p(T),\qquad d_p(T)\in\mathbb Z_p[[T]].
\tag{6}
$$
For split multiplicative reduction, $T\mid d_p(T)$. The source cites
Kato's Theorem 17.4 and Kobayashi's treatment of the exceptional zero.
Lemma 1 verifies the representation hypothesis for both primes here.

The weaker rational divisibility in SW Theorem 7.4 would suffice for
the good-ordinary order comparison whenever its hypotheses apply:
multiplication by a power of $p$ does not change $T$-order. The proof
here uses (6) directly at $3$ and therefore does not rely on extending
the wording of Theorem 7.4 to an unstated residual-image case.

## 4. Certified nonzero coefficients and the odd primary groups

The reproducible
[script](../../compute/scripts/certify_exceptional_primes.py)
and its [JSON output](../../compute/data/exceptional_primes_389a1.json)
give, in Sage 10.7,
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_3(E,T)&=O(3^5)+O(3^2)T
 +(2+2\cdot3+O(3^2))T^2+(2+O(3))T^3+O(T^4),\\
L_{389}(E,T)&=O(389^4)+O(389)T+O(389)T^2\\
&\hspace{10mm} +(355+O(389))T^3+(51+O(389))T^4+O(T^5).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
The first uses `series(3, prec=4)` and the second `series(2, prec=5)`.
Both use the exact `eclib` modular-symbol implementation and the
trivial Teichmüller component, with no quadratic twist.

**[THEOREM, certified computation]** The indicated $T^2$ coefficient
at $3$ and $T^3$ coefficient at $389$ are nonzero: their valuations are
zero and their absolute precisions are respectively two and one.
Consequently
$$
\operatorname{ord}_T L_3(E,T)\le2,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_T L_{389}(E,T)\le3.
\tag{8}
$$
No lower coefficient displayed as $O(p^k)$ is treated as an exact zero.
A certified nonzero coefficient gives an upper bound regardless of
whether earlier coefficients have been computed to sufficient precision
to resolve them.

*Proof of finiteness at $3$.* Equations (5), (6), and (8), with the
exact rank two, give
$$
2\le\operatorname{ord}_T f_{E,3}
\le\operatorname{ord}_T L_3(E,T)\le2.
$$
All inequalities are equalities. Theorem 6.1 therefore gives
$\#A_3<\infty$ and nondegeneracy of the $3$-adic height pairing.

*Proof of finiteness at $389$.* This time $T\mid d_{389}$ in (6), so
$$
2\le\operatorname{ord}_T f_{E,389}
\le\operatorname{ord}_T L_{389}(E,T)-1\le2.
$$
Again all inequalities are equalities, proving $\#A_{389}<\infty$
and nondegeneracy of the height pairing of Theorem 6.1. This also proves
the two exact analytic $p$-adic orders in (2). $\square$

If the exceptional factor had been omitted, the $389$-adic computation
would yield only $\operatorname{ord}_T f_{E,389}\le3$, which is
insufficient to invoke the equality criterion at rank two.

**Software verification.** The installed
`pAdicLseriesOrdinary.series` documentation states that each coefficient's
precision is proved; the same guarantee appears in the
[official Sage documentation](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/arithmetic_curves/sage/schemes/elliptic_curves/padic_lseries.html#sage.schemes.elliptic_curves.padic_lseries.pAdicLseriesOrdinary.series).
The source computes finite modular-symbol sums and truncates individual
coefficients using `_prec_bounds`, rather than assigning an observed
floating-point accuracy. SW §3, especially Propositions 3.1 and 3.5,
explains the coefficientwise convergence and precision bounds.
For this input the modular symbols are $p$-integral; both residual
representations are irreducible. The installed code uses denominator
bound zero in this case. No numerical `ellanalyticrank` call or BSD
leading-value estimate appears in the certificate script.

The valuation-zero observations in (7) are used in this section only
to prove the coefficients are nonzero. A unit coefficient alone does
not prove triviality of $A_p$; the additional regulator and local-factor
comparison is supplied in §6.

## 5. The two-primary group

**[THEOREM, exact computation]** The script obtains
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{Sel}_2(E/\mathbb Q)=2
\tag{9}
$$
with both PARI and the direct `mwrank` object. Calling the latter directly
avoids reusing Sage's algorithm-independent Selmer-rank cache.

The installed Sage implementation of `selmer_rank(algorithm="pari")`
returns the actual $2$-Selmer dimension, not merely an algebraic rank
bound. It reconstructs that dimension as
$$
r_2+\dim E(\mathbb Q)[2]+s
$$
from `ellrank` output $[r_1,r_2,s,L]$. The PARI manual states that the
$2$-Selmer dimension and the Cassels-pairing quantity used here are
computed unconditionally; see
[PARI, `ellrank`](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html/Elliptic_curves.html#ellrank).
The local implementation and manual were read, and the returned values
$r_2=2$, $s=0$, and $\dim E(\mathbb Q)[2]=0$ were checked.

*Proof that $A_2=0$.* The finite Kummer exact sequence is
$$
0\longrightarrow E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_2(E/\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)[2]\longrightarrow0.
$$
Its left term has dimension two by rank two and the absence of rational
$2$-torsion. Equation (9) makes the right term zero. Any nonzero
$2$-primary torsion group has an element of order two: from an element
of order $2^n$, multiply by $2^{n-1}$. Thus
$\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)[2]=0$ forces all of $A_2$ to vanish, including any
possible divisible part. $\square$

## 6. Regulator valuations upgrade finiteness to triviality

Let $R_p$ denote the canonical cyclotomic $p$-adic regulator on a full
Mordell–Weil basis, with the logarithm normalized by $\log_p(p)=0$.
Put $g_p=\log_p(1+p)$, so $v_p(g_p)=1$ for both odd primes considered.
The Iwasawa normalization of the rank-two regulator is
$\operatorname{Reg}_\gamma=R_p/g_p^2$; this is SW §4.4. At a split
multiplicative prime the additional factor in the leading-term formula
is $\mathcal L_p/g_p$, not just $\mathcal L_p$.

Because the Tamagawa product and rational torsion order are one,
define the two complete algebraic factors by
$$
B_3=\frac{(1-\alpha^{-1})^2 R_3}{g_3^2},\qquad
B_{389}=\frac{\mathcal L_{389}R_{389}}{g_{389}^3},
\tag{10}
$$
where $\alpha$ is the $3$-adic unit root of $X^2+2X+3$.
Having already proved finiteness and equality of characteristic order
with rank, the leading-term assertion in SW Theorem 6.1 gives
$$
v_p(f_{E,p}^*)=v_p(B_p)+v_p(\#A_p),\qquad p=3,389.
\tag{11}
$$
Here $f_{E,p}^*$ is the nonzero coefficient of degree two in the
characteristic series. No complex BSD formula enters (11).

**[THEOREM, certified saturation and computation]** A full basis is
$P=(-1,1)$, $Q=(0,-1)$. The
[basis certificate](../../compute/data/bsd_archimedean_interval.json)
records exact rank two, full saturation, the index three of the raw
descent points, and the exact coordinate relations. In particular
$P+Q=(4,8)$, so $Q,P+Q$ is also a full basis. The latter is used for
the $3$-adic regulator. Using the unsaturated raw descent points would
multiply the regulator by $3^2$ and give the wrong valuation for this
application.

**[THEOREM, certified $3$-adic regulator]** PARI returns
$$
R_3=2+2\cdot3+3^2+3^3+2\cdot3^6+3^7+O(3^8),
\qquad v_3(R_3)=0,
\tag{12}
$$
from
`ellpadicregulator(E,3,10,[[0,-1],[4,8]])`. The
[PARI definition of this routine](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html/Elliptic_curves.html#ellpadicregulator)
is the Gram determinant for the canonical cyclotomic height. At good
ordinary reduction this is the unit-root height: its local description
uses `ellpadics2` to select the unit-root line. It is the regulator
before division by a chosen cyclotomic generator's logarithm, so the
$g_3^{-2}$ in (10) must be applied separately. PARI supports this
calculation at $3$, even though Sage's high-level positive-rank
`p_primary_bound` algorithm is not implemented at that prime.
The value and absolute precision in (12) were independently recomputed
while auditing the updated certificate.

To check the multiplier valuation without numerical assumptions, write
$\beta=3/\alpha$. Then
$$
(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)=1-a_3+3=6.
$$
The factor $1-\beta$ is a unit and $\alpha$ is a unit. Thus
$v_3(1-\alpha^{-1})=1$, and
$$
v_3(B_3)=2+0-2=0.
\tag{13}
$$

**[THEOREM, certified $389$-adic values]** Sage's Tate-curve regulator
and $\mathcal L$-invariant give
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{389}&=232\cdot389^2+160\cdot389^3+\cdots+O(389^{14}),\\
\mathcal L_{389}&=191\cdot389+370\cdot389^2+\cdots+O(389^8).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
Their exact certified valuations are two and one. The source of
`L_invariant` computes $\log_{389}(q_T)/v_{389}(q_T)$, with
$\log_{389}(389)=0$, matching the convention in (10). Consequently
$$
v_{389}(B_{389})=2+1-3=0.
\tag{15}
$$
An independent recomputation of (14) and (15) gave
$B_{389}=355+151\cdot389+\cdots+O(389^7)$, a unit.
The installed Tate-curve regulator routine explicitly uses the Werner
correction and the SW sign convention; its implementation returns the
rank-two Gram determinant. The separate $\mathcal L$-factor in (10)
has therefore not already been included in $R_{389}$.

*Proof of triviality at $3$ and $389$.* The equality of orders already
proved in §4 gives $\operatorname{ord}_T d_3=0$ and
$\operatorname{ord}_T d_{389}=1$ in (6). Comparing first nonzero
coefficients therefore yields
$$
[T^2]L_3=f_{E,3}^*d_3(0),\qquad
[T^3]L_{389}=f_{E,389}^*[T]d_{389}.
$$
Both coefficients on the left are units by (7). Every factor on the
right is integral by (6); hence $v_p(f_{E,p}^*)=0$. Equations
(11), (13), and (15) now give $v_p(\#A_p)=0$. Since $A_p$ is a finite
$p$-group, this forces $\#A_p=1$ for $p=3,389$. $\square$

As a separate software check, `E.sha().p_primary_bound(389)` returns
the exponent zero. Its
[documented contract](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/arithmetic_curves/sage/schemes/elliptic_curves/sha_tate.html#sage.schemes.elliptic_curves.sha_tate.Sha.p_primary_bound)
is a proved upper bound $p^e$ for the primary group, under hypotheses
verified here. The installed implementation checks the reduction and
Galois-image conditions before using the valuation comparison. The
conjectural interpretation of a bare `an_padic` value is not used.
The direct proof (10)–(15) explains why the result here is unconditional.

## 7. Reproduction and the remaining uniform obligation

Run from the repository root:

```sh
DOT_SAGE="$PWD/.tools/sage-home" .tools/sage/bin/sage -python \
  compute/scripts/certify_exceptional_primes.py
```

The script checks the equation, conductor, algebraic rank, torsion,
both $2$-Selmer calculations, residual image at $389$, reduction types,
the saturated-basis certificate, and the valuations and absolute
precisions of the decisive coefficients and regulators. It also checks
the $\mathcal L$-invariant and the provable exponent bound at $389$.
The theorem arguments in §§2–6 turn those data into (1).
The Galois-image deduction in Lemma 1 is written out rather than encoded
as an unverified assumption in the computation.

**[THEOREM, conditional reduction]** If a two-prime unit Kurihara witness
is proved for **every** prime $p\notin\{2,3,389\}$, then the
[unit-index theorem](uniform-witness-attack.md) gives $A_p=0$ at every
such prime. Together with (1), primary decomposition then proves
$\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)=0$.

If a future uniform argument excludes additional prime divisors of an
integer $C$, the present note does not cover those additional primes.
One must establish their primary finiteness separately. Thus completing
the obligations at $S_0$ does not by itself discharge the finite
exception set of an as-yet-unproved almost-all-prime theorem. Neither
version supplies the complex BSD leading-term comparison.
