# Coherent integral objects and their leading realizations

Date: 2026-09-12. Owner: coordinator. The new deductions passed the
[independent review](review-coherent-moment.md). The scope is a proposed route to a uniform integral comparison, not a
proof of BSD or an elliptic-curve counterexample.

## 1. The candidate implication to test

The [previous interpolation countermodels](derived-comparison-attack.md)
allow arbitrary coefficients depending on p. A stronger proposed route would
start with one fixed integral algebraic object, primitive at all good primes,
and try to deduce a primitive leading specialization outside a fixed set of
denominator primes. This note tests that stronger implication and isolates
the exact integral comparison it needs.

A current motivation is Banwait's rank-two CM criterion in
[arXiv:2609.08431v1, Theorem B and §§2.5, 6, 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08431v1).
Under its stated excluded-prime hypotheses, the second p-adic coefficient's
unit condition tests both a unit normalized regulator and trivial primary
Sha. The paper expresses this through critical Hecke values using the Katz
measure. We use it as a source of the comparison problem, not as a theorem
that all the required unit conditions hold. This source is a September 2026
preprint; the elementary construction below is independent of its theorems.

## 2. One fixed object, not an arbitrary family

Let $R=\mathbb Z[U,U^{-1}]$, with augmentation ideal $I=(U-1)$, and set
$$\theta=U+U^{-1}-2=\frac{(U-1)^2}{U}.$$
This is one integral group-ring element, with coefficients independent of p.
It is the signed atomic measure on the multiplicative group generated by 2
having weights $1,1,-2$ at $2,2^{-1},1$.

For each odd prime p, define
$$m_p=\frac{\log_p2}{\log_p(1+p)}\in\mathbb Z_p,\qquad
\phi_p(U)=(1+T)^{m_p},\qquad F_p(T)=\phi_p(\theta).$$
The logarithm is the Iwasawa logarithm with $\log_p p=0$.
Since 2 is a p-adic unit, its logarithm lies in $p\mathbb Z_p$, while
$v_p(\log_p(1+p))=1$. The binomial coefficients of $m_p\in\mathbb Z_p$
are integral: approximate $m_p$ by nonnegative integers and use continuity
of each binomial polynomial. Thus $\phi_p$ is a well-defined ring map to
$\mathbb Z_p[[T]]$.

**[NEW] Proposition 2.1.** For every odd p:

1. $m_p\ne0$ and $\operatorname{ord}_T F_p=2$.
2. $F_p(-T/(1+T))=F_p(T)$.
3. $[T^2]F_p=m_p^2$.
4. If $k=v_p(m_p)$, then $\mu(F_p)=0$ and $\lambda(F_p)=2p^k$.

*Proof.* If $\log_p2=0$, the inverse logarithm/exponential on
$1+p\mathbb Z_p$ would give $2^{p-1}=1$ in $\mathbb Q_p$, contradicting
the nonzero rational integer $2^{p-1}-1$. Put $A=(1+T)^{m_p}$. Then
$$F_p=A+A^{-1}-2=(A-1)^2/A.$$
Since $A-1=m_pT+O(T^2)$, the first and third assertions follow.
The involution sends $A$ to $A^{-1}$, proving the second.

Write $m_p=p^k u$ with $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. Modulo p,
$$\overline A=(1+T^{p^k})^u
=1+\bar u T^{p^k}+O(T^{2p^k}).$$
This identity follows from the Frobenius identity and continuous binomial
expansion. Hence $\overline{F_p}$ has first nonzero coefficient
$\bar u^2$ in degree $2p^k$. It is a nonzero mod-p series, proving both
the vanishing of mu and the stated lambda. $\square$

The global leading class of $\theta$ is primitive:
$$\theta\equiv(U-1)^2\pmod{I^3}.$$
But the induced map on augmentation cotangent spaces is multiplication by
$m_p$, and its degree-two map is multiplication by $m_p^2$. A primitive
global leading class therefore need not have a unit leading realization.
This is an integral index of the realization map, not failure of primitivity
of the original object or of the full realized series.

## 3. A certified exceptional prime

**[NEW] Proposition 3.1.**
$$v_p(m_p)=v_p(2^{p-1}-1)-1.$$
In particular at $p=1093$ the second coefficient of $F_p$ has valuation two,
although its central order is two, its functional-equation sign is positive,
and its mu-invariant is zero. Its first unit coefficient occurs in degree 2186.

*Proof.* On $1+p\mathbb Z_p$, the first term of the logarithm series has
strictly smaller valuation than all the subsequent terms. Thus
$v_p(\log_p z)=v_p(z-1)$. Apply this to $z=2^{p-1}$, use that $p-1$ is a
unit, and divide by $\log_p(1+p)$ of valuation one. The exact integer
calculation $v_{1093}(2^{1092}-1)=2$ gives the result by Proposition 2.1.
$\square$

We make **no assertion** that the exceptional primes for this object are
infinite. This example disproves an all-good-primes implication and a rule
that the only exceptions come from denominators of the fixed group-ring
coefficients. It does not disprove an unspecified almost-all-primes claim.

The natural normalized identity in this example is nevertheless exact:
$$\frac{[T^2]F_p}{m_p^2}=1\quad\text{for every odd }p.$$
At the real place the exponential realization
$U\mapsto e^{s\log2}$ similarly has second Taylor coefficient $(\log2)^2$
and normalized ratio one. These are specified realizations of the same
group-ring identity. The model illustrates the desired cancellation of a
regulator-like factor; it does not identify that factor with an elliptic height.

## 4. A positive criterion for leading tensors

The general mechanism behind the example can be stated exactly.

**[NEW] Lemma 4.1 (leading symbol under a logarithmic realization).** Let
$M\simeq\mathbb Z^d$, let $\theta\in I^r\mathbb Z[M]$, and identify
$\operatorname{gr}_I\mathbb Z[M]$ with $\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}M$.
Write $Q=\sigma_r(\theta)$ for the degree-r symbol. For a characteristic-zero
field K and an additive map $\ell:M\to K$, the exponential realization
$[m]\mapsto\exp(s\ell(m))$ satisfies
$$[s^j]\theta_\ell(s)=0\ (j<r),\qquad [s^r]\theta_\ell(s)=Q(\ell).$$
For $K=\mathbb Q_p$ and $\ell(M)\subset\mathbb Z_p$, the binomial realization
$[m]\mapsto(1+T)^{\ell(m)}$ likewise has $[T^r]\theta_\ell=Q(\ell)$.

*Proof.* In a basis of M the group ring is a Laurent polynomial ring and
its augmentation ideal is generated by $X_i-1$. In degree r, replace each
$X_i-1$ by $s\ell(e_i)+O(s^2)$, or by $T\ell(e_i)+O(T^2)$ respectively.
Terms in $I^{r+1}$ do not contribute. This proves the statements without
analytic convergence assumptions on the formal exponential. $\square$

**[NEW] Lemma 4.2 (integrality after a primitive tensor comparison).**
Let $Q,D\in\operatorname{Sym}^r\mathbb Z^d$, with D nonzero and primitive
(the gcd of its integer coefficients is one). If $Q=cD$ in
$\operatorname{Sym}^r K^d$ for a characteristic-zero field K and a scalar c,
then $c\in\mathbb Z$.

*Proof.* Choose integer coefficients $b_i$ giving a Bezout relation
$\sum_i b_i D_i=1$ among the coefficients of D. Then
$c=\sum_i b_i Q_i\in\mathbb Z$. $\square$

For example, D may be the determinant of a matrix of linear forms, provided
its coefficient tensor is primitive. If a single integral symbol Q is
proved proportional to that determinant tensor, its normalized coefficient
is an integer in every realization with nonzero determinant. This is stronger
than testing proportionality after one numerical evaluation: for a single
evaluation Q(ell)/D(ell), no polynomial proportionality follows.

## 5. Attempt to apply the criterion to BSD

The fixed CM theta construction motivates asking for an integral leading
symbol which is identified with an arithmetic regulator tensor. The calculation
above shows why its whole-measure primitivity alone is not enough. One must
also identify the integral leading tensor and the realization index, allowing
the same nonunit factors on the analytic and height sides to cancel.

**[GAP Tensor comparison].** For an elliptic curve with proved equal algebraic
and analytic rank r, construct a finite free integral module M, an actual
integral symbol Q obtained from the relevant arithmetic/analytic construction,
and a primitive regulator tensor D in $\operatorname{Sym}^r M$, together with
specified real and p-adic realization maps. Prove the polynomial/tensor
identity $Q=cD$, and prove that the real realized quotient equals the BSD
quotient with its exact period, torsion, Tamagawa, and full-lattice factors.

This is a sufficient target for integrality of the complex quotient by Lemma
4.2. It does not by itself determine Sha or establish the rank statement.
For 389a1, such integrality would settle its separate leading-number target
because the quotient is already rigorously between 0.9931 and 1.0077.

An arbitrary formal module with variables named after heights does not meet
the target: it manufactures the desired tensor without identifying it with
the actual L-function. Nor does the presently read CM moment criterion supply
the tensor comparison. Its polynomial moment weights vary with p (weight
$2p-1$ critical values), and its unit test includes the normalized p-adic
regulator. The attempted inference from a fixed generating theta object to
a primitive leading coefficient therefore stops at a specific realization
and regulator comparison, rather than at existence of the global object.

No full BSD assertion is concluded. This attempt improves the proposed
p-independent-object approach by specifying exactly which integral map and
tensor identity would have to be proved, and by testing the weaker shortcut
on one genuinely coherent integral family.
